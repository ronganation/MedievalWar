## 4. What This Tranche Substantively Added

### 4.1 Norway settlement identity

Norway now has a coherent settlement-development model built around multiple possible paths rather than one universal town ladder. Distributed communities, local centers, markets and assemblies, emerging town-port settlements, regional centers, royal and household centers, ecclesiastical centers, maritime hosts, protected sites, inland crossings, northern service centers, attached sites, independent strategic nodes, coastal networks, and temporary sites can coexist as different expressions.

Distributed, seasonal, attached, independent, and networked forms may be mature outcomes. A location does not need to become a city to count as developed. Geography, population, property, routes, institutions, military capability, economy, information, and Opening State remain separately owned.

### 4.2 Building atlas and host eligibility

The Norway building atlas translates settlement-development functions into readable building, service, complex, regional, military-interface, economic, route, governance, ecclesiastical, and Norse Theater cards. The host-eligibility matrix tests which families and branches can plausibly operate in different settlement and strategic-site expressions.

Eligibility does not equal availability, construction, operation, output, or an opening fact. Buildings do not create people, specialists, goods, institutions, units, ships, crews, authority, property, routes, or trade.

### 4.3 Named-site candidacy and historical firewalls

The candidacy layer applies the expression system to named Norwegian places and networks without creating exact bindings. Nidaros retains separate settlement, assembly, ecclesiastical, sacred-memory, royal-presence, market, maritime, and regional-network interfaces. Bergen remains an emergence-area candidate rather than an established January 1062 city. Oslo, Tonsberg, Sarpsborg, Borgund, Giske, Oreting, and the Nidelva mouth retain explicit later-history and ownership firewalls.

Candidacy is not Registry admission, Opening State activation, ownership, control, population, route operation, port operation, market activity, institutional existence, or military capacity.

### 4.4 Progression, pressure, decline, and recovery

The progression authority defines eligibility, emergence, consolidation, specialization, balance, plateau, stagnation, disruption, regression, reorientation, recovery, and partial or renewed operation. Progression depends on supported capacity and owner receipts rather than population alone or a simple building count.

The pressure and event portfolio distinguishes background conditions, recognized pressure, acute crisis, response, persistent consequence, stabilization, recovery, reorientation, and residual change. Pressure does not guarantee crisis. Crisis does not guarantee regression. Recovery need not restore the former role.

### 4.5 History, owner receipts, and visible chronicle

The history authority separates owner state, committed occurrence, evidence and account, observer knowledge, and visible chronicle. The presentation layer may explain owner-backed history but does not create truth, current state, public knowledge, or exact past events.

Contradictory accounts, stale knowledge, restricted information, corrections, retractions, residual layers, and later discoveries remain representable without silently rewriting prior knowledge.

### 4.6 Player and AI investment choices

The choice portfolio introduces finite investment priorities, reservation channels, project-load states, opportunity costs, recovery allocation, and Norway-specific comparative choices. The player and AI may maintain, repair, expand, specialize, distribute, concentrate, protect, reorient, mothball, reserve, or hold.

No choice creates money, labor, population, materials, specialists, rights, institutions, projects, units, ships, or crews. Maintenance, emergency reserves, local defense, political consent, route capacity, housing, supply, and alternative regional development all remain real opportunity costs.

### 4.7 Regional networks and inter-settlement dependence

Norway's settlement system now functions as a network rather than a set of self-sufficient places. The network portfolio defines regional civic centers, markets, craft and repair centers, rural and fishery support zones, maritime hosts, inland crossings, royal itinerary hosts, ecclesiastical centers, protected sites, district military-service support, warning and relay nodes, northern service centers, attached sites, independent nodes, and temporary hosts.

Relationships may be potential, available, active, constrained, disrupted, blocked, substituted, recovering, residual, or unknown. Direction and benefit may be asymmetric. A map connection does not create a flow. Redundancy requires real fallback capacity rather than duplicate labels.

### 4.8 Regional specialization, balance, and resilience

The specialization and resilience layer defines specialization depth, regional balance, concentration and distribution models, resilience dimensions, fallback classes, stress tests, and recovery reorganization. Norway's regions may specialize without becoming identical, self-sufficient, or dependent on one universal center.

The system tests blocked maritime hosts, Nidaros multi-owner disruption, lost inland crossings, damaged specialist centers, local-defense hollowing, unavailable royal hosts, interrupted ecclesiastical and records functions, northern seasonal failure, community loss, and overcommitted town-port emergence.

### 4.9 Comparative planning scenarios

Twelve comparative scenarios exercise settlement, cluster, regional-network, and interregional-gateway decisions. Every scenario includes three credible options and a hold option. The scenarios cover local growth, landing networks, protection, markets and assemblies, Nidaros multi-function balance, southern maritime-inland gateways, western fjord craft and ports, inland crossing loss, itinerant government, ecclesiastical development, local defense versus concentrated military service, and northern permanent versus seasonal capacity.

No scenario is scripted history or a universal best-choice ranking. Candidate options do not approve projects, reserve resources, activate events, or guarantee results.
