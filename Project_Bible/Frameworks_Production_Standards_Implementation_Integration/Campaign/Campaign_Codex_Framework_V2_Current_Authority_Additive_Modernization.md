Medieval War — Systems/Campaign_Codex_Framework.md

# Overview

The Campaign Codex serves as Medieval War's in-game encyclopedia, reference system, historical archive, and learning tool.

The Codex exists to:

- Explain systems
- Explain mechanics
- Explain historical context
- Explain characters
- Explain dynasties
- Explain units
- Explain factions
- Explain buildings
- Improve accessibility
- Reduce player confusion

The Codex should function as a living reference guide that evolves alongside the campaign.

Players should never be required to leave the game to understand how the game works.

---

# Core Philosophy

Complex systems require clear explanations.

Depth should not require confusion.

Historical authenticity should remain accessible.

The Codex serves as the bridge between:

Game Systems

↓

Player Understanding

↓

Strategic Decision Making

The goal is not to simplify the game.

The goal is to explain it.

Overview

Macbeth ruled Alba during a turbulent period of Scottish history and emerged from the powerful Moray political sphere.

His reign demonstrated the continuing importance of regional authority within Scotland and challenged traditional assumptions regarding kingship, legitimacy, and dynastic succession.

Although absent at campaign start, Macbeth remains one of the most influential figures shaping the political landscape inherited by Malcolm III.

---

Historical Significance

Macbeth represents one of Scotland's most important alternative political paths.

His reign illustrates:

- The strength of regional power centers
- The importance of House Moray
- The instability of early Scottish succession
- The balance between regional authority and royal authority

His defeat created the conditions that allowed House Dunkeld to strengthen royal control and pursue greater national consolidation.

---

Legacy

Macbeth's influence remains visible through:

- House Moray
- Scottish Regional Politics
- Royal Consolidation
- Dynastic Legitimacy Debates
- Historical Momentum

Players beginning in 1061–1062 enter a Scotland still shaped by the consequences of Macbeth's reign.

---

Related Codex Entries

- Malcolm III
- House Dunkeld
- House Moray
- Scottish Consolidation Arc
- Historical Momentum
- Dynasty System

---

Campaign Relevance

Macbeth is not part of the active campaign roster.

However, his actions directly shaped the political environment inherited by Malcolm III.

His entry demonstrates how Historical Momentum can continue influencing events long after a historical figure has died.

## V2 authority statement

This V2 layer preserves the complete original Codex framework and modernizes it into the shared Campaign knowledge, explanation, history, discovery, accessibility, and player-learning authority for Medieval War.

The Codex explains owner-approved state. It does not create, activate, mutate, repair, or infer that state.

## Purpose and player promise

The Codex must let the game teach itself without displacing the campaign map, strategic planning, Realm identity, warfare, diplomacy, economy, settlement development, or character and dynasty causality.

The normal player experience remains concise:

```yaml
DefaultExperience:
  CampaignMapCentrality: true
  ContextualHelp: ImmediateWhenNeeded
  CodexReadingRequiredForBasicPlay: false
  SummaryFirst: true
  OptionalDepth: true
  HiddenStateProtection: required
  RoutineNotificationSpam: prohibited
  ExternalManualRequired: false
```

The Codex should answer five questions quickly:

1. What is this?
2. Why does it matter now?
3. What is known, unknown, stale, contested, or externally owned?
4. Which systems and owners affect it?
5. What can the player do, and what remains outside the player’s current authority or knowledge?

## Notification and discovery discipline

The Codex may receive discovery notices for meaningful changes, such as:

- a newly known system or actor;
- a primary Objective;
- a major milestone;
- a confirmed Designation change;
- a victory condition;
- a major historical event;
- a new institution, route, unit family, or region entering player knowledge.

Routine decay, minor relationship changes, low-salience progress, and repeated rediscovery should enter summaries rather than generate repeated notifications.

## AI explanation boundary

The Codex may explain:

- high-level Realm AI priorities;
- owner-visible inputs;
- selected and rejected alternatives;
- stale, unknown, or excluded inputs;
- objective or strategic proposal reasons;
- public decision receipts.

It may not expose:

- hidden external private state;
- secret AI plans unavailable to the player;
- future knowledge;
- internal randomness streams in normal play;
- every raw score or implementation detail.

```text
Explainable AI != Omniscient AI
Proposal Explanation != Owner Command
Excluded Input != Missing System
```

- owner-confirmed Campaign History references;
- stable entry aliases and version mappings;

The Codex must not reapply Opening State, recreate entities, restore resolved disputes, reinstate lost Designations, reactivate expired Objectives, or overwrite Campaign divergence.

AdvancedSaveProtection:
  ReapplyOpeningState: prohibited

## Determinism and replay

Codex presentation does not own Simulation determinism, but a dynamic view must be reproducible from the same:

- authoritative state;
- knowledge and visibility state;
- owner versions;
- localization version;
- accessibility mode;
- presentation rules.

Replay may reconstruct Codex views from recorded owner state and Campaign History receipts. Replay must not use later knowledge when presenting an earlier turn unless the replay mode explicitly allows an omniscient developer view.
