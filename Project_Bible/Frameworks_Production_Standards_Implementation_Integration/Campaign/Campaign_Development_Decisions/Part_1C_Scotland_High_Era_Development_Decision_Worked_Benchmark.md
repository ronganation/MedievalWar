## 4. Player-Facing Split-Panel Prototype

### 4.1 Traditional Scottish Development

**Identity:** Consolidate the Realm through stronger Crown direction, burgh and civic institutions, integrated regional hosts, durable field armies, frontier control, and a deeper domestic military architecture.

**Major advantages:**

- Stronger coordinated spear, armored-foot, civic, castle, and retained-service development.
- Better integration of regional hosts into sustained territorial campaigning.
- Stronger conventional cavalry architecture through knightly shock, selected reserve control, and frontier operations.
- Better internal resilience, reinforcement organization, and southward land-campaign potential.
- Clearer investment channels through Crown, magnate, burgh, castle, and frontier institutions.

**Material tradeoffs:**

- Greater political competition among Crown, magnates, burghs, regional providers, and specialist institutions.
- Higher equipment, horse, cadre, civic, castle, and recovery burdens.
- Maritime and western opportunities remain possible but are less strongly favored.
- Dense field-army development can create frontage, supply, and specialist-recovery pressures.

**Preserved:** Existing western units, veterans, island relationships, and valid maritime capability.

### 4.2 Scoto-Norse Western Sea Development / Lords of the Western Sea

**Identity:** Develop Scotland's western and northern relationships into a Norse-Gaelic, island, coastal, and maritime-compatible system of regional warfare without converting Scotland into Norway or a generic Viking faction.

**Major advantages:**

- Stronger western regional and lordship relationships.
- Layered spear, shield, axe, javelin, sling, bow, and mounted-support combinations.
- Better readiness to exploit valid island, coastal, port, transport, and amphibious opportunities.
- Stronger diplomatic and cooperative development possibilities with Scandinavian powers.
- Optional retaliatory or reverse-sea-road campaigning when war and world state justify it.

**Material tradeoffs:**

- Fragmented providers and stronger dependence on political relationships, routes, reception points, and recovery capacity.
- No broad domestic heavy-cavalry institution or routine knightly mass.
- Western land forces remain dependent on infantry protection, missiles, terrain, transport, and supply.
- Selection does not create ships, crews, ports, transport capacity, horses, people, or equipment.

**Preserved:** Existing Crown, burgh, inland, common-host, and Traditional-associated formations already valid in campaign state.

## 5. Direction-Weighting Matrix

| Category | Traditional Scottish Development | Lords of the Western Sea |
|---|---|---|
| Political emphasis | Crown, territorial consolidation, burghs, integrated regional service | Crown-western bargaining, island and coastal lordship, relationship-based regional service |
| Infantry emphasis | Coordinated host, defensive spear, armored sectors, civic and specialist development | Affordable western frontage, retained shields, household axes, regional assault, maritime-compatible infantry |
| Missile emphasis | Civic crossbows, developed field archery, selected retained archery | Sling and javelin harassment, practical western bows, selected war-bow concentration |
| Mounted emphasis | Knightly shock, reserve control, frontier operations | Reaction shaping, pursuit, conditional regional mobility, scarce medium-heavy contact |
| Geographic emphasis | Internal routes, frontier, Lowlands, burgh and castle network | Isles, western seaboard, coastal routes, Irish Sea and western approaches where supported |
| Diplomacy | Broad Realm diplomacy with no compulsory hostility toward Scandinavia | Cooperative Scandinavian relations favored when useful; war remains possible but not mandatory |
| Campaign fantasy | A stronger consolidated Scotland capable of carrying war south | A western maritime Scotland able to cooperate, raid, retaliate, or project power along sea roads |
| Principal risk | Expensive institutional depth and political competition | Provider fragmentation and dependence on real maritime inputs |

### Persistence contract

- A Traditional decision does not disband Western veterans or invalidate a physically and politically valid western provider.
- A Western decision does not erase burgh crossbowmen, existing Crown institutions, feudal cavalry, or inland service already present.
- New recruitment may become favored, slower, costlier, conditional, or blocked according to provider, pool, region, infrastructure, equipment, horse, route, and political truth.
- Similar equipment does not trigger automatic deletion or merger. Distinct provider, access, progression, cost, availability, battlefield purchase, and campaign flavor remain valid differentiators.

## 9. Diplomacy and Maritime Contract

Friendly relations with Scandinavian powers remain a valid Western outcome. The path may support trade, access, alliance, negotiated service, shared sea-road security, or other owner-authorized relationships. It does not require hostility.

If war develops, retaliation or reverse-sea-road conquest can become an objective or opportunity. It is never treated as an automatic obligation or as proof that Western development means becoming Scandinavian.

## 13. Failure, Recovery, Redirection, and Convergence

### Traditional failure

Institutional competition, missing equipment, weak Crown reach, depleted specialists, or inadequate recovery may yield partial development rather than a clean binary failure. Existing achievements remain as legacy.

### Western failure

Missing ships, crews, ports, transport, reception, supply, routes, or provider loyalty can block maritime outputs while leaving valid land, lordship, missile, infantry, or diplomatic development intact.

### Redirection

Redirection changes future emphasis. It does not refund all investment, recreate losses, reset diplomacy, or delete valid units.

### Crown-Isles convergence

Convergence is a conditional reconciliation state, not an automatic best ending. It requires credible Crown-western cooperation, compatible investment, valid providers, and a supporting world state. Crown-Isles Companions function as a relationship example, not as proof that convergence is guaranteed.
