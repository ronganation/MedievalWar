## 1. Purpose and Direct Verdict

This stress test determines whether the shared decision grammar survives four structurally different Realm cases without forcing them into Scotland's two-direction model:

1. England: a scenario-defining two-direction continuity and transformation choice.
2. Normandy: two independent, potentially cumulative development channels inside one persistent Norman roster.
3. Barcelona-Aragon: a three-path preformation interface connected to formation, recognition, governance, inheritance, and activation boundaries.
4. Lower Lotharingia: recurrent operational decisions and dilemmas driven by pressure and crisis rather than one permanent identity fork.

## 4. England Stress Test

### 4.1 Structural form

```yaml
realm: England
decision_family: DirectionalDevelopment
presentation_form: TwoDirectionComparison
directions:
  - AngloSaxonContinuity
  - AngloNormanEnglishDevelopment
scenario_defining_choice: true
shared_english_foundation_persists: true
```

England is the legitimate strong binary case. The shared grammar must support it without converting that exception into a universal rule.

### 4.2 What the shared framework can own

- Visibility, readiness, offering, provisional selection, review, commitment, and resolution states.
- Carryover and persistence disclosure.
- Effect and resource-truth classification.
- Clear warning where future recruitment or settlement-development opportunities diverge.
- Save-mode protection and Codex comparison.
- AI ability to choose, delay, or remain unresolved.

### 4.3 What England must own

- The political and cultural meaning of continuity and Anglo-Norman English development.
- Which units persist, improve, gain conditional successors, or receive parallel additions.
- Settlement, burh, castle, household, regional, and institutional relationships.
- Exact military, political, diplomatic, architectural, and emergence consequences.
- Any convergence, hybridization, or later reconciliation rules.

### 4.4 Failure condition

The framework fails England if selecting one direction instantly deletes the other direction's existing people, veterans, settlements, buildings, or valid formations. It also fails if path selection is treated as a free roster swap.

### 4.5 England result

```yaml
result: Pass
reason: SharedLifecycleAndPersistenceRulesFitAScenarioDefiningTwoDirectionChoice
required_adapter: EnglandCarryoverProgressionAndSettlementOverlay
binary_model_transferable_to_all_realms: false
```

## 5. Normandy Stress Test

### 5.1 Structural form

```yaml
realm: Normandy
decision_family: IndependentDevelopmentChannels
presentation_form: ParallelProgramCardsOrPortfolioView
channels:
  - InternalNormanMilitaryDevelopment
  - ContinentalFrenchAdoptionAndInfluence
mutual_exclusivity_default: false
persistent_norman_roster: true
```

Normandy is the decisive anti-binary test. Internal Norman development and Continental French adoption or influence are development portfolios within one persistent Norman roster. They may compete for resources or create specific incompatible investments, but they are not automatically two mutually exclusive cultures.

### 5.2 Channel behavior

Each channel uses the common lifecycle independently:

```yaml
ChannelLifecycleCandidate:
  - Hidden
  - Foreshadowed
  - VisibleNotEligible
  - EligibleForReview
  - ProvisionallySelected
  - CommitmentReview
  - CommittedPendingResolution
  - ResolvedActive
  - LegacyOnly
  - FailedOrAbandoned
```

A Realm can therefore have Internal Norman development active, Continental French influence merely foreshadowed, and a separate reform blocked by missing institutions without contradiction.

### 5.3 What the shared framework can own

- Independent lifecycle instances.
- Competition and opportunity-cost disclosure.
- Effect, persistence, and resource-truth ledgers.
- Ordering and coexistence fixtures.
- No-double-counting expectations where mounted and foot expressions draw on one personnel family.
- AI evaluation of each program without requiring a one-time identity selection.

### 5.4 What Normandy must own

- Norman identity and roster continuity.
- Household knightly service, mounted and foot assignment, and pool boundaries.
- Adoption sources and institutional requirements.
- Which programs combine, compete, or remain incompatible.
- Exact French-influence content and access.
- Exact research, reform, settlement, and military consequences.

### 5.5 Failure condition

The framework fails Normandy if it demands a single permanent selection between Internal Norman and Continental French development, duplicates personnel across foot and mounted expressions, or lets research manufacture units, horses, equipment, specialists, or pools.

### 5.6 Normandy result

```yaml
result: Pass
reason: SharedGrammarSupportsMultipleIndependentDecisionInstances
required_adapter: ProgramPortfolioWithCompetitionCoexistenceAndOrdering
universal_split_panel_required: false
```

## 6. Barcelona-Aragon Stress Test

### 6.1 Structural form

```yaml
realm_context: BarcelonaAndAragonPreformation
decision_families:
  - DirectionalDevelopment
  - ConstitutionalOrFormation
  - RecognitionAndActivation
presentation_form: ThreePathPreformationInterfaceWithFormationHandoffs
opening_1062_combined_faction_state: Unformed
formed_faction_identity: BarcelonaAragon
```

Barcelona-Aragon proves that a development decision can interact with formation without owning formation truth. Its current authority family separates the three-path player interface from formation, territory, inheritance, governance, recognition, emergence, and activation owners.

### 6.2 Stage separation

```yaml
BarcelonaAragonStageCandidate:
  PreformationDevelopment:
    owner: PreformationCampaignInterfaceAndRealmAuthorities
  FormationEligibility:
    owner: FormationTerritoryAndInheritanceAuthority
  RecognitionReadiness:
    owner: EmergenceRecognitionAndActivationAuthority
  GovernanceExpression:
    owner: GovernanceExpressionsAndInternalIntegrationAuthority
  Activation:
    owner: OpeningRegistryPayloadRuntimeOwnersWhenAuthorized
```

Choosing a preformation path may change objectives, weighting, recruitment relationships, or future eligibility. It must not itself create the combined faction, transfer territory, assert inheritance, award recognition, or activate a runtime Realm.

### 6.3 What the shared framework can own

- Three-card comparison using the same consequence categories as two-card decisions.
- Visibility, readiness, blocking, and offering per path.
- Persistence of valid Barcelona and Aragon content.
- Effect and resource-truth ledgers.
- Commitment safety and Codex comparison.
- Handoff state between development choice and formation eligibility.

### 6.4 What Barcelona-Aragon must own

- The names and political meanings of all three paths.
- Union, independence, recognition, inheritance, territory, and governance conditions.
- Preformation recruitment and objective effects.
- Formed-faction roster transition and continuing native content.
- Activation authority and later-start compatibility.

### 6.5 Failure condition

The framework fails Barcelona-Aragon if it treats path resolution as automatic union or activation, collapses formation and recognition into one click, or forces a two-panel UI.

## 7. Lower Lotharingia Operational and Crisis Stress Test

### 7.1 Structural form

```yaml
realm: LowerLotharingia
decision_families:
  - OperationalDoctrine
  - CrisisResponse
  - InstitutionalReform
  - DiplomaticAlignment
presentation_form: ContextualDecisionAndDilemmaPortfolio
permanent_identity_fork: false
recurrent_decisions: true
```

Lower Lotharingia proves that the framework must support recurring, pressure-driven choices rather than a single era-wide direction. The Realm's contested ducal authority, campaign pressures, operational choices, dilemmas, jurisdictional questions, urban cooperation, territorial integration, and institutional investment require multiple bounded decisions generated by world state.

### 7.2 Operational decision instance

```yaml
OperationalDecisionInstanceCandidate:
  TriggerContext: Required
  CrisisOrOpportunityWindow: Required
  AvailableResponses: OneOrMore
  ReadinessAndBlocking: Required
  ImmediateOperationalEffects: Classified
  ProgressiveOrInstitutionalEffects: ClassifiedWhenPresent
  FailureExpiryOrSupersession: Required
  LegacyConsequences: Required
  RealmIdentityReplacement: false
```

Some choices may resolve quickly, expire, recur, or be superseded. They still require deliberate review when consequences are material.

### 7.3 What the shared framework can own

- Contextual visibility and offering.
- Time-bounded or pressure-bounded readiness.
- Options with classified effects and material tradeoffs.
- Partial success, expiry, supersession, and legacy.
- AI ability to choose a response, delay where valid, or fail to respond.
- Save safety proportional to consequence.

### 7.4 What Lower Lotharingia must own

- Exact opening pressures and ducal constraints.
- Exact dilemmas, response options, costs, deadlines, rewards, standing effects, and recovery routes.
- Law, custom, jurisdiction, urban privilege, ecclesiastical, territorial, and diplomatic consequences.
- Whether a choice is repeatable, unique, escalating, or tied to a named crisis.

### 7.5 Failure condition

The framework fails Lower Lotharingia if every decision becomes a permanent identity path, if exact uncalibrated costs or deadlines are fabricated, or if a crisis response silently creates active opening instances.

## 10. Required Framework Refinements After Stress Testing

Part 1B remains sound, but Part 1D identifies four clarifications for any later successor:

1. A decision container must support one mutually exclusive group, multiple independent programs, or recurring contextual instances.
2. Presentation cardinality must be Realm-owned rather than hard-coded to two panels.
3. Formation, recognition, and activation must be explicit external owner handoffs, not generic decision effects.
4. Operational decisions require expiry, recurrence, escalation, and supersession metadata where the Realm-owned authority uses them.

These are additive clarifications. They do not require rewriting completed Part 1B during this workstream.
