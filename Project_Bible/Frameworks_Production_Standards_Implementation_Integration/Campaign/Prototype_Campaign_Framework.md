# Purpose

The Prototype Campaign Framework defines the first fully playable campaign experience for Medieval War.

This prototype serves as the foundation for:

- Campaign Systems
- Character Systems
- Dynasty Systems
- Government Systems
- Trade Systems
- Recruitment Systems
- AI Systems
- Siege Systems
- Naval Systems

The prototype is not a limited historical scenario.

The prototype is a living historical sandbox.

History is encouraged.

History is not guaranteed.

The systems generate history.

---

# Core Design Pillar

The systems generate history while players become attached to the people shaping it.

The player should feel connected to:

- Characters
- Dynasties
- Kingdoms
- Cultures
- Governments

while still seeing the world evolve around them.

---

# Campaign Start Date

Late 1061 AD / Early 1062 AD

---

# Why Late 1061 / Early 1062?

The campaign begins immediately before several major historical turning points.

This allows players to participate in history rather than simply inherit historical outcomes.

Advantages:

- Edward the Confessor remains alive.
- Harold Godwinson remains a rising power rather than an established king.
- Tostig remains politically relevant.
- Edgar Ætheling remains relevant.
- William of Normandy is already established.
- Harald Hardrada remains a significant actor.
- Gruffydd ap Llywelyn remains important.
- Welsh frontier campaigns are underway.
- The English succession question is unresolved.
- The Norman Ambition Arc has not yet reached its climax.

History possesses momentum.

History has not yet become destiny.

The historical world exists.

The outcome remains open.

# Historical Momentum Framework

Historical Momentum represents the major political, military, economic, religious, cultural, and dynastic forces acting upon the world at campaign start.

Historical Momentum is not scripted history.

Historical Momentum is not predetermined history.

Historical Momentum is pressure.

The systems create opportunities.

Players and AI create outcomes.

---

# Core Philosophy

History influences the campaign.

History does not control the campaign.

Historical Momentum exists to provide:

- Direction
- Context
- Opportunities
- Motivations
- Plausible Historical Development

without forcing predetermined outcomes.

---

# Historical Momentum vs Scripted History

Incorrect:

- William always invades.
- Harold always becomes king.
- Wales always loses.
- Edgar always becomes irrelevant.

Correct:

- Norman ambitions exist.
- Harold possesses influence.
- Wales faces pressure.
- Edgar possesses legitimacy.

History nudges.

Players decide.

Systems react.

# Ideal Campaign Opening

Too Early:

Nothing important is occurring.

Players wait for history.

---

Too Late:

History has already occurred.

Players inherit outcomes.

---

Ideal:

Right Before The Storm.

The player experiences:

- Emerging Opportunities
- Historical Pressure
- Growing Uncertainty

while retaining freedom of action.

# Campaign Start Design Rule

Whenever possible:

Campaigns should begin shortly before major historical transitions.

Players should be able to:

- Participate In History
- Redirect History
- Prevent History
- Create New History

rather than merely inherit outcomes.
