Version 2.0 modernizes the Character Development Framework against the current constitutional character architecture.

The revision integrates:

- Character Network Framework

The framework replaces any earlier assumption that development is primarily a level, experience-point bar, trait ladder, or manually selected ability tree.

## Purpose

The Character Development Framework defines how strategically relevant people gain, lose, demonstrate, and transform capacity through participation in the campaign.

Development occurs through:

- age and maturation
- education
- mentorship
- household formation
- office service
- military command
- governance
- diplomacy
- institutional service
- crisis
- success
- failure
- captivity
- exile
- recovery
- historical memory

The framework exists to ensure:

```text
Characters develop because they live through the campaign.

Characters do not develop because a generic experience bar filled.
```

## Core Design Thesis

```text
Potential
+
Opportunity
+
Experience
+
Reflection and Recognition
=
Development
```

Development is function-specific.

A successful commander does not automatically become a strong administrator.

A capable child does not automatically possess adult authority.

A title does not create competence.

A remembered reputation does not equal current capacity.

## Medieval II and CK3 Balance Rule

### Preserve the Medieval II Core

Character development must improve or alter visible campaign functions:

- army command
- recruitment and muster
- provincial government
- office execution
- diplomacy
- succession readiness
- regency
- institutional cooperation
- public order
- regional acceptance
- claimant viability

The player should experience development mainly through strategic use:

- give command
- appoint office
- assign a mentor
- choose a broad education
- grant supervised responsibility
- retain or dismiss after failure
- protect or expose a claimant

### Borrow Selectively From Character-Driven Strategy

Use:

- education
- mentorship
- traits as tendencies
- office experience
- childhood development
- relationships
- memories
- changing roles

### Do Not Import By Default

- universal character levels
- yearly stat-point allocation
- large ability trees
- perk currencies
- repetitive childhood events
- guaranteed growth from time alone
- every minor courtier receiving full development simulation
- linear progression with no setbacks
- historical adulthood preloaded during childhood

### Player Workload Rule

The player makes occasional strategic development choices.

Routine progression resolves automatically.

Recommended normal frequency:

```text
0 to 1 major character-development decision
per realm turn
```

Crisis, succession, or regency periods may temporarily increase this.

Full development simulation is reserved for strategically relevant characters.

Eligible characters include:

- rulers
- heirs

## Development Dimensions

Do not use one universal Character Level.

Track relevant dimensions separately.

### Command Readiness

Ability to lead troops, coordinate subordinates, interpret terrain, maintain discipline, and execute campaign objectives.

### Governance Readiness

Ability to administer justice, revenue, officials, petitions, estates, and public order.

### Diplomatic Readiness

Ability to negotiate agreements, interpret interests, manage protocol, and sustain external relationships.

### Institutional Readiness

Ability to work through offices, councils, chapters, monasteries, households, archives, and law.

### Dynastic Readiness

Ability to manage succession, branches, dependents, marriage obligations, and household continuity.

### Strategic Judgment

Ability to select priorities, assess risk, coordinate theaters, and avoid overextension.

### Regional Familiarity

Knowledge of local elites, geography, customs, routes, institutions, and military resources.

### Cultural Familiarity

Practical ability to operate across cultural communities and political customs.

### Religious and Ecclesiastical Familiarity

Understanding of religious institutions, appointment pathways, patronage, recognition, and mediation.

### Maritime Readiness

Ability to manage fleets, ports, transport, coastal supply, island networks, and maritime coalitions.

### Narrative and Memory Readiness

Ability to use evidence, patronage, archives, ceremony, and memory responsibly and effectively.

### Household Independence

Ability to maintain advisers, records, finance, protection, communication, and political identity.

Only relevant dimensions should be active for each character.

## Aptitude, Capacity, Readiness, and Authority

These concepts remain separate.

### Aptitude

Natural or early demonstrated potential.

### Capacity

Current ability to perform a function.

### Readiness

Capacity combined with experience, recognition, support, and eligibility.

### Authority

Formal or accepted right to perform the function.

```text
Aptitude
≠
Capacity
≠
Readiness
≠
Authority
```

A character may be capable but unauthorized.

A character may hold authority but remain unprepared.

## Development Sources

### Education

Provides structured preparation and access to mentors, texts, institutions, courts, or military households.

### Mentorship

Provides practical guidance, access, correction, protection, and network transmission.

### Apprenticeship

Provides supervised responsibility in a real office or strategic role.

### Command Experience

Develops military capacity through planning, movement, logistics, battle, siege, occupation, and recovery.

### Governance Experience

Develops administrative capacity through estates, provinces, justice, officials, taxation, and petitions.

### Diplomatic Experience

Develops negotiation through embassies, mediation, coalitions, marriages, hostages, and peace settlements.

### Institutional Service

Develops procedural knowledge through courts, churches, councils, chapters, archives, and households.

### Crisis Experience

May accelerate development while creating strain, injury, grievance, fear, or overconfidence.

### Failure

May create lessons, damage confidence, reduce recognition, or expose incapacity.

Failure does not always improve a character.

### Captivity and Exile

May create foreign access, cultural familiarity, grievance, survival skills, dependency, or political isolation.

### Observation

Allows minors and apprentices to gain familiarity without adult authority.

## Apprenticeship

An apprenticeship requires:

- supervising actor or institution

## Supervised Responsibility

Supervised responsibility is a bridge between education and independent authority.

Track:

- task
- supervisor
- geographic scope
- resources
- allowed decisions
- prohibited decisions
- review outcome
- public visibility

Success increases readiness.

Failure may reveal a mismatch without destroying the character's future.

## Diminishing Returns

Repeated experience in one domain yields diminishing returns unless responsibility, complexity, or context changes.

This prevents farming:

- trivial battles
- ceremonial offices
- repetitive embassies
- safe estate tasks

## Cross-Domain Transfer

Some experience transfers partially.

Examples:

- logistics may support administration
- estate management may support governance
- coalition command may support diplomacy
- chapter administration may support institutional governance
- exile may support cultural familiarity

Transfer must remain limited.

A great commander does not automatically become a great ruler.

## Setbacks

Setbacks may reduce readiness, recognition, or opportunity.

Possible setbacks:

- serious defeat
- failed administration
- public scandal
- illness
- injury
- captivity
- exile
- dismissal
- mentor death
- household collapse
- institutional censure
- claimant capture

Setbacks may create new development paths.

They do not always permanently reduce capacity.

## Recovery

Recovery may require:

- time
- reduced workload
- successful lesser responsibility
- reconciliation
- medical or household support
- restored office
- new mentor
- public success
- institutional recognition

Recovery should avoid repetitive personal-event management.

## Aging

Age affects:

- learning rate
- physical capacity
- accumulated experience
- health risk
- command endurance
- strategic judgment
- memory and reputation

Aging is not a universal decline curve.

Different functions age differently.

An older commander may lose endurance while gaining judgment and political authority.

## Health and Injury

Health and injury may affect:

- command
- travel
- workload
- learning
- succession urgency
- founder dependency

The framework does not create a medical minigame.

Use concise strategic consequences and recovery periods.

## Recognition

Development must be recognized by relevant audiences to become readiness.

Possible audiences:

- ruler
- dynasty
- army
- regional elite
- institution
- church
- foreign court
- public

A capable character without recognition may remain excluded.

A well-recognized character without capacity may create risk.

## Development and Archetypes

Development enables archetype emergence.

Archetypes summarize current strategic function.

Development tracks how the character became capable of that function.

```text
Development
≠
Archetype
```

A character may possess readiness without an available archetype opportunity.

A character may occupy an archetype while strained or underprepared.

## Development and Founder Dependency

Development reduces founder dependency by creating:

- substitute commanders
- administrators
- regents
- diplomats
- institutional leaders
- prepared heirs

Founder-centered AI should prioritize role gaps rather than generic character leveling.

## Development and Dynasty AI

Dynasty AI uses development to:

- prepare heirs
- mentor branch members
- create office candidates
- preserve restoration leaders
- reduce extinction risk
- build household continuity

Dynasty AI should not maximize all members equally.

## Development and Realm Personality

Realm Personality modifies valuable development paths.

Examples:

- maritime realm values port, fleet, and coalition experience
- fragmented realm values mediation and regional familiarity
- expansion realm values command and operational execution
- institutional realm values administration and law

Realm Personality influences opportunity, not destiny.

## Development and Historical Momentum

Momentum creates development opportunities and pressure.

Examples:

- succession crisis creates council and claimant experience
- invasion creates command and resistance experience
- minority creates regency and guardianship experience
- church conflict creates ecclesiastical statesmanship experience
- expansion creates occupation and frontier governance experience

Momentum does not award automatic skill.

## Development and Historical Memory

Past performance may become reputation or memory.

Memory affects recognition and opportunity.

Memory does not directly alter present capacity.

A character remembered as victorious may receive command despite current decline.

A capable character remembered for failure may struggle to receive another opportunity.

## Failure and Learning

Failure can produce:

- improved judgment
- caution
- grievance
- damaged recognition
- loss of office
- new mentor
- archetype transformation

Learning from failure depends on:

- survival
- reflection
- support
- accountability
- opportunity to recover

Failure does not grant automatic development bonuses.

## AI Behavioral Rules

AI should:

- develop characters for identified strategic role gaps
- use supervised responsibility
- distinguish aptitude, readiness, and authority
- distribute opportunity
- consider mentor capacity
- allow recovery
- preserve strategically important minors
- recognize domain-specific experience
- explain major development decisions

AI should not:

- maximize generic levels
- assign command solely for growth
- risk every heir simultaneously
- give offices solely because a character needs experience
- treat victory as complete military development
- turn every failure into a lesson bonus
- generate constant development notifications
- assume time alone creates competence

## Information and Uncertainty

The player may not know exact aptitude, hidden ambition, private confidence, or future transformation.

The default UI should show observed readiness and demonstrated experience.

Potential should remain uncertain until tested.

## Validation Rules

Fatal:

- development state references a missing character
- minor receives adult authority from development alone
- development grants office, title, or claim directly
- current readiness is stored in static definition data
- evidence-free experience is created without a valid event or assignment
- dead or inactive character continues routine development
- character level is used as the canonical universal development value
- deterministic state changes rely on unseeded randomness

Warning:

- character has every development domain active
- repeated trivial tasks generate full experience
- mentor exceeds capacity without penalty
- readiness ignores recognition or eligibility
- title automatically creates competence
- development path predetermines historical adulthood
- every turn creates low-impact development decisions
- failure always grants improvement
- memory directly changes capacity

## Final Design Summary

The Character Development Framework makes growth a consequence of participation in the medieval campaign.

Its core sequence is:

```text
Potential
↓
Education, Mentorship, and Opportunity
↓
Supervised or Independent Experience
↓
Recognition, Readiness, and Milestones
↓
Office, Command, Archetype, or Succession Opportunity
↓
Success, Failure, Recovery, Transformation, or Legacy
```

The framework succeeds when characters become different because they:

- commanded armies
- governed regions
- negotiated coalitions
- survived captivity
- served institutions
- learned from mentors
- failed and recovered
- received or lost recognition

The framework fails if development becomes either:

```text
A generic experience bar
```

or:

```text
A high-maintenance RPG system that pulls attention away from the campaign map
```

The intended principle is:

```text
Characters do not level up.

Characters become capable,
recognized,
strained,
proven,
or diminished
through the history they actually live.
```
