## 1. Executive Verdict

Medieval War already has constitutional owners for character identity, development, and current strategic function. Character emergence therefore requires an integration contract, not another growth framework.

This authority connects campaign opportunities and Historical Emergence admission proposals to the existing Character Development and Character Archetype owners. It defines which evidence may be consumed, how function-specific readiness is judged, how archetype changes are distinguished from office or appointment, how setbacks and recovery affect later opportunities, and how characters may transform, combine, suspend, or leave strategic functions without becoming permanent classes.

### 7.1 Readiness rules

- Readiness is function-specific.
- Readiness may expire or become stale.
- Readiness does not override current obligations or availability.
- A character may be ready for supervised administrative work but not independent office.
- A character may be ready for subordinate command but not campaign command.
- A character may be highly experienced but currently incapable, inaccessible, rejected, or unsupported.
- Lack of readiness may lead to formation, mentorship, narrower responsibility, delay, another candidate, or no appointment.

## 15. Benchmark Crosswalk

### 15.1 Rodrigo Diaz de Vivar

- Later military and political prominence cannot grant opening command readiness.
- Court formation, subordinate responsibility, service transitions, exile, reconciliation, contracted command, independent action, and rule require separate evidence and owner transactions.
- Military, diplomatic, political, and independent functions may expand, combine, redirect, fail, or never occur.

### 15.2 Yusuf al-Mutaman

- Learning remains a process rather than a genius trait.
- Administrative, diplomatic, institutional, scholarly, regional, continuity, and noninheritance functions require specific formation and evidence.
- Competence does not create office, institutional authority, heirship, or succession.

### 15.3 Dynamic campaign-grown character

- Exposure precedes supervised work.
- Supervised work produces dated evidence.
- Evidence may support bounded readiness, limitation, recovery, or mismatch.
- Strategic relevance and owner acceptance are required before simulation-tier or archetype expansion.

## 18. Static Acceptance Fixtures

### Positive

- Exposure creates familiarity but no competence.
- Supervised administrative work supports readiness for a bounded administrative function.
- Military success does not automatically create diplomatic readiness.
- A failed task produces a specific limitation and recovery path.
- A character redirects from military service to governance after valid evidence and opportunity.
- A character combines ruler and military functions only when both current owner states exist.
- A formerly prominent character returns to lightweight simulation without losing history.
- A dynamic candidate surpasses a named candidate for one function while the named candidate remains viable elsewhere.
- No transformation occurs because evidence, support, or owner state is insufficient.

### Negative

- Award generic experience points that make every function stronger.
- Convert age, office, dynasty, fame, or later history into competence.
- Treat one success as mastery.
- Treat one failure as universal permanent incompetence.
- Assign a permanent class from a temporary office.
- Create office, command, resources, or relationships from an archetype change.
- Let Historical Emergence assign competence or permanent function.
- Let a transformation duplicate the character.
- Reapply historical development over an advanced divergent save.

## 24. Final Verdict

Every transformation preserves one identity and remains dependent on current owner state.
