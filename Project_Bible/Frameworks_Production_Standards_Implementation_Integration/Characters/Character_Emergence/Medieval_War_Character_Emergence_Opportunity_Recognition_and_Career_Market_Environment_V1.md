## 1. Executive Verdict

Medieval War requires a living campaign environment in which historically grounded, historical-equivalent, and campaign-grown people can become strategically important because current conditions produce a real need, a discoverable opportunity, credible access, evidence of readiness, receiving-side interest, and an owner-valid transaction.

The environment is not a recruitment screen, celebrity roster, free-courtier generator, universal labor market, character vending system, or historical event scheduler. It is a network of courts, households, governments, armies, institutions, communities, routes, communications, patronage, offices, crises, political transitions, and audience-specific knowledge through which people become visible and careers become possible.

## 2. Purpose and Design Question

This authority answers:

> How does the campaign world create, expose, compare, support, block, redirect, and retire meaningful character opportunities without spawning people from vacancies, forcing historical biographies, or reducing development to generic statistical progression?

It defines:

- opportunity sources and classes;
- opportunity ownership and lifecycle;
- candidate visibility and discovery;
- readiness, access, acceptance, and support;
- patron, sponsor, institution, household, Realm, and receiving-side behavior;
- competition among named, historical-equivalent, and dynamic candidates;
- contact, audience, offer, consent, appointment, service, review, and exit separation;
- failure, interruption, recovery, redirection, retirement, and return;
- representation and simulation-tier changes;
- player and AI information boundaries;
- static acceptance fixtures and a definition-only handoff.

### 5.4 Band competition rule

The bands are sourcing and validation classes, not quality tiers. A dynamic person may surpass a named person for one current function. A named person may remain best supported for another. An equivalent may be considered only from an independent identity source. No valid candidate is an acceptable result.

### 14.1 Transaction rules

- No stage silently creates the next stage.
- A recommendation creates no appointment.
- An offer creates no consent.
- Consent creates no office or resources.
- Appointment creates no competence.
- Service creates no permanent ownership.
- Successful performance creates no automatic promotion.
- Failed performance does not erase prior evidence, obligations, or identity.

## 24. Static Acceptance Fixtures

### 24.1 Positive fixtures

- A court vacancy exists, but no person is generated solely from the vacancy.
- A known but unavailable named character is not offered a role.
- A dynamic campaign-grown person becomes visible through dated supervised performance.
- A named character and dynamic character compete for different functions without either being deleted.
- An independently sourced historical equivalent is reviewed after the named route becomes impossible.
- A nonmilitary institutional character becomes strategically important without becoming a general.
- A receiving institution delays appointment because funding or mandate is absent.
- A candidate accepts a narrower supervised role and later develops through evidence.
- A failed officeholder redirects into another valid career domain.
- A retired character returns to lightweight simulation while retaining relationships and memory.
- No candidate qualifies and the owner distributes work institutionally.
- A cross-Realm offer fails because access, consent, obligations, or transport cannot be resolved.

### 24.2 Negative fixtures

- Generate a talented courtier because an office is vacant.
- Spawn a historical person because a date window opens.
- Give a famous person priority over all currently valid candidates.
- Copy a named person’s identity or biography into an equivalent.
- Treat kinship, religion, culture, or Theater membership as automatic discovery or preference.
- Turn recommendation into appointment or appointment into competence.
- Create money, equipment, forces, institutions, routes, followers, or reputation from opportunity need.
- Reveal private intent or future biography to player or AI.
- Keep duplicate active bodies for court, army, and map representations.
- Treat failure as permanent global incompetence.
- Treat no candidate or nonoccurrence as missing content.
- Reapply historical career defaults over an advanced save.

## 31. Final Verdict

Medieval War now has a project-wide environment describing how meaningful character careers arise from the campaign world. Opportunities originate in real political, military, governmental, institutional, household, diplomatic, economic, regional, and crisis conditions. Candidates become visible through actor-specific knowledge and social or institutional channels. Named, historical-equivalent, and dynamic people compete through current readiness, access, relationships, authority, support, and strategic fit rather than fame or file order.

The environment preserves valid delay, reduced scope, institutional solutions, failure, redirection, retirement, return to lightweight simulation, and nonoccurrence. It allows important military and nonmilitary lives to emerge without manufacturing people, replaying biography, or turning the campaign into an omniscient character marketplace.
