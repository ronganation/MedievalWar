## 1. Purpose and Player Promise

This is a new bounded design proof case, not a successor to Scotland’s historical suite and not a replacement resistance framework. It tests whether conquest can expose meaningful leadership opportunities without a date-spawn, free rescue army, or automatic faction resurrection.

The player promise is: conquest has understandable consequences, occupation conduct matters, local people retain agency, and resistance has real limits. A threatened human Scotland may benefit from the same opportunities as AI Scotland, but neither receives guaranteed rescue. An invading human or AI may consolidate successfully without being punished by an inevitable celebrity army.

## 8. Opportunity Shapes and Leadership Mandates

Person-first, movement-first, institution-first, crisis-first, opportunity-first, and collective/leaderless shapes may overlap. A local defender may gain recognition before a broad movement forms; several networks may already exist before anyone can coordinate them.

Separate mandates include local defense, communication, provisioning, political mediation, foreign negotiation, regional coordination, and field command. A good organizer is not automatically a battlefield general. Appointment creates no skill; military command creates no force. A governor or mediator may resolve the need without armed escalation.

## 10. Resource and Loss Accounting

Every proposed armed response needs a source-to-use account: who is available, who has already been committed, which equipment is usable, where supply comes from, how assembly is reached, and what civilian or household service is lost.

Defeated army survivors are not automatically fresh troops. Refugees are not automatically soldiers. Captured weapons need custody, condition, transport, and training review. Foreign promises are not delivered subsidy. Mountains and forests are not infinite concealment or supply. Coastal geography is not a fleet.

A leader can improve coordination or willingness through actual actions and relationships. This is distinct from manufacturing people or resources. Death, wounds, capture, dispersal, lost arms, broken routes, fatigue, fear, and exhausted support persist across leader changes.

## 13. End-to-End Early Occupation Trace

Hypothetical trace: a Norwegian occupation changes local control; a property dispute and requisition burden affect specific households; messages connect some affected groups; an existing service person has witnessed defense and provisioning experience.

1. Public response interprets the specific grievances and audiences. Shared culture does not imply shared political position.
2. Scotland emergence reviews the relevant opportunity against local pathways and external locks. No new live pathway is allocated by this benchmark.
3. The person becomes discoverable through reports and recommendations. Recognition creates neither skill nor command.
4. The exact later Wallace identity is excluded when chronology and life state fail. The existing person is classified from independent source, not renamed.
5. Capacity review finds enough shelter and messengers for coordination but insufficient equipment and supply for a field force.
6. A bounded local organizer or mediator proposal may proceed to owners; the military proposal remains blocked.
7. Accommodation, policy change, or lost support may close the need. Alternatively, later real capacity may permit mobilization.

This is a designed causal trace, not a simulated campaign result.

## 14. Player Presentation and Workload

Normal presentation should show a compact crisis card: what changed, which known audiences are affected, a small prioritized set of leading known causes, provisionally two to four where presentation testing supports it, current response/capability class, relevant person or collective, and the most useful options with costs and blockers. Full source and owner details remain on demand.

Illustrative player text, not historical quotation: “Local households are sheltering messengers after disputed requisitions. A locally experienced service organizer is gaining recognition, but arms, supply, and command readiness remain insufficient for a field force. Review the levy burden, negotiate local guarantees, or investigate the network.”

Avoid a prompt for every rumor, household or minor grievance. Group related observations, retain uncertainty, and escalate notifications only for meaningful decisions. No exact hidden supporter count, perfect candidate location or predicted rebellion date is displayed.

## 15. Counterplay and Fairness Review

Before major irreversible escalation, provide an actor-visible opportunity to intervene or mitigate where knowledge, access and authority permit. This is not a guaranteed cure after earlier capacity has been lost. A covert network may remain partly unknown; fair design requires meaningful warning or discoverable risk, not omniscient disclosure.

Compare paired human/AI fixtures with identical authoritative state and visible information. Eligibility and resource gates must match. Strategy may differ, but no controller receives special identity preference, free emergence resources or knowledge. Difficulty economics remain outside this benchmark. No battle-AI quality, runtime balance or difficulty performance is claimed.

## 16. Restoration, Settlement, and Post-Victory Recovery

Winning a battle, retaking a settlement, or recognizing a leader does not itself restore government. Restoration needs political recognition or another valid political basis, functioning administration, resources, protection and owner-approved territorial transitions. No universal casus-belli gate is introduced.

After restoration or occupation settlement, retain local disputes, property loss, displaced households, damaged institutions, supply deficits and rival leaders. Military victory does not erase grievance. Suppression does not prove durable order. Population exhaustion can reduce armed capacity without being described as reconciliation or successful pacification.
