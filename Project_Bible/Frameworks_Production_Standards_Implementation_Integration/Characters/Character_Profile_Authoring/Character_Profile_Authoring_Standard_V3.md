### Core architecture thesis

One recurring ownership problem should have one constitutional owner. Other authorities may reference that owner, consume outputs, contribute domain-specific context, and validate their boundary. They may not duplicate authoritative state, silently create a competing subsystem, store mutable outcomes in static definitions, or override Opening State within their own scope.

# 1. Concise Verdict

The canonical Medieval War character profile is a bounded authority for one person's durable identity, opening-relevant interpretation, demonstrated formation, present strategic function, uncertainty, and permitted specialist references. It explains why the person matters and how the person may act without absorbing mutable state owned by titles, offices, dynasties, institutions, relationships, claims, governance, succession, underage roles, traits, reputations, Historical Memory, military forces, routes, property, or AI execution.

A profile is complete when a designer can answer these questions without inventing runtime structure:

1. Who is this person at the campaign opening?
2. Why is this person strategically relevant?
3. What formed this person before the opening?
4. What has this person demonstrably learned or done?
5. What can this person plausibly do now, and what remains conditional?
6. What are the person's present limitations and dependencies?
7. Which people, institutions, regions, households, and systems matter to the person?
8. What can the person plausibly know?
9. Which authorities own offices, relationships, memories, claims, roles, resources, and mutable state?
10. How may the person develop, fail, recover, transform, withdraw, or leave a legacy?
11. How should AI reason as this person without hindsight or owner leakage?
12. What does the player experience through this person?

The standard is Static-Checked only until separate evidence proves more.

# 2. Character Authoring Philosophy

## 2.1 What a character profile is

A character profile is a human-readable design authority for one canonical person. It establishes:

- durable historical identity;
- bounded historical uncertainty;
- campaign-opening relevance;
- strategic function;
- demonstrated pre-opening formation and readiness;
- present personality and decision tendencies, where supported;
- valid current Archetype references;
- permitted development directions without guaranteed outcomes;
- references to external records that own mutable or shared state;
- authoring restrictions that prevent later history from leaking backward;
- AI and player-facing interpretation without runtime mutation.

The profile must remain readable by historians, designers, content authors, AI designers, migration reviewers, and implementation teams without requiring those readers to mistake prose for runtime truth.

## 2.2 What a character profile is not

A profile is not:

- the person's current title ledger;
- the office or institution the person serves;
- a dynasty state record;
- a relationship database;
- a claim record;
- a regency or guardianship bundle;
- a universal attribute sheet;
- a Character Level or experience bar;
- a biography that treats future history as destiny;
- a repository for every nearby system;
- an excuse to create character-specific frameworks;
- an Unreal implementation document.

Description does not transfer ownership.

## 2.4 Completeness and future flexibility

A complete profile preserves, where relevant:

- present strategic function and current limits;
- demonstrated formation and function-specific readiness;
- values, restraints, decision tendencies, and information limits;
- important people, institutions, households, regions, and why each matters;
- dynasty, succession, claim, title, office, governance, command, and institutional relevance;
- mandate, resources, logistics, communication, staff, regional acceptance, and recovery dependencies;
- workload, concentration, replacement, and continuity risks;
- plausible transformation, compromise, restoration, failure, and divergent outcomes;
- consequences of death, incapacity, capture, exile, disgrace, absence, office loss, or isolation;
- character-specific AI priorities and explainable reasoning;
- player-facing strategic meaning.

The authoring operation is:

```text
Preserve relevant meaning
-> identify constitutional owner
-> reference external state
-> explain person-specific participation
-> keep mutable truth outside the profile
```

Do not confuse concision with incompleteness. Do not confuse detail with ownership.

## 2.5 Opening-state discipline

Describe only pre-opening formation as demonstrated experience. Keep later titles, victories, marriages, rebellions, offices, deaths, sainthood, epithets, reputations, and outcomes conditional. Preserve disputed dates and motives. Separate present function from future transformation. Avoid writing the historical winner backward into the starting personality.

## 3A.1 Candidate-band neutrality

Named historical, historical-equivalent, and dynamic campaign-grown people use the same profile-quality, evidence, ownership, and uncertainty standards. Named status does not permit later biography to leak backward. Historical equivalents require independent identity and evidence. Dynamic people may receive full depth when current strategic relevance justifies it.

## 3A.2 Admission, promotion, reduction, and reentry

A profile may document evidence and questions relevant to simulation-tier review, current-function review, reduction, retirement, or reentry. Character Foundation owns tier; Archetype owns current-function interpretation; Development owns qualified readiness; Network owns actor-visible access and relationships. The profile stores none of those mutable decisions.

## 3A.3 Supporting-person boundary

Tutors, retainers, physicians, heralds, guides, chaplains, household specialists, and other providers may remain abstract or person-backed under current Foundation and Ancillary authorities. # 6. Character Network Guidance

## 6.1 Core rule

Relationships remain external to the profile. A profile explains why a relationship matters, evidence basis, possible cooperation and strain, and the relevant external owner. It does not own current trust, loyalty, grievance, dependency, obligation, knowledge, promise, visibility, provenance, or reverse-edge values.

Relationships are directed, potentially asymmetric, multidimensional, mutable, information-limited, and capable of surviving disagreement in one dimension while weakening in another. Never copy one person's relationship language as the reverse relationship.

## 6.2 Family

Record durable kinship facts and strategic context. Do not infer loyalty, trust, claim support, shared knowledge, household unity, succession preference, or alignment.

## 6.3 Allies and temporary partners

Distinguish personal trust, alliance, treaty, coalition contract, affinity, service, temporary alignment, and external-sovereign cooperation. External ruler cooperation does not create vassalage or internal subordination.

## 6.4 Rivals

Identify basis such as succession, title conflict, office overlap, regional interest, patronage competition, grievance, ideology, institution, or command precedence. Later conflict is not opening hostility without evidence.

## 6.5 Patrons and clients

Explain access, protection, commission, sponsorship, command opportunity, or reward. Patronage does not transfer identity, title, office, unlimited loyalty, ownership of client, or all future service.

## 6.6 Dependents

Describe dependency by function: household, military protection, finance, sponsorship, information, legal representation, education, custody, or office reliance.

## 6.7 Fosterage

Fosterage is a distinct relationship and household arrangement. It does not equal guardianship, hostage status, adoption, loyalty, or complete cultural assimilation.

## 6.8 Hostages

Hostage status is a conditional political and custodial relationship with holder, purpose, terms, protection obligations, duration, release conditions, and information consequences. It is not a Trait or identity.

## 6.9 Qualitative state and zero

Unknown is not zero. Absent is not zero. NotApplicable creates no dimension. Unsupported qualitative bands reject or quarantine. Profiles do not create qualitative-to-numeric mappings.

## 6.10 Preferred reference pattern

Preferred:

> The opening Network should represent separate directed relationships with the ruler, household protector, regional patron, and rival claimant. The profile establishes why each direction matters; Character Network owns current dimensions, knowledge, provenance, and change.

Avoid:

> Loyal to ruler: 85. Rival hostility: 60. Guardian: Person X.

# 7. Historical Memory Guidance

## 7.1 Required distinctions

### Historical fact

A proposition the project treats as true within an authority and confidence boundary.

### Historical evidence

A source, testimony, record, object, or observation supporting or challenging a proposition, with provenance, custody, access, reliability, contradiction, and audience limits.

### Narrative

A selected and framed interpretation. Narrative may be persuasive, partisan, mistaken, incomplete, suppressed, or revised. Narrative does not create fact.

### Memory

What a person, household, dynasty, institution, community, or audience recalls and how recollection affects judgment. Memory may differ from truth.

### Legacy figure

A deceased or prior figure whose example, descent, institution, claim tradition, or Reputation matters. A legacy figure has no present agency.

## 7.2 Profile usage

A profile may summarize pre-opening experience, identify past events shaping decisions, reference facts, evidence, narratives, memories, and legacies, distinguish personal participation from institutional memory, and identify future Memory possibilities as conditional.

A profile must not create Memory records, treat archives as eyewitness experience, make all known history available to the person, convert Narrative into claim evidence, activate later Reputation or epithet, or allow a deceased figure to command or know current events.

## 7.3 Institutional continuity is not personal memory

Inheriting an office, institution, archive, household, or title may grant bounded access to records and obligations. It does not transfer predecessor trust, eyewitness experience, private promises unless separately binding, friendships, grievances as personal emotion, or complete knowledge.

## 7.4 Opening and future Memory

Distinguish opening Memory grounded in participation, inherited or institutional references, disputed Memory, and dormant future possibilities. Future Memory authoring notes do not act at opening.

# 8. Underage Guidance

## 8.1 Protected minors

A protected minor matters through personhood, dynasty, household, claim, title expectation, hostage status, marriage significance, institutional meaning, or future continuity. Protection does not remove agency. Importance does not create adult authority.

Focus on bounded age, stage, age-appropriate preferences, safety, household continuity, education, demonstrated development, recognition, dependence, capture risk, restrictions, and multiple possible futures.

## 8.2 Typed role separation

Keep separate:

- legal guardian;
- daily custodian;
- residential custodian;
- household keeper;
- educator;
- mentor;
- political protector;
- military protector or executor;
- foster household;
- hostage holder;
- hostage guarantor;
- estate or revenue administrator;
- dynasty representative;
- ecclesiastical sponsor;
- regent or regency council;
- claimant sponsor.

One adult may perform several roles only through separate valid arrangements.

## 8.3 Education

Author educational direction, completed formation, current readiness, milestones, and tensions among education, safety, recognition, and independence. Do not author universal education score, guaranteed aptitude, fixed adult personality, automatic competence from tutor prestige, or routine lesson micromanagement.

## 8.4 Fosterage

Explain identity, security, language, household belonging, politics, evidence strength, external relationships, and prohibited inference. Fosterage is not guardianship, hostage status, adoption, loyalty, or complete assimilation.

## 8.5 Majority and readiness

Legal adulthood does not automatically grant major command, independent government, office eligibility, marriage autonomy, regency termination, coronation readiness, or sound strategic judgment.

## 8.6 Player workload

The Underage system should surface major protection, education, household, succession, hostage, and transition decisions, not repetitive childhood management.

# 9. Trait, Archetype, and Reputation Guidance

## 9.1 Trait references

Traits are opening-appropriate, evidence-aware personal tendencies, dispositions, capability expressions, or conditions recognized by the Trait owner. They are distinct from office, title, relationship, Reputation, and outcome.

Do not use a Trait to encode King, Bishop, Heir, Guardian, Future Conqueror, Future Saint, current loyalty, claim possession, later Reputation, full personality, or universal competence.

## 9.2 Archetype references

Archetype summarizes current strategic function. Use one primary and up to two meaningful secondary Archetypes. Prefer no secondary over decorative duplication. Move future functions to conditional transformation. Most children use developmental roles. Archetype grants no authority, title, office, command, claim, or eligibility.

## 9.3 Reputation

Reputation is audience-specific reception. It is not objective truth, universal opinion, or permanent Trait.

## 9.4 Current consolidation boundary

Until the current Trait and Reputation owner decisions and Registries are complete, profiles may state bounded design intent and audience context but may not invent canonical Trait IDs, activate Trait instances, create Reputation state, or convert later epithets into opening identity.

# 18. Validation Rules

## 18.1 Fatal defects

Reject a profile if:

- canonical identity is missing, duplicated, or silently invented;
- one person has competing active identities;
- person merges with office, title, institution, dynasty, household, or Realm;
- profile creates or changes Opening State;
- later history is preloaded as opening truth;
- child receives adult agency;
- one guardian owns all child roles;
- relationship direction is inferred symmetrically;
- Unknown or Absent is treated as zero;
- kinship creates loyalty, command, alignment, or knowledge;
- profile grants office, title, claim, succession, command, relationship, force, route, property, or institution state;
- candidate reference is treated as runtime activation;
- legacy figure receives living agency;
- historical date alone forces outcome;
- implementation claim lacks direct evidence;
- richer source is replaced by shorter incomplete summary;
- structured records are malformed or unfenced;
- hashes are claimed after content change without refresh.

- major character lacks demonstrated formation;
- biography lacks strategic function;
- major character lacks failure or removal paths;

- role modules do not match actual function;

# 19. Risks and Risk Controls

## 19.1 High risks

- Treating template as schema or implementation contract.
- Copying England or Normandy length mechanically.
- Leaving titles, offices, claims, relationships, Memory, guardianship, or dynasty state inside profile.
- Using prose numbers as runtime state.
- Importing later outcomes.
- Treating minors as adult rulers with disabled options.
- Recreating monolithic Character System.
- Creating Realm-specific character frameworks.
- Duplicating cross-Realm people.
- Replacing complete source with condensed reconstruction.

## 19.2 Medium risks

- Writing every active character as Realm anchor.
- Using Archetype as class or identity.
- Using Trait as office, title, Reputation, relationship, or destiny.
- Treating institutional continuity as personal Memory.
- Treating dynasty membership as opinion or knowledge.
- Treating one guardian as all child roles.
- Describing alliance without external sovereignty and terms.
- Making optional sections mandatory through precedent.
- Treating qualitative readiness as hidden universal level.

## 19.3 Lower but material risks

- Excessive biography obscuring function.
- Excessive uncertainty avoiding baseline decisions.
- Repeating same relationship summary in both profiles.
- Promoting lightweight people prematurely.
- Omitting player clarity.
- Confusing Static-Checked quality with implementation.

## 19.4 Controls

- Apply universal minimum, then role modules.
- Remove optional sections only when no strategic or source-grounded value is lost.
- Include opening is/is-not/future boundary.
- Include specialist reference summary.
- Include prohibited assumptions.
- Use Avoid, Migrate, Quarantine, Supersede consistently.
- Stop when identity or chronology is ambiguous.
- Keep implementation claims evidence-bounded.
- Judge length by information value.
- Require source-to-final map for mature reconciliations.
