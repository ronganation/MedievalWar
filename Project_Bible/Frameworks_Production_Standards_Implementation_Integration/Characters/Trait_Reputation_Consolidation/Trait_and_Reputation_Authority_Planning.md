# Concise Verdict

The minimum safe constitutional model requires two bounded specialist authorities:

1. **Trait Authority** owns persistent personal tendency and condition definitions, character-specific trait state, acquisition/evolution/removal, visibility, conflicts, and typed strategic influence outputs.
2. **Reputation Authority** owns audience-specific reputation definitions, character-audience reputation state, adoption and loss of public labels, visibility, summary derivation, and typed reputation outputs.

Neither authority owns identity, lifecycle, simulation tier, activation, development, archetypes, relationships, offices, titles, claims, law, authority, historical fact, evidence, narrative, memory, or strategic results. Each reads authoritative inputs and emits bounded outputs or proposals to the systems that own consequences.

# Authority Model

## Constitutional Placement

Trait and Reputation are specialist character authorities beneath Character Foundation and beside Development, Underage, Archetype, Network, and Historical Memory.

Character Foundation remains the sole character-family foundation authority for:

- person identity;
- lifecycle;
- simulation tiers;
- activation and retirement;
- bounded runtime core;
- specialist interfaces.

Trait and Reputation must integrate through typed references, commands, events, and derived summaries. Neither may restore a monolithic character object or place specialist state inside the Foundation runtime core.

## Authority Sequence

```text
Campaign fact and owner state
        ↓
Known evidence, experience, conduct, condition, and audience access
        ↓
Trait or Reputation owner evaluates its bounded state
        ↓
Typed influence output or proposed command
        ↓
Owning strategic subsystem validates and executes consequences
        ↓
Resulting performance, relationships, evidence, memory, and reception feed later evaluation
```

A trait or reputation never directly mutates an army, territory, office, title, claim, relationship, law, institution, or historical fact.

# Trait Authority

## Minimum Constitutional Scope

Trait Authority owns the minimum reusable system required to represent relatively persistent personal tendencies, habits, learned characteristics, and qualifying physical or health conditions without treating them as destiny or universal modifier bundles.

### Trait Authority owns

1. **Trait definition contract**
   - stable definition reference once an authorized registry process exists;
   - display and localization references;
   - constitutional trait category;
   - valid acquisition sources;
   - persistence expectations;
   - conflict and compatibility rules;
   - evolution and removal rules;
   - visibility rules;
   - AI interpretation metadata;
   - strategic influence channels;
   - validation metadata.

2. **Character trait runtime state**
   - character reference;
   - trait-definition reference;
   - active, latent, suppressed, recovering, or removed status where authorized;
   - acquisition source reference;
   - acquisition turn or date;
   - strength, expression, or confidence only where constitutionally necessary;
   - known-by or visibility references where private state exists;
   - evolution/removal progress where authorized;
   - migration provenance.

3. **Trait acquisition evaluation**
   - validation that a qualifying repeated behavior, development milestone, major event, injury, illness, institution, sustained experience, age, or recovery source exists;
   - prevention of unsupported one-action permanent traits;
   - deterministic threshold and conflict processing.

4. **Trait evolution, suppression, recovery, and removal**
   - owner-controlled state transitions;
   - validation of prerequisites and conflicts;
   - deterministic transition reasons;
   - no automatic cross-category ladder into reputation, archetype, office, title, or memory.

5. **Trait conflicts and compatibility**
   - incompatible trait-state checks;
   - duplicate-concept checks;
   - mutually constraining expression where both concepts may validly coexist;
   - no identity-level prohibition based solely on display labels.

6. **Trait visibility**
   - whether the trait is public, observed, suspected, private, or unknown where required;
   - typed information handoff to UI and AI;
   - no automatic universal knowledge.

7. **Trait influence interface**
   - bounded contributions to action utility, readiness growth, stress, recovery, relationship response, public interpretation, and risk;
   - owner-specific converter requests rather than direct strategic mutation;
   - reason tags or equivalent deterministic explanation data.

8. **Trait validation and migration contract**
   - source-label migration;
   - duplicate and collision detection;
   - legacy modifier contamination detection;
   - save-version handling;
   - quarantine of ambiguous legacy state.

## Trait Authority does not own

- CharacterId policy, aliases, or redirects
- person identity
- birth chronology, age calculation, lifecycle, death, activation, retirement, or simulation tier
- current health truth or medical processing
- development domains, aptitude, capacity, readiness, experience, milestones, mentorship, or education progress
- underage age bands, guardianship, childhood education direction, eligibility gates, or agency transition
- current archetype or strategic function
- directed relationships, loyalty, conspiracies, obligations, trust, grievance, or information access
- office, title, claim, institution, dynasty, household, jurisdiction, law, mandate, or authority
- fact records, evidence, witnesses, narratives, memories, commemoration, reception, later reputation, or legacy
- realm preference weights or AI objectives
- strategic output state such as morale, taxes, public order, construction speed, loyalty, or diplomacy results
- AI execution authority

## Trait boundary rules

- Trait is not identity.
- Trait is not destiny.
- Trait is not capacity, readiness, eligibility, authority, or performance.
- Trait is not office, title, claim, relationship, archetype, memory, or reputation.
- Trait effects are contextual inputs, not unconditional ownership of outcomes.
- Trait visibility does not create knowledge without an authorized information path.
- Trait acquisition does not overwrite the campaign fact record.
- One trait does not compel one action.
- A trait collection is not a replacement for Development or Archetype.

# Reputation Authority

## Minimum Constitutional Scope

Reputation Authority owns the minimum reusable system required to represent how a specific audience currently perceives a person based on known actions, received evidence, narratives, relationships, institutional recognition, and memory. Reputation is not a universal character property.

### Reputation Authority owns

1. **Reputation definition contract**
   - stable definition reference once an authorized registry process exists;
   - display and localization references;
   - applicable audience types;
   - qualifying source categories;
   - adoption and loss conditions;
   - incompatibility and coexistence rules;
   - public-label or epithet rules;
   - strategic influence channels;
   - visibility and summary rules;
   - validation metadata.

2. **Character-audience reputation runtime state**
   - subject character reference;
   - audience reference;
   - reputation-definition reference;
   - awareness;
   - acceptance or rejection;
   - strength or salience where required;
   - visibility/publicity;
   - supporting source references;
   - contradiction or rival-label references;
   - adoption, suspension, decline, or loss state;
   - last major change and deterministic reasons;
   - migration provenance.

3. **Audience-specific evaluation**
   - evaluate only information available to the audience;
   - consume known conduct, service, promise performance, office performance, relationship information, evidence, narrative, institutional recognition, and Historical Memory reception;
   - allow different audiences to hold conflicting reputations for the same character.

4. **Epithet and public-label adoption**
   - validate that a qualifying audience has adopted the label;
   - distinguish private assessment, local reputation, institutional reputation, and broadly public epithet;
   - permit labels to emerge, compete, weaken, or disappear;
   - prohibit later historical epithets at opening without opening-date evidence.

5. **Reputation summary derivation**
   - create concise public or observer-specific summaries for UI and AI explanation;
   - derive summaries from audience states rather than creating a single universal reputation score;
   - preserve uncertainty and incomplete information.

6. **Reputation influence interface**
   - provide typed, audience-scoped influence to diplomacy, recruitment, morale, claimant reception, institutional cooperation, regional acceptance, public order, patronage, and other owners;
   - never directly mutate those systems;
   - record reasons and source references for explainability.

7. **Reputation validation and migration contract**
   - prevent universal reputation fields;
   - detect opening-date later fame;
   - distinguish epithet, trait, archetype, memory, title, and office labels;
   - preserve audience and source provenance;
   - quarantine unsupported or ambiguous legacy reputation state.

## Reputation Authority does not own

- deterministic campaign facts
- evidence creation, authenticity, accuracy, custody, witness state, narrative authorship, narrative content, memory preservation, or legacy
- person identity, lifecycle, activation, simulation tier, health, or location
- trait state or trait acquisition
- development, readiness, capacity, or performance truth
- current archetype or strategic function
- directed personal relationship truth
- titles, offices, claims, legitimacy, succession, law, jurisdiction, institutional recognition procedure, or authority
- Realm Personality preference weights
- AI objectives or direct mutation
- global universal fame, prestige, dread, or reputation truth
- guaranteed epithets or date-scripted public identities

## Reputation boundary rules

- Reputation is audience-specific.
- Reputation is not fact.
- Reputation is not memory, although memory may support reputation.
- Reputation is not trait, although traits may influence interpretation.
- Reputation is not archetype, although current function may affect audience interpretation.
- Reputation is not title, office, claim, legitimacy, authority, or performance.
- Known performance may feed reputation; reputation does not rewrite performance.
- Institutional adoption is not the same as universal public acceptance.
- Contradictory reputations may coexist across audiences.
- A public summary is derived and observer-bounded, never constitutional truth.

# Historical Memory and Reputation Boundary

The current Historical Memory authority remains intact. Trait and Reputation planning must not absorb or duplicate Historical Memory.

| Concept | Constitutional Owner | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Campaign event and deterministic fact | Campaign History or applicable fact owner | Reputation consumes only valid known references and cannot alter facts |
| Evidence, authenticity, custody, witnesses | Historical Memory | Reputation references evidence available to an audience but does not own evidence truth |
| Narrative, patronage, contradiction, preservation | Historical Memory | Reputation consumes audience exposure and reception inputs without owning narrative production |
| Memory tradition and reception of a past event | Historical Memory | Reputation may derive a current character label from received memory but cannot replace the memory record |
| Current audience-specific perception of a character | Reputation Authority | Owns the bounded character-audience reputation state |
| Later legacy after death | Historical Memory | Reputation state may be an input to legacy but does not own legacy truth |

This boundary preserves the passed audit's assignment of the nine reputation candidates to the Historical Memory reception domain while defining the minimum future Reputation specialist responsibility. During migration, Historical Memory source references remain authoritative. Reputation records, when implemented, must reference rather than duplicate those records.

| Foundation owns | Trait/Reputation may do | Trait/Reputation must not do |

# Other Specialist Boundaries

## Character Development

Development owns experience, capacity, readiness, education continuation, milestones, setbacks, recovery, and transformation. Trait Authority may consume validated Development events as acquisition or evolution evidence. Reputation may consume known performance or recognition. Neither duplicates Development state.

## Underage Character

Underage owns childhood education direction, age-specific restrictions, guardianship requirements, underage arrangements, and transition to independent agency. Traits must not script later adult personality. Reputation must not preload later adult fame. Underage observations may remain uncertain and cannot become fixed destiny.

## Character Archetype

Archetype owns current strategic function. Trait may influence role fit. Reputation may describe how audiences receive the function. Neither authority may activate an archetype, grant an office, or treat function as permanent identity.

## Character Network

Network owns directed relationships and information access. Traits may influence responses, and Reputation may consume audience information access, but neither stores trust, loyalty, rivalry, conspiracy, obligation, grievance, or knowledge as internal trait/reputation truth.

## Office, Title, Claim, Law, and Authority Owners

These owners retain position, jurisdiction, eligibility rules, recognition procedures, mandates, grants, and legal truth. Trait and Reputation may influence evaluation or acceptance, but cannot create office, title, claim, legitimacy, jurisdiction, exemption, or authority.

# Implementation Principles

## 1. Definitions, scenario data, and runtime state remain separate

### Trait definition data

May contain category, acquisition sources, conflicts, evolution/removal rules, visibility rules, AI interpretation, strategic influence channels, validation, and UI labels. It cannot contain current holders or mutable campaign outcomes.

### Trait scenario data

Opening States may initialize only opening-valid character trait state supported by opening-date evidence. Later-acquired traits and future outcomes remain absent.

### Trait runtime state

Stores current character-trait state, source, transition status, visibility, and migration provenance under Trait Authority.

### Reputation definition data

May contain audience applicability, qualifying source categories, adoption/loss rules, label conflicts, UI rules, influence channels, and validation. It cannot contain current audience acceptance.

### Reputation scenario data

Opening States may initialize only reputations demonstrably existing at the opening date for specific audiences. Later epithets remain absent.

### Reputation runtime state

Stores current subject-audience-reputation state, awareness, acceptance, salience, supporting references, contradiction, visibility, and deterministic reason trace.

## 2. Typed references, not embedded ownership

Trait and Reputation records reference canonical characters and authoritative source records. They do not copy offices, titles, claims, relationships, Development state, evidence, memory, or archetypes into their own records.

## 3. Owner-validated commands and events

- Acquisition, evolution, suppression, removal, adoption, decline, and label change use typed owner commands.
- Source systems emit validated events or provide queryable state.
- Trait/Reputation owners validate state transitions.
- Strategic systems consume typed influence outputs and retain mutation authority.
- AI reads and proposes; AI never mutates authoritative state directly.

## 4. Contextual effects

Legacy fixed modifier packages must be replaced by bounded contextual converters. Examples:

- Brave may influence risk utility or morale response, but does not own morale.
- Corrupt may influence misconduct risk, but does not own treasury leakage or evidence.
- Pious may influence religious-action utility, but does not grant office or church recognition.
- The Conqueror may influence reception among an informed audience, but does not grant legitimacy or title possession.

## 5. Sparse, strategic acquisition

Major persistent traits should arise from repeated behavior, sustained experience, significant events, conditions, institutions, or thresholds. Reputation labels require audience adoption. Neither system should create frequent low-impact prompts.

## 6. Observer and audience boundaries

- Private or uncertain traits are not universally visible.
- Reputation is always audience-specific.
- UI and AI receive only information available to the observer.
- Hidden state must not leak through summary panels or decision scoring.

## 7. No universal scores

Prohibited:

- one universal Trait Power value;
- one universal Reputation, Fame, Prestige, or Dread score as constitutional truth;
- converting the legacy Authority score into either system;
- summing all traits or reputations to choose offices or authority.

## 8. Performance feedback without ownership collapse

Performance may:

- provide evidence for trait acquisition or evolution;
- affect audience reputation;
- feed Development, Network, Historical Memory, and institutions.

Performance remains owned by the applicable strategic or historical system. Trait and Reputation store only their bounded interpretation state.

## 9. Save and migration safety

- Read the Character Trait Library as a legacy catalogue only.
- Preserve audit category, owner, and action as locked migration inputs.
- Migrate runtime holders to owner-specific records.
- Preserve source label and provenance.
- Quarantine ambiguous duplicate concepts.
- Do not reapply Opening State defaults to advanced saves.
- Do not write the legacy generic trait container after conversion.
- Do not invent identifiers when a canonical destination reference does not yet exist.

## 10. Determinism

- Process by stable canonical character reference and destination reference once available.
- Version definition content and migration maps.
- Sort audiences and source references deterministically.
- Use seeded streams only where approved uncertainty is required.
- Record tie-break and transition reasons.
- Equal state, inputs, content version, migration version, and seed must produce equal state and diagnostics.

## 11. Performance and evaluation cadence

- Evaluate acquisition/adoption on meaningful events and bounded turn phases.
- Use dirty flags and local character/audience evaluation.
- Do not scan every trait against every character or every reputation against every audience each frame.
- Lightweight characters receive only the minimum state required by strategic relevance.

# Validation Requirements

## Fatal

- Trait or Reputation state is stored as identity or inside the Foundation runtime core beyond an approved specialist reference.
- A trait represents office, title, claim, relationship, current archetype, memory, reputation, or guaranteed behavior.
- Reputation is stored as one universal score or accepted identically by all audiences.
- Later epithet or fame is active at opening without opening-date evidence.
- Reputation changes deterministic fact, evidence, narrative, or memory.
- Trait or Reputation grants office, title, claim, eligibility, authority, command, or jurisdiction directly.
- Trait or Reputation directly mutates an owning strategic subsystem.
- An underage education choice fixes adult personality or reputation.
- A dead or inactive character produces new living conduct without valid record-based processing.
- A migration guesses an ambiguous destination or invents an ID.
- Static definitions contain current holder, current audience acceptance, or other mutable runtime state.
- Equal state and seed produce different results.

## Warning

- A trait has no strategic influence channel.
- A reputation has no audience.
- A trait contains broad unconditional percentage bonuses.
- A reputation has no qualifying source reference.
- A display name is used as an identifier.
- Trait and Development duplicate an experience/capacity value.
- Reputation and Historical Memory duplicate evidence, narrative, reception, or memory state.
- Trait and Archetype duplicate current function.
- Trait and Network duplicate loyalty, conspiracy, trust, grievance, or information access.
- One action always creates a major permanent trait.
- One event always creates a universal epithet.
- Routine evaluation or prompts exceed player-workload limits.

# AI Boundary

AI may:

- read known Trait and Reputation state;
- evaluate contextual influence;
- explain reasons;
- propose commands such as investigation, appointment, reward, dismissal, patronage, reconciliation, commemoration, or public recognition.

AI may not:

- add or remove traits directly;
- assign or erase reputation directly;
- mutate evidence, memory, relationships, offices, titles, claims, laws, institutions, armies, territory, or facts;
- infer hidden traits or audience acceptance without information access;
- select authority solely from trait or reputation summaries.

Trait and Reputation owners validate their own state transitions. Other owning systems validate and execute strategic proposals.
