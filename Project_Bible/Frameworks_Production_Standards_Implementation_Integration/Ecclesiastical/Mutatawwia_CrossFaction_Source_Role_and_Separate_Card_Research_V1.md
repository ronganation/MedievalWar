Mutatawwi'a survives as a cross-faction research category and mobilization hypothesis, not as one universal recruitable unit. The reviewed scholarly source concerns the pre-Seljuq period and includes a description of voluntary participation without formal enrollment by the ruler. That supports testing a decentralized or non-state-enrolled distinction. It does not establish one western Islamic corps, weapon set, formation, morale profile, building chain, or faction roster card.

For the Almoravids, a second card is held. Existing Ghazi Infantry already owns organized, recognized, mission-bound assault and objective security. Mutatawwi'a requires a different campaign transaction and battlefield decision, not merely lighter equipment, lower cost, weaker discipline, a different religious label, or a renamed volunteer card.

## 2. Historical and comparator boundary

The reviewed Deborah Tor study explicitly frames the Mutatawwi'a in a pre-Seljuq context and examines privatized religious warfare and public order. The evidence reviewed does not directly establish an Almoravid, Almohad, or Taifa Mutatawwi'a corps.

The Medieval II: Total War entry depicts poorly equipped, reckless religious volunteers functioning more like a melee mob than disciplined troops. That is useful as a readable gameplay comparator only. Exact statistics, universal cudgel equipment, fanatic stereotypes, unbreakable morale, suicide behavior, and a universal recruitment chain are rejected.

The reviewed Bennison material supports treating Almoravid and Almohad regimes separately and cautions against stereotypes that overemphasize Berber barbarism, fanaticism, or jihad ideology. It does not directly prove a Mutatawwi'a unit.

```text
Term in one historical context
!= universal corps
!= western Islamic attestation
!= identical faction unit

Volunteer
!= untrained
!= poorly equipped
!= uncontrollable

Religious purpose
!= fanatic statistic
!= morale immunity
!= automatic participation
```

## 7. Battlefield hypotheses for later consumer testing

These are design hypotheses, not admitted roles or historical claims.

### 7.1 Rally and reinforce

A temporary local body reinforces a threatened line, camp, gate, or objective but lacks the cohesion for planned Ghazi-style breach assault.

```yaml
core_verb_candidate: RallyAndReinforce
strength_hypotheses:
  - EmergencyLocalResponse
  - ShortDefensiveCommitment
  - SupportNearMeaningfulObjective
collision_risks:
  - CivicMilitia
  - GarrisonInfantry
  - EmergencyMuster
```

### 7.2 Gather and pressure

A loosely integrated body supports close pressure after regular troops create contact but cannot independently prepare, assault, and secure a strongpoint.

```yaml
core_verb_candidate: GatherAndPressure
strength_hypotheses:
  - FollowUpPressure
  - LocalNumericalCommitment
  - SupportAgainstDisorderedTargets
collision_risks:
  - LevyInfantry
  - AssaultSupportInfantry
  - GhaziInfantry
```

### 7.3 Gather and defend

A place- or mission-bound body defends a threatened community, settlement, route, or charged objective but has narrow expedition utility.

```yaml
core_verb_candidate: GatherAndDefend
strength_hypotheses:
  - PlaceBoundCommitment
  - ShortDefensiveMorale
  - LocalKnowledgeWhereSupported
collision_risks:
  - RibatGuardians
  - UrbanGarrisonInfantry
  - FrontierInfantry
```

None currently clears the Almoravid whole-roster collision test. A universal “rush and die” role, cudgel mob, fanatic morale, or religious-building recruitment is rejected.

## 8. Campaign-political value

The concept is strongest as a campaign-political distinction. A partially autonomous response may create decisions about whether a ruler recognizes, coordinates, redirects, tolerates, opposes, or suppresses a gathering; whether contributors accept army command; and what occurs when participants consider the cause complete.

These are design questions, not sourced universal procedures. Future consumers must define their own authority, reception, contributor, service, conduct, and dispersal contracts.

```yaml
research_lifecycle_vocabulary:
```

## 11. AI and UI boundary

A future campaign AI must evaluate purpose, communication, reception, contributor response, command acceptance, equipment, supply, regular alternatives, political risk, and dispersal. It may not spawn the unit from a religious building or treat every appeal as successful.

Battle AI requires a consumer-specific role and may not simply charge the nearest enemy because the label is religious. UI would need to disclose who initiated or recognized the gathering, contributor sources, command relationship, mission, service scope, readiness, strongest blocker, cost, and dispersal outlook.

No AI, UI, payload, schema, or runtime implementation is authorized here.
