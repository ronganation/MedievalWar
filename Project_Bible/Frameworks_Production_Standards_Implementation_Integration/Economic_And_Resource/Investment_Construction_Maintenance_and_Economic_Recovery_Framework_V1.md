## 1. Specialist Decision

A separate specialist is justified.

The Economic Foundation supplies generic entities, conservation, transactions, receipts, persistence, migration, and rollback. Property owns assets and rights. Production owns productive transformation. Treasury owns funds, reservations, commitments, and disbursements. Trade and Delivery owns material movement. Population owns labor. Geography, Settlement, Route, Port, Institution, Military, and other physical-asset owners own the actual thing being built, maintained, repaired, or restored.

None of those owners alone governs the complete reusable cross-owner project lifecycle:

```text
Need And Objective
-> Feasibility And Owner Consent
-> Scope And Design
-> Rights And Site Readiness
-> Funding And Reservations
-> Labor Materials Tools Expertise Transport And Security
-> Mobilization
-> Staged Work
-> Inspection And Acceptance
-> Commissioning Or Reopening
-> Productive And Economic Recovery
-> Review Maintenance Adaptation Or Closure
```

The Capability Matrix identifies recurring investment, construction, maintenance, damage, and recovery capability across France, Flanders, Barcelona, Brittany, Burgundy, Valencia, Zaragoza, and Denmark, while noting that no current shared Economic Project owner was verified. This Framework closes that recurring coordination gap without absorbing the physical assets or Realm projects themselves.

```text
Project Lifecycle != Physical Asset Ownership
Construction Authority != Building Instance
Funding != Materials
Repair != Recovery
Completion != Commissioning
```

## 2. Modern Medieval II Design Promise

Development should be slower, place-bound, materially constrained, interruptible, strategically legible, and capable of failure or recovery. A construction icon should not create a building after a timer expires regardless of war, labor, rights, materials, transport, season, damage, or Treasury state.

### 2.1 Preserve

- Medieval II's readable construction queues, settlement improvement, roads, ports, walls, Markets, farms, mines, workshops, religious structures, and recruitment-supporting facilities;
- visible cost, duration, benefit, and strategic purpose;
- settlement and regional specialization;
- clear links among development, economy, recruitment, defense, trade, Supply, and public recovery;
- a limited number of meaningful player priorities.

### 2.2 Selective inheritance


### 2.3 Modern Medieval II expansion

- projects compete for funds, labor, skilled workers, tools, animals, materials, transport, storage, security, and seasonal opportunity;
- several owners may fund, authorize, build, operate, benefit from, or maintain the same project without merging ownership;
- repair may restore physical function while economic recovery remains incomplete;
- temporary works, phased works, emergency stabilization, adaptive reuse, abandonment, salvage, and reversible projects are supported;
- AI may maintain, repair, expand, replace, adapt, mothball, abandon, relocate, or seek access rather than always build something new;
- project plans remain moddable without hard-coded historical outcomes.

## 3. Authority Scope

### 3.1 This Framework owns

- reusable Project Definition and Project Instance contracts;
- proposal, feasibility, authorization, design, scoping, scheduling, mobilization, execution, inspection, acceptance, commissioning, reopening, closure, and post-project review semantics;
- funding-plan interfaces and cross-owner reservation coordination;
- labor, expertise, tools, animals, materials, energy, water, facility, transport, storage, security, and route requirement envelopes;
- staged construction, maintenance, stabilization, repair, replacement, restoration, adaptive reuse, dismantling, salvage, and economic-recovery lifecycles;
- dependency, bottleneck, competing-demand, suspension, failure, delay, cost-change, scope-change, and rollback contracts;
- project observations, AI reasoning, player presentation, saves, migration, determinism, validation, and Realm conformance.

### 3.2 This Framework does not own

- physical Geography, settlement, Province, route, crossing, port, building, fortification, institution, workshop, mine, farm, Market, warehouse, residence, or military asset identity;
- Property ownership, possession, access, construction right, operation right, revenue right, transfer, compensation judgment, or restitution judgment;
- Population, labor truth, skills, health, migration, displacement, households, or recruitment pools;
- production outputs, stock, materials, tools, animals, money, accounts, carrier assets, military Supply, or delivery;
- Government mandate, Law, jurisdiction, permits, policy, taxation authority, enforcement, or appeal;
- exact Realm projects, costs, durations, designs, workforces, quantities, or January 1062 construction state;
- Registry, Opening State, payload, importer, Campaign schema, save format, or Unreal implementation.

## 37. Player Experience

Default project summary should show:

- purpose and affected asset or place;
- current lifecycle stage;
- expected strategic benefit;
- funding, labor, material, transport, access, security, and season status;
- principal bottleneck;
- current progress based on accepted work;
- expected next milestone;
- damage, delay, scope change, and risk;
- commissioning or recovery status;
- valid next action.

Routine maintenance and low-impact work automate through policy. The player handles major construction, strategic repairs, scarce-capacity conflicts, emergency stabilization, important institutional or military projects, severe delays, major cost changes, and postwar recovery priorities.

## 38. Performance and Abstraction

Use:

- stage- and milestone-based progress;
- project and resource bands where exact quantities add no strategic value;
- event-driven dirty sets;
- bounded active-project portfolios;
- cached owner snapshots;
- automated routine maintenance;
- exception-driven player alerts;
- regional summaries for recovery programs.

Avoid:

- per-worker or per-stone simulation;
- elapsed-time progress without accepted work;
- universal construction speed;
- one settlement development score as complete truth;
- every minor repair becoming a player decision;
- unbounded AI project queues.

## 44. Validation Rules

### Fatal

- definition or queue creates a physical asset;
- money creates materials, labor, tools, animals, transport, or progress;
- elapsed time creates progress without accepted work;
- project owner mutates physical assets or external owners directly;
- completion bypasses inspection, acceptance, or commissioning;
- repair automatically creates economic recovery;
- the same funds, labor, materials, tools, or delivery are allocated twice;
- rollback creates net asset, materials, money, progress, or recovery;
- foreign private project state is authored locally;
- Opening State overwrites advanced project history;
- equivalent inputs produce nondeterministic results.

### Error

- missing project owner, asset, site, right, authority, scope, funding, labor, materials, route, security, inspection, acceptance, or receipt;
- stale owner version;
- invalid stage transition;
- unmet dependency;
- progress exceeds accepted work;
- funding, labor, materials, transport, or site reservation conflicts;
- completion lacks physical-owner acceptance;
- recovery claim lacks cross-owner evidence;
- unresolved external lock.

### Review

- opening project, condition, cost, duration, design, workforce, material quantity, or progress unknown;
- later building or infrastructure projected backward;
- damage extent or cause uncertain;
- maintenance history unresolved;
- recovery target or prior baseline disputed;
- institution, community, Household, or external contribution unclear;
- physical completion and economic recovery conflated.

### Warning

- every minor repair reaches the player;
- one project score hides several bottlenecks;
- prestige construction crowds out maintenance without explanation;
- nominal completion hides defects or missing operation inputs;
- rebuilt asset is presented as recovered settlement;
- static design is presented as runtime implementation.

## 49. Final Framework Position

The Investment, Construction, Maintenance, and Economic Recovery Framework closes the shared economic lifecycle from strategic need through accepted work and recoverable function.

Buildings, roads, ports, workshops, Markets, fortifications, institutions, productive sites, and recovery programs do not appear because money was paid or a timer expired. Projects require rights, funding, labor, expertise, materials, tools, animals, transport, storage, security, season, staged work, inspection, acceptance, and commissioning. Damage can be stabilized, repaired, replaced, adapted, or abandoned. Physical rebuilding may be necessary but is not sufficient for economic recovery.

The player should choose whether to maintain a crucial road, repair a mill, stabilize a damaged port, expand a Market, restore an institution, replace an obsolete facility, rebuild after occupation, or abandon an unsustainable project. Those choices should compete honestly with armies, households, harvests, Supply, debt, relief, and other projects without forcing the player to administer every worker, stone, timber load, and minor repair.
