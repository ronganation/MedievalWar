The current working set does not contain the exact source text for the Governance Framework, Realm Personality Framework, Delegated Authority Framework, Ecclesiastical Authority Framework, Law and Policy Framework, or Project Bible V2.1. This audit therefore applies the locked constitutional boundaries already established by project authority and prior passed ownership tests:

# 1. Concise Verdict

Governance remains constitutionally clear after Character Network, Historical Memory, and Trait/Reputation consolidation, provided that Governance is confined to **administrative structure, institutional operating capacity, administrative reach, office structures and responsibilities, realm coordination, implementation pathways, and performance of government machinery**.

Governance does not own preference weights, law, legitimacy, formal authority, delegated grants, religious office recognition, person identity, relationships, memory, reputation, factual history, dynasty membership, succession entitlement, or underage arrangements.

The principal modernization need is not a new framework. It is a bounded separation of five layers that legacy designs often collapse:

1. **Preference:** Realm Personality defines what the realm tends to prefer.
2. **Rule:** Law and Policy defines what is binding, permitted, prohibited, privileged, exempt, or jurisdictionally valid.
3. **Authority:** Office, Title, Delegated Authority, Ecclesiastical Authority, law, custom, or institutional recognition defines who may act and within what scope.
4. **Administration:** Governance defines the structures, offices, coordination, reach, capacity, dependencies, and implementation pathways through which the realm attempts to act.
5. **Performance:** Government outcomes emerge from valid authority, administrative capacity, people, networks, resources, geography, information, compliance, and historical conditions.

Governance should be **Modernized**, not redesigned. Current ownership can remain intact if legacy universal governance scores, ruler-as-government assumptions, office/authority conflation, law ownership, and relationship/memory/reputation contamination are removed or referenced through typed external records.

# 2. Constitutional Ownership Review

## Governance

### Governance owns

- authoritative administrative-structure definitions and runtime state;
- realm administration topology;
- administrative units and their relationships to offices, institutions, regions, and the realm;
- office structures as administrative positions within Governance scope;
- office responsibilities, operational dependencies, reporting paths, and coordination paths;
- administrative reach and coverage;
- institutional operating capacity relevant to government execution;
- state-side fiscal, military, legal, and religious administrative interfaces;
- coordination among government offices and administrative institutions;
- policy-execution state after Law and Policy supplies a valid rule;
- legal-enforcement administration after Law and Policy supplies the binding rule and jurisdiction;
- government-performance state and diagnostics;
- centralization/decentralization as administrative structure and distribution, not preference or legitimacy;
- Governance-side provenance, save state, migration acceptance, validation, and deterministic processing.

### Governance does not own

- person identity, lifecycle, activation, simulation tier, or character core;
- realm preference weights or AI objectives;
- law, policy, custom, privilege, exemption, jurisdictional rule, or legal validity;
- office-holder identity as person truth;
- title possession, claims, succession, legitimacy, or dynasty membership;
- formal authority merely because an office or administrative structure exists;
- delegated grants of power;
- religious office appointment, recognition, installation, or ecclesiastical jurisdiction;
- relationships, loyalty, trust, patronage, fealty consequences, information access, or contact networks;
- evidence, narrative, memory, historical records, reception, or legacy;
- Trait or Reputation state;
- fiscal resources, armies, settlements, regions, routes, or institutions as external objects;
- AI execution.

## Administration

Governance owns administration as the machinery that converts valid rules, authority, people, resources, and information into attempted state action.

Administration includes:

- offices and administrative institutions;
- responsibilities;
- dependencies;
- reporting and coordination;
- staffing requirements;
- workload routing;
- reach and coverage;
- execution state;
- enforcement administration;
- operational bottlenecks;
- administrative failure and recovery;
- performance summaries.

Administration does not confer authority automatically. A structure may exist without a valid holder, a holder may exist without sufficient capacity, and valid authority may exist without effective administrative reach.

## Realm Administration

Governance owns the realm-wide arrangement of administrative structures, regional administration, and coordination among central, regional, local, military, fiscal, legal, and state-facing religious administration.

Realm Administration must not become:

- Realm Personality;
- Realm AI;
- ruler identity;
- one ruler's universal skill score;
- title ownership;
- legitimacy;
- a monolithic centralization value;
- a replacement for regions, institutions, offices, laws, or delegated grants.

## Institutional Capacity

Governance owns government-institution operating capacity only where that capacity concerns administrative execution.

Capacity may depend on:

- staffed offices;
- office-holder availability;
- Development-owned readiness and domain capacity;
- Character Foundation availability;
- Character Network cooperation, trust, contact, and information pathways;
- fiscal and logistical resources;
- geography and routes;
- institutional continuity;
- law and policy complexity;
- delegated authority;
- ecclesiastical cooperation;
- historical disruption;
- workload and dependencies.

Governance does not own those external inputs. Governance owns the administrative interpretation and resulting execution capacity.

## Administrative Reach

Governance owns the effective reach of government administration into regions, institutions, offices, and populations.

Administrative reach must remain distinct from:

- legal jurisdiction;
- title ownership;
- military occupation;
- cultural acceptance;
- legitimacy;
- loyalty;
- geographic ownership;
- information certainty.

Reach may consume routes, travel, communications, office networks, regional institutions, Network access, resources, and security. Reach does not create those inputs.

## Office Structures

Governance owns office structures within its administrative scope:

- office purpose;
- place in the administrative topology;
- responsibility portfolio;
- reporting relationships;
- dependencies;
- staffing requirements;
- workload channels;
- coordination interfaces;
- replacement and vacancy consequences at the administrative level.

Governance does not own:

- the person occupying the office;
- the title associated with an office;
- the authority source merely because the office exists;
- eligibility rules owned by Law, Institution, Ecclesiastical Authority, Underage, Succession, or another specialist;
- appointment procedure when another constitutional owner exists;
- delegated powers granted to the holder.

## Office Responsibilities

Governance owns the administrative responsibility definition and current responsibility allocation among offices.

Responsibility is not:

- authority;
- eligibility;
- capacity;
- readiness;
- performance;
- person identity;
- relationship loyalty.

An office may carry responsibilities while the holder lacks capacity, information, cooperation, or sufficient authority to execute them.

## Office Delegation

Delegated Authority owns the grant of scoped powers, including grantor, recipient, scope, jurisdiction, duration, conditions, revocation, and validity.

Governance may own:

- the administrative workflow that requests or consumes a valid delegation;
- task routing after delegation;
- coordination effects;
- dependency changes;
- workload distribution;
- execution and reporting state.

Governance cannot create a delegation merely by assigning responsibility.

## Office Dependency

Governance owns administrative dependencies among offices and institutions, such as:

- required inputs;
- approvals;
- reporting;
- sequencing;
- information delivery;
- resource routing;
- operational handoffs;
- fallback and vacancy consequences.

Character Network may represent directed relationships, cooperation, and access among holders or institutions. The Network does not replace Governance dependency structures, and Governance does not own Network trust or relationships.

## Realm Coordination

Governance owns state-side coordination mechanisms among valid offices, institutions, regional administrations, delegated actors, and state-facing ecclesiastical structures.

Coordination consumes:

- valid authority;
- office responsibilities;
- Network cooperation and contact;
- Character availability;
- information access;
- routes;
- resources;
- policy requirements;
- historical disruption.

Coordination cannot create authority, loyalty, law, or factual compliance.

## Policy Execution

Law and Policy owns the binding rule, jurisdiction, privilege, exemption, policy state, and legal parameters.

Governance owns:

- implementation planning;
- office assignment;
- administrative coverage;
- communication and coordination;
- execution progress;
- compliance administration;
- bottlenecks;
- reported performance;
- bounded enforcement requests.

Governance does not rewrite the policy because execution is difficult or because an office prefers another result.

## Legal Enforcement

Law and Policy owns what rule is binding and where. Governance owns the state-side administrative process of enforcement.

Other owners may retain:

- courts and judicial procedure;
- office authority;
- military force;
- ecclesiastical sanctions;
- local custom;
- title jurisdiction;
- imprisonment or punishment procedure;
- evidence and historical records.

Governance coordinates enforcement only through valid authority and owner interfaces.

## Government Performance

Governance owns administrative-performance state and derived diagnostics, not universal character competence or legitimacy.

Performance may include:

- execution reliability;
- delay;
- coverage;
- coordination quality;
- resource conversion;
- collection effectiveness;
- enforcement effectiveness;
- reporting quality;
- institutional resilience;
- bottlenecks;
- failure and recovery;
- regional variation.

Performance does not automatically change law, authority, legitimacy, title, Reputation, relationships, or factual history. Those systems consume valid outcomes through their own rules.

## Centralization and Decentralization

Governance owns centralization/decentralization only as administrative structure:

- concentration or distribution of offices and responsibilities;
- location of decision and execution points;
- dependency chains;
- regional autonomy in administration;
- reporting depth;
- delegation usage;
- local institutional reliance;
- coordination burden;
- reach and resilience tradeoffs.

Governance does not own:

- a universal centralization score that replaces structure;
- realm preference for centralization;
- legal constitutional rules defining autonomy;
- title ownership;
- legitimacy;
- loyalty;
- direct authority.

Realm Personality may prefer centralization or decentralization. Law and Policy may permit or constrain structures. Delegated Authority may distribute powers. Governance represents the administrative structure and operational consequences.

## Fiscal Administration

Governance owns fiscal-administration structures, responsibilities, collection workflows, reporting, dependencies, and performance.

Governance does not own:

- treasury balances;
- tax law;
- exemptions or privileges;
- trade and economic production;
- title ownership;
- collector identity;
- corruption evidence;
- character traits or Reputation.

## Military Administration

Governance owns state-side military administration such as muster administration, supply coordination, office responsibilities, reporting, regional command-administration interfaces, and implementation dependencies.

Governance does not own:

- armies;
- battle command;
- military commission authority;
- campaign-theater delegation;
- Development readiness;
- Character Archetype;
- military relationships;
- levy law;
- factual battle history.

## Religious Administration

Governance owns state-side administrative interfaces with religious institutions, including coordination, fiscal interaction, communication, recognized state responsibilities, and execution of lawful state policy.

Ecclesiastical Authority owns:

- religious office procedure;
- nomination, appointment, election, recognition, confirmation, consecration, installation, and removal where ecclesiastically applicable;
- church jurisdiction and recognized religious authority;
- church institutional procedure.

Governance cannot make a bishop valid merely by recording the office in state administration.

## Church-State Interaction

Church-state interaction is shared through typed interfaces, not shared ownership.

- Governance owns state administrative structures and actions.
- Ecclesiastical Authority owns church authority and religious-office process.
- Law and Policy owns binding civil or recognized legal rules, privileges, exemptions, and jurisdictional parameters.
- Delegated Authority owns scoped grants.
- Character Network owns directed relationships, trust, patronage consequences, contact, and information pathways.
- Historical Memory owns evidence, narrative, memory, reception, and legacy.
- Reputation owns current audience-specific perception.

No combined record should silently become sole owner of both church and state authority.

# 3. Boundary Review

## Realm Personality

Realm Personality is the sole owner of realm preference weights. Realm AI consumes those preferences when evaluating and proposing actions.

Governance may expose administrative facts and predicted consequences to Personality and AI. Governance does not own preferences such as conciliation, coercion, centralization, reform, patronage, religious accommodation, or military emphasis.

Personality does not create office structures, capacity, reach, responsibilities, or execution state.

## Law and Policy

Law and Policy owns:

- binding rules;
- policy state;
- jurisdiction;
- privileges;
- exemptions;
- prohibitions;
- permissions;
- eligibility rules where constitutionally assigned;
- legal change procedure.

Governance consumes the valid rule and owns implementation. Governance may report inability, delay, noncompliance, and performance. Governance cannot change the law by failing to enforce it.

## Delegated Authority

Delegated Authority owns scoped grants of power. Governance owns administrative structures that request, route, support, coordinate, and evaluate the execution of those grants.

A responsibility assignment is not a delegation. An office is not a delegation. A relationship is not a delegation. A title is not a delegation.

## Ecclesiastical Authority

Ecclesiastical Authority owns religious office procedure and recognition. Governance owns state-side administrative interaction and any state office structures that coordinate with church institutions.

Civil recognition does not automatically create ecclesiastical validity. Ecclesiastical recognition does not automatically create state jurisdiction.

## Character Foundation

Character Foundation owns person identity, lifecycle, activation, simulation tier, and bounded core. Governance references people as holders, candidates, delegates, administrators, advisers, or participants.

Governance cannot turn office identity into person identity, preserve a dead holder as active, create adult agency, or duplicate lifecycle state.

## Character Network

Character Network owns directed relationships, trust, loyalty, patronage consequences, fealty consequences, obligations, political alignment, contacts, visibility, and information-access pathways.

Governance may consume Network cooperation and access. Governance owns office dependency and administrative coordination. Governance cannot store holder relationships as administrative truth, and Network cannot replace office structures or formal authority.

## Historical Memory

Historical Memory owns facts by reference only through the authoritative fact owner, plus evidence, narrative, historical reception, memory, records, preservation, and legacy.

Governance may consume valid evidence and historical records for enforcement, appointments, privileges, exemptions, or reporting. Governance does not own the evidence, record, narrative, or memory.

Government performance events may become facts and later memory through owner interfaces. Governance does not rewrite factual history.

## Trait and Reputation

Trait Authority owns persistent personal tendencies and qualifying conditions. Reputation Authority owns current audience-specific perception.

Governance may consume Traits and Reputation as bounded inputs to risk, cooperation, public reception, or candidate evaluation. Governance cannot make Trait or Reputation a substitute for eligibility, authority, office, capacity, readiness, or performance.

## Dynasties

Dynasty authorities own dynasty identity, membership, branches, headship, continuity, and strategy. Governance may administer recognized interfaces with dynastic actors or households but cannot make dynasty membership an office, grant authority, or create loyalty.

## Succession

Succession and Claim authorities own claim basis, candidacy, stages, recognition, installation, and transfer. Governance may coordinate administration during succession, vacancy, or regency, but cannot create a claim or determine title transfer merely through administrative preference.

## Underage Governance

Underage Character owns age-specific restrictions, guardianship, wardship, fosterage, underage arrangements, eligibility gates, protection, education direction, and transition to independent agency.

Governance owns administrative structures that operate during a minority. Regency, delegated authority, guardian, protector, tutor, household head, and office holder remain distinct.

A minor title holder does not automatically possess adult agency. A guardian does not automatically govern the realm. A regent requires valid authority. Governance coordinates the administration around those valid records.

# 8. Legacy Contamination Review

## Ruler as Government

Legacy systems may treat the ruler's Administration or Authority score as the realm's government.

**Disposition:** Supersede. A person is not the administration. Governance structures persist through holder change, vacancy, minority, incapacity, and succession.

## Universal Governance or Control Score

A single Governance, Administration, Control, Centralization, Influence, or Authority score may combine personnel, law, reach, resources, legitimacy, relationships, and performance.

**Disposition:** Refactor. Decompose into administrative structure, reach, dependencies, capacity, execution, and performance, with external references to their owners.

## Office as Trait

Tax Collector, Governor, Chancellor, Marshal, Bishop, Regent, Adviser, or similar functions may appear as traits or character categories.

**Disposition:** Relocate. Office structures and responsibilities belong to Governance or the appropriate institution. Holder identity is a Character reference. Authority and appointment procedure remain external.
