## 3. Architecture Decision

The Framework Architecture Map requires one constitutional owner for each recurring ownership problem. It recognizes Integration Authorities as documents that define exchanges among framework families without taking their state. Historical Emergence is already identified as the emergence integration owner.

The current Historical Emergence V2 authority already owns:

- emergence definitions;
- named historical identity candidate rules;
- dynamic equivalent candidate rules;
- eligibility windows and world-state requirements;
- role-need interpretation;
- candidate admission proposal lifecycle;
- duplicate historical identity preflight;
- biological, chronological, and geographic plausibility review;
- rarity and saturation;
- decision traces and explanations;
- historical-versus-dynamic candidate comparison.

It expressly does not own canonical identity, activation, development, archetypes, traits, reputation, relationships, memory, dynasties, offices, titles, claims, succession, regency, delegation, government, law, military command, material capacity, diplomacy, Opening State, saves, or runtime implementation.

Therefore:

```text
New umbrella framework
!=
stronger integration
```

A second framework would create competing ownership. The correct deliverable is a template and benchmark content package beneath `Framework.HistoricalEmergence`.

## 5. Governance Connection

Governance is materially connected, but governance should not be absorbed into the Historical Emergence authority.

Governance contributes career openings such as:

- regional administration vacancy or overload;
- frontier government need;
- occupation or emergency administration;
- weak communication between ruler and region;
- institutional fragmentation;
- law or policy implementation demand;
- delegated responsibility;
- negotiated regional acceptance;
- ruler absence, minority, incapacity, or succession pressure;
- growing territorial complexity without matching administrative reach.

Governance owners must determine:

- whether a position or mandate exists;
- who may appoint, select, recognize, reject, review, or remove;
- the geographic and functional scope;
- available resources and institutional support;
- legal and customary eligibility;
- regional acceptance and resistance;
- responsibility load;
- revocation, handback, failure, and succession of the function.

Historical Emergence may interpret those conditions as an opening for a candidate. It may not appoint the candidate, create the office, manufacture regional consent, or write governance results.

```yaml
governance_interface:
  provides:
    - RoleNeed
    - VacancyOrMandateOpportunity
    - Jurisdiction
    - EligibilityInputs
    - AdministrativeReach
    - RegionalAcceptanceContext
    - ResponsibilityAndResourceBoundary
    - ReviewRemovalAndHandbackRules
  does_not_transfer:
    - OfficeOwnership
    - MandateOwnership
    - LawOwnership
    - AppointmentAuthority
    - RegionalConsent
    - GovernanceOutcome
```

This interface means ongoing governance refinement can improve character-emergence inputs without blocking the present pathway design. The benchmark must reference the accepted governance owner available at the time and preserve unresolved inputs rather than invent them.

## 6. Three Character Bands

The three-band model should be adopted as a content and candidate-selection rule beneath Historical Emergence.

### Band 1: Named Historical Character

A specifically researched person with a stable historical identity or a reviewable candidate identity.

Requirements:

- defensible identity evidence;
- chronology and biological plausibility;
- duplicate and cross-Realm identity preflight;
- bounded origin and location treatment;
- no later achievement, epithet, office, title, claim, relationship, or reputation preloaded without opening evidence;
- at least one plausible career route;
- valid nonoccurrence, obscurity, failure, displacement, or early-death treatment.

### Band 2: Historical-Equivalent Character

A generated or selected person who can fill a historically plausible strategic role when the named individual is ineligible, absent, chronologically impossible, blocked, already resolved elsewhere, or unsuitable because the campaign has materially diverged.

Requirements:

- role need remains valid;
- social, regional, institutional, or household source exists;
- identity is original and must not inherit the named person's biography, relationships, claims, later reputation, or unique destiny;
- capacity and opportunity are independently established;
- equivalent selection does not guarantee equivalent success.

### Band 3: Dynamic Career Character

An already existing lightweight, support, or generated character who becomes strategically important through lived campaign development.

Requirements:

- valid Character Foundation record or owner-approved generation route;
- development, opportunity, network access, and audience;
- promotion from lightweight to active simulation only when strategic relevance exists;
- career importance earned through office, command, institutional service, crisis, patronage, travel, or other owner-backed opportunity;
- no requirement to imitate a historical celebrity.

```yaml
candidate_preference_rule:
  named_historical_candidate: PreferredWhenIdentityChronologyAndWorldStateRemainPlausible
  historical_equivalent_candidate: PermittedWhenRoleNeedPersistsAndNamedRouteFails
  dynamic_career_candidate: PermittedWheneverExistingCampaignPeopleProduceAValidActor
  guaranteed_candidate: false
  guaranteed_success: false
  guaranteed_legend: false
```

## 8. Multiple Entrance Route Standard

Every major named benchmark should normally test more than one entrance route where the evidence and design support it.

Permitted route families include:

1. Existing household or court service
2. Regional or local service recognition
3. Office, institution, or administrative recruitment
4. Military commission or subordinate command
5. Patronage, mentorship, or sponsorship
6. Frontier, invasion, succession, religious, economic, or governance crisis
7. Exile, dismissal, displacement, captivity release, or political rupture
8. Adventurer, company, retinue, or contract route
9. Foreign court, cross-Realm service, or diplomatic introduction
10. Claimant, restoration, resistance, or opposition route
11. Scholarly, ecclesiastical, legal, medical, artistic, or technical recognition
12. Dynamic development from an existing campaign character

A route definition must describe source, access, audience, authority, resources, player or AI options, interruption, failure, and valid nonoccurrence.

## 9. Major Character, Agent, and Ancillary Boundary

The framework should not force every significant person into a map agent.

```yaml
representation_modes:
  PrincipalCharacter:
    use_when: PersonOwnsOrContestsMajorStrategicDecisionsAndRequiresPersistentIdentity
  MobileAgentOrEnvoy:
    use_when: TravelAndMapPositionCreateMeaningfulStrategicCounterplay
  ArmyAttachedCharacter:
    use_when: StrategicFunctionDependsOnAnArmyFollowingOrCommandContext
  CourtHouseholdOrInstitutionCharacter:
    use_when: AccessPatronageOfficeAdviceOrProcedureMatterMoreThanIndependentMapMovement
  AncillaryOrRetinuePresentation:
    use_when: PersonIsStrategicallyRelevantThroughAttachmentButDoesNotNeedIndependentAgency
  AbstractServiceOrOfficeRecord:
    use_when: FunctionMattersButPersistentIndividualSimulationAddsNoStrategicValue
```

Ancillary presentation must not erase a person's identity when the person carries claims, relationships, testimony, office responsibility, mobility, or independent choices. Conversely, historical fame alone does not justify an independent campaign-map agent.

## 10. Named Historical Identity Versus Achieved Importance

The benchmark must separate existence, availability, service, recognition, prominence, and legend.

```yaml
importance_ladder:
  - DefinedOrHistoricallyReferenced
  - BiologicallyAndChronologicallyEligible
  - ExistingLightweightPerson
  - Discoverable
  - AvailableForAPathway
  - AdmittedCandidate
  - ActivatedCharacter
  - ServingPatronInstitutionRealmArmyOrCause
  - RecognizedBySpecificAudience
  - StrategicallyProminent
  - WidelyRemembered
  - LegendaryThroughLaterMemory
```

Named identity does not guarantee movement up the ladder. A named historical person may remain obscure, serve in another role, fail, be displaced, die, retire, or become important for different reasons. A dynamic character may rise farther than the named candidate when campaign conditions support that result.

## 14. Static Acceptance Tests

The future template and every benchmark must pass at least these tests:

1. Named candidate eligible and accepted
2. Named candidate eligible but rejected by a patron or institution
3. Named candidate blocked by chronology or lifecycle
4. Named candidate exists but remains lightweight or obscure

6. Named candidate changes Realm or Theater without identity duplication
7. Named candidate dies, retires, is captured, or becomes unavailable before prominence
8. Historical-equivalent candidate selected because the named route is invalid
9. Dynamic campaign character selected because development and opportunity favor that person

12. Office exists but candidate lacks eligibility or recognition

14. Event opportunity exists but no durable Character admission is accepted
15. Candidate becomes strategically important without becoming a map agent
16. Candidate receives no later epithet or legendary reputation without runtime evidence and memory adoption
