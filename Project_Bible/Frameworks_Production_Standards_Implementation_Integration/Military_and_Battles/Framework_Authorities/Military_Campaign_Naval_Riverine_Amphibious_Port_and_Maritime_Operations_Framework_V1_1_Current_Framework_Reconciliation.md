## 17. Port Access, Control, Function, and Throughput

Port use depends on owner-fed access, controller, approach, depth, pilotage, weather, defenses, labor, storage, condition, inspection, congestion, blockade, repair, and onward links. The vocabulary is descriptive and creates none of those inputs. A controlled port may be closed or handling-degraded; a repairing port may retain limited functions; restricted reopening can permit selected traffic before full restoration. Throughput remains bounded by specific berths, anchorages, quays, boats, tides, workers, stores, animals, roads, and river links.

The authoritative model preserves access, control, functionality, blockade, damage, repair, congestion, and security as independent dimensions. It is never reduced to one open-or-closed boolean.

## 18. Embarkation Planning and Sequencing

Embarkation matches vessels to people, units, animals, equipment, baggage, supplies, boats, destination, and landing sequence. Load order changes stability, access, unloading, readiness, and emergency response. A delayed, damaged, dispersed, or access-restricted operation must preserve the original manifest and record every authorized transfer or release.

## 23. Crew, Mariners, Pilots, Rowers, and Specialists

Operations require real people with availability, location, skill, health, service basis, language, and assignment. Vessels, ports, buildings, and research create no crews, pilots, rowers, shipwrights, carpenters, sailmakers, navigators, handlers, or landing specialists. Missing, isolated, wounded, captured, or repair-assigned people remain owner records.

## 24. Command, Signals, and Maritime Coordination

Command depends on valid authority, vessel grouping, signals, visibility, weather, shared practice, messengers, and subordinate initiative. Delay, diversion, dispersal, isolation, and contact loss can fragment acknowledgement without canceling an objective. Command owners decide resumption, regrouping, changed route, suspension, or termination.

## 25. Navigation, Pilotage, Charts, and Local Knowledge

Navigation uses coasts, depth, current, tide, wind, weather, landmarks, pilots, soundings, charts, river knowledge, and shelters. Reports retain confidence and staleness. Diversion requires an owner-valid alternate route or port assessment; grounding triggers assessment rather than automatic destruction.

## 26. Weather, Wind, Tide, Current, Ice, Fog, and Visibility

Conditions alter departure, speed, grouping, detection, anchorage, river reach, landing, port approach, damage, isolation, and return. Weather and tide can delay, divert, scatter, ground, suspend, or reopen an operation. A favorable window never guarantees passage, landing, port function, or recovery.

## 27. Seasonal Maritime Operations

Winter, Spring, Summer, and Autumn affect storms, daylight, fog, river flow, floods, ice, coastal exposure, port access, demand, and repair opportunities. Campaign Season advances one ordered step and never replaces Historical or Biological progression.

## 29. Riverine Navigation and Reach

River operations depend on depth, width, current, bends, shoals, bridges, ferries, seasonal level, banks, tow or propulsion, landings, and hostile control. Navigability is vessel- and time-specific. Infrastructure handoffs provide crossing, approach, landing, load, restriction, damage, repair, and bypass inputs without transferring riverine operation ownership.

## 30. River Patrol, Transport, and Crossing Support

River craft may patrol, scout, escort, transport, supply, support crossings, control landings, or move engineers. Effects remain tied to navigable reaches and banks. A delayed or isolated flotilla retains its objective and assignment while continuation evaluates support, alternate landings, rescue, repair, and withdrawal.

## 31. Maritime Reconnaissance, Patrol, and Screening

Patrols observe routes, coastlines, ports, vessels, smoke, sails, signals, and landings through bounded access, endurance, visibility, and reporting. Screens protect convoys and landing forces and deny observation. MissingOrContactLost records uncertainty; it does not classify an element as destroyed, captured, or released.

## 32. Detection, Identification, Tracking, and Contact

Contacts progress from indication to bearing, vessel count band, type estimate, owner claim, course, speed, and cargo or troop hypothesis. Horizon, coast, weather, darkness, rivers, islands, deception, dispersal, and report delay matter. Last-known markers never track secretly and contact loss does not erase ownership.

## 33. Convoy Organization and Escort

Convoys balance common speed, route, signals, spacing, protection, cargo priority, refuge, dispersal, and delivery. Escorts retain supply, crew, readiness, owner, and competing mission. A scattered convoy preserves identifiable vessel, cargo, escort, and operation relationships until regrouped, diverted, rescued, landed, returned, or terminated.

## 34. Interception, Pursuit, Evasion, and Disengagement

Interception requires detection, geometry, speed, endurance, route, weather, command, and willingness to engage. Pursuit can separate forces. Evasion can use coasts, rivers, weather, darkness, ports, shallow water, dispersal, or deception. Contact does not force battle, and interruption states do not automatically become FailedOrAborted.

## 35. Naval Battle Initiation Boundary

The contact and battle frameworks determine engagement, deployment, withdrawal, pursuit, and outcome. This authority supplies vessel, grouping, port, route, weather, cargo, interruption, and ownership context and receives provisional receipts. It does not sink ships, kill crews, capture vessels, assign victory, or resolve missing elements.

## 36. Blockade, Port Denial, and Coastal Interdiction

Blockade and interdiction remain partial, conditional, reversible, and contestable. Port operational state records partial or heavy blockade alongside other functions and restrictions. A fleet does not permanently close all access, create sea control, disable every port function, or prevent bypass through alternate routes, landings, weather gaps, relief, or inland links.

## 43. Approach, Assembly, Transfer, and Ship-to-Shore Movement

Landing requires assembly, navigation, approach, launch or beaching, sequencing, control, protection, and shore exit. Each transfer consumes time. Delay, diversion, dispersal, grounding, or damage can separate troops from animals, baggage, ammunition, commanders, and reserves without automatically ending the operation.

## 44. Unopposed, Opposed, Disrupted, and Suspended Landings

Unopposed means no verified active resistance at landing, not safety. Opposed landings route contact to battle owners. Disrupted landings may scatter, delay, divert, ground, lose boats, or separate echelons. Suspended landings preserve authority, objective, vessels, custody, landing plan, reservations, and return obligations pending reassessment.

## 45. Beachhead, Riverhead, Port Entry, and Expansion

A beachhead or riverhead is a limited protected operating area enabling follow-on landing, rally, supply, or withdrawal. It does not establish occupation, title, port ownership, or automatically make a damaged or contested port operational. Expansion requires real force, route, command, logistics, engineering, intelligence, and owner-valid operations.

## 46. Port Assault, Entry, Seizure, Function, and Reopening Boundary

Maritime forces may support approach, landing, blockade, obstruction removal, quay action, and transfer. Battle, siege, occupation, and infrastructure owners resolve assault, entry, capture, controller, condition, functionality, repair, inspection, and reopening. Port capture does not transfer title; port control does not guarantee operational state; reopening may carry restrictions.

## 47. Amphibious Sustainment and Follow-On Echelons

Landing success requires boats, port or beach handling, weather windows, supplies, animals, engineering, medical support, escorts, reserve vessels, inland routes, and functioning transfer sites. Follow-on echelons compete for lift. Interruption states preserve active reservations and identify what has been released, delayed, diverted, scattered, isolated, landed, or returned.

## 48. Evacuation, Extraction, Reembarkation, and Withdrawal

Evacuation prioritizes wounded, prisoners, protected persons, troops, animals, records, baggage, and equipment under authority and finite capacity. Reembarkation requires access, port or landing functionality, boats, security, manifests, rally, weather, and destination. Missing, grounded, isolated, or repairing elements remain unresolved rather than silently abandoned or destroyed.

## 49. Damage, Grounding, Wreck, Fire, Flooding, and Loss of Function

```text
Damage != Destruction
Grounded != Destroyed
Wreck Located != Wreck Recovered
Vessel Captured != Vessel Owned
Vessel Seized != Vessel Serviceable
Missing != Destroyed

```

Damage evidence records hull, rig, propulsion, steering, rowing, cargo, fire, flooding, grounding, crew, boats, assignment, restrictions, and repair need. DamagedOrDegraded and Grounded can coexist with an active, delayed, suspended, rescue, or termination state.

## 50. Repair, Refit, Careening, Shipyard, and Field Maintenance

Repair requires a real vessel, evidence, crew or specialists, tools, materials, access, infrastructure capability, security, supply, inspection, and time. The infrastructure V1.1 feeds shipyard worksite capability, work parties, finite reservations, maintenance, repair evidence, and restricted reopening. Repairing preserves assignment, owner, damage, custody, and operation consequences until inspection confirms function.

## 51. Capture, Prize, Salvage, Recovery, and Ownership

Captured or salvaged vessels, cargo, equipment, records, animals, and people retain identity, owner, custodian, location, condition, claims, care needs, and duplicate protection. Capture is provisional. Termination and migration do not transfer title, classify missing vessels as captured, or make seized vessels serviceable.

## 52. Search, Rescue, Missing, Isolated, Survivors, and Wounded

Search and rescue competes with weather, current, visibility, capacity, enemy threat, objective, and return. MissingOrContactLost records uncertain location and condition; Isolated records loss of dependable support, communication, route, or recovery. Neither state means destroyed, dead, captured, released, or owned by another actor.

## 53. Piracy, Private Service, Commerce Protection, and Legal Boundary

Private hostile action, licensed service, piracy claims, commerce protection, neutrality, safe conduct, and prize disputes belong to law, diplomacy, property, economy, and character owners. Delay, diversion, capture, repair, port restriction, and termination preserve relevant service terms and claims. Maritime success does not establish permission or title.

## 54. Allied, Vassal, Mercenary, Regional, and Contracted Maritime Forces

External vessels, crews, commanders, transports, pilots, and port services retain owner, contract, geography, mission, command relationship, supply, repair, cargo, prisoner, withdrawal, and return terms. Dispersal, isolation, suspension, and termination preserve those obligations. Host command does not absorb external property or personnel.
