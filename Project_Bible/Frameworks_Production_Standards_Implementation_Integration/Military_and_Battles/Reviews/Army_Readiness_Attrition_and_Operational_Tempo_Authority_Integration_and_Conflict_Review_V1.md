## 6. Strongly Compatible Areas

### 6.1 Classic campaign surface

The readiness specification and army-ecosystem framework agree that the player should primarily interact with an army, commander, familiar unit cards, movement or stance, and concise readiness or supply information. Deep support causality should remain available without becoming mandatory low-level administration.

### 6.2 Typed causality instead of one power score

Both files reject a universal unexplained army score. The readiness summary can remain, but it must be reproduced from typed dimensions and expose the strongest limiter, warning, and corrective action.

### 6.3 Objective-relative capability

The subject's mission-capability profiles align with the army-ecosystem framework's purpose profiles and composition assessment. An army may be coherent for local defense but unsuitable for pursuit, siege, or distant expedition.

### 6.4 Reinforcement is not readiness

Both files explicitly reject instant cohesion or readiness from arriving replacements. Personnel, equipment, supply, command integration, service terms, and recovery must remain separately accountable.

### 6.5 Army can win and remain operationally weak

Both files preserve the distinction between tactical victory and campaign capability. Battle outcome needs typed transfer into fatigue, cohesion, morale, equipment, animals, command, withdrawal, and recovery state.

### 6.6 Player warning discipline

Both files require concise warnings before major overextension or hidden catastrophe and reject constant low-value alerts.

### 6.7 AI parity

Both files prohibit invisible AI exemptions for supply, support, transport, recovery, readiness, attrition, or difficulty.

## 7. Terminology Collision Review

### 7.1 Readiness versus composition coherence

```text
Army Composition Coherence != Army Readiness
```

Composition coherence asks whether the force possesses the combat and support relationships suitable for an objective. Readiness asks whether those capabilities are presently functional and sustainable under current state.

Required successor rule:

```yaml
composition_readiness_relationship:
  composition_assessment_owner: Military_Army_Composition_as_an_Institutional_Ecosystem_Framework_V1.md
  readiness_assessment_owner: Army readiness authority
  composition_result_consumed_by_readiness: true
  readiness_result_consumed_by_composition_planning: true
  circular_runtime_ownership_created: false
```

### 7.2 Supply state versus readiness state

```text
Supply State != Readiness State
```

`Fully Supplied`, `Supplied`, `Extended`, `Strained`, `Unsupplied`, `Collapsing`, and `Recovering` remain supply-state vocabulary under the supply owner. `Ready`, `Committed`, `Worn`, `Degraded`, `Exhausted`, `Broken`, and `Recovering` are readiness-summary candidates.

Collision: `Recovering` appears in both state families.

Disposition: Retain both concepts but require qualified labels in data, debugging, and advanced UI:

```yaml
qualified_state_names:
  SupplyRecovering: Supply owner state
  ReadinessRecovering: Readiness summary state
```

The ordinary UI may display `Recovering` when context is unambiguous. Tags and schemas must not use the bare term without namespace.

### 7.3 Operational tempo versus logistics posture

```text
Operational Tempo != Logistics Posture
```

The supply framework's `Standard March`, `Forced March`, `Forage`, `Encamp and Recover`, `Winter Quarters`, `Raid Supply`, and `Coastal Supply` remain logistics postures unless the supply owner later reclassifies them.

The readiness authority's `Rest`, `Cautious`, `Standard`, `High`, and `Emergency` remain intensity states.

Collision: `Forced March` is described as a mission or movement action in the readiness file but is a logistics posture in the supply file.

Disposition: Do not create a second Forced March enum. Treat forced movement as an owner-routed action or posture that requests High or Emergency tempo and is constrained by movement, supply, command, cohesion, and readiness.

### 7.4 Rest versus Encamp and Recover

```text
Rest Tempo != EncampAndRecover Posture
```

Rest is the intended operational intensity. Encamp and Recover is the logistical or army posture supporting recovery. Rest without safety and support may fail to produce meaningful recovery.

### 7.5 Broken versus operational collapse

`Broken` is a candidate readiness summary. `Operational Collapse` is an escalation outcome. The successor should clarify that Broken is not automatic destruction, rout, surrender, or deletion. It indicates inability to conduct coherent ordinary field operations.

### 7.6 Morale, cohesion, fatigue, and readiness

The V1 subject separates these concepts correctly but describes campaign-level fatigue and reorganization interfaces within its owned scope. The successor must narrow that wording:

```yaml
readiness_owner_may:
  - define required inputs
  - summarize mission consequence
  - route proposals and findings
  - define readiness-facing validation
readiness_owner_may_not:
  - own exact morale formulas
  - own exact cohesion formulas
  - own exact fatigue formulas
  - apply casualties or desertion
  - execute battle or siege outcomes
```

## 9. Missing or Underdeveloped Interfaces

### 9.1 Army ecosystem interface

The successor must explicitly consume:

- command core;
- scouting and information capacity;
- supply and transport capacity;
- camp and quartering capacity;
- medical and recovery capacity;
- engineering and siege capacity where applicable;
- animal and handler capacity where applicable;
- communication and record capacity;
- security, escort, and reserve capacity;
- owner contingent and obligation relationships;
- route, withdrawal, and return capacity;
- composition-coherence result and strongest blockers.

### 9.2 Information confidence

The readiness V1 includes forecast confidence but its mission contracts should explicitly consume scouting and communication confidence from the army ecosystem and information owners.

### 9.3 Contingent service terms

Mixed armies may contain royal, household, regional, civic, levy, allied, mercenary, religious, or contracted components with different service and withdrawal conditions. Readiness should not aggregate them into false universal loyalty or endurance. The successor should require component-level blocker references when one contingent materially limits the army.

### 9.4 Detachment support inheritance

The successor should add:

```text
Detachment != Full Parent Support Inheritance
```

A split force needs assigned command, support share, supply, communications, withdrawal, and reunion or return plans. Readiness must recompute after division.

### 9.5 Large-army burden

The successor should explicitly consume route congestion, concentration and departure delay, camp and sanitation burden, command and message difficulty, visibility, withdrawal difficulty, local burden, and recovery difficulty from the army ecosystem.

### 9.6 Support-to-card boundary

Readiness deficiencies do not automatically justify new support unit cards. The army ecosystem's support-card admission gate controls whether a support capacity deserves a visible roster card.

### 9.7 Current unit-standard interface

The successor should reference the verified current `Unit_Definition_and_Card_Authoring_Standard_V1_2.md` when defining future designer-unit coverage. It must not create another competing unit standard.
