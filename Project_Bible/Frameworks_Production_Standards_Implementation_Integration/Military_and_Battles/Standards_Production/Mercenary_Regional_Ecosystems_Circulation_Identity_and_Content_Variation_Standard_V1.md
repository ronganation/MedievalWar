## 2. Authority Verdict

Adopt a shared regional authoring standard that distinguishes battlefield role from source identity, ecosystem circulation from a recruitment pool, and historically supported regional expression from cosmetic variation.

Current benchmark decision: `NoBenchmarkReady`. Candidate areas remain future challenge scopes only. No region, company, unit, market, route, pool, price, quantity, or current availability is admitted by this standard.

## 3. Purpose

The standard gives future authors a repeatable way to develop historically grounded mercenary service without flattening different sources into a few generic hired-unit labels. It supports comparable military roles expressed through materially distinct people, institutions, equipment traditions, service forms, operating networks, and employer relationships when evidence supports each distinction.

Its central test is not whether a regional name can be invented. It is whether a supported source, route, service relationship, content identity, meaningful difference, finite capacity, and owner route can be demonstrated without duplicating or overruling existing authorities.

## 5. Scope

This standard governs authoring and validation rules for:

- regional mercenary ecosystem descriptions and source relationships;
- home-source, origin, operating, contact, market, and service geography distinctions;
- circulation vocabulary and its permitted interpretive effects;
- source-first naming and regional identity retention;
- shared role classification and source-owned regional expression;
- meaningful variation and rejection of cosmetic duplication;
- service-route and capacity-collision comparison;
- unit, company, and ecosystem entrance tests;
- benchmark entrance and nonmutation requirements;
- regional player-information and AI explanation requirements;
- proposed future implementation handoff requirements.

It does not establish which real region, company, person, unit, or service relationship satisfies those rules.

## 6. What This Standard Owns

This standard owns reusable authoring constraints, evidence requirements, comparison fields, validation cases, nonduplication rules, benchmark entrance criteria, and future review gates for regional mercenary content.

## 11. Regional Mercenary Ecosystem Definition

A regional mercenary ecosystem is a source-backed, owner-routed set of relationships among one or more home sources; people and military traditions; settlements or institutions; possible market/contact contexts; routes and transport; employers and political authorities; service customs and forms; equipment and training traditions; military demand; movement or disruption pressures; finite source capacity and commitments; return and replacement paths; actor-visible information; and historical/era context.

An ecosystem is not a single recruitment pool. It may cross political borders only where exact evidence and owner authorities support those links. A Realm, Theater, Province group, or trade route is not automatically an ecosystem.

## 12. Ecosystem Scales

Use only the scales supported by evidence; do not require every ecosystem to include every scale.

| Scale | Review meaning | Minimum authoring questions |
|---|---|---|
| Local source | Bounded originating people, capability, or service relationship | Who owns the source and its capacity? |
| Settlement or institutional source | Source tied to an owner-defined place or institution | Does the institution/site actually support the service claim? |
| Regional source | A source whose identity or capacity is regionally bounded | What exact authority defines the Region and source? |
| Connected regional cluster | Multiple sources linked by evidence-backed relationships | What proves each connection and shared owner route? |
| Frontier zone | A bounded interface area with source and access dependencies | Which geography and political owners define it? |
| Trade-linked circuit | A possible circulation path linked to owner-defined trade context | Does trade contact merely inform discoverability, or does another owner grant access? |
| Maritime circuit | A possible sea-linked service or movement relationship | Which owners establish port, vessel, crew, route and capacity? |
| Religious or institutional network | A possible service relationship across institutions | Which institution and religious owners authorize it? |
| War and displacement field | A context for movement, separation, return or demobilization | Which event and population owners verify consequences? |
| Company operating range | A bounded service range for a specifically evidenced company | Who owns the company, operating limits and access? |
| Long-distance service network | A supported connection across wider geography | What independent evidence supports every link and return path? |
| Cross-Theater circulation path | A possible connection across Campaign Theaters | Which shared and Realm authorities validate the crossing? |

For each used scale, record required evidence, owner, permitted claim, prohibited inference, availability implications, identity implications, replacement implications, player explanation, AI constraints, and unresolved dependencies.

## 14. Regional Identity

Identity must be supported by evidence about the people/source and service relationship, not inferred from the employer, map location, cultural label, or battlefield role alone.

A regional identity review must answer: who or what the source is; where the capability originates; what settlement, company, institution, community or tradition supports it; why external service is evidenced; what role is supported; what materially distinguishes it from a comparable source; which route permits the proposed service; what source supports replacement; what historical interval applies; and what player-facing name communicates the evidence without false precision.

If any critical identity element is unknown, preserve an explicitly generic internal role or quarantine the identity claim. Unknown is not approval.

## 15. Naming Standard

Use source-first naming. A player-facing or authority-facing identity may be grounded in an evidenced source region, settlement, community, company, captain-led following, institution, guild/urban association, religious institution, equipment tradition, service form, operating region, attested external name, attested self-designation, or justified translated term.

Do not require the word “Mercenary” in every unit name. Do not invent regional labels merely to make lists look complete. Translation may improve readability but must preserve identity scope and must not imply an attested self-name where none is established.

Each naming proposal records the source language/term if known, translation policy, evidence, naming authority, audience, uncertainty, variant-collision search, and rejected alternatives. The standard itself proposes no regional unit or company name.

## 16. Generic Role and Regional Expression

Use a two-layer authoring method:

| Layer | Purpose | Examples of role concepts, not content admissions |
|---|---|---|
| Shared role classification | Describe battlefield/campaign function for comparison | spear line, general melee, shock infantry, missile infantry, crossbow role, skirmisher, mounted scout, mounted missile, mounted shock, escort, guard, siege specialist, maritime fighting contingent, guide |
| Source-owned regional expression | Describe evidenced people, institution, service, and military content | source, service basis, company/following structure, equipment, armor, shields, weapon construction, ammunition, mounts, training, experience, formation, cohesion, command, route familiarity, maritime/siege function, operating range, replacement, political meaning, era |

A role classification is not an exact unit candidate. Statistics remain with the approved military-content and battle/balance owners.

## 17. Meaningful Variation Standard

Every proposed regional unit or company variant must identify at least one evidenced substantive difference affecting campaign or battlefield meaning. Separate unit definitions should normally require multiple independent meaningful differences and a documented nonduplication decision.

Review differences across: service source; regional eligibility; institutional support; equipment; training or experience; formation; campaign mobility; terrain/route use; maritime or siege function; contract duration; employer restrictions; command; replenishment/replacement; supply and transport burden; casualty recovery; political consequence; institutional meaning; AI use; and player decision value.

The evidence must establish the difference and its scope. A field populated with a guess is not a meaningful difference.

## 18. Cosmetic Duplication Rejection

Reject separate regional unit definitions whose only difference is name, color, banner, icon, model decoration, accent, minor statistical adjustment, generic cultural bonus, or generic terrain bonus.

Where the underlying military content is the same, use one approved unit definition and distinct owner-authorized service/access references. If variation is presentation-only, treat it as presentation under its own owner and do not create a second unit.

## 19. Regional Flavor Without Identity Essentialism

Derive regional flavor only from evidence-backed military institutions, equipment production/access, service customs, settlement/market relationships, Geography, routes, maritime practice, political relationships, training and experience, historical contact, war/service history, technology/craft, and social/institutional organization.

Never assign fixed loyalty, courage, discipline, aggression, treachery, reliability, intelligence, skill, or threat from ethnicity, race, language, religion, culture, nationality, or geographic origin. Identity does not determine behavior. A source may affect access to language, equipment, institution, route familiarity, or replacement only where those are supported material relationships with relevant owners.

## 21. Service-Route Distinction

Keep domestic recruitment, regional recruitment, controller access, allied contingent, affinity contingent, mercenary contract, personal retinue, institutional service, religious service, external military service, and campaign-specific support separate.

One source unit family may be referenced through multiple service routes without duplicating the definition. Routes may have distinct employer, payment, duration, command, replenishment, replacement, access, withdrawal, and political consequences only where owners support those differences.

Service route does not create underlying source capacity, agreement, access, or force.

## 22. Company and Unit-Family Relationship

Keep company identity, service source, force composition, unit family, active unit instance, company roster capability, contracted deployment, current survivors, experience, and replacement source distinct.

A company may reference more than one unit family; a family may be referenced by more than one company. A company name does not create a unit, and a regional unit name does not create a company. Company depletion does not rewrite the canonical definition. Company experience does not transfer to every unit instance of a family.

No company schema, captain, broker, or actual company identity is created by this standard.

## 23. Regional Source and Finite Capacity

Every capacity claim must identify its capacity owner, source reference, eligible use, geographic/institutional scope, competing commitments, current reservation state, replenishment/replacement owner, and evidence/version. Capacity is finite and reconciled across domestic, regional, allied, contract, retinue, institutional, religious, and other permitted consumers.

No pool size, recovery rate, company size, replacement quantity, readiness level, or capacity instance is supplied here. A source may be historically attested yet not have current eligible capacity.

## 24. Circulation Inputs

Circulation review may consume owner-provided observations about source capacity, peace/war, demobilization, displacement, exile, succession conflict, political exclusion, economic disruption, market contact, trade/maritime connection, route safety, port access, season, religious expedition, institutional sponsorship, prior service, employer demand, competing commitments, arrears/disputes, casualty burden, return feasibility, and known information.

Each input must name its owner, effective context, source/provenance, visibility, confidence and expiry/currentness rule. Circulation is not a single owner truth score.

## 25. Circulation Effects

Circulation evidence may inform discoverability, possible contact, supported operating range, uncertainty, possible employer options, return constraints, and replacement feasibility. The responsible owner decides whether these effects are valid.

No circulation input automatically creates a company, person, offer, equipment, transport, consent, access, capacity, market, route, service readiness, or recruitment right. It cannot override private state or external-owner refusal.

## 26. Availability Stages

Availability is a multi-stage, owner-backed assessment, never one undifferentiated boolean:

1. Content definition exists and is admitted for review.
2. Historical and regional eligibility is supported.
3. A source is evidenced.
4. Contact is possible.
5. An offer may be sought or received.
6. A company/following candidate is valid, if this content type requires one.
7. Finite capacity is owner-confirmed.
8. Source and employer authorities permit negotiation.
9. Access, route, payment, supply, command, arrival, and return are feasible.
10. Agreement is accepted by relevant owners.
11. Capacity is reserved.
12. Force assembly is confirmed by military owners.
13. Arrival is confirmed by movement/access owners.
14. Readiness is confirmed by military owners.
15. Service begins under effective terms.

Do not collapse those stages into `Available`. A concise future player summary may group stages only if the underlying blockers remain typed and inspectable.

## 27. Markets and Contact Contexts

Market and contact contexts are owner-routed interfaces, not active world objects created by this standard. Possible evidence categories for later review include settlements, ports, fairs, musters, courts, guild networks, religious institutions, frontiers, trade routes, maritime circuits, diplomatic missions, authorized brokers/captains, veteran networks, and demobilization contexts.

No settlement, port, guild, court, war, or route implies a mercenary market or available company. Market ownership, exchange, prices, Treasury and payment remain with Economic and Material Flow and fiscal owners; contact/representation remains with Character, Information and Diplomacy owners.

## 28. Route, Maritime, Port, and Crossing Interfaces

Canonical routes, nodes, ports, crossings, sea/river conditions, and strategic access belong to Geography and applicable maritime owners. Physical transport and delivered supply belong to Logistics/Supply owners. Political permission belongs to Diplomacy/Law owners.

An author must not infer circulation, route safety, access, transport capacity, arrival, or return from a line on a map, trade relationship, port label, Theater association, or past movement. The standard defines no route, port, sea circuit, crossing, or vessel.

## 29. War, Displacement, Exile, Demobilization, and Migration Interfaces

War, political exclusion, exile, flight, displacement, demobilization, migration, casualty, and return are separate owner-provided events or conditions. Each may affect discoverability or feasibility only through evidence and receipts from History, Population, Character, Law, Diplomacy, Military, Economy, and Geography owners as relevant.

None creates a surviving company, available people, consent, transport, capacity, or service automatically. No population-to-force conversion is authorized. Return, resettlement, reintegration, and replacement remain distinct.

## 30. Institution, Guild, Religion, and Government Interfaces

Institutional, guild, religious, household, and Government claims require exact owner references and evidence of the relevant authority, service relationship, scope, and effective interval. A label or association does not establish sponsorship, recruitment, permission, capacity, reliability, or command.

The standard creates no guild effect, religious authorization, Government preference, obligation, legal rule, or institution. Law/Policy governs lawful obligation, consent and enforcement; Diplomacy governs agreement/access; Character and household owners govern Persons and relationships.

## 31. Era Development

Era is an authoring context for supported change, not a universal unlock switch. Record the exact evidence-backed date range and owner classification for each candidate. An era label alone does not prove existence, service form, availability, equipment, or organization.

Development across the project's Early, High, and Late contexts may be considered where sources support changes in institutions, contact, contracting, service range, equipment, record keeping, discipline, or organization. No dates, numerical progression, or automatic transition are established here.

## 32. Early-Era Treatment

For any future Early-era candidate, test rather than assume whether evidence supports hired bands, captain-led following, exiles, adventurers, regional specialists, guards, escorts, maritime service, temporary campaign service, or household-adjacent contract service.

Require contemporary/near-contemporary evidence as appropriate, separate later interpretation from opening knowledge, and preserve uncertainty. Do not project later persistent-company organization into the 1062 opening without direct evidence and owner approval.

## 33. High-Era Treatment

For any future High-era candidate, review whether evidence supports expanded urban/commercial contact, more formal organization, cross-regional specialists, brokerage, institutional contracting, longer service range, administrative records, or specialized missile, mounted, siege, guard, and maritime service.

These are questions for evidence review, not predicted universal developments or availability rules.

## 34. Late-Era Treatment

For any future Late-era candidate, review whether evidence supports larger organization, persistent company identity, advanced finance/contracting, wider operating range, developed intermediary networks, specialized service, or stronger records, negotiation, discipline, and closure.

No later-era form is presumed, and no content may be projected backward into an earlier Campaign opening.

## 35. Theater and Realm Relationship

An ecosystem may be limited to one Realm, connect Realms, cross Theaters, follow a maritime/frontier path, follow an institution, or change with political control only when supported by evidence and owner contracts.

Theater placement is organizational/strategic, not ownership or access. Realm rosters may reference potential service as a dependency but do not own an external ecosystem by default. Do not copy one external ecosystem into every Realm package.

## 36. Unit-Content Admission Test

Before admitting a regional unit-family expression, require:

- exact source references and evidence classification;
- supported identity, role, effective interval and geographic scope;
- service-route basis and owner;
- finite-capacity and availability owner;
- employer/access basis and route/muster feasibility;
- payment or obligation route, equipment source, Supply/transport requirements;
- authorized command, casualty and replacement path;
- withdrawal, return, and persistence route;
- visibility and actor-knowledge policy;
- AI use and player-facing identity;
- meaningful difference from compared unit families;
- nonduplication/capacity-collision review;
- named owner dispositions and unresolved-dependency ledger.

If only the battlefield role is supported, keep the candidate at role-classification level or route it through the correct domestic, regional, allied, retinue, institutional, or other service authority.

## 37. Company-Content Admission Test

Before any future company proposal is admitted, require evidence for the company/following identity or a careful statement that the exact identity is unknown; its people/source and authorized representative; service and employer relationship; geographic operating limits; composition references; finite capacity and current commitments; contract/obligation basis; contact and information visibility; supply, command, casualty, replacement, withdrawal, demobilization and return paths; historical interval; and owner approvals.

Do not infer a company from a unit card, a captain from a name, a broker from contact, persistence from a repeated label, or current availability from historical presence. No company is created by this standard.

## 38. Regional Ecosystem Admission Test

An ecosystem proposal requires a bounded historical/geographic scope, identified owners and dependencies, source evidence for each material link, scale selection, distinction between home and operating geography, supported circulation/contact relationships, capacity and commitment owner, route and service feasibility, currentness/visibility policy, replacement and return route, cross-region comparison, fabrication/uncertainty ledger, player/AI explanation, and owner review.

Missing links remain unknown or blocked. Do not fill them with a generalized mercenary market or inferred network.

## 39. Nonduplication and Collision Rules

For every candidate, compare stable source identity, underlying people/capacity, unit-family definition, equipment/support, geographic eligibility, service basis, active commitments, and relevant effective window across all consumers.

One person, equipment source, animal, transport capacity, support resource, or finite pool unit cannot be counted concurrently for incompatible uses. Different service routes do not require duplicate unit definitions. Duplicate or ambiguous identities are quarantined for the Registry and owner authorities; no IDs or aliases are assigned here.

## 40. Player Information

An eventual player-facing summary may expose only authorized, actor-visible owner information: known source identity/region/network; unit/company identity if confirmed; service route; operating range; restrictions; supported capability and meaningful difference; discoverability and availability stage; legitimately known price/obligation; arrival conditions; Supply/transport responsibility; command relationship; replacement source; withdrawal/return rules; service history; uncertainty; political consequences; and alternatives.

Do not expose unsupported exact values, undisclosed terms, private intent, external-private data, or another actor's knowledge. Unknown, hidden, stale, contradictory, and not-applicable remain distinct.

## 41. AI Decision Requirements

AI may compare owner-backed need/role gap, source legitimacy, regional eligibility, finite capacity, meaningful difference, service route, payment, Supply, route, arrival, command, replacement, withdrawal, return, political consequences, uncertainty, and alternatives including domestic/regional/allied/retinue/institutional service, delay, a reduced objective, or abstention.

AI must use actor-visible inputs, explain evidence and blockers, and submit owner-routed proposals. It must not prefer a source because of a cultural label, generate variants to fill a role catalogue, receive free/global service, infer access from a Region label, or execute owner state directly.

## 42. Positive Validation Fixtures

Future explicitly synthetic, non-content fixtures should demonstrate that:

- comparable crossbow roles retain distinct source identity only when evidence supports it;
- a unit family may appear through several service routes without duplicate capacity;
- foreign service preserves source identity;
- a regional name communicates supported source and meaningful difference;
- company identity remains separate from unit identity;
- circulation changes discoverability without creating source capacity;
- a known route does not create market access;
- regional eligibility may coexist with current unavailability;
- one source area may lack a service form present in another;
- an internal generic role may remain generic until identity evidence is adequate;
- historical uncertainty blocks a falsely precise name;
- later-era organization is not projected into an earlier opening.

Fixtures must use placeholder references only, be labeled synthetic, and create no actual regional content.

## 43. Negative Validation and Hard Fails

Hard-fail a proposal that:

- copies a generic role into every region or uses cosmetic rename only;
- uses a flat cultural bonus or identity-based reliability/loyalty;
- treats Region, route, market, settlement, Theater, wealth, or war as owner/access/capacity/supply;
- creates a company from a unit card or a unit from a company name;
- counts the same people/support in regional and contract pools;
- erases source identity through foreign service;
- projects later organization into the 1062 opening without evidence;
- treats a legacy list as a current fact;
- treats missing evidence as zero, approval, or availability;
- admits a role/name despite evidence supporting a more precise distinct identity;
- invents regional names or facts for flavor.

## 44. Regional Benchmark Entrance Standard

A future benchmark must have bounded geographic and historical scope; controlling regional/Realm authorities; candidate service forms; source-backed unit-family and, where relevant, company/captain/institution/contact evidence; circulation and route evidence; era treatment; finite capacity and replacement ownership; employer access; payment, Supply, command, casualty, withdrawal and return interfaces; uncertainty/fabrication-risk ledger; player and AI information requirements; nonduplication comparison; explicit owner gates; no Opening State activation; no payload; and no Unreal implementation.

A benchmark must be allowed to conclude that no exact company, unit, or access claim can be admitted. Entrance is for a bounded review challenge, not production authorization.
