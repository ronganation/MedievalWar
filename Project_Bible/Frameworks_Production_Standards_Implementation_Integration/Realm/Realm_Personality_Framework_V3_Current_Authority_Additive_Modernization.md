## Purpose

The Realm Personality Framework defines how a realm understands its position and converts historical pressure, geography, institutions, political structure, economy, military capacity, dynastic interests, and public memory into strategic preferences.

Realm Personality answers:

- What does this realm value most?
- What does this realm fear?
- What kinds of solutions appear legitimate?
- What kinds of risk will the realm accept?
- How does the realm prefer to expand, defend, negotiate, govern, and settle succession?
- Which institutions and actors does the realm trust?
- How does success alter realm behavior?
- How does crisis expose internal contradictions?

Realm Personality does not answer:

- which army moves this turn
- which character personally agrees
- who legally owns a title
- whether sufficient treasury, recruitment, supply, or authority exists
- what historical outcome must occur

Those are resolved by the appropriate systems and AI layers.

## Core Design Thesis

```text
Historical Reality
+
Realm Structure
+
Current Conditions
↓
Realm Personality Interpretation
↓
Weighted Strategic Preferences
↓
Realm AI Decision
```

Realm Personality is an interpretation layer.

It is not a self-contained AI and not a faction bonus package.

## Medieval II and Total War Heritage Rule

### Preserve the Medieval II Core

Realm personalities must make factions immediately legible through:

- strategic objectives
- military posture
- diplomatic behavior
- territorial priorities
- settlement policy
- recruitment preferences
- succession reactions
- campaign presentation

The player should be able to recognize a realm's strategic character from map behavior without reading every internal variable.

### Stainless Steel Influence

Realm identity should incorporate:

- historical geography
- regional military traditions
- frontier and heartland differences
- plausible political pressure
- culturally grounded recruitment and diplomacy

### Shogun 2 Discipline

Every personality value must have a clear purpose.

Avoid bloated personality matrices that do not alter decisions.

### Rome II, Attila, and 1212 AD Influence

Personality may shape:

- province specialization
- religious policy
- devastation response
- army identity
- regional integration
- imperial overstretch behavior

### Empire and Napoleon Influence

Personality may value strategic geography differently:

- ports
- river routes
- forts
- crossings
- resource sites
- maritime corridors

### Selective CK3 Influence

Personality may interpret:

- dynastic succession
- claims
- regency
- relationships
- legitimacy
- marriage politics

It must not turn the realm into court-management AI.

## Non-Script Rule

```text
Personality changes probability,
priority,
and acceptable cost.

Personality does not force decisions.
```

A warlike realm may choose peace when:

- supply is inadequate
- succession is unstable
- treasury is weak
- coalition risk is high
- the objective is low value

A cautious realm may choose war when:

- survival is threatened
- opportunity is exceptional
- legitimacy requires action
- delay is more dangerous

## Personality Statement

A one-paragraph statement should explain how the realm sees itself and its strategic environment.

The statement should answer:

- what the realm believes it is
- what threatens that identity
- what kind of future appears desirable

## Core Values

Recommended count:

```text
3 to 6
```

Possible values:

- stability
- autonomy
- prestige
- legitimate kingship
- regional consent
- institutional continuity
- expansion
- maritime access
- sacred legitimacy
- dynastic continuity
- noble cooperation
- military reputation
- trade security
- cultural survival

Values should be ranked or weighted.

## Core Fear

Every realm needs one principal systemic fear.

Examples:

- England: succession fracture and constitutional breakdown
- Normandy: expansion exceeding governance capacity
- Norway: royal strength failing to become durable kingship
- Ireland: outside domination or one dynasty destroying network legitimacy
- Scotland: regional fragmentation defeating kingdom consolidation
- Wales: division enabling external conquest

The fear should produce strategic decisions, not merely narrative tone.

## Central Question

Every personality should define one campaign question.

Examples:

```text
Can England transfer legitimacy without destroying stability?
```

```text
Can Normandy scale ambition into durable rule?
```

```text
Can Norway build institutions before founder strength disappears?
```

```text
Can Ireland create wider authority without erasing regional autonomy?
```

The central question guides scenario design and AI priorities while preserving alternate outcomes.

- unsupported claimant installation

A rejected solution may still occur during extreme pressure or ruler-dynasty conflict.

## Ruler Interaction

The ruler may:

- reinforce realm personality
- redirect it
- strain it
- temporarily contradict it
- transform it over time

A ruler cannot instantly rewrite realm identity.

Persistent divergence may cause:

- noble resistance
- institutional friction
- public-order pressure
- dynastic conflict
- long-term personality evolution

## Dynasty Interaction

Dynasty AI asks what benefits the house.

Realm Personality asks what fits the realm's identity and strategic order.

Possible tensions:

- dynasty seeks partition, realm favors unity
- dynasty seeks foreign claim, realm favors consolidation
- dynasty seeks office monopoly, realm favors balance
- dynasty seeks restoration, current realm favors accommodation

## Character Network Interaction

Network power centers mediate realm personality.

A nominally centralized realm may act consultatively because:

- nobles control musters
- bishops control recognition
- regional rulers control access
- consorts and households coordinate government

Personality cannot bypass actual network capacity.

## Founder Dependency Interaction

Founder-centered realms may temporarily behave more boldly than institutions can sustain.

Personality should distinguish:

- confidence generated by the founder
- durable realm capacity
- transition preparedness

High prestige under a founder should not be mistaken for institutional security.

## Underage and Regency Interaction

A minor ruler may preserve realm personality symbolically while government is executed by a regency.

Regent behavior may:

- preserve realm norms
- centralize temporarily
- favor dynasty interests
- empower institutions
- produce regional resistance

## Ecclesiastical Authority Interaction

Personality may weight:

- church cooperation
- crown appointment influence
- monastic patronage
- sacred legitimacy
- mediation
- institutional independence

Actual canonical authority and recognition remain owned by the Ecclesiastical Authority Framework.

## Character Archetype and Development Interaction

Realm Personality influences which strategic functions are valued and which development opportunities are available.

Examples:

- maritime realm develops Naval Magnates and Port Lords
- consultative realm values Statesmen and Coalition Brokers
- expansion realm develops Operational Executors
- founder-dependent realm needs Institution Builders and Prepared Heirs

The realm does not assign identity or destiny to individuals.

## Historical Momentum Integration

Historical Momentum supplies pressure.

Realm Personality weights response.

Example:

```text
Succession Crisis
+
England Personality
→
Consultation, recognition, and stability weighting
```

```text
Succession Crisis
+
Normandy Personality
→
Preparation, claim, and operational opportunity weighting
```

The same momentum may produce different outcomes.

## Historical Emergence Integration

Realm Personality affects which emergent roles gain support.

Possible examples:

- unifier
- claimant
- restorer
- reformer
- resistance leader
- military entrepreneur
- regional kingmaker
- ecclesiastical mediator

It does not predetermine which person fills the role.

## Personality Stability and Evolution

Realm Personality is persistent but not immutable.

Possible change sources:

- long occupation
- successful unification
- institutional reform
- repeated succession outcomes
- religious transformation
- regional integration
- loss of heartland
- maritime expansion
- durable multi-realm government
- catastrophic defeat

Changes should be:

- slow
- evidence-based
- visible
- historically plausible
- reversible only through further long-term change

## Personality State Layers

### Core Identity

Slowest-changing values and fears.

### Strategic Posture

Current interpretation of threat and opportunity.

### Crisis Mode

Temporary emergency weighting.

### Success Mode

Temporary or transitional weighting after major achievement.

### Transformation Pressure

Long-term accumulated pressure toward identity change.

## Realm AI Integration

Realm AI receives:

- personality weights
- current realm state
- primary objective
- military and economic constraints
- network support
- dynasty pressure
- momentum
- risk

Realm Personality must not issue commands directly.

## Primary Objective Rule

Realm AI normally maintains:

```text
One Primary Objective
+
Zero to Two Supporting Objectives
```

Personality weights candidate objectives.

It does not create unlimited simultaneous agendas.

Suggested formula:

## Validation Rules

Fatal:

- personality directly owns mutable realm state
- personality forces a historical outcome by date
- personality grants title, army, office, resource, or claim directly
- realm personality and ruler personality are stored as one object
- current AI objective is stored in static personality definition
- personality bypasses supply, treasury, military, succession, or authority requirements
- runtime personality uses unseeded nondeterministic changes

Warning:

- realm has no core fear or central question
- more than six core values are active
- personality is only a bonus package
- preferred solutions have no strategic output
- rejected solutions are impossible under all circumstances
- realm AI follows personality despite catastrophic constraints
- personality duplicates Realm AI logic
- realm identities differ only in numeric aggression
- current posture generates repeated low-impact player alerts

## Final Design Summary

The Realm Personality Framework gives each realm a persistent but adaptable strategic identity.

Its core sequence is:

```text
Historical Reality and Realm Structure
↓
Values, Fear, and Central Question
↓
Interpretation of Current Pressure
↓
Weighted Strategic Preferences
↓
Realm AI Decision
↓
Visible Map, Military, Diplomatic, Institutional, and Succession Behavior
```

The framework succeeds when England, Normandy, Norway, Ireland, Scotland, and Wales confront the same pressure differently for understandable reasons.

The framework fails if personality becomes either:

```text
A cosmetic paragraph with no AI consequence
```

or:

```text
A rigid script that ignores armies,
resources,
institutions,
and player-created history
```

The intended principle is:

```text
A realm has a character.

A ruler can shape that character.

History can transform that character.

But no personality is stronger than reality.
```

## 2. Purpose and Player Promise

Realm Personality is the persistent but adaptable interpretation layer through which a Realm understands pressure, opportunity, risk, identity, contradiction, and possible strategic responses.

Realm Personality exists to make each Realm legible on the Campaign Map through understandable differences in priorities, proposals, acceptable costs, crisis responses, and long-term transformation.

Realm Personality must preserve the Modern Medieval II campaign spine:

- Realms remain readable through Settlements, Provinces, routes, armies, recruitment, diplomacy, succession, institutions, and strategic objectives.
- Personality influences the proposals and explanations produced from that state.
- Personality never replaces material conditions, legal authority, owner transactions, or player-created history.
- Internal depth remains selectively exposed through concise summaries and optional detail.

```yaml
  - IdentityFormula

  - OnePrimaryContradiction
```

## 7. Core Values, Fear, and Central Question

Core Values identify durable priorities. Core Fear identifies the principal systemic failure the Realm tries to avoid. The Central Question identifies the Campaign tension that binds Realm identity to strategic choice.

```text
Core Value != Permanent Bonus
Core Fear != Automatic Penalty
Central Question != Scripted Answer
Identity Formula != Numeric Modifier
Narrative Statement != Runtime State
```

Values should generally remain bounded to three to six active high-level principles. Additional nuance belongs in attitudes, contradictions, posture, and owner-backed current conditions rather than an ever-growing value list.

## 8. Decision Style and Consultation

Decision Style interprets how the Realm prefers to gather information, consult political actors, delegate authority, tolerate dissent, and resolve uncertainty.

Decision Style cannot invent the political bodies, offices, participants, Information access, or legal procedures required to act.

```text
Consultative Preference != Council Exists
Centralized Preference != Centralized Capacity
Fast Response Preference != Valid Mandate
Consensus-Seeking != Universal Consent
Personal-Royal Preference != Unlimited Royal Authority
```

Political Constitution and Governance owners retain participation, Recognition, legitimacy, mandate, Law, Office, and Government state.

```text
Authority Model != Government State

Political Necessity != Constitutional Permission
Emergency Preference != Government Transformation
```

- ambition versus scalability;
- Royal prestige versus Founder dependency;
- wider kingship versus regional autonomy;
- consolidation versus local custom;
- expansion versus Supply;
- Dynasty continuity versus Realm unity;
