## 1. Executive verdict

Faction development is showing where the current shared systems must become more concrete, interactive, and player-readable.

The existing military authority already contains the correct constitutional direction:

- recruitment is finite;
- population is not identical to recruitable population;
- buildings support recruitment but do not manufacture soldiers;
- equipment access is not equipment inventory;
- technology and era labels do not create people, equipment, training, or active units;
- roster development is conditional and realm-specific;
- AI receives no hidden exemptions;
- units and development choices must explain decisive reasons, blockers, costs, and alternatives.

The future work should make those principles operational across factions while remaining readable to the player.

```text
Population constituency supplies social capacity.
Military source supplies lawful or customary eligibility.
Equipment and training determine what can be organized.
Institutions and technology alter capability over time.
Regional state determines where capability can be realized.
Finite recruitment pools summarize the supportable result.
Player and AI see reasons, costs, blockers, and alternatives.
```

Do not compress population, recruitment, training, technology, and interface into one undifferentiated replacement system. Develop them as linked authorities with explicit contracts.

## 2. Why faction work exposed the need

Abstract systems alone cannot reveal every requirement. Realm work supplies concrete tests.

- England requires continuity, reform, and possible hybridization without enforcing conquest.
- Normandy requires internal development and selective continental influence within a persistent Norman roster.
- North African realms expose social, tribal, urban, religious, regional, contractual, and institutional recruitment sources.
- Norway exposes long continuity with uneven development from district, free-farmer, maritime, household, regional, town, craft, and later specialist foundations.
- Denmark and Sweden will share Norse Theater comparison questions while retaining separate histories, populations, institutions, geography, resources, and development.

Generic labels such as levy, peasant, militia, professional, noble, tribal, foreign, or elite cannot explain a unit by themselves. The game must be able to show:

- who supplies the people;
- why those people may serve;
- which authority or agreement can call them;
- which institution organizes them;
- which equipment is available;
- what training and cohesion exist;
- where the unit can be raised;
- which other duties are weakened;
- how losses are replaced;
- what political, economic, labor, and logistical burden follows;
- why the unit differs from another troop with similar weapons.

Faction work should continue to inform shared frameworks. Shared frameworks must not erase faction discoveries through universal cookie-cutter ladders.

## 3. Controlling principles

### 3.1 Preserve the Modern Medieval II campaign spine

Preserve:

- visible armies and settlements;
- finite replenishing recruitment pools;
- clear regional recruitment;
- understandable unit roles;
- strong faction identity;
- satisfying military development;
- meaningful garrisons, sieges, recovery, and replacement;
- historical atmosphere;
- player freedom to change history.

Modernize the causality beneath those surfaces:

- finite people;
- obligations and contracts;
- equipment ownership and access;
- training and experience;
- political compliance;
- household and regional relationships;
- financial capacity;
- routes, transport, supply, and seasons;
- replacement and recovery;
- conditional technology adoption;
- explainable AI.

### 3.2 Historical foundation with playable flexibility

History supplies institutions, terminology, chronological ranges, social foundations, military traditions, equipment evidence, political relationships, constraints, and plausible contacts.

Game design may add readable umbrella names, classified gameplay adaptations, counterfactual development paths, and meaningful unit distinctions when exact historical regiment titles are unavailable.

Uncertainty must not erase good gameplay. Gameplay need must not fabricate certainty.

### 3.3 Realm identity without deterministic weakness

Do not assign a hidden permanent judgment such as backward or slow-developing.

Each realm begins with concrete conditions:

- population distribution;
- settlement network;
- legal and customary service;
- government reach;
- household and regional power;
- craft and trade capacity;
- equipment access;
- routes and transport;

Players and AI may change these conditions causally. A realm can outperform history without ceasing to be itself.

## 5. General population breakdown

### 5.1 Two-axis model

Separate social identity from military access.

```text
Axis A: Who are the people in civil society?
Axis B: Why and through which institution may some serve?
```

Example:

```text
Free rural householders
+ coastal district obligation
+ valid muster
+ actual equipment
+ assembly and command
= possible district contribution
```

The same people might instead provide bows, crews, provisions, transport labor, local watch, or no military force if conditions fail.

### 5.2 Shared functional constituencies

Player-facing names and membership must be realm-specific.

#### Rural householders and agricultural communities

Possible military interfaces:

- local defense;
- district obligation;
- levy or militia service;
- spear, bow, sling, or javelin service where supported;
- transport and labor;
- ship provisioning or crews;
- emergency mobilization;
- later equipment adoption.

Required distinctions:

- free versus dependent status;
- household property;
- seasonal labor pressure;
- legal obligation;
- muster authority;
- local-defense cost;
- regional variation;
- compliance or resistance.

#### Townspeople, artisans, merchants, and trading communities

Possible military interfaces:

- town watch and urban militia;
- guards;
- crossbow or other equipment-intensive service;
- equipment production and repair;
- engineering and siege support;
- paid troops;
- imported equipment;
- financial contribution.

Boundaries:

- weapon producer is not automatically a soldier;
- merchant contact is not equipment stock;
- town existence is not a militia;
- guild existence is not a guild-named combat unit.

#### Noble, landed, royal, and magnate households

Possible military interfaces:

- household guards;
- retainers;
- mounted service;
- officers and command;
- better-equipped foot;
- restricted elite formations;
- politically contingent forces.

Boundaries:

- title is not a regiment;
- nobles are not an unlimited knight pool;
- horses, equipment, training, and consent remain separate;
- household troops committed to war leave other duties exposed.

#### Pastoral, mobile, highland, frontier, island, and border communities

Possible interfaces:

- scouts, guides, skirmishers, local specialists, frontier defense, patrol, regional infantry, missile troops, and light horse where actual horse systems support it.

Boundaries:

- geography does not create a universal combat bonus;
- pastoral does not automatically mean cavalry;
- frontier identity does not grant free raiding.

#### External, contracted, migrant, and diaspora communities

Possible interfaces:

- mercenaries;
- specialist knowledge;
- foreign equipment;
- allied contingents;
- contractual service;
- culturally distinct regional units;
- hybrid formations.

Boundaries:

- contracts expire;
- allegiance, payment, supply, and return matter;
- external people do not become a permanent domestic pool automatically;
- equipment contact does not equal local manufacturing.

#### Religious, order, clerical, and institutional communities

Possible interfaces:

- order troops;
- guards;
- sponsored contingents;
- logistics and medical support;
- conditional volunteers.

Boundaries:

- religion does not spawn soldiers;
- a church does not automatically unlock an order;
- foundation, recognition, property, membership, command, equipment, geography, and mission require verification.

### 5.3 Overlap and no double counting

People and households may hold overlapping attributes. A free farmer may own a ship. A craftsperson may join a town watch. A magnate household may include mounted and dismounted personnel.

```text
Identity may contain multiple attributes.
A current military commitment reserves the relevant eligible capacity for one incompatible duty.
```

## 7. Finite recruitment pools and recovery

### 7.1 Goal

Preserve Medieval II-style finite replenishing pools while making recovery causal.

The player should understand:

- how many formations can be raised;
- why access is blocked;
- what recovery requires;
- which region supplies the unit;
- what other duty is weakened;
- what development can improve future capacity.

The player should not allocate every person, weapon, horse, ship, ration, or bolt manually.

### 7.2 Intersecting capacities

```text
personnel capacity
∩ service or contract capacity
∩ political compliance
∩ equipment capacity
∩ training capacity
∩ command capacity
∩ settlement or institutional support
∩ financial capacity
∩ logistics capacity
∩ replacement capacity
= supportable recruitment pool
```

### 7.3 Pool families

Future work should define:

- household pools;
- district and obligation pools;
- regional pools;
- town and civic pools;
- institutional pools;
- equipment-constrained pools;
- horse-constrained pools;
- ship-and-crew pools;
- contract pools;
- order pools;
- event and emergence pools;
- replacement-only survivor pools where justified.

A unit may consume several capacities. One source may support mutually exclusive options.

### 7.4 Recovery inputs

- demographic and household survival;
- return from service;
- wounded recovery;
- renewed consent or service terms;
- equipment repair and replacement;
- training throughput;
- restored security;
- treasury;
- route access;
- seasonal labor needs;
- political recovery;
- return of ships, horses, and specialists.

Do not use one universal timer.

### 7.5 Visible consequences

Recruitment may reduce workforce, home defense, ship crews, household protection, craft output, equipment stocks, treasury, civil construction, political cooperation, and replacement speed.

Consequences must create strategy without becoming spreadsheet punishment.

## 8. Training, organization, experience, and cohesion

### 8.1 Required separation

Do not compress quality into one score.

```yaml
training_dimensions:
  weapon_familiarity: Safe and effective use of assigned equipment
  collective_drill: Ability to move and fight as an organized group
  command_integration: Ability to understand and execute orders
  cohesion: Ability to maintain purpose under pressure
  service_experience: Accumulated real service and battle exposure
  institutional_continuity: Ability to preserve standards between musters
  replacement_absorption: Ability to integrate new personnel
  special_environment_competence: Site maritime siege mounted terrain or climate skill
```

### 8.2 Proposed descriptive bands

```yaml
Improvised: Hastily assembled with little collective preparation
Basic: Familiar with local service or equipment but weak formation discipline
Practiced: Repeated service and workable command routines
Trained: Deliberate weapon and formation preparation
Well_Trained: Strong collective practice and dependable command response
Veteran: Sustained surviving experience that cannot be purchased instantly
Select_Household_or_Professional: Restricted intensively supported personnel, not automatically best everywhere
```

Do not force one linear ladder. Veteran local troops may lack formal drill. A newly trained guard may lack battle experience. A town militia may be practiced in local defense and poor on expedition.

### 8.3 Development and degradation

Possible development causes:

- repeated musters;
- drills and institutions;
- surviving veterans;
- reforms;
- stronger command;
- equipment standardization;
- seasonal or permanent service;
- war experience;
- foreign service;
- household patronage;
- town or order support.

Possible degradation:

- casualties;
- rapid expansion;
- poor replacements;
- commander loss;
- inactivity;
- unpaid service;
- political division;
- hunger and supply failure;
- equipment inconsistency.

### 8.4 Formation and spacing

1212's spacing idea is useful, but Medieval War should explain organization visibly. Possible terms include Loose, Open, Standard, Close, and Dense where tactically valid.

Tighter is not universally better. It may improve control while increasing vulnerability to missiles, terrain disruption, or fatigue.

### 8.5 AI use

AI should understand protection needs, replacement difficulty, local versus expeditionary suitability, garrison use, training investment, veteran preservation, and when basic troops are preferable to specialists.

## 9. Knowledge, technology, adoption, and realm development

### 9.1 Core rule

```text
Research reveals, organizes, authorizes, or improves possibility.
Research does not manufacture people, weapons, horses, ships, institutions, or compliance.
```

### 9.2 Three linked layers

#### Shared knowledge

Examples include crossbow manufacture, fortification, siegecraft, metallurgy, horse management, ship design, record keeping, supply organization, medicine, and drill.

Knowledge can spread through trade, war, captured equipment, foreign service, migration, dynastic contact, religious institutions, craftspeople, mercenaries, scholars, alliances, and patronage.

#### Realm programs

Possible Norway programs:

- District Muster Inspection;
- Royal Muster Coordination;
- Crossbow Acquisition and Maintenance;
- Bolt Production Support;
- Coastal Stores Expansion;
- Household Service Reform;
- Fortified Site Provisioning;
- Maritime Supply Organization;
- Town and Craft Patronage;
- Horse Access and Mounted-Service Support where plausible;
- Regional Warning and Assembly;
- Siege and Fieldwork Support.

Other realms require different programs and starting access.

#### Regional realization

A known capability becomes available only where people, institutions, facilities, equipment, training, control, routes, supply, money, and time resolve.

### 9.3 Asymmetry

Every faction should not receive the same tree with renamed nodes.

- Normandy may begin with practices Norway must adopt.
- Norway may begin with district and maritime capabilities absent elsewhere.
- North African realms may develop through different urban, tribal, religious, caravan, horse, craft, and frontier interfaces.
- England may pursue continuity, reform, or hybridization.

### 9.4 Opportunity cost

Technology should force meaningful choices:

- broad district readiness versus elite development;
- bow provision versus crossbow adoption;
- home defense versus expedition;
- towns and workshops versus magnate households;
- ships versus sustained land forces;
- command professionalization versus broad manpower access;
- fortification versus field mobility.

Avoid a tree where the correct answer is always to research everything.

### 9.5 Required record

Each development should identify historical basis, range, influence, sponsor, institutions, people, equipment, training, economy, population, region, acceptance, military need, cost, roster effect, AI use, player explanation, and counterfactual status.

AI should be able to prefer basic units, recover instead of recruit, protect home regions, choose bows when crossbows are unsupported, develop equipment where useful, use contracts, or reject a development path.

AI receives no infinite manpower, free equipment, ignored resistance, instant training, building-generated troops, hidden pools, or universal capability access.

## 23. Final principle

Modern Medieval II should let the player command armies without spreadsheet punishment while keeping armies connected to the world that produces them.

A soldier is not generated by a building. A crossbowman is not generated by a technology. A knight is not generated by a title. A fleet is not generated by a port. A regiment is not generated by a historical name.

People, obligations, institutions, equipment, training, command, geography, politics, economy, routes, supply, time, and player choice combine to make capability possible.

Shared systems should calculate that depth. The interface should explain the decisive result. Factions should express it differently. The player should be able to change history without escaping causality.
