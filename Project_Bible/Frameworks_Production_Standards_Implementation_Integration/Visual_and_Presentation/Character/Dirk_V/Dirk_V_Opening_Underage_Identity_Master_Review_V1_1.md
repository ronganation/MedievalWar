## 7. Unsupported Heraldry and Ornament Review

### 7.1 Large background animal device

**Decision: Blocking defect.**

The large gold animal device occupies a substantial portion of the dark-blue background hanging and sits close enough to the head to become part of the identity read. This review does not determine whether the animal resembles a historically associated device. The controlling issue is narrower: neither a Holland device nor a personal Dirk device has been authorized for this asset. The symbol therefore risks being interpreted as canonical personal, dynastic, or territorial heraldry.

The size, contrast, placement, and repetition of the device prevent the source master from passing directly into derived testing.

### 7.2 Shoulder-fastening animal motif

**Decision: Blocking reinforcing defect.**

A circular gold-toned fastening is compatible with high-status household dress. Standing alone, its small central motif could have remained a minor decorative uncertainty. It becomes blocking as a reinforcing detail because the background hanging repeats the same or a closely related animal device. The repetition visually converts two decorative elements into an apparent heraldic system.

The fastening itself should remain. Only the central motif requires neutralization.

### 7.3 Gold trim density

**Decision: Minor but included in the bounded correction.**

The garment trim is coherent and technically well formed, but the combined gold coverage at the neckline, mantle edge, shoulder line, and front garment borders competes with the face more than the approved priority order permits.

The garment should remain visibly high quality. The correction should reduce visual dominance through a modest decrease in brightness, pattern density, or total gold coverage without reducing Dirk to low-status clothing.
