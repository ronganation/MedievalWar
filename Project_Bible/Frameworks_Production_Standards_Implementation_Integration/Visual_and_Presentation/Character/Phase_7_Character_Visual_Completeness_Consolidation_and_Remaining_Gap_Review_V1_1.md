## 5. Medieval Graphic Presentation Direction

### 5.1 Core hybrid

Medieval War should not choose between realistic character identity and medieval artistic presentation as if only one can exist. The stronger direction is a layered hybrid:

```yaml
identity_source_layer: RealisticHistoricallyGroundedCharacterMaster
card_art_layer: MedievalGraphicInterpretationAndFraming
information_layer: ClearModernStrategyGameUI
battlefield_layer: ReadableFactionAndRoleIconography
```

The realistic identity master preserves who the character is. The medieval graphic layer determines how that identity is presented inside a particular game surface. The information layer preserves Total War-style usability.

### 5.2 Adaptation principle

*Pentiment* is useful as a directional reference because it demonstrates that historical interfaces and figures can feel authored by their period rather than placed inside generic fantasy panels. Medieval War should adapt that principle rather than copy the exact line work, animation, proportions, typography, or page composition.

The intended Medieval War result is:

> **Historically grounded realism interpreted through manuscript, seal, parchment, painted-panel, metalwork, and heraldic design cues, while remaining readable at campaign and battle-management scale.**

```yaml
exact_Pentiment_style_copy: prohibited
medieval_authored_feeling: desired
realistic_identity_abandoned: false
full_photoreal_card_surface: not_required
modern_readability_abandoned: false
faction_specific_variation: desired
```

### 5.3 Character and general cards

Character and general cards may derive from the realistic accepted identity master while applying bounded treatment such as:

- restrained painted or inked edge treatment;
- parchment, vellum, painted wood, textile, or aged-plaster fields;
- a heraldic, dynastic, ecclesiastical, civic, military, or household surround appropriate to the role;
- simplified tonal grouping for compact readability;
- manuscript-inspired border rhythm without excessive ornament;
- rank, office, condition, loyalty, or status conveyed through small seals, badges, tabs, and frame components;
- removable crown, mantle, armor, travel clothing, mourning treatment, captivity cues, or office insignia when the current state justifies them;
- realistic facial identity retained beneath the stylization.

```yaml
character_card_identity_source: AcceptedIdentityMaster
character_card_treatment: DerivedMedievalGraphicPresentation
face_replacement_during_stylization: prohibited
role_state_baked_permanently_into_identity: prohibited
compact_readability_priority: high
ornamental_density: restrained
```

A card does not need to look like an untouched realistic portrait crop. It may be deliberately processed into a medieval graphic presentation as long as the character remains recognizable and the treatment is reproducible.

### 5.4 Unit cards

Unit cards need not use the character-identity pipeline. Unit cards should prioritize formation identity, battlefield role, recruitment source, equipment silhouette, and faction or regional flavor.

Suitable approaches include:

- a manuscript-like figure or small group rendered with stronger anatomical credibility than literal medieval marginal art;
- shield, banner, weapon, horse, vessel, or armor silhouettes integrated into the composition;
- faction-specific border materials and restrained palette families;
- a consistent role-readable composition across infantry, missile, cavalry, specialist, artillery, naval, mercenary, and regional categories;
- selective heraldic or civic motifs only when owned and appropriate;
- a subdued background field that does not compete with the unit silhouette.

```yaml
unit_card_primary_read: BattlefieldRoleAndFactionIdentity
literal_medieval_anatomical_distortion_required: false
generic_fantasy_card_style: prohibited
photographic_diorama_required: false
role_readability_at_small_scale: mandatory
regional_and_mercenary_distinction: supported
```

The goal is not antiquarian imitation. The goal is a believable medieval visual language serving a modern grand-strategy and real-time-battle game.

### 5.5 Change without remaking the identity masters

The accepted realistic portraits remain useful. Medieval styling can be added later through derived card treatments rather than generating replacement faces.


This keeps the current work intact while opening a practical route toward a more distinctive Medieval War interface.

## 9. Remaining-Gap Review Method

### 9.1 Opening State characters

Review characters already born and required at campaign opening. Classify each as applicable:

```yaml
opening_state_categories:
  - BattlefieldOrGovernmentCharacter
  - AgentCharacter
  - DynasticOrPoliticalWoman
  - UnderageDynasticCharacter
  - EcclesiasticalCharacter
  - CourtOrHouseholdCharacter
```

Being underage does not exclude a character from Opening State when already born. Opening presence remains separate from adult capacity, command eligibility, office control, and marriage agency.

### 9.2 Historical Emergence characters

Characters not appropriate for Opening State must be reviewed for bounded Historical Emergence driven by date windows and world-state conditions.

```yaml
historical_emergence_requires:
  - justified_date_window
  - compatible_world_state
  - valid_realm_or_region_context
  - nonduplication_check
  - authority_owner
  - failure_or_nonappearance_path
```

### 9.3 Visual need classification

```yaml
visual_need_dispositions:
  - ExistingAcceptedIdentitySufficient
  - IdentityDirectionNeededBeforeAnyImage
  - StaticIdentityEventuallyNeeded
  - SharedArchetypeOrLowerCostRepresentationSufficient
  - HistoricalEmergenceVisualDeferred

```

An accepted realistic identity may later receive a derived medieval card treatment without changing its disposition or reopening its identity authority.
