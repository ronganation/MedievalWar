## 5. Service-Form Classification
Ordinary faction recruitment is not assumed because the specialist mission is narrow. Regional recruitment may be stronger where knowledge of wagon and bridge protection is locally bounded. Controller access would need a visible condition. Household service is possible only with evidence of personal attachment; allied or external service may better explain temporary presence. Mercenary contract is one hypothesis, not a conclusion. Institutional, emergency, or religious service may fit the work. Rebel, outlaw, captive, refugee, exile, or displaced-person labels require separate evidence.

## 6. Battlefield Role and Total War Identity
Primary role: campaign-support engineering guard. Secondary role: wagon and bridge protection. Formation behavior favors a compact detachment that obeys a task rather than chasing kills. The rhythm is approach, perform, disengage, and preserve scarce expertise. It should defend itself but never replace a full line. Preferred targets are exposed specialists or vulnerable edges; massed infantry and fresh shock formations are dangerous. Terrain matters: deliberate with tools and baggage; resilient around prepared work sites. Fatigue, ammunition, tools, mounts, water, or ships should create limits. Synergy comes from escort, screening, and timing. Spamming is unattractive because the mission is narrower than the attention burden. No numerical statistics are proposed.

## 7. Equipment and Material-Culture Direction
Primary weapons should follow the source-area question, with practical sidearms and serviceable protection. Plausible shields, helmets, bows, crossbows, tools, tack, or deck-safe clothing remain uncertain and must be researched. No mount should be implied by visual flourish. Baggage communicates purpose without an exact inventory. Repair and replacement are constraints, not a recipe. Visual variety should show individual means; anachronistic plate, famous heraldry, standardized national colors, and unsupported uniformity are prohibited.

## 8. Formation, Command, and Experience
A credible intermediary translates the employer mission into local action, but this draft invents no captain or named leader. Internal command may remain source-based while battlefield orders come from the employer. Communication uses practiced proximity, signals, or runners. Experience comes from repeated specialist work, not an unexplained elite tag. If leadership is lost, the group becomes cautious and mission-focused. Contract service does not automatically transfer command, identity, or political loyalty.

## 9. Campaign Employment
The strategic reason to hire is pioneer protection and wagon defense. Suitable missions are escort, screening, siege, maritime, engineering, or reconnaissance duties named above; unsuitable missions are prolonged open-field replacement of a battle line. Duration, restrictions, transport, supply burden, and political risk need later design. Access must be visible and conditional. Withdrawal, dismissal, return, replacement, and demobilization remain possible outcomes. No contract, fixed offer, schedule, or hidden source list is proposed.

## 10. Finite Recruitment and Replacement Logic
Limits arise from trained people, trusted intermediaries, suitable arms, protection, animals, tools, ammunition, water, transport, and competing obligations. Training time should constrain replacement. Casualties can remove irreplaceable knowledge, while rushed substitutes should feel less experienced without exact statistics. The design must avoid collisions with household, allied, or regional recruitment. No numeric recruitment or sustainment rule is defined.
