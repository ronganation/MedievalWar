## 2. Total War Pitch
An upland company that throws, falls back, and strikes again. The card shows round shields, bright javelins, wool cloaks, and a formation that wants broken ground. It solves the problem of harassing heavier troops before contact and makes a ridge feel like a tactical asset. This is a creative working proposal, not an established historical unit.

## 5. Battlefield Profile
Primary role: mobile javelin harassment. Secondary role: rough-ground flank guard and pursuit screen. A loose formation throws, then closes around shields when threatened. Short spears finish isolated targets. It is useful on hills and broken fields, but poor in a sustained line fight, under concentrated missiles, or against cavalry. It prefers exposed archers, tired infantry, and marching columns. A steady spear line gives it room to withdraw. Spamming it removes the anchor it needs and makes the army fragile.

## 6. Campaign and Recruitment Identity
The concept originates in upland service where local knowledge and seasonal obligations limit replacement. A ruler hires it to make a rough approach costly, not to replace household foot. Access might follow contact with an Irish lord, a frontier request, or a campaign-season agreement. Losses are difficult to replace because practiced movement matters more than identical equipment. A contract ends when an expedition leaves or obligations call the company home. No market, price, pool, route, or availability record is created.

## 7. Medieval II and Stainless Steel Flavor
The Medieval II loop is simple: excellent harassment when terrain helps, poor staying power when it does not. The Stainless Steel flavor is a local silhouette and a recruitment choice tied to geography. The card communicates javelins, hills, and retreat discipline. Its memorable strength is throw, wheel, and return; its painful limitation is inability to hold a center. The campaign story is a foreign ruler borrowing local mountain skill temporarily.

## 8. Visual and Audio Direction
Use layered wool, small round shields, leather protection, and mixed javelin bundles. Keep banners narrow and heraldry restrained. Card art should place the unit on a rocky slope. Animations favor quick volleys and backward steps. Voices are terse commands without accent parody. Avoid plate, late firearms, fantasy tartans, and modern camouflage.
