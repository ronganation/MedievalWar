# Court Politics System
Medieval War — Systems/Court_Politics_System.md

## Overview
The Court Politics System governs internal faction dynamics: succession, intrigue, scandals, loyalty shifts, political factions, noble families, religious influence, guild influence, province governance, settlement specialization politics, and agent-driven manipulation.

It blends:
- CK3’s character-driven politics  
- Medieval II’s loyalty and authority mechanics  
- Rome II’s political parties  
- Attila’s instability model  
- Warhammer/Attila province governance  
- CK3-style noble houses and dynasties  

This system interacts directly with:
- Loyalty & Public Order System  
- Agent System  
- Religious System  
- Diplomacy & Trade System  
- Era Progression System  
- Faction Identity System  
- Event System  
- Supply & Logistics System  
- Settlement Classification System  
- Province System  
- Cultural Geography System  
- Economy & Resources System  
- Guild Politics  
- AI Master Template  

Court politics determine whether a faction remains stable, fractures, or collapses into civil war.

# ============================
# 1. Court Structure
# ============================

Each faction has a **Court**, consisting of:

## 1.1 Ruler
- King / Queen / Sultan / Caliph / Emperor / High Chief  
- Highest authority  
- Determines faction stability  
- Influences diplomacy  
- Influences religion  
- Influences succession  
- Influences province governors  
- Influences guild privileges  
- Influences settlement specialization  
- Influences cultural and religious policy  

## 1.2 Heir
- Next in line  
- Gains traits over time  
- Can be contested  
- Can be assassinated  
- Can trigger succession crises  
- Can be influenced by nobles, guilds, religious leaders  
- Can be manipulated by Court Advisor or Shadow Agents  

## 1.3 Council Members
- Court Advisor  
- Treasurer  
- Marshal / Military Advisor  
- Religious Advisor  
- Diplomatic Advisor  
- Cultural Advisor  
- Guild Representative  
- Province Governor Liaison  

Council members influence:
- Loyalty  
- Public order  
- Diplomacy  
- Religion  
- Economy  
- Military  
- Guild politics  
- Province stability  
- Settlement specialization  
- Cultural integration  

## 1.4 Noble Families / Clans
- Represent internal power blocs  
- Have loyalty values  
- Have cultural/religious preferences  
- Can rebel  
- Can support succession  
- Can oppose the ruler  
- Can control provinces  
- Can influence settlement specialization  
- Can sponsor guilds  
- Can manipulate trade routes  
- Can manipulate supply networks  

## 1.5 Political Factions
- Royalists  
- Reformists  
- Traditionalists  
- Religious Zealots  
- Merchant Guild Bloc  
- Military Bloc  
- Cultural Bloc  
- Provincial Autonomy Bloc  

Political factions shift based on:
- Era  
- Religion  
- Economy  
- War  
- Agents  
- Events  
- Province stability  
- Settlement specialization  
- Cultural geography

# ============================
# 2. Succession System
# ============================

Succession determines who becomes ruler when the current ruler dies, abdicates, or is deposed.

## 2.1 Succession Types
- Primogeniture  
- Elective  
- Religious appointment  
- Clan-based succession  
- Military coup  
- Guild-backed succession  
- Provincial election  

## 2.2 Succession Crises
Triggered when:
- Heir is unpopular  
- Heir is of different religion  
- Heir is of different culture  
- Heir has low loyalty  
- Nobles oppose succession  
- Court Advisor manipulates succession  
- Shadow Agent sabotages succession  
- Guilds oppose succession  
- Provinces oppose succession  

Effects:
- Loyalty drops  
- Public order drops  
- Noble rebellions  
- Civil war  
- Diplomatic penalties  
- Religious intervention  
- Guild intervention  
- Province instability  
- Settlement instability

# ============================

- High instability  
- Frequent noble rebellions  
- Weak central authority  
- Cultural fragmentation  
- Religious fragmentation  

### High Era
- Stronger courts  
- Stronger religious influence  
- Stronger noble families  
- Stronger guilds  
- Province edicts  

### Late Era
- Centralized states  
- Strong bureaucracy  
- Reduced noble power  
- Increased economic politics  
- Settlement specialization fully developed  

Era progression also affects:
