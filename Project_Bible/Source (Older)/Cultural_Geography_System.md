# ============================
# 1. Cultural Regions
# ============================

Every region belongs to a **primary culture** and may contain **secondary cultural minorities**.

## 1.1 Primary Cultural Regions
Examples (Early Era):
- Anglo-Saxon (England Midlands, North)  
- Anglo-Norman (South-East, Normandy)  
- Gaelic (Scotland, Ireland)  
- Welsh (Wales)  
- Norse (Northern coastal pockets)  

## 1.2 Secondary Cultural Minorities
Examples:
- Norse minorities in Scotland  
- Anglo-Saxon minorities in Norman-held regions  
- Gaelic minorities in border marches  
- Welsh minorities in border counties  

Minorities influence:
- Public order  
- Loyalty  
- Recruitment  
- Cultural pressure  
- Religious behavior  
- Province identity  
- Settlement identity

# ============================
# 2. Cultural Pressure
# ============================

Cultural pressure represents how strongly a culture spreads into neighboring regions.

## 2.1 Sources of Cultural Pressure
- Population density  
- Settlement type (cities spread culture faster)  
- Roads & infrastructure  
- Trade routes  
- Religious influence  
- Guild influence  
- Court politics  
- Agents (Priests, Scholars, Diplomats)  
- Era progression  
- Terrain & climate (cultures spread faster in familiar terrain)  
- Province adjacency  
- Cultural events  
- Faction identity traits  

## 2.2 Cultural Pressure Effects
- Increased cultural assimilation  
- Increased cultural unrest (if foreign culture dominates)  
- Increased diplomatic alignment  
- Increased recruitment suitability  
- Increased guild presence  
- Province cultural shifts  
- Settlement cultural shifts  
- Religious conversion speed changes  

---

# ============================
# 3. Cultural Assimilation
# ============================

Assimilation determines how quickly a conquered region adopts the ruler’s culture.

## 3.1 Assimilation Speed Factors
- Settlement type (cities faster, castles slower)  
- Cultural pressure  
- Religious alignment  
- Terrain familiarity  
- Climate familiarity  
- Supply stability  
- Court politics stability  
- Guild influence  
- Agent activity  
- Era progression  
- Province stability  
- Faction identity  

## 3.2 Assimilation Stages
1. **Occupation**  
   - High unrest  
   - High resistance  
   - Limited recruitment  
   - High agent hostility  

2. **Integration**  
   - Moderate unrest  
   - Mixed recruitment  
   - Cultural blending events  
   - Province identity begins shifting  

3. **Assimilation**  
   - Low unrest  
   - Full recruitment  
   - Cultural bonuses apply  
   - Settlement specialization stabilizes

# ============================
# 4. Cultural Resistance
# ============================

Resistance represents how strongly a region opposes foreign rule.

## 4.1 Resistance Sources
- Strong cultural identity  
- Harsh terrain (Highlands, Wales, Ireland)  
- Religious differences  
- Court politics (local nobles resisting)  
- Guild resistance (local guilds refusing integration)  
- Supply instability  
- Seasonal hardship  
- Foreign settlement type mismatch  
- Province instability  
- Cultural events  

## 4.2 Resistance Effects
- Public order penalties  
- Loyalty penalties  
- Increased rebellion chance  
- Reduced recruitment  
- Reduced tax income  
- Increased agent hostility  
- Cultural uprising events  
- Province-wide unrest  
- Settlement instability

# ============================
# 5. Cultural Homeland Bonuses
# ============================

Cultures gain bonuses in their homeland terrain and settlement types.

## 5.1 Examples

### Anglo-Saxon
- Plains & forest movement bonus  
- Lower unrest in Midlands/North  
- Better levy recruitment  

### Anglo-Norman
- Plains & farmland bonuses  
- Urban settlement bonuses  
- Cavalry recruitment bonuses  

### Gaelic (Scotland, Ireland)
- Highland, marsh, and forest bonuses  
- Ambush bonuses  
- Skirmisher recruitment bonuses  

### Welsh
- Mountain & forest bonuses  
- Ambush bonuses  
- Longbow recruitment bonuses  

### Norse
- Snow, coastal, and forest bonuses  
- Raiding bonuses  
- Naval supply bonuses  

Homeland bonuses affect:
- Movement  
- Supply  
- Attrition  
- Recruitment  
- Public order  
- Loyalty

# ============================
# 6. Cultural Terrain Affinities
# ============================

Cultures interact differently with terrain (from Terrain & Climate System).

Examples:
- Highlanders → hills, mountains, snow  
- Irish → forests, marsh, fens  
- Normans → plains, farmlands, urban  
- Norse → snow, coastal, forest  
- Welsh → mountains, forests  

Effects:
- Movement bonuses  
- Supply bonuses  
- Attrition resistance  
- Ambush bonuses  
- Recruitment suitability  
- Agent success modifiers  

---

# ============================
# 7. Cultural Settlement Preferences
# ============================

Cultures prefer certain settlement types.

Examples:
- Anglo-Norman → cities, castles  
- Anglo-Saxon → towns, villages  
- Gaelic → forts, rural settlements  
- Welsh → mountain forts  
- Norse → coastal towns  

Effects:
- Reduced unrest in preferred settlements  
- Increased recruitment  
- Increased economic output  
- Increased cultural pressure  
- Improved supply stability  

---

# ============================
# 8. Cultural Diplomacy Modifiers
# ============================

Cultures influence diplomacy.

Examples:
- Anglo-Saxon ↔ Gaelic → distrust  
- Anglo-Norman ↔ Anglo-Saxon → tension  
- Gaelic ↔ Norse → mixed (raiding vs alliance)  
- Welsh ↔ Anglo-Norman → hostility  
- Anglo-Norman ↔ French → rivalry  

Modifiers affect:
- Alliance likelihood  
- Trade agreements  
- Vassalage acceptance  
- War weariness  
- Diplomatic pressure  
- Religious diplomacy  
- Cultural blocs

# 11. AI Cultural Strategy
# ============================

AI uses cultural geography to:
- Choose invasion targets  
- Avoid culturally hostile terrain  
- Prioritize homeland defense  
- Exploit cultural terrain affinities  
- Manage assimilation  
- Deploy agents  
- Build settlement types that match culture  
- Form alliances based on cultural alignment  
- Trigger cultural events  
- Manage provinces  
- Manage settlements  

AI templates include:
- Highland Defender  
- Plains Empire  
- Forest Ambusher  
- Coastal Raider  
- Cultural Assimilator  
- Cultural Isolationist  

AI adapts across eras:
- Early: cultural fragmentation  
- High: cultural blocs  
- Late: cultural stabilization
