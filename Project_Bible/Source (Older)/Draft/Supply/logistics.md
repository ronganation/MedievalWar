# Logistics System

## Overview
Logistics governs army movement, reinforcement, attrition, siege starvation, and campaign sustainability. Strong logistics allow armies to move quickly and maintain strength; weak logistics lead to attrition and collapse.

---

# Movement Logistics

## Road Movement
- Fast movement
- Lower fatigue
- Vulnerable to rebel disruption

## Forest Movement
- Slow movement
- Increased fatigue
- Ambushes more effective

## Hill Movement
- Slow uphill
- Fast downhill
- Increased fatigue uphill

## Marsh Movement
- Very slow
- High fatigue
- Irish units gain bonuses

---

# Reinforcement Logistics

## Reinforcement Conditions
Armies reinforce faster when:
- Connected to supply routes
- Near towns or castles
- Region loyalty is high
- Economy is strong

Reinforcement slows when:
- Rebels block roads
- Ports are blockaded
- Raids occur
- Population is low

---

# Siege Logistics

## Starvation
Occurs when:
- Settlement is surrounded
- Supply routes are cut
- Port is blockaded

Starvation effects:
- Reduced morale
- Reduced defense
- Increased attrition
- Lower recruitment

## Siege Equipment Logistics
Siege equipment requires:
- Timber
- Iron
- High settlement level
- Strong economy

---

# Winter Logistics

## Winter Effects
- Reduced movement
- Increased fatigue
- Lower morale
- Higher attrition
- Supply reduced

## Faction Winter Traits
Scots: Reduced penalties  
Norse: Moderate penalties  
Anglo-Saxons: Moderate penalties  
Normans: High penalties  
Irish: Moderate penalties  

---

# Naval Logistics

## Naval Supply
Ports provide:
- Coastal supply
- Reinforcement routes
- Trade income

## Blockades
Blockades cause:
- Supply loss
- Trade loss
- Morale penalties

---

# Logistics Penalties

## Raids
- Supply disruption
- Higher attrition
- Slower movement

## Rebellions
- Supply routes cut
- Reinforcement halted

## War Exhaustion
- Slower movement
- Higher upkeep
- Lower morale

---

# Notes
Logistics is essential for campaign sustainability. Armies must maintain supply, avoid attrition, and protect trade routes to remain effective.
