# Supply System

## Overview
Supply determines how effectively armies can fight, move, and survive. Armies require food, equipment, and stable routes to maintain strength. Supply is influenced by economy, trade routes, loyalty, terrain, weather, and faction traits.

---

# Supply Sources

## Towns
- Provide basic supply.
- Higher settlement levels give more supply.
- Loyalty increases supply efficiency.

## Castles
- Provide strong supply.
- Increase supply radius.
- Reduce attrition.

## Ports
- Provide naval supply.
- Enable coastal reinforcement.
- Vulnerable to blockades.

## Trade Routes
- Provide long-distance supply.
- Disrupted by rebels, raids, and war.

---

# Supply Radius
Each supply source projects a radius:
- Village: Small
- Town: Medium
- Large Town: Large
- City: Very Large
- Castle: Very Large
- Port: Coastal radius

Armies outside supply radius suffer penalties.

---

# Supply Penalties

## Low Supply
Effects:
- Increased fatigue
- Lower morale
- Reduced attack
- Reduced defense

## No Supply
Effects:
- Severe morale loss
- High fatigue
- Movement penalties
- Attrition

## Attrition
Occurs when:
- Army is out of supply
- Region loyalty is low
- Winter conditions apply
- Siege lasts too long

Attrition reduces:
- Unit health
- Morale
- Army size

---

# Supply Recovery
Armies recover supply when:
- Resting in friendly territory
- Near towns or castles
- Connected to trade routes
- Near ports

Recovery is faster in:
- High-loyalty regions
- High-population regions
- Fertile terrain

---

# Faction Supply Traits

## Normans
- Strong supply through castles and ports.
- Vulnerable if coastal routes are disrupted.

## Anglo-Saxons
- Strong local supply.
- Weak long-distance supply.

## Scots
- Strong supply in hills.
- Weak supply in lowlands.

## Irish
- Strong local supply.
- Weak long-distance supply.

## Norse
- Strong coastal supply.
- Weak inland supply.

## Rebels
- No formal supply.
- Rely on raiding.

---

# Notes
Supply interacts with economy, loyalty, trade routes, terrain, weather, and recruitment. Armies must maintain supply to remain effective.
