# Settlement Classification System
Medieval War — Systems/Settlement_Classification_System.md

## Overview
The Settlement Classification System defines how settlements are structured, upgraded, specialized, and influenced by terrain, climate, culture, religion, supply, and politics.

Medieval War uses a **Province-Based Settlement Structure**, inspired by:
- Medieval II (City/Castle progression)
- Attila / Warhammer (Major + Minor settlements)
- CK3 (regional hierarchy, cultural influence)
- Thrones of Britannia (province capitals + minor settlements)

This system integrates:
- Terrain & Climate System  
- Cultural Geography System  
- Global Map Systems  
- Supply System  
- Faction Identity System  
- Era Progression System  
- Agent System  
- Court Politics System  
- Guild Politics System  
- Diplomacy & Trade System  
- Loyalty & Public Order System  
- Event System  
- AI Master Template  
- Province System  
- Religious System  
- Economy & Resources System  

---

# ============================
# 1. Province Structure
# ============================

Each **Province** contains:
- **1 Major Settlement** (City or Castle)
- **2–4 Minor Settlements** (Villages, Ports, Resource Sites, Trade Hubs, Religious Centers, Forts)

Provinces provide:
- Province-wide public order  
- Province-wide supply  
- Province-wide cultural pressure  
- Province-wide economic bonuses  
- Province-wide terrain modifiers  
- Province-wide edicts (High Era)  
- Province-wide AI strategic behavior  
- Province-wide religious identity  
- Province-wide cultural identity  

Provinces unify settlement behavior into a single regional identity.

# ============================
# 3. Minor Settlements
# ============================

Minor settlements provide specialization and strategic depth.

## 3.1 Villages
- Basic food production  
- Basic population growth  
- Low supply  
- Low cultural pressure  
- Vulnerable to raids  
- Terrain-dependent output (farmlands, plains, floodplains)  
- High cultural resistance  
- High religious resistance  

## 3.2 Ports
- Naval supply  
- Trade income  
- Ship recruitment (later eras)  
- Vulnerable to blockades  
- Weather-dependent output (storms, fog)  
- Cultural mixing via trade  
- Religious mixing via trade  

## 3.3 Trade Hubs
- High trade value  
- Merchant Guild bonuses  
- Road network synergy  
- Vulnerable to sabotage  
- Cultural trade influence  
- Province-wide economic boosts  

## 3.4 Religious Centers
- High religious pressure  
- Priest recruitment  
- Religious events  
- Cultural conversion  
- Crusade/Jihad/Holy Mission targets  
- Province-wide religious identity influence  

## 3.5 Resource Sites
Resource sites integrate directly with Global Map Systems:

### Mines
- Iron resource  
- Heavy infantry recruitment  
- High economic value  
- Mine collapse events  
- Province-wide industrial bonuses  

### Timber Camps
- Timber resource  
- Archers & siege equipment  
- Forest terrain synergy  
- Fire/storm events  
- Province-wide construction bonuses  

### Horse Pastures
- Cavalry recruitment  
- Cultural horse affinity  
- Disease events  
- Province-wide cavalry bonuses  

### Grain Fields
- Food supply  
- Population growth  
- Supply stability  
- Famine events  
- Province-wide supply bonuses  

## 3.6 Forts
- Defensive fallback  
- Visibility  
- Agent detection  
- Supply radius extension  
- AI fort network building  
- Province-wide defensive influence

# ============================
# 5. Cultural & Religious Influence
# ============================

Settlements interact with Cultural Geography and Religious Systems.

## 5.1 Cultural Influence
- Cities spread culture faster  
- Castles resist foreign culture  
- Villages retain native culture longer  
- Religious centers amplify cultural pressure  
- Trade hubs spread cultural blending  
- Ports spread cultural mixing via trade  
- Province cultural identity shaped by settlement mix  

## 5.2 Religious Influence
- Religious centers spread religion  
- Cities amplify religious conversion  
- Castles resist religious conversion  
- Ports spread religious mixing  
- Cultural resistance increases religious unrest  
- Province religious identity shaped by settlement mix
