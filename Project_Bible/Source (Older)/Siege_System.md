# Overview

The Siege System governs the encirclement, bombardment, starvation, assault, defense, surrender, occupation, and destruction of fortified settlements.

Sieges are intended to be one of the defining features of Medieval War.

A fortified settlement should never function as a simple battlefield encounter.

Instead, fortifications create strategic problems that attackers must solve through:

- Supply
- Logistics
- Siege Equipment
- Engineering
- Technology
- Attrition
- Diplomacy
- Religion
- Politics
- Time

The Siege System integrates:

- Building System
- Recruitment System
- Population System
- Campaign Sustainment & Logistics System
- War Weariness System
- Character System
- Technology & Innovation System
- Government Templates
- Loyalty & Public Order System
- Court Politics System
- Cultural Geography System
- Religious System
- Event System
- AI Master Template

---

# Core Siege Philosophy

Fortified settlements should require preparation.

The attacker must choose:

- Starve
- Bombard
- Negotiate
- Mine
- Assault

Every choice has consequences.

A successful siege should feel earned.

---

# Settlement Classification

## Unfortified Settlements

Examples:

- Villages
- Resource Sites
- Trade Hubs
- Fishing Settlements
- Agricultural Settlements

Unfortified settlements may be assaulted immediately.

No siege equipment required.

---

## Fortified Settlements

Examples:

- Motte & Bailey
- Castle
- Fortress
- Citadel

Fortified settlements require siege preparation.

Immediate assault is not available unless the attacking army already possesses siege equipment.

---

# Mandatory Preparation Rule

A fortified settlement requires a minimum preparation period.

Rule:

If the army does not possess siege equipment:

Minimum Siege Time:
1 Turn

This represents:

- Encirclement
- Camp construction
- Reconnaissance
- Equipment preparation
- Supply organization

---

# Siege Equipment

Siege equipment falls into two categories:

## Constructed During Siege

Built after arrival.

Examples:

### Ladders

Preparation:
1 Turn

Purpose:
Scale walls

Advantages:
Fastest preparation

Disadvantages:
Highest casualties
Highest fatigue
Worst assault method

Ladders are emergency assault tools.

---

### Battering Ram

Preparation:
1 Turn

Purpose:
Destroy gates

Advantages:
Fast
Inexpensive
Reliable

Disadvantages:
Gate-focused

---

### Siege Tower

Preparation:
2 Turns

Purpose:
Deliver troops onto walls

Advantages:
Reduced casualties
Protected approach

Disadvantages:
Longer preparation
Can be destroyed

Preferred assault method.

---

## Pre-Recruited Siege Equipment

Constructed before campaign movement.

Examples:

- Catapult
- Mangonel
- Trebuchet
- Bombard
- Great Bombard
- Ballista

Recruited through military recruitment.

Require:

- Siege Workshop
- Fortress
- Citadel
- Resources
- Technology

# Siege Phases

## Phase 1: Encirclement

Army enters siege stance.

Automatically represents:

- Road control
- Trade disruption
- Local raiding
- Supply interception
- Reconnaissance

Effects:

- Trade reduced
- Recruitment reduced
- Supply reduced

---

## Phase 2: Preparation

Attacker chooses:

- Ladders
- Rams
- Towers
- Mining Operations
- Bombardment

Preparation creates choices before combat.

---

## Phase 3: Active Siege

The settlement remains under siege.

Possible activities:

- Starvation
- Bombardment
- Negotiation
- Disease outbreak
- Counterattacks

---

## Phase 4: Assault

Attacker initiates battlefield combat.

Assault launches a Total War style battle.

Equipment determines assault options.

---

## Phase 5: Resolution

After victory:

- Occupy
- Sack
- Raze
- Integrate
- Install Governor
- Create Vassal

- Negotiated surrender
- Conditional surrender
