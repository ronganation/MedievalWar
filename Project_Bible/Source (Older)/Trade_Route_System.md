# Overview

The Trade Route System governs how goods, wealth, information, technology, religion, culture, and influence move across the medieval world.

Trade routes connect:

- Settlements
- Provinces
- Factions
- Religious Centers
- Guild Networks
- Maritime Networks

Trade is one of the primary drivers of:

- Economy
- Technology Diffusion
- Cultural Exchange
- Religious Exchange
- Diplomacy
- Supply
- Prosperity

Trade routes are strategic assets worth defending, controlling, and fighting over.

---

# Core Philosophy

Trade is more than income.

Trade creates:

- Wealth
- Influence
- Innovation
- Stability
- Strategic dependence

A prosperous faction often depends on stable trade routes.

Trade routes create medieval globalization.

# Land Routes

The most common trade route.

Examples:

- London ↔ York
- Paris ↔ Rouen
- Edinburgh ↔ Newcastle

Benefits:

- Moderate Income
- Stable Supply
- Cultural Exchange

Risks:

- Rebellions
- Banditry
- Blockades
