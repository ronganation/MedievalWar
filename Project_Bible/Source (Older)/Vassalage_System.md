# ============================
# 1. Types of Subjugation
# ============================

Medieval War includes multiple forms of subjugation, each with unique rules.

## 1.1 Vassal State
A faction that retains autonomy but owes:
- Military support  
- Diplomatic obedience  
- Tribute  
- Loyalty  

## 1.2 Protectorate
A faction under military protection that owes:
- Tribute  
- Diplomatic obedience  
- Limited military support  

Protectorates cannot declare war independently.

## 1.3 Tributary
A faction that pays regular tribute but retains:
- Full autonomy  
- Independent diplomacy  
- Independent military  

Tributaries are the weakest form of subjugation.

## 1.4 Marcher State
A border vassal with:
- High military autonomy  
- Defensive obligations  
- Reduced tribute  
- Cultural autonomy  

Marcher states are common in frontier regions.

## 1.5 Religious Vassal
A faction subjugated through:
- Papal authority  
- Caliphate authority  
- Patriarchate authority  
- Tribal Council authority  

Religious vassals owe:
- Religious obedience  
- Crusade/Jihad/Holy Mission participation  
- Religious tribute  

## 1.6 Cultural Vassal
A faction subjugated through:
- Cultural dominance  
- Cultural pressure  
- Cultural assimilation  

Cultural vassals owe:
- Cultural obedience  
- Reduced autonomy  
- Cultural integration

# ============================
# 2. Vassal Loyalty
# ============================

Vassal Loyalty determines how stable the relationship is.

**0–100 scale**
- 0–25: Rebellious  
- 26–50: Unstable  
- 51–75: Stable  
- 76–100: Loyal  

## 2.1 Sources of Loyalty

### Positive
- Shared culture  
- Shared religion  
- Strong ruler authority  
- Strong Court Advisor influence  
- Low war weariness  
- High supply stability  
- High trade value  
- Successful wars  
- Province stability  
- Settlement stability  
- Diplomat missions  
- Religious agent missions  

### Negative
- Cultural mismatch  
- Religious mismatch  
- High war weariness  
- Oppressive tribute  
- Oppressive policies  
- Lost territory  
- Supply collapse  
- Trade collapse  
- Court politics instability  
- Agent sabotage  
- Province instability  
- Settlement unrest  

---

# ============================
# 3. Vassal Obligations
# ============================

Vassals owe their overlord:

## 3.1 Military Obligations
- Provide auxiliary troops  
- Join wars when called  
- Defend overlord territory  
- Provide garrisons for marcher states  

## 3.2 Economic Obligations
- Pay tribute  
- Provide resources  
- Provide trade access  
- Provide supply access  

## 3.3 Diplomatic Obligations
- Follow overlord diplomacy  
- Cannot declare war on overlord allies  
- Must break alliances with overlord enemies  

## 3.4 Religious Obligations
- Join Crusades/Jihads/Holy Missions  
- Follow religious rulings  
- Pay religious tribute  

## 3.5 Cultural Obligations
- Accept cultural integration  
- Reduce cultural resistance  
- Support cultural pressure  

---

# ============================
# 4. Vassal Rights
# ============================

Vassals retain:

## 4.1 Autonomy Rights
- Internal governance  
- Cultural identity  
- Religious identity  
- Settlement development  
- Province development  

## 4.2 Diplomatic Rights (limited)
- Trade agreements  
- Non-aggression pacts  
- Alliances (if not conflicting with overlord)  

## 4.3 Military Rights
- Independent armies  
- Independent recruitment  
- Independent supply networks

# ============================
# 5. Vassal Integration
# ============================

Vassals can be integrated into the overlord’s faction.

## 5.1 Integration Requirements
- High loyalty  
- Shared culture  
- Shared religion  
- Strong ruler authority  
- Strong Court Advisor influence  
- High province stability  
- High settlement stability  
- Low war weariness  

## 5.2 Integration Process
- Cultural assimilation  
- Religious conversion  
- Province absorption  
- Settlement absorption  
- Diplomatic absorption  
- Military absorption  

## 5.3 Integration Effects
- New provinces added  
- New settlements added  
- New recruitment options  
- New cultural bonuses  
- New religious bonuses  
- New economic bonuses

# ============================
# 6. Vassal Rebellions
# ============================

Vassals can rebel if loyalty drops.

## 6.1 Rebellion Triggers
- Loyalty < 30%  
- Cultural mismatch  
- Religious mismatch  
- Oppressive tribute  
- Oppressive policies  
- High war weariness  
- Lost territory  
- Court politics instability  
- Agent sabotage  
- Province instability  
- Settlement unrest  

## 6.2 Rebellion Types
- Diplomatic rebellion  
- Military rebellion  
- Cultural rebellion  
- Religious rebellion  
- Noble rebellion  
- Province-wide rebellion  
- Full independence war  

## 6.3 Rebellion Effects
- Loss of vassal  
- Loss of tribute  
- Loss of military support  
- Loss of diplomatic support  
- Province instability  
- Settlement instability  
- Court politics crisis

# ============================
# 7. Agent Integration
# ============================

Agents interact with vassals:

### Diplomats
- Improve loyalty  
- Improve diplomacy  
- Reduce rebellion chance  
- Improve integration chance  

### Priests / Imams / Orthodox Priests / Shamans
- Improve religious alignment  
- Reduce religious rebellion chance  
- Improve religious vassal loyalty  

### Court Advisor
- Manipulate vassal loyalty  
- Manipulate vassal diplomacy  
- Influence integration  
- Influence rebellion  

### Shadow Agents
- Sabotage vassal loyalty  
- Trigger rebellions  
- Trigger scandals  
- Destabilize provinces  

### Supply Officers
- Improve supply stability  
- Reduce supply-based rebellion chance

# ============================
# 8. Court Politics Integration
# ============================

Vassalage is deeply tied to court politics.

Effects:
- Nobles demand vassal autonomy  
- Nobles demand vassal integration  
- Guilds demand vassal trade  
- Religious leaders demand vassal obedience  
- Court Advisor manipulates vassal loyalty  
- Succession crises affect vassal loyalty  

Vassals often choose sides during civil wars.

---

# ============================
# 9. Diplomacy Integration
# ============================

Vassalage heavily influences diplomacy.

Effects:
- Overlord gains diplomatic weight  
- Vassals follow overlord diplomacy  
- Vassals cannot betray overlord allies  
- Vassals join overlord wars  
- Vassals increase diplomatic pressure  
- Vassals increase trade value  

Diplomacy becomes complex when multiple vassals exist.

---

# ============================
# 10. Religious Integration
# ============================

Religious institutions influence vassalage.

Effects:
- Papacy can demand vassal obedience  
- Caliphate can demand vassal obedience  
- Patriarchate can demand vassal obedience  
- Tribal Council can demand vassal obedience  
- Religious orders influence vassal loyalty  

Religious vassals are the most stable type.

---

# ============================
# 11. Cultural Integration
# ============================

Culture strongly affects vassalage.

Effects:
- Shared culture increases loyalty  
- Cultural mismatch increases rebellion  
- Cultural pressure increases integration  
- Cultural resistance increases instability  

Cultural vassals are the easiest to integrate.

---

# ============================
# 12. Province Integration
# ============================

Vassal provinces behave differently.

Effects:
- Reduced public order penalties  
- Reduced cultural resistance  
- Reduced religious resistance  
- Reduced supply penalties  
- Reduced economic penalties  

Province identity shifts during integration.

# ============================
# 13. Era Integration
# ============================

- Frequent rebellions  
- Strong cultural autonomy  

### High Era
- Stronger vassal loyalty  
- Stronger religious influence

- Strong economic influence

Era progression affects:
- Vassal loyalty

- Vassal diplomacy  
- Vassal integration
