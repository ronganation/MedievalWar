## Design Thesis

England's ecclesiastical order is not a single church actor or loyalty score. It is a distributed system of people, offices, sees, dioceses, chapters, monasteries, churches, communities, estates, archives, relic custodians, jurisdictions, recognition audiences, customary relationships, routes, and local dependencies. Royal government can receive ecclesiastical support without owning church institutions. A church actor can possess practical domestic authority while external or canonical recognition remains disputed. A monastery or chapter can preserve local government, records, refuge, and property continuity without becoming a province or military faction.

The strategic purpose of the system is to make ecclesiastical institutions legible as participants in kingship, succession, law, diplomacy, regional coordination, documentary government, relief, sanctuary, education, property, communication, and historical memory while preserving their distinct owners and limits.

## Recognition Layer Model

```yaml
recognition_layers:
  Royal:
    question: does lawful royal government recognize or employ the person or office
  Domestic:
    question: do relevant English institutions and political actors recognize practical authority
  Chapter:
    question: does the relevant chapter or institutional body recognize the office or holder
  Metropolitan:
    question: is metropolitan authority accepted by relevant suffragans and institutions
  CanonicalConfidence:
    question: what confidence and dispute state exists under controlling church-law and Evidence authorities
  External:
    question: how do foreign ecclesiastical actors receive the office or holder
  Papal:
    question: what papal recognition, dispute, communication, or uncertainty is owner-supported
  Ceremonial:
    question: is the actor eligible for a specified rite under current law, recognition, place, and evidence
```

These layers may diverge. Failure in one layer does not automatically erase every function in another.
