## 1. Purpose

This portfolio defines how England's settlements, strongholds, burhs, ports, estates, institutions, crossings, and strategic sites may specialize without turning regions into identical templates or fragile one-center economies. It governs concentration, distribution, regional balance, fallback capacity, substitution limits, resilience investment, disruption absorption, and recovery reorganization. It does not assign exact 1062 settlement roles, routes, capacities, or values.

```text
Specialization is not self-sufficiency.
Regional balance is not identical development.
Concentration is not automatically efficiency.
Distribution is not automatically resilience.
Redundancy is not free duplication.
A substitute is not always equivalent.
A damaged network may recover in a different form.
A major center does not invalidate smaller specialized places.
```

## 21. Balance Does Not Mean Symmetry

```text
A balanced region does not need one town, one castle, one port, one monastery, and one market of equal strength.
Balance means that critical functions, burdens, access, and recovery options fit the region's actual geography, population, institutions, politics, and strategic role.
An imbalanced region may still be intentionally powerful, but the risks and neglected functions must remain visible.
```

## 31. Resilience Investment Choices

- Protect the existing critical node or create an alternative.
- Deepening one center or distributing capability across several centers.
- Maintain reserve capacity or consume that reserve for current expansion.
- Preserve historical routes and sites for contingency use or accept their decline.
- Invest in warning, records, relief, storage, repair, or transport before visible expansion.
- Accept dependency on an external gateway or deepen local fallback.
- Build recovery capacity in a major center or distribute relief among institutions and local centers.
- Concentrate specialists for quality and coordination or disperse enough capacity to avoid total loss.

## 53. Positive Fixtures

- A region deepens a port gateway while also strengthening inland storage and alternate land access.
- Several medium towns and burhs divide market, administration, warning, and recovery functions instead of duplicating one capital.
- A major castle remains militarily valuable but depends on town, estate, route, and service-settlement support.
- A bypassed market town reorients toward craft, institution, or route service without automatic stage loss.
- An administrative center preserves authorized duplicate records at another institution for continuity.
- A region accepts lower peak efficiency to maintain alternate crossings and relief routes.
- A recovery hub assists damaged settlements while the donor burden is recorded.
- Anglo-Norman stronghold development preserves functioning burh, market, institution, and regional networks.
- Anglo-Saxon continuity develops masonry, ports, specialist workshops, or exceptional centers when evidence and inputs support them.
- A concentrated southern network recognizes government-disruption risk and funds fallback capacity.

## 54. Negative Fixtures

- Every region receives an identical market, castle, port, abbey, and recovery center.
- One regional capital performs every function without dependency or overload.
- Specialization creates specialists, goods, trade, forces, or revenue automatically.
- Redundancy has no construction, staffing, maintenance, or opportunity cost.
- An alternate road or port is assumed equivalent without capacity, rights, security, and distance review.
- Concentration is always superior because the highest tier has the most buildings.
- Distribution is always resilient regardless of coordination and route weakness.
- One disrupted node automatically collapses the entire region.
- Recovery restores the former network exactly and automatically.
- This portfolio assigns exact January 1062 roles, routes, capacities, or Opening State records.
- All twelve specializations are treated as mandatory regional quotas.
- All resilience dimensions are shown as simultaneous bonuses or penalties.
- A specialization, balance, resilience, or fallback label grants an effect without supported operating state.
