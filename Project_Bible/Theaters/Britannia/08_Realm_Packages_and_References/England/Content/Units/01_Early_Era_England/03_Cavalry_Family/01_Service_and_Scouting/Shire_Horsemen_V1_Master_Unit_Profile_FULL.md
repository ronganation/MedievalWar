## 3. Designer One-Screen Summary

- **Class:** Early organized local light cavalry.
- **Source:** Eligible shire, estate, burh, household, road-service, messenger, patrol, and lesser-landholder riders intersected with suitable ordinary riding or service horses, officers, tack, weapons, basic mounted practice, supply, and lawful release.
- **Battle promise:** Economical mounted mobility for screening, escort, flank security, anti-skirmisher pressure, reserve movement, controlled disengagement, and restrained pursuit.
- **Best use:** Open or road-rich terrain, protecting missile troops, forcing exposed skirmishers to move, escorting convoys, covering withdrawal, and exploiting genuine collapse.
- **Worst use:** Braced spears, noble or professional cavalry, prolonged melee, dense missiles, woods, marshes, streets, walls, blocked crossings, and deep unsupported campaigning.
- **Strategic cost:** Removes riders, horses, messengers, patrols, escorts, carts, handlers, tack, fodder, officers, and local mobility from shires, estates, burhs, and households.
- **Not the role:** Mounted Fyrd mob, cheap Mounted Thegns, Early Mounted Sergeants, generic Hobilars, mounted archers, mounted javelin skirmishers, or perfect reconnaissance.

## 5. Player-Facing Description

Shire Horsemen are locally organized English riders drawn from shire service networks, lesser landholders, prosperous households, estates, burhs, road and messenger duties, and other approved mounted relationships. The formation provides England with affordable mounted presence without pretending that local riders possess the training, armor, mounts, or shock discipline of Mounted Thegns.

The unit is strongest before and after decisive contact. Shire Horsemen screen flanks, escort messengers and convoys, protect archers, threaten exposed skirmishers, reinforce a weak sector, cover withdrawal, and pursue enemies already in genuine disorder. The unit may make limited contact against vulnerable targets, but rapidly loses effectiveness in fixed melee.

Every mounted body consumes a real network. Riders, service horses, saddles, tack, handlers, fodder, officers, local knowledge, and route duties cannot be used simultaneously by patrols, messengers, households, transport, Guild cavalry, Mounted Thegns, or later professional units.

## 47. Anti-Skirmisher Function

The unit can deny comfortable firing positions, threaten withdrawal routes, protect friendly missile troops, and punish isolation. Anti-skirmisher is a mission relationship, not a damage multiplier. Stakes, ditches, woods, missiles, spears, escort cavalry, and bait preserve counterplay.

## 48. Flank Security and Mobile Reserve

Flank security provides observation, warning, deterrence, interception, and rapid local reinforcement. Committing the unit removes that protection. Reserve posture preserves horse condition, cohesion, and decision time.

## 49. Infantry and Missile Support

Fyrd and trained spears provide safe rally anchors and mounted deterrence. Ceorl and other sword infantry hold compressed ground. Foot skirmishers contest difficult terrain. Archers and English Crossbow Levy shape targets. Shire Horsemen protect exposed flanks, threaten enemy missiles, and cover withdrawal.

## 50. Mounted Thegn Cooperation

Shire Horsemen may screen Mounted Thegns, remove enemy light troops, secure secondary flanks, protect rally, carry reports, and perform routine pursuit. Mounted Thegns retain noble household identity, stronger shock, command protection, better equipment, and higher political value.

## 51. Later Mounted Sergeant Cooperation

Mounted Sergeants may lead, stabilize, or reinforce Shire Horsemen in later periods, but professional officers, horses, equipment, and pools remain finite. Proximity does not grant professional morale, charge control, or recovery.

## 52. Disengagement and Rally

Break contact requires functioning mounts, spacing, officers, signals, open routes, limited enemy fixation, and supporting formations. Rally requires partner recognition, safe ground, known points, horse control, officer continuity, and time.

## 53. Pursuit and Restraint

Pursuit begins only after genuine disorder. It can yield prisoners, standards, horses, baggage, and information. It also risks ambush, hidden reserves, exhausted mounts, scattered files, local losses, and failure of the original mission.

## 54. Controlled Raiding Boundary

A raid requires authority, military objective, target identification, time and force limits, route and return plan, horse and supply review, prisoner and property policy, and friendly or neutral property boundaries. The unit card grants no free devastation or automatic loot.

## 55. Dismount Boundary

Prebattle or assignment-based dismount may support camp defense, blocked routes, horse preservation, or emergency action. Dismounted riders do not create a free infantry card. Live dismount requires holders, custody, equipment, AI, pathfinding, casualty routing, persistence, and remount support.

## 56. Morale, Loyalty, Discipline, and Reputation

Morale is confidence in officers, mounts, support, mission, and escape. Loyalty concerns owners and obligations. Discipline governs alignment, property, withdrawal, pursuit, prisoners, and return. Reputation records useful service, courage, waste, theft, horse loss, and treatment by authorities.

## 57. Rider and Horse Fatigue

Horse and rider fatigue progress separately. Distance, pace, acceleration, terrain, heat, cold, rain, water, fodder, load, fear, wounds, saddle fit, contact, and pursuit affect both. A fresh mount cannot restore an exhausted rider.

## 65. Weather Effects
