## 3. Designer One-Screen Summary

**Source:** selected local, civic, ward, county, regional, or lord-directed service personnel equipped and drilled with military bills under bounded obligations.  
**Role:** local frontage support, anti-raid response, gate and broad-street support, guarded counterattack, and imperfect bill capability before or below professional Billmen maturity.  
**Strengths:** locally available under qualifying owners, cheaper institutional threshold than professional Billmen, useful multi-action bills, familiar-site defense, and adaptation value.  
**Weaknesses:** uneven bills and armor, shallow officers, slower assembly, restricted campaigning, rapid cohesion loss away from familiar support, missiles, flanks, compression, and inside-reach danger.  
**Strategic cost:** watch, labor, household duty, spear militia, civic defense, workshops, and local political cooperation.  
**Progression:** Bill Militia may coexist with Billmen, feed qualified volunteers and experience into Billmen, or remain a localized defensive institution.

## 4. Positive Player Identity

Bill Militia are locally raised infantry trained to use bills in organized but uneven formations. Bill Militia can protect a gate, reinforce a civic or regional frontage, counterattack raiders or committed enemies, and provide a community with bill capability before a professional corps is available. Bill Militia are not cheap Billmen. Bill Militia depend on local authority, familiar officers, mixed equipment, short obligations, and nearby support.

## 5. Historical and Design Boundary

The candidate draws on the broader historical plausibility of urban and local military service and the later English bill tradition. The exact Bill Militia name, chronology, geographic extent, owner mix, equipment, and formal organization require focused research. This profile is a gameplay composite designed to test whether the local militia layer creates a distinct campaign and battlefield decision. Historical uncertainty blocks final promotion but does not justify an underdeveloped candidate.

## 6. Opening and Era Firewall

Bill Militia are unavailable in 1062. Existing local and civic service systems may later supply people, assembly habits, and officers, but do not create military bills or bill drill. The first possible formations require an appropriate later chronology, weapon access, local need, owner authority, and Experimental or Localized Adoption. No settlement tier, culture value, date, or building creates automatic access.

## 8. Relationship to City Guard

The current City Guard profile identifies a professional urban defensive institution. Bill Militia are not routine professional guards. Bill Militia assemble from bounded local service, use mixed or locally maintained bills, and may return to labor and household obligations after service. City Guard remain better for standing security, patrol, command continuity, and immediate response. Bill Militia provide massed local bill frontage when a qualifying threat and owner justify mobilization.

## 9. Relationship to Spear Militia and Regional Infantry

Spear militia remain easier to equip, more broadly available, and often more compatible with shields. Bill Militia require distinct weapons and training. Local spear experience may support selection but does not grant bill proficiency. Regional trained infantry may offer stronger field endurance but are not automatically civic defenders. Every transfer removes people and equipment from the prior source before Bill Militia readiness increases.

## 10. Relationship to Heavy Bill Militia Candidate

Heavy Bill Militia remain deferred. Bill Militia V1 establishes the parent militia service cycle, equipment variability, local-security cost, and limited field role needed for the future collision test. A Heavy Bill Militia card can survive only if heavier equipment does not erase the militia identity or duplicate Heavy Billmen and City Guard. V1 does not authorize that successor.

## 13. Personnel Roles

A functioning body may require local captains, file leaders, markers or messengers, bill-armed files, reserve personnel, weapon inspectors, shaft and fitting workers, armor and sidearm maintainers, medical support, storekeepers, and assembly administrators. Smaller local bodies may combine roles, which creates vulnerability if one specialist is lost. Rendered formation headcount does not create free officers or support personnel.

## 14. Social and Political Composition

Service may arise through civic obligation, ward duty, regional expectation, local lordship, negotiated privilege, pay, emergency defense, or mixed arrangements. Different arrangements alter willingness, duration, discipline, equipment, and the political cost of extension. The profile does not treat “militia” as one social class or one national pool. Refusal, bargaining, exemption, substitution, and local factional disputes belong to campaign owners.

## 16. Armor Shields and Sidearms

Protection may include ordinary clothing, padding, helmets, selected mail, and owner-specific equipment. No shield is assumed during two-handed bill use, although transport or alternate-duty shields require separate evidence. Sidearms are important in compressed fighting but must be physically issued. Bill Militia should normally show more variation and poorer fit than Billmen. An unusually well-armored body does not automatically become Heavy Bill Militia.

## 17. Workshop Store and Training Contract

Bill Militia require bill heads, suitable shafts, fittings, inspection, repair tools, secure storage, issue records, assembly ground, and space to drill working range and file movement. Local workshops may support repeated formations without reaching professional Billmen quality. Damp storage, mixed lengths, missing fittings, weak shafts, unavailable trainers, or occupied assembly grounds lower readiness. A named building is a support representation, not a unit factory.

## 18. Finite Pools and No Double Counting

One person cannot simultaneously work, serve in the watch, staff a gate, join spear militia, City Guard, Bill Militia, or Billmen. One bill, helmet, mail set, sidearm, officer, trainer, workshop slot, store, or route cannot support multiple active formations. Mobilization must subtract people and assets from local assignments, and demobilization must return surviving people and equipment to their actual owners.

## 19. Recruitment Contract

Recruitment requires a lawful local owner, an eligible personnel list, released people, military bills, officers, trainers, workshop and storage support, armor and sidearms where available, assembly space, service terms, food, water, medicine, records, and a named local or field assignment. A qualifying settlement may still fail because people are needed for harvest, watch, fire response, transport, household survival, or another military formation.

## 20. Recruitment Funnel

Recognize a local threat or adaptation opportunity, identify the lawful owner, protect essential security and labor, select personnel, negotiate or enforce service, release people from assignments, inspect bodies and experience, allocate officers, sort bills by type and length, issue armor and sidearms, inspect workshops and stores, drill files, test guard and counterattack movements, define duration and geographic limits, verify supply and return routes, then authorize readiness. Failure produces delay, smaller files, local-only service, degraded equipment, or a block.

## 23. Adaptation and Emergence

Bill Militia are a natural expression of Experimental and Localized Adoption. A locality may observe professional Billmen, hire instructors, copy or commission bills, return veterans to local service, or respond to raids and protected enemies. Knowledge alone does not create a formation. Localized Adoption occurs when one owner can repeatedly assemble people, weapons, officers, and support. Realm Maturity belongs primarily to Billmen, not to automatic universal Bill Militia access.

## 26. Service Limits

Local service is the default. Field service requires a second gate covering authority, consent or obligation, duration, geography, pay, replacement watch, transport, quarters, and return. Extensions raise desertion, refusal, political conflict, household hardship, and cohesion risk. Familiar-site confidence should not become a universal home-territory bonus. Service limits must be visible and enforceable.

## 27. Local Security and Labor Cost

Mobilization may reduce watch patrols, gate staffing, market order, fire response, workshops, transport, household production, harvest activity, and ordinary spear or sword militia. These costs vary by season and owner. A locality may field Bill Militia yet become more vulnerable to sabotage, unrest, fire, crime, or a second attack. Demobilization restores survivors gradually rather than instantly repairing local disruption.

## 28. Service and Recovery Cycle

List personnel, call service, assemble, issue equipment, drill, deploy locally or obtain field permission, rotate where obligations require, record absence and casualties, recover weapons and people, demobilize, return survivors to prior duties, repair bills and armor, settle pay or privilege, restore watch and labor, integrate replacements, and rebuild cohesion. Personnel, wounds, officers, equipment, local trust, and economic recovery use separate clocks.

## 31. Local Frontage Doctrine

Bill Militia are strongest when defending a prepared local frontage with familiar exits, officers, stores, and supporting troops. Suitable missions include a broad gate, yard, straight street, bridge exit, manor approach, temporary barrier, or flank of ordinary civic infantry. The formation should not control an entire settlement or battle line. Local knowledge improves preparation and command but does not override weapon-space, missile, or flank threats.

## 32. Guarded Counterattack Doctrine

Bill Militia may counterattack raiders, attackers committed at a gate, or enemies fixed by spear, shield, or guard formations. The counterattack should be short, officer-led, and tied to a clear rally point. The unit should avoid extended pursuit. Success depends on timing, target disorder, weapon order, local confidence, and support. An unsupported charge into intact heavy infantry or cavalry is poor doctrine.

## 33. Thrust Cut Hook and Leverage

Militia bills may support thrusting, cutting, hooking, and leverage, but mixed weapons and uneven training make action reliability variable. Thrusting contests entry lanes. Cutting requires space. Hooking may disturb shields, weapons, straps, limbs, reins, or exposed gear. Leverage may assist nearby infantry. Failed actions can entangle weapons, pull personnel out of line, or expose gaps. No action guarantees penetration, dismount, or line break.

## 34. Anti-Cavalry Boundary

Bill Militia can threaten cavalry that is stalled, confined, tired, disordered, or already engaged near a prepared frontage. Bill Militia lack the depth and reach of pikes and the consistency of Billmen. Fresh cavalry with a clean lane, missiles, flank access, or repeated charges remains dangerous. Horses may refuse or deviate from credible points, but no hidden militia bonus stops charges.

## 35. Anti-Protection Boundary

Bills can pressure protected opponents through points, edges, hooks, mass, and leverage. Militia effectiveness varies with weapon quality, distance, target motion, armor and shield geometry, hit location, training, fatigue, and support. Poor fittings and mixed reach reduce coordinated pressure. The unit receives no universal armor-piercing rule.

## 36. Inside Reach and Sidearms

When enemies cross the bill heads, personnel may shorten grip where plausible, draw issued sidearms, fall behind shield-bearing support, open local passages, or disengage. Shallow training and mixed equipment make transitions less reliable than Billmen. A blocked gate, crowd, cart, wall, or rear file can trap the formation. No sidearm appears without a recorded equipment source.

## 37. Battlefield Rhythm

Assemble behind local support, move to a bounded frontage, establish working distance, provide short controlled thrust and pressure, reinforce rather than replace the holding line, counterattack only when the enemy commits, avoid pursuit, rotate tired files if officers can manage it, and disengage before compression or flanking causes fragmentation. Distant campaigning and unfamiliar formations should make this rhythm harder to sustain.

## 38. Combined Arms

Spear and shield militia provide broad holding power. City Guard supply standing command and security. Billmen provide mature bill pressure. Missiles disrupt attackers. Cavalry or mobile infantry punish bypass. Engineers and labor may prepare barriers under separate authority. Bill Militia add localized bill support and a short counterattack layer. Bill Militia should fail if used as a self-sufficient professional army.

## 40. Counterplay

Opponents can use missiles, cavalry flanks, longer weapons, shielded pressure, feints, bypass, fire, smoke, officer targeting, night attack, compression, and terrain. Strategic attacks can disrupt workshops, stores, local leaders, harvest, watch, transport, and political cooperation. Forcing Bill Militia into distant or prolonged service can be as effective as defeating the formation directly.

## 41. Morale Cohesion and Fatigue

Confidence comes from familiar files, local purpose, trusted captains, reliable bills, prepared ground, visible family and property stakes, supporting guards, and a clear return path. Confidence falls through unpaid or extended service, officer loss, missile casualties, weapon failure, flank penetration, fire, household crisis, and abandonment by professional troops. Repeated two-handed actions, waiting under threat, armor, mud, and local labor demands create real fatigue.

## 43. Layered Recovery

Recovery separately restores wounded people, officers, bills, shafts, fittings, armor, sidearms, file familiarity, workshops, stores, watch coverage, household labor, owner trust, pay, and service legitimacy. Battlefield control affects equipment recovery. Captured or lost bills cannot be replaced by headcount. Replacements may know local service but lack bill drill. Professional migration into Billmen permanently reduces some militia depth unless new people are trained.

## 44. Supply and Logistics

Bill Militia require food, water, service pay or obligation, bills, spare heads and shafts, fittings, tools, armor, sidearms, medicine, storage, assembly points, records, and local routes. Local deployment may use nearby stores but still consumes community resources. Field service demands transport, quarters, replacement supplies, and stronger administration. “Militia” does not mean free sustainment.

## 47. Campaign AI Recruitment

AI recruits Bill Militia when a qualifying owner faces a local threat, sufficient people and bills exist, professional Billmen are unavailable or unnecessary, local-security losses are acceptable, and suitable defensive geometry exists. AI refuses when City Guard or spear militia solve the task, mobilization would collapse labor or watch coverage, field service exceeds obligations, equipment is too mixed, or mature Billmen are a better strategic investment.

## 48. Battle AI

AI keeps Bill Militia near assigned local objectives, supports spear or shield infantry, protects flanks, avoids long pursuit, uses short guarded counterattacks, recognizes missile and cavalry danger, and withdraws before compression or officer loss causes fragmentation. AI should show poorer rapid redeployment and sustained pressure than Billmen, but stronger familiar-site assembly and bounded defense.

## 49. Autoresolve

Autoresolve considers owner, service distance, local familiarity, readiness layers, weapon consistency, officers, mission, working range, support, flanks, missiles, cavalry approach, terrain, fatigue, supply, and withdrawal. Militia, bill, local, anti-armor, and anti-cavalry labels cannot become universal multipliers. Local stakes may affect morale only through explicit owner and campaign state.
