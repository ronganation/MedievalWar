## 3. Designer One-Screen Summary

**Source:** selected experienced infantry, paid or household service, regional and institutional support, officers, trainers, military bills, armor, sidearms where issued, workshops, and sustained drill.  
**Role:** flexible bill infantry for line support, controlled counterattack, assault support, pressure against protected infantry, and exploitation of cavalry or enemies already stalled or disordered.  
**Strengths:** multi-function reach; thrust, cut, hook, and leverage opportunities; useful formation pressure; adaptable field and siege support; stronger close pressure than ordinary spear formations.  
**Weaknesses:** no shield by default, exposed to missiles, dependent on working distance and lateral space, vulnerable when flanked or compressed, not a pike wall, and costly to rebuild after trained-personnel or weapon losses.  
**Progression:** Billmen remain the broad mature formation. Heavy Billmen may develop later through a narrower source, heavier equipment, stronger officers, deeper drill, and greater service cost without making Billmen obsolete.

## 4. Player-Facing Identity

Billmen are disciplined English infantry armed with military bills and trained to use reach, cutting edges, thrusting points, hooks, and leverage in organized files. They support the battle line, punish exposed or disordered protected troops, threaten cavalry caught in poor contact, and assist assaults. They are not invulnerable armor killers, stationary anti-cavalry stakes, or elite heavy infantry under a cheaper name.

## 5. Historical and Design Classification

The bill family and its English military association provide the historical foundation. The exact standardized company, uniform weapon form, fixed equipment set, and universal tactical doctrine remain gameplay abstractions. “Billmen” is retained as a clear roster name while terminology, chronology, regional distribution, weapon forms, and institutional organization await focused historical review. The profile avoids claiming one instantaneous national reform or one identical bill across the full campaign.

## 6. Opening and Chronology Firewall

Billmen are unavailable on 1 January 1062. Opening spear traditions may supply later personnel experience, officers, concepts of ordered files, and institutional memory, but do not prove bill equipment or mature bill doctrine. A High Era label creates only a possible historical window. Actual emergence depends on weapon production, personnel, instruction, owners, campaign demand, and formation maturity.

## 7. Family Placement

Billmen occupy the mature flexible center of England's later long-shafted infantry development. Ordinary spear formations retain broader availability, shields, and economical defensive service. Billmen add a more varied weapon-action vocabulary and stronger pressure against selected protected targets. Heavy Billmen later concentrate scarcer people, equipment, armor, and doctrine. Pike and halberd candidates remain parallel or later questions, not automatic replacements.

## 8. Relationship to Spear Formations

Spear units remain better suited to broad line service, simpler replacement, shield-compatible defense, and opening-era continuity. Billmen require new weapons and drill. A spearman may become eligible only after release from the prior assignment, physical re-equipment, training, officer approval, and preparation. Billmen do not inherit every spear formation, morale, shield, or cavalry-defense capability, and spear formations do not disappear when bills mature.

## 9. Relationship to Heavy Billmen

The Billmen to Heavy Billmen progression is intentionally preserved. Billmen are the broader, more accessible, more replaceable mature force. Heavy Billmen must represent a narrower concentration of experienced people, stronger protection, superior weapons and maintenance, deeper officer and formation competence, longer service, and higher recovery costs. Both may coexist. Heavy Billmen cannot exist merely as Billmen with increased attack, armor, morale, and charge values.

## 10. Relationship to Pike and Halberd Candidates

Pike-like formations emphasize extreme reach, depth, and frontage denial but suffer severe turning, flank, and inside-reach constraints. Billmen use shorter and more flexible multi-function weapons and can contribute to pressure and assault support. Halberdiers remain unresolved because terminology, chronology, weapon form, urban ownership, and overlap with bills require evidence. Billmen therefore do not serve as a placeholder for every later polearm.

## 12. Personnel Sources

Potential personnel include experienced line infantry, selected shire or regional service populations, paid professional soldiers, household retainers, contract troops, and qualified civic or institutional personnel where a field-service pathway exists. No one source is universal. Agricultural familiarity with a tool does not create military bill competence. Veterans and officers remain finite and may be required elsewhere.

## 13. Social Composition and Service Consequences

Mobilization may remove experienced infantry, household guards, local officers, workshop labor, trainers, and able service populations from other duties. Different owners may produce formations with different obligations, campaign duration, discipline, equipment consistency, and political meaning. The profile does not collapse shire, civic, professional, household, and contract sources into one national manpower pool.

## 14. Military Bill Equipment Boundary

A military bill requires an appropriate head, reliable shaft, secure fittings, balance, reach, repair, and training. Civilian tools, improvised heads, poorly seasoned shafts, mismatched lengths, and weak fittings may support emergency files but reduce formation reliability. The profile does not assume one exact bill form at all dates. Weapon profiles must remain chronology-, workshop-, owner-, and role-dependent.

## 16. Armor, Clothing, and Sidearms

Protection may include padded garments, helmets, mail, and later reinforcement where chronology and owners support it. Mail is preserved from the legacy card as evidence, not a universal first-emergence requirement. No shield is assumed by default because two-handed bill use and formation spacing compete with shield carriage. Sidearms may support compressed or broken-formation fighting but require their own equipment allocation. Heavy Billmen own the stronger later protection question.

## 17. Finite Pool and No Double Counting

One person cannot simultaneously serve in a spear unit, Billmen, Heavy Billmen, garrison, household guard, civic militia, workshop, training cadre, or another active formation. One bill, armor set, sidearm, officer, instructor, workshop slot, contract, drill space, or supply allocation cannot support several active units. Rendered headcount does not generate free campaign people or equipment.

## 29. Thrusting Doctrine

Thrusts contest approach lanes, threaten exposed targets, and help maintain working distance. Effect depends on point form, alignment, reach, footwork, target direction, density, ground, and formation support. Thrusting does not turn Billmen into pikes. Deep rear-rank contribution and cavalry deterrence remain more limited and situational than in a purpose-built very-long-weapon formation.

## 30. Cutting Doctrine

Cutting actions use the bill's edge and leverage against accessible targets, equipment, limbs, shields, and formation edges where weapon form and space permit. Cutting requires recovery room and exposes the wielder during mistimed actions. Dense files, crowds, trees, walls, friendly bodies, and compressed melee can prevent useful arcs. A cut does not automatically defeat armor.

## 31. Hooking Doctrine

Hooking is a conditional attempt to control or disturb shields, weapons, limbs, reins, riders, straps, or exposed equipment. Success depends on hook form, angle, contact, target movement, leverage, formation support, and the ability to recover. Failed hooks may entangle the weapon, pull the billman forward, expose gaps, or surrender working distance. Hooking never guarantees dismount, shield removal, or capture.

## 32. Leverage and Formation Pressure

Bills may apply leverage and repeated local pressure to disrupt guards, manipulate shields, widen gaps, and support nearby infantry. Effects accumulate through coordinated files, timing, officers, fatigue, and target disorder. Heavy or protected enemies remain dangerous. The role is controlled pressure and exploitation, not a universal line-breaking switch.

## 33. Anti-Cavalry Boundary

Billmen threaten cavalry most effectively when aligned, supported, steady, and able to exploit hesitating, stalled, disordered, tired, or confined horses and riders. Bills can thrust, hook, cut, or leverage against accessible targets, but lack the extreme reach and deep denial of a true pike formation. Fresh cavalry with space, missile support, flank access, or a clean charge remains dangerous. Broken Billmen lose much of their deterrent.

## 34. Anti-Protection Boundary

Billmen may pressure protected infantry using points, edges, hooks, mass, and leverage. Resolution depends on bill construction, strike path, working distance, target motion, armor material and geometry, shield position, hit location, fatigue, and support. Legacy armor-piercing identity is preserved as a targeting and role signal, not guaranteed penetration or flat damage.

## 35. Shock and Counterattack Boundary

The legacy shock role becomes a controlled counterattack and assault-support capability. Billmen can advance into disrupted opponents, support a breach, counterattack a committed enemy, or exploit a successful hold. They should not lead reckless unsupported charges into intact spears, heavy cavalry, prepared missiles, or dense elite infantry. Momentum, cohesion, approach, ground, and support govern success.

## 36. Inside-Reach and Sidearm Response

When enemies enter inside the working head, Billmen may shorten grip where plausible, pull rear files back, open passages, receive support, attempt disengagement, or transition to issued sidearms. These actions consume time and cohesion. No sidearm appears without campaign equipment. Once the formation loses spacing, officers, flank security, and support, dangerous individuals may remain but the unit has stopped functioning as organized Billmen.

## 37. Battlefield Rhythm

Advance behind or beside supporting troops, maintain working space, threaten approach lanes, apply controlled thrusts and pressure, exploit shields or exposed targets, refuse premature pursuit, counterattack where an enemy is fixed, and disengage before compression or flanking destroys the formation. Billmen win through adaptable disciplined pressure, not one automatic charge or passive bonus.

## 39. Counterplay

Opponents can use arrows, crossbows, artillery, skirmishers, cavalry on open flanks, longer weapons, shielded advance, terrain, feigned withdrawal, compression, rear attack, officer targeting, night action, smoke, fatigue, and route disruption. Enemies can force Billmen to turn, lose working distance, break file alignment, or abandon an advantageous frontage. The unit remains strong only while its causal conditions survive.

## 40. Morale, Cohesion, and Command

Morale arises from trained files, trusted officers, reliable bills, protection, successful contact, supporting troops, pay or obligation, and confidence in withdrawal. Failed hooks, broken shafts, cavalry penetration, missile losses, flank collapse, sidearm shortage, officer death, and trapped files can cause rapid degradation. Medium-high legacy morale is evidence, not immunity.

## 41. Fatigue and Labor

Marching with long weapons, maintaining spacing, repeated thrusts, cuts, hooks, recoveries, armor, and close pressure create significant fatigue. Mud, slopes, heat, cold, hunger, thirst, compression, broken equipment, and repeated formation changes increase labor. Training improves pacing and recovery discipline but cannot remove physical limits.

## 43. Recovery and Replacement

Battlefield control, time, routes, transport, owner claims, enemy pressure, and equipment condition determine recovery. Bills may be repaired through new shafts, refitting, head replacement, straightening where appropriate, or full replacement. Captured weapons require inspection and compatible training. Replacement personnel may be physically capable but unfamiliar with the file. Money cannot instantly restore officers, cohesion, experience, or political cooperation.

## 44. Supply and Logistics

Billmen require food, water, pay or obligation, bills, spare heads and shafts, fittings, tools, maintenance, armor, sidearms, quarters, medicine, route access, and records. Long shafts complicate storage, transport, doors, ships, woods, and siege passages. The legacy Medium supply burden remains directionally useful but is not promoted as an exact runtime value.

## 46. Siege and Settlement Use

Strong uses include supporting approaches, guarding siege works, defending broad gates and yards, counterattacking breaches after an enemy is fixed, controlling accessible streets with sufficient space, and protecting engineers or missile troops. Poor uses include ladders, narrow stairs, crowded wall walks, severe rubble, cramped alleys, and unsupported breach entry. Urban or castle familiarity is site knowledge, not an automatic combat multiplier.

## 47. Strategic Assignments

Potential strategic functions include garrison reinforcement, guard of routes and stores, protection of siege equipment, escort of important infantry assets, visible institutional military development, and concentration of regional or professional infantry capability. These assignments consume readiness and cannot coexist everywhere. Local and household security may weaken when personnel are detached.

## 50. AI Recruitment

AI recruits Billmen when a mature source, suitable weapons, officers, workshops, a need for flexible bill infantry, and acceptable personnel competition exist. AI avoids recruitment when ordinary spears solve the mission more economically, Heavy Billmen are required for a scarcer task, missile-heavy enemies dominate, routes and terrain are unsuitable, or weapon and officer pools are depleted.

## 51. AI Battlefield Use

AI protects flanks, avoids wasteful frontal charges, maintains working reach, uses Billmen beside holding infantry, counterattacks committed or disordered enemies, exploits stalled cavalry, and withdraws before compression or encirclement. AI must recognize missiles, pikes, dense terrain, narrow passages, exhausted files, broken shafts, missing sidearms, and loss of officers as reasons to change mission.

## 52. Autoresolve

Autoresolve considers mission, formation state, working range, target protection, cavalry approach, flanks, missile exposure, ground, officers, weapons, armor, fatigue, support, escape, and supply. Bill, anti-cavalry, anti-protection, and shock labels do not create universal multipliers. Encirclement, unsuitable terrain, inside-reach combat, and poor readiness remain dangerous.

## 57. Encyclopedia Contract

## 58. Visual Identity

The silhouette should show unmistakable military bills, practical helmets, mixed padding and mail appropriate to maturity, no universal shield, and visible sidearms only where issued. Variants should reflect owner, region, workshop, equipment condition, and development stage. Avoid identical halberds, fantasy oversized heads, universal partial plate, bright uniformity, and palette-swapped spear infantry.

## 59. Animation Direction

Required families include march with long shafts, dress line, ready point, measured thrust, cut, hook attempt, successful and failed leverage, recovery, weapon entanglement, receive mounted threat, counterattack, compressed grip, sidearm transition, broken head or shaft, flank reaction, disengagement, rally, surrender, casualty, and equipment recovery. Animation communicates attempts and states, not guaranteed outcomes.

## 60. Audio and Accessibility

Audio should communicate shafts, fittings, boots, formation cadence, bill contact with shields and armor, wood failure, officer commands, compression, rally, and withdrawal. Weapon family, readiness, range state, and danger must also be legible through silhouette, posture, spacing, icons, text, animation, and contrast. Color alone is insufficient.
