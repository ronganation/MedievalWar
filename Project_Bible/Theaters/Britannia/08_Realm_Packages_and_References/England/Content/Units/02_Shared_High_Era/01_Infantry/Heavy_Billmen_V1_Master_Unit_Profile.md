## 3. Designer One-Screen Summary

**Source:** selected veteran Billmen and other qualified professional or household infantry, strong officers, superior military bills, reliable armor, sidearms, workshops, pay, and sustained drill.  
**Role:** scarce heavy bill infantry for disciplined reserve, sustained pressure against committed protected infantry, controlled counterattack, defense or recovery of critical ground, and difficult assault support.  
**Strengths:** superior cohesion under pressure, stronger protection, better equipment consistency, deeper recovery discipline, and more reliable coordinated bill work than ordinary Billmen.  
**Weaknesses:** expensive and slow to assemble, no shield by default, vulnerable to concentrated missiles and flanking, heavy fatigue burden, high personnel and armor opportunity cost, and severe recovery consequences.  
**Progression:** a caused concentration beyond Billmen, not a replacement for Billmen and not a free upgrade.

## 4. Player-Facing Identity

Heavy Billmen are selected, heavily equipped English bill infantry organized for harder and longer close-pressure assignments than ordinary Billmen. They hold a disciplined reserve, reinforce a stressed frontage, counterattack committed enemies, and pressure protected troops when working distance and support survive. Their armor and experience improve endurance, not invulnerability. Their rarity, preparation, and recovery costs keep ordinary Billmen central to the roster.

## 5. Historical and Design Boundary

English bill traditions support the broad concept, while one uniform national Heavy Billmen corps is a gameplay synthesis. The exact “Heavy Billmen” label, first repeatable chronology, armor distribution, bill forms, source institutions, and company organization require focused review. The profile retains the readable Medieval II progression while replacing simple numerical tiering with owner, personnel, equipment, doctrine, and opportunity-cost causes.

## 6. Chronology and Opening Firewall

Heavy Billmen are not available in 1062 and do not appear merely because ordinary Billmen exist. They require a later High or Late conditional window, mature bill institutions, veteran availability, stronger armor and workshop depth, officers capable of sustained formation control, and a campaign need worth the concentration. Partial plate wording is retained only as later chronology evidence, never as first-emergence default equipment.

## 7. Progression from Billmen

Billmen are the principal developmental foundation. Selected Billmen may transfer experience, officers, and institutional knowledge, but every transfer consumes the Billmen pool and requires authorization, re-equipment, retraining, and preparation. Heavy Billmen do not convert all mature Billmen, and the campaign should often prefer several ordinary formations over one heavy concentration. Coexistence is mandatory to preserve the progression rather than turn it into replacement.

## 9. Non-Duplication with Men-at-Arms

Men-at-Arms remain broader armored professional melee infantry with their own weapon, protection, service, and dismount questions. Heavy Billmen are distinguished by bill-centered working-distance doctrine, hook and leverage opportunities, formation-pressure behavior, and specific weapon-space liabilities. Armor quality alone cannot transfer a person into Heavy Billmen. The same veteran, mail, sidearm, officer, or contract cannot support both assignments simultaneously.

## 10. Non-Duplication with English Foot Knights

English Foot Knights draw from knightly households and status-bearing service. Heavy Billmen do not inherit knightly social standing, heraldic cohesion, ransom identity, or noble command merely because they wear strong armor. Individual knights may serve only through explicit owner-authorized reassignment where historically and mechanically justified. The formation's core source remains professional or household infantry rather than a disguised foot-knight card.

## 11. Non-Duplication with Dismounted King’s Men and Royal Billmen

Dismounted King’s Men are a royal owner-specific elite with poleaxe and knightly-household questions. Heavy Billmen are broader than a royal body and cannot claim Crown exclusivity, royal prestige, or an automatic poleaxe identity. “Royal Billmen” is preserved as a legacy later-development idea only. A future royal profile must pass a separate owner, personnel, political, and battlefield test rather than becoming an automatic final tier.

## 13. Personnel Source Architecture

Primary candidates are proven Billmen, selected professional infantry, experienced household retainers, and contract troops with appropriate service history. Conditional sources may include magnate or royal establishments without redefining the whole unit as royal. Status alone is insufficient. Physical fitness, cohesion, discipline, bill experience, armor familiarity, willingness, owner release, and campaign availability must all be verified.

## 14. Veteran Selection and Cohesion

Veterancy improves judgment, spacing, recovery, target choice, and confidence but does not automatically create a coherent Heavy Billmen company. Selected personnel may come from different owners and habits. Officers, repeated drill, equipment consistency, and preparation are required to integrate them. Stripping too many veterans from ordinary Billmen may weaken the wider army more than the new heavy formation adds.

## 15. Heavy Bill Equipment Architecture

“Heavy bill” denotes a higher-grade and deliberately supported weapon set, not one universal oversized head. Profiles may vary in point, cutting edge, hook, shaft length, balance, reinforcement, and owner style. Greater mass or reinforcement can improve selected actions but may increase fatigue and recovery time. Workshop quality, fit, shaft integrity, and user familiarity matter more than visual bulk.

## 17. Armor and Protection Chronology

Introduction may combine good padding, helmets, selected mail, and uneven reinforcement. Mature Heavy Billmen require more consistent protection than Billmen, but not universal plate. Later development may introduce transitional reinforcement or plate elements where chronology, owner, fit, and workshop sources support them. Horse armor and mounted equipment are outside scope. Stronger armor raises fatigue, heat, cost, repair demand, and competition with men-at-arms and knightly infantry.

## 18. Sidearm and Close-Range Contract

A sword, falchion, dagger, axe, or other sidearm requires evidence and physical issue. Sidearms are critical when contact compresses inside the bill head, shafts break, or formation order fails. Heavy Billmen should have more reliable sidearm access than ordinary Billmen only where the equipment source supports it. Sidearms do not convert Heavy Billmen into better Men-at-Arms or Foot Knights.

## 19. Finite Pool and No Double Counting

One person cannot simultaneously serve as Billman, Heavy Billman, Man-at-Arms, Foot Knight, Dismounted King’s Man, garrison elite, household guard, trainer, or workshop labor. One bill, mail set, plate element, helmet, sidearm, officer, contract, workshop slot, drill space, or recovery allocation cannot support several active assignments. Conversion must subtract from the predecessor source before the heavy formation becomes available.

## 20. Recruitment Contract

Recruitment requires a lawful high-capability owner, eligible veterans, experienced officers, heavy military bills, reliable protection, sidearms where required, priority workshops, repair capacity, trainers, drill space, pay or obligation, quarters, routes, food, water, medicine, records, and a high-value assignment. Castle-plus, Elite Polearm Yard, Feudal Barracks, date, culture, or wealth are support indicators only.

## 21. Recruitment Funnel

Establish a justified heavy-bill need, identify an owner, audit competition with Billmen and other heavy infantry, select veterans, obtain release, inspect wounds and fitness, allocate officers, fit bills and armor, issue sidearms, prioritize workshop support, establish pay and service terms, drill sustained pressure and reserve commitment, verify combined arms and withdrawal, then authorize readiness. Failure produces delay, reduced contingent, mixed equipment, owner-limited service, or a visible block.

## 26. Service Cycle

Authorize the concentration, release veterans, issue and fit high-grade equipment, integrate files, mobilize, reserve or commit the formation, rotate when service and fatigue require, record casualties and equipment loss, recover bodies and assets, demobilize, return surviving people, restore Billmen and household capacity where possible, repair armor and bills, replace officers, and retrain. The heavy formation should remain unavailable while critical layers recover.

## 27. Opportunity Cost

Heavy Billmen consume the exact resources that other valuable formations need: veteran Billmen, professional infantry, household retainers, officers, mail, later reinforcement, sidearms, workshop priority, pay, quarters, and political attention. Concentration can reduce the number and quality of ordinary Billmen, Men-at-Arms, Foot Knights, garrisons, and local guards. Losses therefore affect army depth and owner confidence, not only treasury value.

## 30. Disciplined Reserve Doctrine

Heavy Billmen should normally remain behind or beside a reliable line until a high-value commitment appears. Reserve use preserves cohesion, limits missile exposure, and lets officers choose favorable working distance. Suitable commitments include reinforcing a failing frontage, counterattacking a committed enemy, sealing a breakthrough, protecting an important commander or route, and exploiting a disrupted protected formation. Prestige does not justify premature deployment.

## 31. Sustained Pressure Doctrine

The formation's defining battlefield improvement is the ability to maintain organized pressure longer than Billmen through selected personnel, stronger protection, equipment consistency, officers, and file rotation. Pressure may widen gaps, exhaust defenders, contest shields, and create opportunities for supporting infantry. It does not guarantee line failure. Fatigue, terrain, compression, missiles, officers, and rear threats still determine endurance.

## 32. Thrust, Cut, Hook, and Leverage

Heavy Billmen use the same causal action families as Billmen but with improved consistency, recovery discipline, and equipment support. Thrusting contests lanes and gaps. Cutting pressures exposed targets where space exists. Hooking conditionally disturbs shields, weapons, limbs, reins, riders, or equipment. Leverage aids local manipulation and formation pressure. Armor and veterancy cannot guarantee a hit, hook, penetration, or dismount.

## 33. Anti-Cavalry Boundary

Heavy Billmen are more reliable than Billmen against cavalry caught in stalled, confined, tired, disordered, or supported contact. Stronger armor and cohesion may help survive imperfect impact. They are still not a purpose-built pike wall. Fresh heavy cavalry with a clean lane, missile preparation, flank access, superior mass, or repeated charges can break them. Horse and rider outcomes remain shared-system results.

## 34. Anti-Protection Boundary

Selected weapons, stronger users, better recovery, and sustained file pressure make Heavy Billmen a credible threat to protected infantry. Effect depends on weapon geometry, alignment, reach, target motion, armor and shield geometry, hit location, fatigue, officers, and support. The legacy armor-piercing label remains evidence and target-selection guidance. No attack ignores armor by declaration.

## 35. Counterattack and Assault Support

Heavy Billmen can deliver a controlled counterattack after an enemy becomes committed or disordered, reinforce an assault where the approach and flanks are secure, and contest protected defenders in a critical passage. Unsupported frontal shock remains poor doctrine. The formation should not charge prepared pikes, fresh heavy cavalry, massed missiles, or intact elite infantry merely because the legacy card called it shock infantry.

## 36. Inside-Reach Response

When pressure enters inside useful bill distance, Heavy Billmen rely on armor, officers, trained grip changes where plausible, sidearms, relief by supporting troops, local withdrawals, and file rotation. Heavier equipment may buy time but also worsens congestion and fatigue. If sidearms, exits, officers, or support fail, the formation can become trapped and lose expensive people and equipment quickly.

## 37. Battlefield Rhythm

Remain protected as a reserve, identify a decisive but achievable commitment, advance with flank and missile support, establish working distance, apply organized bill pressure, rotate or recover files, refuse uncontrolled pursuit, and disengage before encirclement or exhaustion. Commit again only after cohesion and weapon order return. The formation's value lies in selective endurance, not constant frontline exposure.

## 39. Counterplay

Opponents can draw Heavy Billmen into missile exposure, bait premature commitment, attack flanks, use longer weapons, compress the formation, deny withdrawal, exhaust the armored body, target officers, force repeated turns, exploit terrain, sever workshop and armor replacement, and threaten the owner politically. Defeating the source and recovery network can matter as much as defeating one unit in battle.

## 40. Morale, Cohesion, and Command

High confidence derives from veteran files, trusted officers, strong protection, weapon reliability, owner support, reputation, and a clear reserve mission. It is not immunity. Officer loss, armor and weapon failure, failed counterattack, cavalry penetration, missile attrition, trapped withdrawal, sidearm shortage, and collapse of supporting troops can break the body. Surrender or organized withdrawal may be rational.

## 41. Fatigue and Armor Burden

Stronger armor, heavier or reinforced bills, sustained pressure, repeated recovery, heat, mud, hills, confined fighting, and long reserve marches increase fatigue. Veterans pace effort more effectively but remain physical people. Exhausted Heavy Billmen may retain protection while losing working distance, file timing, hook control, and withdrawal speed. Fatigue can turn a premium formation into an expensive trap.

## 43. Layered Recovery

Recovery separately restores wounds, veteran depth, officers, bill equipment, armor fit, sidearms, cohesion, workshop backlog, owner confidence, pay, replacement integration, and the ordinary Billmen formations depleted to create the heavy body. Battlefield control affects recovery of expensive armor and weapons. Captured personnel or equipment may produce ransom, service, intelligence, political, or reuse questions through shared owners.

## 44. Supply and Logistics

Heavy Billmen require medium-high logistical support in the legacy direction: high-quality food, water, pay, quarters, heavy bills, spare heads and shafts, fittings, armor repair, sidearms, tools, medicine, transport, secure routes, records, and workshop priority. Out-of-supply conditions should degrade cohesion, equipment maintenance, fatigue, owner cooperation, and replacement, not merely apply attack and morale penalties.

## 46. Siege and Settlement Use

Strong uses include reserve behind gates, counterattack in broad yards or breaches, defense of siege works, reinforcement of a secure approach, protection of critical engineers or artillery, and assault support after missiles or other infantry fix the enemy. Poor uses include ladders, narrow stairs, crowded wall walks, unstable rubble, cramped alleys, and unsupported breach entry. Urban and castle labels do not override weapon-space requirements.

## 47. Strategic and Political Functions

Heavy Billmen can demonstrate owner capacity, reinforce high-value garrisons, support royal or magnate campaigns without becoming royal troops, protect critical siege assets, and serve as a deterrent reserve. Their concentration may alarm rivals, empower an owner, weaken ordinary local forces, or create disputes over pay, armor, command, and service duration. These consequences belong to campaign owners and events, not hidden unit modifiers.

## 50. AI Recruitment

AI recruits Heavy Billmen only when mature Billmen sources, veteran depth, strong officers, armor, workshops, high-value missions, and acceptable opportunity costs exist. AI avoids recruitment when ordinary Billmen solve the task, the heavy body would hollow out the army, missiles dominate expected battles, routes are unsuitable, equipment recovery is impossible, or another heavy infantry formation better fits the mission.

## 51. AI Battlefield Use

AI holds Heavy Billmen in disciplined reserve, protects them from missile attrition, commits against an achievable decisive task, maintains working reach, rotates or withdraws under fatigue, refuses uncontrolled pursuit, and preserves escape routes. AI must understand when Men-at-Arms, Foot Knights, pikes, or ordinary Billmen are better choices. It should not spend the unit on low-value frontal attrition.

## 52. Autoresolve

Autoresolve considers reserve state, mission, working range, target commitment, protection, weapon condition, officers, veteran depth, fatigue, flanks, missiles, cavalry approach, ground, supply, owner cost, escape, and recovery. Heavy, elite, anti-armor, anti-cavalry, and shock labels cannot become universal multipliers. Scarcity increases consequences, not guaranteed victory.

## 53. Campaign UI Contract

Show owner, source formations, veterans, officers, heavy bills, armor, sidearms, workshops, training, adaptation and maturity state, preparation time, service terms, recovery, competition with Billmen and other heavy infantry, political cost, and strongest blocker. The player must see which ordinary formations and equipment pools are weakened by concentration.

## 54. Battle UI Contract
