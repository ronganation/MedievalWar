Ordinary Seax Warriors remain broader, cheaper, faster to recover, easier to equip, and better suited to routine regional defense, sparse woodland, villages, escorts, and secondary frontage. ## 23. Production and Repair Economy

Mail requires iron, rings, riveting or joining practice, skilled labor, tools, time, fuel, transport, inspection, and repeated repair. Shields require boards, bosses, grips, coverings, rims, paint, and drying. Swords require blades, hilts, scabbards, sharpening, and fitting. Wealth can purchase inputs but cannot guarantee production, delivery, allocation, or expertise.


## 24. Buildings and Sites

Burhs, castles, halls, estates, armories, workshops, markets, roads, bridges, depots, and training grounds can support the formation. Each site contributes specific functions such as records, repair, storage, assembly, quarters, or supply. No building is a self-sufficient unit generator, and site damage can selectively impair readiness layers.

## 32. Training Program

Training includes sword-and-shield contact, movement and breathing under mail, relief of spear frontage, reserve entry, shielded approach, restrained counterattack, urban navigation, controlled withdrawal, equipment care, rally points, casualty handling, and mixed-unit coordination. Training quality depends on surviving cadres and time, not a universal building bonus.

## 34. Officer and Veteran Pipeline

New replacements contribute headcount; veteran file members preserve local cohesion; file-leader candidates require experience, recognition, communication, and discipline; company officers require authority, logistics, tactical judgment, and regional acceptance. Promotion restores command depth gradually, and leaders assigned to training are unavailable to full field command.


## 35. Command and Information Model

Orders pass through captains, standards, messengers, horns or calls, file leaders, and known rally points. Smoke, darkness, noise, terrain, mixed groups, casualties, disputed authority, and broken routes create delay or partial compliance. The model should allow local action without perfect real-time control.


## 36. Leadership Loss and Succession

Officer loss first damages local facing, relief timing, pursuit control, rally, and withdrawal. Recognized subordinates may preserve partial command. Severe cumulative losses can fragment the formation. A single casualty does not trigger deterministic universal rout, but leadership depth remains strategically valuable.


## 37. Morale

Morale draws from regional purpose, trusted officers, equipment confidence, successful relief, familiar comrades, lawful treatment, visible support, and safe return routes. It declines through encirclement, failed relief, abandoned flanks, sustained missiles, unpaid extended service, officer deaths, home-region loss, exhaustion, and repeated waste.


## 38. Cohesion

Cohesion depends on stable files, shield intervals, local leadership, shared practice, equipment compatibility, and clear purpose. Obstacles, crowding, armor exhaustion, smoke, mixed replacements, flank pressure, pursuit, and repeated transitions degrade it. Protected time, officers, water, space, and nearby friendly stability support reform.


## 39. Fatigue and Armor Burden

Mail increases survival but also heat, water demand, movement cost, wet-weather burden, recovery time, and casualty evacuation difficulty. Shields and repeated contact compound fatigue. Training improves pacing rather than erasing physical limits. A mailed unit may be less effective than ordinary Seax Warriors in pursuit, extended skirmishing, or severe terrain.


## 40. Morale Memory and Reputation

The formation can remember successful defense, line relief, abandonment, failed assault, unpaid service, protected standards, recovered mail, heavy officer losses, and safe return. Reputation influences recruitment confidence and owner trust through explicit records. It is not a magical aura and can diverge between companies.

## 43. Equipment Degradation

Mail can lose rings, tear, corrode, bind, fit poorly, or become burdensome when wet. Shields split, warp, lose grips, and shed coverings. Swords dull, damage edges, break, or disappear. Helmets, straps, clothing, and footwear fail independently. Protection consistency can decline before headcount.


## 44. Equipment Allocation Decisions

The owner may concentrate best mail among veteran front files, distribute protection more evenly, retain equipment for garrisons, or issue captured gear rapidly. These choices alter survival, cohesion, fatigue, visual consistency, and politics. The profile defines consequences but does not prescribe one optimal configuration.

## 46. Formation Geometry

The unit needs enough frontage for relief but less depth than spear blocks. Close order improves mutual protection and local mass while increasing missile density, congestion, turning cost, and fatigue. Open order improves broken-ground passage but reduces shield overlap. Depth helps replacement but can trap rear files in streets or crossings.


## 47. Shieldwall Execution

Shieldwall entry requires time, compatible shields, alignment, officers, and workable ground. Maintenance reduces mobility and raises fatigue. Turning is slow. Missile impact, uneven shields, casualties, and obstacles create gaps. Mailed Seax Warriors should enter and recover better than ordinary Seax Warriors while remaining below selected household guards in severe crisis.


## 48. Relief Under Pressure Doctrine

Relief is the unit’s signature proposition. A successful action needs a viable lane, a parent line still able to disengage, compatible frontage, surviving officers, reserve space, and sufficient fatigue margin. Mail helps incoming files survive the dangerous handover. It does not eliminate disruption.


## 49. Relief Outcomes

```yaml
CleanRelief: tired troops disengage and mailed files stabilize frontage
PartialRelief: mixed files hold but suffer command and cohesion loss
FailedRelief: collision gaps panic transfer and breakthrough risk
EmergencyInterposition: mailed files enter immediately with heavy local cost
CoveredWithdrawal: mailed files hold while another body retreats and reforms
```
Different outcomes must route casualties, fatigue, morale memory, and officer loss accurately.


## 50. Defensive Counterpressure

After stabilizing a front, Mailed Seax Warriors can push against tired or disordered enemies in controlled local action. Counterpressure should be shorter and more restrained than elite shock assault. Success relies on support and stopping discipline. Overextension exposes the unit to spears, cavalry, and flank attack.


## 51. Sword-and-Shield Resolution

Swords provide compact versatility but limited reach and armor pressure. Shields protect by angle, condition, spacing, and active use. Outcomes depend on footing, fatigue, opponent weapon, armor fit, local numbers, officers, target disorder, and supporting formations. No weapon tag automatically determines victory.


## 52. Contact, Relief, and Reform Cycle

```text
Approach under supporting spears or missiles
-> establish protected reserve position
-> identify failing or compressed frontage
-> enter through a controlled relief lane
-> absorb dangerous contact under mail and shields
-> stabilize and apply defensive counterpressure
-> halt before overextension
-> cover withdrawal or rotate damaged files
-> reform around officers and standards
```


## 53. Formation Failure States

Late deployment, blocked relief lane, mixed shield spacing, missing officers, exhausted mailed files, armor heat stress, pursuit fragmentation, broken standard contact, lost withdrawal route, and simultaneous flank pressure can leave a numerically strong formation tactically unusable until reform.

## 55. Counterplay

Opponents can preserve reach, refuse contact, shoot the approach, threaten with cavalry, widen the battle, attack multiple faces, block relief lanes, exhaust the mailed body, target officers, disrupt supply, or force prolonged pursuit. Armor raises the cost of defeating the formation but does not remove counters.


## 56. Combined-Arms Relationships

Trained or Mailed Shire Spearmen receive mounted pressure and fix enemies. Archers, javelins, and crossbows disrupt targets. Mailed Seax Warriors relieve and stabilize close combat. Saxon Axemen add aggressive local pressure. Mounted troops secure flanks and pursue. Guards and Foot Knights answer crises beyond regional capacity.


## 57. Field Battle Use

Best deployed in the second line, behind spear frontage, near missile troops, or on a threatened flank. The player gains value from timing relief, preserving officers, and avoiding unnecessary pursuit. Using the formation as routine frontage wastes mail and cadre advantage.


## 58. Siege Defense

Excellent candidate for gates, wall relief, stairs, halls, yards, inner courts, stores, and breach containment. Strong shields and mail improve survival in compressed fighting, while smoke, fire, overhead missiles, disease, crowding, and blocked exits remain serious threats.


## 59. Siege Offense

Suitable after suppression or access is secured. The formation can protect specialists, widen a foothold, clear interiors, and resist counterattack. It is poor as disposable ladder infantry, unsupported bridge attackers, or repeated breach shock troops. Mail increases climbing and waiting fatigue.

## 62. Terrain Comparison with Parent

Mail improves survival in contact but worsens heat, wet-ground burden, climbing, and extended movement. Ordinary Seax Warriors retain advantages in mobility, cheaper replacement, broad regional use, and some broken-ground duties. Mailed Seax Warriors gain most in settlement defense, relief under pressure, and sustained compact fighting.

```yaml
SnowAndIce: traction shelter clothing and supply suffer
Heat: armor water and fatigue pressure sharply rise
Storm: assembly crossing missile support and routes may fail
```
Weather changes the interaction of equipment and doctrine rather than applying unexplained faction bonuses.


## 64. Ordered Seasonal Cycle

Winter prioritizes stores, shelter, clothing, roads, and return. Spring creates mud, flooding, and route repair. Summer improves movement but increases heat and labor competition. Autumn combines harvest, rain, declining roads, and demobilization pressure. Campaign season advances one ordered Winter, Spring, Summer, Autumn step per strategic turn.


## 65. Strategic Geography

Valid S03 source regions, shire centers, burhs, castles, estates, armories, workshops, markets, roads, bridges, fords, depots, ports where relevant, and secure return routes shape access. Control is necessary but insufficient. Distance and route quality affect assembly, mail movement, replacements, wounded return, and equipment recovery.


## 66. Supply and Campaign Endurance

The formation needs food, water, fuel, shelter, carts or pack support, repair materials, spare shield components, mail maintenance, sword care, medical support, footwear, clothing, pay or maintenance, and secure routes. Endurance exceeds ordinary Seax Warriors only when this larger support burden is met.


## 67. War Weariness and Regional Cost

Repeated concentration of mailed veterans can weaken home-region defense, labor, garrisons, and ordinary pools. Distant service, equipment loss, failed relief, arrears, delayed return, and catastrophe reduce future cooperation. Successful defense, fair reward, recovered equipment, and returning cadres can strengthen confidence.


## 68. Casualty Quality Model

Equal headcount losses produce unequal damage. Captains, file leaders, mail-equipped veterans, instructors, standards, equipment owners, and trusted messengers carry disproportionate institutional weight. A formation may return to nominal size while remaining officer-depleted, mail-depleted, inexperienced, or politically mistrusted.

## 70. Layered Recovery

Recovery separately restores fit headcount, wounds, veteran cadre, officers, mail coverage, swords and shields, cohesion, institution health, regional cooperation, campaign confidence, and routes. Fast replenishment without cadres or equipment produces a visibly hollow formation. Nearby source regions may help only if capacity and cooperation remain.


## 71. Ransom, Capture, and Return

Captured higher-value or well-equipped personnel may draw ransom, exchange, coercion, recruitment pressure, or political bargaining. Missing personnel remain unresolved until information arrives. Returned veterans can restore experience if reintegrated. Professional or mailed status does not guarantee elite ransom treatment.


## 72. Equipment Recovery After Battle

Battlefield control, search time, transport, ownership, damage, enemy removal, and legal distribution determine recovery. Mail stripped from casualties is not instantly serviceable. Recovered gear may return to households, armories, the formation, or claimants according to owner rules.

## 74. Battle AI

AI should hold the formation as a second line or threatened-flank reserve, coordinate with spears and missiles, enter through viable relief lanes, exploit urban cover, halt pursuit, and reform. It should not receive cavalry unsupported, launch repeated bridge attacks, or chase skirmishers. Difficulty improves decisions, not hidden statistics.

Autoresolve should account for formation state, relief role, shields, mail condition, officers, spears nearby, missiles, cavalry, terrain, fatigue, flank pressure, supply, and encirclement. It must not treat “mailed” as a universal quality multiplier or erase the value of cheaper parent units.

## 86. Emergence States

Unavailable means only parent service exists. Experimental means a selected mailed detachment forms. Introduction means a very small uneven formation becomes recruitable. Adoption means recurring sources and officers develop. Maturity means a finite established branch. Strain means casualties or equipment loss reduce quality. Decline or reversion returns survivors to parent expressions where appropriate.


## 87. Chronology and Historical Emergence

The historical window permits the development but never guarantees it. Emergence requires source survival, suitable equipment and repair, officers, authority, regional cooperation, campaign need, and sustainment. Early development must remain historically compatible. Delayed, partial, regional, or failed emergence are valid outcomes.


## 88. Coexistence and Reversion

Ordinary Seax Warriors remain useful throughout the branch. Anglo-Norman Swordsmen may coexist in professional centers. Mailed Seax Warriors may lose equipment coherence and temporarily operate closer to the parent without losing formation history. Reversion requires accountable transfer and cannot refresh casualties or duplicate equipment.


## 89. Regional Variation Without Roster Bloat

Woodland, wealthy, frontier, recently integrated, devastated, and major-route regions may vary in mail coverage, shield style, officer depth, service duration, recovery, and preferred missions. A new card is justified only when source, owner, access, battlefield behavior, or campaign cost changes materially.


## 90. Cross-Cultural Integration

English, Anglo-Danish, Norman-influenced, regional, and contract-experienced personnel may contribute if accepted into the source and practice. Mixed language and custom can increase order delay or integration burden. Bilingual leaders, repeated service, fair treatment, and shared success can reduce those costs.
