## 1. Copy-First and Non-Loss Boundary

This profile is an additive successor design built after full review of the 1,389-line Thegn Spearmen master profile. It preserves the complete parent system inventory: S04 ownership, noble and regional service, loyalty and cultural response, population and law interfaces, shared-source competition, access, equipment, training, command, morale, formations, counterplay, all 25 terrain contexts, weather, supply, siege use, AI, casualties, lineage, presentation, legacy evidence, and validation boundaries. The parent remains active and is not replaced.

## 2. Current Design Verdict

Royal Thegn Spearmen are a conditional Anglo-Saxon Continuity development that concentrates selected S04 spear retainers around the king, royal household, royal officers, and strategically important royal sites. Their identity is royal command anchor, anti-mounted reserve, standard and ruler protection, succession-crisis fidelity, and controlled relief of decisive frontage. They are not Thegn Spearmen with a crown icon or cheaper Housecarls.

## 3. One-Screen Designer Card

Class: rare royal-household spear-and-shield guard. Battle promise: hold the most politically and tactically decisive frontage under severe command pressure, protect the ruler and royal standard, receive mounted threats, reinforce a failing line, and withdraw in order. Best use: royal center, flank anchor, crossing, gate, inner defense, command reserve, succession crisis. Worst use: routine garrison, pursuit, loose screening, attritional assault, unsupported missile exposure, enveloped open ground.

## 4. Historical and Counterfactual Boundary

The historically grounded foundation is noble and household spear service, royal retinues, standards, oaths, estates, equipment, lord protection, fortified-site defense, and the gradual concentration of selected retainers. The standardized later unit is a campaign-development composite. It does not assert an identical permanent regiment, universal royal armor, guaranteed chronology, or automatic loyalty.

## 5. Distinct Unit Survival Test

The card survives only if royal ownership changes recruitment, oath and succession behavior, political consequence, officer density, assignment, access, equipment concentration, command resilience, and battlefield duty. If the result is merely stronger Thegn Spearmen, the concept becomes a royal assignment and equipment profile rather than a separate roster slot.

## 6. Parent Persistence

Ordinary Thegn Spearmen remain broader, cheaper, more regionally distributed, and available to qualifying noble households. They retain routine noble defensive frontage, household protection, crossings, and local strongpoints. Royal concentration consumes selected parent capacity rather than upgrading every S04 formation.

## 7. Royal Owner Qualification

A crown, palace, or royal title is not sufficient. The formation needs a recognized ruler or lawful royal authority, functioning royal household, accepted officers, selected S04 retainers, equipment, standards, records, routes, quarters, stores, pay or patronage, and political confidence. Minority, captivity, deposition, disputed succession, civil war, confiscation, household fragmentation, and depleted estates can restrict or split access.

## 8. Crown, Household, Region, and Office

The practical source may flow through the king, queen-regent in a historically grounded political role, heir, royal household officers, earls holding royal office, castellans, or delegated commanders according to law and circumstance. Delegation does not transfer ownership freely. Regional elites may serve from obligation, patronage, shared danger, ambition, fear, or negotiated terms, and their response remains finite.

## 9. Royal Political Power

Fielding the unit concentrates prestigious retainers, officers, heirs, clients, weapons, shields, mail, standards, escorts, and coercive capacity. Keeping it at court protects the ruler and succession. Sending it to war may demonstrate authority and win prestige but exposes the royal center, household, treasury, archives, and claimants. Loss may affect legitimacy, succession confidence, elite bargaining, and regional obedience.

## 10. Personnel Composition

The unit may include selected Thegn spear veterans, royal household retainers, trusted file leaders, standard guards, messengers, estate followers, armorers, servants, and medical or supply support. Social proximity does not equal military competence. Replacements need legal acceptance, training, equipment, file integration, and trust.

## 11. S04 Recruitment Contract

Royal Thegn Spearmen consume a qualified subset of S04 Noble and Regional Following. The same retainers compete with ordinary Thegn Spearmen, Thegn Swordsmen, Mounted Thegns, Saxon Foot Guard, Earl’s Horsemen where qualified, household security, escorts, messengers, garrisons, and officers. Noble population informs capacity but does not directly become formations.

## 12. No-Double-Counting Firewall

One retainer cannot serve several active units. One officer cannot command several detached bodies simultaneously. One shield, spear, mail shirt, standard, cart, room, or obligation cannot serve several formations. Royal guard duty, garrison duty, escort, training cadre, wounded recovery, and field service are mutually accountable assignments.

## 13. Recruitment and Selection Funnel

The funnel identifies a valid royal owner, protects essential duties, selects experienced spear cadres and compatible replacements, confirms oaths and lawful release, inspects equipment, assigns officers and files, trains royal-site defense and relief, confirms routes and stores, and establishes return terms. Failure produces delay, reduced size, mixed equipment, weak legitimacy, or no formation.

## 14. Pool Capacity and Recovery

Introduction begins with a very small finite pool. Maturity may establish a reliable but still scarce royal body. Recovery separately restores fit retainers, wounded, officers, veterans, equipment, standards, cohesion, household confidence, regional cooperation, routes, and institutional records. Wealth cannot instantly recreate loyalty, file memory, or lost captains.

## 15. Assignments and Opportunity Cost

Assignments include ruler guard, heir escort, palace and treasury security, standard guard, royal progress escort, strategic garrison, field reserve, training cadre, recovering company, and active royal spear body. Moving the unit to one duty weakens every unfilled duty elsewhere.

## 16. Service Terms, Oaths, and Reward

Service may arise from household duty, oath, office, estate obligation, royal summons, negotiated term, emergency defense, patronage, or paid extension. Oaths shape expectations and consequences but do not create magical morale. Reward, recognition, inheritance, arrears, confiscation, favoritism, and broken promises alter future response.

## 17. Succession and Claimant Splits

A ruler’s death, incapacity, capture, minority, or deposition may test whether files follow the heir, regent, household captain, local earl, claimant, or their own home obligations. The unit may remain coherent, split, negotiate, withdraw, guard a person, secure a site, or defect. Outcomes require owner-routed character, dynasty, law, household, and political states, not a scripted universal result.

## 18. Officer and Command Architecture

Command includes ruler or delegate, royal household captain, spear-file leaders, standard group, messengers, gate and reserve officers, and recognized successors. Officer loss damages timing, turning, relief, brace, rally, and information before it causes rout. Legitimate succession of command can preserve local action.

## 19. Standards, Signals, and Recognition

Royal standards, household markings, horns, calls, messengers, known posts, and officer positions support command. Smoke, walls, darkness, noise, fog, casualties, mixed contingents, and disputed authority create delay. Standards are practical and political rally points rather than flat auras.

## 20. Equipment Ownership

Spears, shields, armor, helmets, sidearms, standards, clothing, carts, tents, and stores may be personal, household, estate, royal, purchased, inherited, gifted, captured, or recovered property. Ownership governs consent, recall, leverage, inheritance, repair, and loss. Royal issue does not erase personal or regional claims.

## 21. Equipment Baseline and Progression

The baseline is spear, large round or compatible shield, knife or short sword, padding, helmets, and variable mail. Development can improve shield consistency, mail coverage, spear quality, officer equipment, standards, footwear, tents, and repair without imposing identical uniforms, universal scale, plate, or fantasy guard styling.

## 22. Equipment Degradation

Spears break, warp, blunt, or disappear. Shields split, lose grips, warp, and shed coverings. Mail tears and corrodes. Helmets dent. Clothing, footwear, tents, straps, carts, and standards degrade. Readiness may decline before headcount, and recovery depends on control of the field, transport, ownership, craftsmen, stores, and time.

## 23. Training Program

Training covers route march, normal line, close order, defensive line, spear brace, Shieldwall entry and exit, turning, frontage relief, standard protection, gate and crossing defense, controlled counterattack, withdrawal, casualty movement, and mixed-unit coordination. Royal status does not replace practice.

## 24. Experience and Veteran Cadres

Experience in cavalry reception, crossing defense, urban fighting, royal progress, siege duty, succession crisis, missile exposure, failed relief, and orderly withdrawal creates different knowledge. Veteran death removes practical memory. New retainers restore numbers before they restore reliability.

## 25. Formation Access

The formation has strong access candidates for March Column, Normal Line, Close Order, Defensive Line, Spear Line, Spear Brace, Shieldwall, Reserve Posture, Controlled Relief, Standard Guard, and Orderly Withdrawal. Advanced Shieldwall and shielded movement remain conditional on compatible equipment, training, officers, fatigue, and runtime validation.

## 26. Shieldwall System

Shieldwall is a stateful formation, not a permanent trait. Entry, maintenance, turning, movement, relief, recovery, and exit have separate reliability. Missiles, uneven ground, fatigue, casualties, rear pressure, lost officers, mixed shields, and interrupted transitions degrade it. Royal Thegns may perform better than lower families without becoming unbreakable.

## 27. Spear Brace and Cavalry Reception

Brace requires time, facing, depth, footing, spear integrity, shield position, officer control, confidence, and visible threat. Cavalry may feint, shoot, flank, dismount, or attack elsewhere. A successful brace can stop or disorder contact but does not guarantee destruction. Failed facing or broken ground may negate the advantage.

## 28. Royal Command Anchor Doctrine

The signature battlefield role is preserving a decisive command-centered sector. The unit deploys near the ruler, standard, key flank, gate, crossing, or reserve line, receives pressure in prepared order, relieves damaged files, and protects withdrawal or reorganization. It should not chase low-value targets or abandon the royal center for a shallow advantage.

## 29. Controlled Relief Doctrine

Relief requires a designated lane, reserve depth, signals, intact officers, a receiving formation, and enough cohesion to exchange frontage without creating a gap. Outcomes include clean relief, partial overlap, delayed relief, blocked relief, or collapse during transition. The process must be visible rather than a hidden morale refill.

## 30. Counterattack Doctrine

Counterattack follows enemy disorder, fatigue, failed mounted contact, compressed frontage, exposed flank, or a prepared local opportunity. Spear-and-shield troops advance only far enough to restore the position, protect the ruler, or secure the chokepoint. Deep pursuit and unsupported assault remain poor uses.

## 31. Matchups and Counterplay

Favorable contexts include mounted threats received in formation, disordered infantry, crossing defense, gate defense, and relief of lower spear lines. Conditional opponents include armored infantry, elite cavalry after failed contact, and household guards. Counters include concentrated missiles, flanking, rear attack, forced turning, fatigue, command disruption, difficult ground, fire, encirclement, and elite assault after cohesion loss.

## 32. Combined Arms

Missiles disrupt enemies and protect approaches. Lower spear families provide broader frontage. Sword and axe troops counterattack or clear compressed fighting. Mounted forces screen flanks and pursue. Housecarls answer royal crisis and rupture. Royal Thegn Spearmen anchor and preserve the royal center without replacing any of these roles.

## 33. Ruler and Character Attachment

A ruler or eligible royal character may be physically associated with the formation where the character system authorizes it. Attachment changes protection duties, command benefits, target value, capture risk, succession consequences, and withdrawal decisions. The profile does not invent character deaths or guarantee the ruler’s battlefield presence.

## 34. Morale, Loyalty, Legitimacy, and Fear

Morale reflects immediate battlefield confidence. Loyalty reflects relationships and obligations. Legitimacy affects acceptance of command. Fear may produce compliance without trust. These states may conflict. A loyal unit may still break when surrounded; a fearful household may muster but recover politically very slowly.

## 35. Cohesion and Formation Memory

Cohesion depends on stable files, officers, shared practice, equipment compatibility, known signals, and casualty state. Formation memory survives through veterans and repeated service. Replacement-heavy bodies may retain high social status while losing tactical reliability.

## 36. Fatigue and Rotation

Armor, shield pressure, spear control, standing brace, repeated relief, heat, mud, uphill movement, missile stress, and close combat create fatigue. Rotation uses reserve files and protected lanes but increases command complexity. Elite status improves pacing, not physiology.

## 37. Casualties and Quality Loss

Track fit, wounded, killed, missing, captured, dispersed, and deserted retainers separately. Officer, veteran, standard bearer, heir, client, messenger, and equipment-owner losses have disproportionate institutional consequences. Equal headcount losses do not cause equal recovery.

## 38. Medical Return and Captivity

Wounded recovery depends on access, evacuation, household care, medicine, shelter, supply, season, and route security. Captured high-status retainers may enter ransom, exchange, hostage, inheritance, office, or succession systems. Capture does not automatically remove all political relationships.

## 39. Post-Battle Accounting

After battle, account for people, officers, veterans, shields, spears, armor, standards, stores, carts, captives, recovered property, grievances, honors, and assignments. Battlefield control affects recovery. Nominal numbers can return before cohesion, confidence, or equipment.

## 40. Supply and Campaign Endurance

Extended service requires food, water, clothing, shelter, equipment repair, transport, pay or patronage, communications, medical support, and secure routes. Royal access may improve organization but also raises expectations and political visibility. Out-of-supply effects attack cohesion, equipment, health, confidence, and return.

## 41. War Weariness and Royal Household Cost

Repeated high-risk use may weaken the royal household, estate administration, officer network, succession security, and elite confidence. Defending the ruler or realm can justify losses more readily than wasteful raids or attritional assaults. Recovery is political and institutional as well as demographic.

## 42. Siege, Gate, Wall, and Inner Defense

Strong roles include wall and gate defense, inner courts, towers, halls, breach reserve, standard protection, evacuation route, and relief of failing defenders. Conditional roles include secured-breach support and siege-camp command guard. Poor roles include expendable ladders, first-wave breach assault, engineering labor, and scattered occupation.

## 70. Royal Guard Formation Transitions

Royal Thegn Spearmen require explicit formation transitions rather than instant posture changes. Marching files must halt, identify frontage, align shields, lower or ready spears, place standards, assign reserves, and confirm flank support before achieving a reliable brace. Leaving the posture requires equally deliberate signals so rear files do not push forward while front files turn or withdraw.

Transition quality depends on training, officer survival, available space, visibility, terrain, fatigue, casualties, shield compatibility, and whether the ruler or standard changes position. Clean entry creates stable anti-mounted reception. Delayed entry leaves some files unready. Fragmented entry produces gaps and crossed spears. False alarm wastes endurance. Interrupted exit may trap files in a posture unsuitable for pursuit or rapid redeployment.

## 71. Royal Reserve Depth and Frontage Management

The formation should preserve a meaningful distinction between fighting frontage and reserve depth. A shallow line covers more space but has fewer replacements and less capacity to absorb casualties. A deeper body protects standards, officers, and the ruler more effectively and supports relief, but concedes frontage and becomes more vulnerable to missiles, congestion, and encirclement.

Commanders may allocate files to the front, local reserve, standard guard, ruler guard, messenger security, gate response, or withdrawal route. These are not free passive bonuses. Every protected function removes spears and shields from another task. AI and player information should reveal when excessive guard concentration has weakened the actual line.

## 72. Mounted Threat Recognition and False Commitment

The formation must distinguish a genuine mounted attack from screening, feint, missile harassment, lateral redeployment, or an attempt to pin the royal reserve. Threat recognition depends on visibility, scouts, messengers, terrain, enemy speed, formation, weapon state, supporting troops, and officer experience.

Premature bracing may surrender mobility and expose the unit to arrows or infantry maneuver. Late bracing risks incomplete facing and broken spear presentation. Continual false alarms increase fatigue and reduce trust in signals. Experienced officers improve classification and timing without receiving perfect knowledge of enemy orders.

## 73. Missile Pressure, Shield Discipline, and Attrition

Shields improve survival only when angle, formation, condition, fatigue, and threat direction are favorable. Concentrated archery, crossbows, javelins, elevated fire, rear fire, and prolonged exposure still damage the formation. Shield protection competes with spear readiness, visibility, movement, and the ability to receive threats from several directions.

Royal Thegns may endure pressure longer than ordinary noble spear files because of officers, equipment, relief, and purpose, but endurance is costly. Damaged shields, wounded front files, tired replacements, broken spear shafts, and interrupted commands steadily reduce brace and relief quality. The correct response may be cover, rotation, repositioning, counterbattery support, cavalry action, or withdrawal rather than passive immunity.

## 74. Breach, Gate, Stair, Tower, and Hall Micro-Contexts

Gate defense rewards controlled frontage, reserve depth, protected officers, and the ability to relieve compressed files. Breach defense introduces rubble, uneven footing, missiles, smoke, bodies, and irregular width. Stairs and towers limit spear length and reinforcement. Halls and courts favor ruler protection and short defensive lines but create fire, crowding, and blocked-exit risks.

The unit's high castle suitability must therefore emerge through preparation, command, equipment, and relief rather than a flat environment bonus. Attackers can suppress, flank, burn, bypass, undermine, exhaust, or isolate the position. Royal Thegns should be formidable defenders without making a fortified site automatically safe.

## 75. Royal Household Absence and Home-Security Consequences

When the formation campaigns, the royal center loses trusted guards, messengers, standard personnel, officers, equipment, and politically visible force. Absence may increase exposure to plots, riots, hostage attempts, treasury seizure, rival claimants, raiding, or coercion of remaining officials where the relevant systems support those outcomes.

The design does not create automatic rebellion whenever the unit leaves. Risk depends on legitimacy, other guards, local forces, relationships, intelligence, fortifications, distance, campaign purpose, and active rivals. The important principle is that elite concentration creates a real strategic trade rather than a free addition to the field army.

## 76. Royal Death, Capture, Rescue, and Withdrawal Priorities

If a royal character is killed, wounded, captured, missing, or isolated, the formation evaluates information quality, recognized succession, rescue feasibility, standard status, enemy pressure, and withdrawal routes. Some files may attempt rescue, others secure the heir or regent, others preserve the standard, and others cover retreat. A single deterministic reaction would erase the political and command systems this unit is meant to express.

Successful rescue may preserve authority at severe casualty cost. Failed rescue may destroy officers and veterans. Orderly withdrawal with an heir or standard may maintain continuity despite battlefield defeat. Surrender may preserve high-status lives for ransom while transferring equipment, standards, and political leverage to the enemy.

## 77. Royal Spear Institution Restoration Plan

A depleted royal spear institution is restored through surviving officers, returned wounded, reconciled households, renewed oaths, replacement retainers, recovered equipment, repaired standards, reopened routes, restored estates, and credible royal authority. Each input repairs a different readiness layer.

Restoration may produce a numerically rebuilt but inexperienced body, a veteran cadre below useful frontage, an equipped formation without political confidence, or a loyal formation without shields and officers. The recovery interface should show these distinctions and avoid a single opaque replenishment timer.

## 78. Final Consistency Assertions

Royal Thegn Spearmen never receive automatic access from a palace, castle, barracks, Noble Hall, cultural percentage, or noble-population count. They never use S05 Housecarls as free replacements. They never duplicate parent Thegn Spearmen during conversion. They never become immune to cavalry, missiles, morale loss, flanking, fatigue, siege hazards, succession conflict, or supply failure. They never receive later equipment solely because time passed. They never promote exact values without direct cross-family testing and owner authorization.
