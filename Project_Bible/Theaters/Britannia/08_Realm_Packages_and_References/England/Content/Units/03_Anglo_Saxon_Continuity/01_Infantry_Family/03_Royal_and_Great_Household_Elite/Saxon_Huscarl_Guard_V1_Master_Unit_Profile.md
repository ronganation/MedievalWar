## 34. Bodyguard Proximity

Near the ruler or owner, purpose and command may improve while freedom to pursue declines. Detached field service increases tactical flexibility but weakens household protection elsewhere. Losing ruler and Guard together can have major political effects without guaranteeing universal army collapse.

## 35. Cohesion and File Integrity

Cohesion depends on familiar officers, shared service, protected flanks, usable axe spacing, and a clear objective. Casualties, mixed units, rubble, weapon changes, pursuit, smoke, darkness, and opposing pressure degrade file integrity. Elite recovery is better, not instantaneous.

## 36. Fatigue and Endurance

Good stamina and disciplined pacing permit intense local action, but two-handed strikes, mail, climbing, heat, mud, repeated counterattacks, and casualty evacuation accumulate fatigue. Exhaustion reduces strike recovery, defense, response, and morale.

## 37. Combat Matchups

```yaml
favorable:
  - fixed shielded infantry when axe spacing exists
  - armored infantry under controlled local engagement
  - disordered attackers inside a breach or street
  - low-quality troops already under morale pressure
  - counterattack against a stalled assault
unfavorable:
  - concentrated missiles during approach
  - fresh spears and polearms with room to work
  - cavalry striking an exposed flank
  - dense forest and severe crossings
  - encirclement by numerous formations
  - prolonged loose pursuit
```

## 38. Counterplay

Opponents can refuse the Guard, screen with missiles, pin with reach weapons, threaten the ruler elsewhere, force repeated marches, attack supply, widen frontage, rotate fresh troops, break the command group, or isolate the formation. Elite status must create difficult problems, not remove counterplay.

## 45. Urban and Hall Combat

Compressed routes may favor courage, armor, familiarity with guard duty, and strong file leadership, while also obstructing long axes. Sword-and-shield profiles improve close interior fighting. Fire, civilians, rubble, doors, stairs, smoke, and blocked retreat remain major constraints.

## 46. Field Battle Use

Best field use is ruler protection, crisis reserve, shieldwall reinforcement under a compatible profile, and controlled counterattack. The Guard should rarely occupy the entire first line and should not chase cavalry or skirmishers.

## 47. Scale, Numbers, and Elite Resistance

A narrower guard can defeat or withstand larger levy bodies through morale, armor, training, officers, formation, and local concentration. Numbers still generate frontage, rotation, surround, and cumulative fatigue. The shared scale authority must calibrate elite potency and formation size together rather than inflating both.

## 48. Supply and Maintenance

The Guard consumes high-quality food, pay or patronage, shelter, mail and helmet repair, axe hafts and heads, shields and sidearms, clothing, footwear, medical care, transport, and secure routes. Prestige increases expectations and political consequences rather than eliminating logistics.

## 49. Equipment Ownership and Recovery

Equipment may belong to a warrior, ruler, household, armory, or inherited guard office. Recovery routes damaged, captured, lost, and retained items to the correct owner. Loot does not automatically create a trained Guard, and replacement kit does not restore veteran cohesion.

## 52. War Weariness and Political Memory

Repeated low-value use, poor supply, unpaid promises, abandonment, defeat, owner death, or catastrophic losses can lower guard confidence and household willingness. Successful defense, fair reward, recovered comrades, protected standards, and meaningful victories can strengthen the institution without creating infinite readiness.

## 53. Prisoners, Ransom, and Loyalty

Guard members may be captured, ransomed, exchanged, recruited by rivals only under exceptional owner-controlled conditions, or die in captivity. Ransom can preserve veteran quality at financial and political cost. Loyalty should reduce casual defection but cannot make every captive unbreakable.

## 55. AI Battlefield Commitment

AI should preserve the Guard behind the main line, monitor ruler and command threats, identify a clear counterattack lane, avoid missile exposure, halt pursuit, and reform quickly. AI should commit in a genuine crisis rather than hoard the formation in a lost battle.

## 56. Autoresolve

Autoresolve should account for guard mission, owner presence, formation, equipment profile, morale, command, terrain, support, fatigue, supply, and enemy reach or missiles. Losses should route to household, political, security, and succession consequences. Raw elite quality cannot erase encirclement or overwhelming pressure.

## 62. Stainless Steel Saxon Huscarls Comparator

The recovered Stainless Steel page presents Saxon Huscarls as superior mercenary two-handed axemen protected by mail, with 63 soldiers and limited regional and chronological availability. This supports post-conquest survival, regional dispersal, and contract access as possibilities. It does not define the domestic Saxon Huscarl Guard, which remains an S05 continuity reform.

## 63. Historical Equipment Comparator

This supports multiple carried weapons and shield-state research while leaving exact proportions unresolved.
