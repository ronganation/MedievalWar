## 2. Current Verdict

Earl's Horsemen are a scarce, owner-bound comital cavalry institution. They provide mounted command reserve, controlled countercharge, flank security, commander protection, regional rapid response, disciplined disengagement, and pursuit control.

They are not generic knights, a realm-wide elite pool, Shire Horsemen with armor, or Mounted Thegns with higher statistics. Their reason to exist is the combination of qualified ownership, political concentration, officer density, selected riders and mounts, remount support, and strategic consequence.

## 3. One-Screen Designer Card

Battle promise: preserve a mobile reserve around a qualified earl, defeat exposed mounted threats, secure a threatened flank, strike committed enemies, recover formation, and turn genuine collapse into controlled pursuit.

Best use includes countercharge, owner protection, flank security, threatened-sector relief, local exploitation, enemy pursuit, and regional response. Worst use includes prepared spear fronts, dense missiles, enclosed terrain, urban assault, repeated shock without recovery, expendable reconnaissance, and prolonged stationary melee.

## 4. Historical and Counterfactual Boundary

Historical foundations include mounted noble and household service, lordly followings, horse ownership, retainers, regional command, escort, reconnaissance, pursuit, and mounted combat. The formal recurring Earl's Horsemen branch is a campaign-development abstraction.

The profile does not claim that every earl possessed an identical regiment, that titles generated cavalry, that every retainer fought mounted, or that early English magnates fielded later knightly institutions unchanged. Historical possibility opens a development path but does not guarantee emergence.

## 5. Distinct Unit Survival Test

The card survives only if comital ownership materially changes recruitment, loyalty, assignments, politics, command, horse access, campaign duties, loss consequences, and tactical control. Ordinary Mounted Thegns must remain broader and more economical, while Feudal Knights retain specialist knightly shock.

If comparison proves that the distinction is only better armor, charge, or morale, this card reverts to an owner-specific Mounted Thegn equipment, readiness, and command profile rather than occupying a redundant roster slot.

## 7. Crown, Earl, Household, and Region Relationship

The formation is not a free royal asset. Crown authority may summon, reward, discipline, or redirect service, but practical response depends on law, custom, obligation, relationships, distance, competing threats, and memory. The earl may provide full, partial, conditional, delayed, or refused service.

Owner-led regional defense can increase purpose and assembly speed. Distant crown expeditions create remount attrition, political absence, duration disputes, reward expectations, and household-security costs. A disputed succession can split officers, riders, horses, and standards between claimants.

## 8. Political Power and Strategic Consequence

Earl's Horsemen visibly concentrate a magnate's clients, relatives, officers, wealth, horses, transport, and coercive reach. Keeping them home can deter rivals and protect estates. Sending them away demonstrates loyalty and may earn prestige, but exposes household administration, succession, communications, and local defense.

Victory may strengthen reputation, patronage, office claims, bargaining power, and confidence. Waste, abandonment, unpaid extension, confiscation, or disproportionate loss may cause grievance, rivalry, reduced cooperation, demands for compensation, or weakened legitimacy. These consequences route through political systems rather than a flat influence modifier.

## 9. Personnel Composition and Eligibility

Eligible personnel include the earl or delegate, household captain, veteran Mounted Thegn cadres, recognized retainers, selected regional followers, file leaders, messengers, standards, grooms, armorers, farriers, guides, and handlers. Not every noble is battle-ready and not every rider belongs in an elite comital body.

Eligibility requires mounted competence, recognized command, physical readiness, compatible equipment, an available mount, no conflicting assignment, and acceptance into the household files. Replacement riders restore headcount before they restore cohesion, confidence, or officer depth.

## 10. Dual-Source Recruitment Contract

S04 owns noble and regional retainers, household officers, political relationships, and competing foot or mounted assignments. S07 owns riders, riding horses, service mounts, military mounts, later warhorse possibilities, remount depth, handlers, conditioning, and mounted practice.

The same retainer cannot fill Thegn Spearmen, Thegn Swordsmen, Mounted Thegns, and Earl's Horsemen simultaneously. The same horse cannot serve several formations. Stable capacity, population, wealth, or pasture alone cannot substitute for either source.

## 11. Horse Capability Ladder

Pack and draft animals have no mounted-combat access. Ordinary riding horses support travel, communication, scouting, and limited service. Service mounts support sustained riding, formation movement, and reserve response. Military mounts require size, health, temperament, conditioning, training, equipment, and compatible riders.

Later warhorses require developed breeding, specialist institutions, chronology, and sustained practice. They are never assumed at the 1062 opening. A formation can contain uneven capability and must not receive the performance of its best mounts across every file.

## 12. Horse Acquisition, Breeding, and Diffusion

Horses enter through household breeding, regional markets, import, gifts, tribute, inheritance, capture, exchange, and negotiated requisition. Each channel has different ownership, route, disease, adaptation, cost, and political consequences.

Contact with continental cavalry may spread knowledge and aspirations, but knowledge does not instantly create horses, tack, lances, officers, or practice. Imported mounts may improve selected files while creating dependence and replacement problems.

## 13. Rider and Mount Compatibility

A suitable rider and a suitable horse do not automatically form a reliable pair. Temperament, cues, balance, equipment, noise tolerance, weapon use, formation spacing, and repeated practice create compatibility.

Emergency reassignment may get a rider mounted quickly but should reduce charge confidence, turning, rally, and fatigue management. Time, handlers, officers, and safe practice improve the relationship.

## 14. Remount Architecture

Remounts sustain operational radius and replace exhausted, injured, sick, lost, captured, or unsuitable mounts. They must be available, compatible, equipped, delivered, and adapted. Fresh mounts do not restore exhausted riders, dead officers, damaged tack, or broken cohesion.

States include healthy reserve, thin reserve, mixed ordinary-horse reserve, route-blocked reserve, equipment-blocked reserve, adaptation required, and exhausted. Players may preserve remounts, rotate files, reduce frontage, shorten pursuit, delay operations, or accept lower quality.

## 15. Stables, Pasture, Estates, and Markets

Stables provide shelter, conditioning, feed storage, veterinary support, tack storage, records, assignment, and assembly. Pasture supports health and breeding. Estates organize obligations and resources. Markets offer acquisition and repair inputs.

None creates trained cavalry independently. A burned stable, lost pasture, disputed estate, blocked market, or damaged bridge should degrade distinct readiness layers rather than acting as a single universal unlock.

## 16. Farriers, Grooms, Saddlers, and Support Train

Farriers maintain hooves and address lameness. Grooms feed, water, clean, observe, and manage horses. Saddlers and leatherworkers maintain tack. Armorers and weapon craftsmen support riders. Guides and transport handlers preserve routes and baggage.

Support personnel are not hidden combatants, but their absence reduces endurance, remount usability, equipment condition, medical return, communication, and recovery. Their loss must be recorded and restored through the owning institutions.

## 17. Equipment Ownership and Property

Weapons, armor, horses, saddles, shields, tack, clothing, baggage, and tools may belong to the rider, household, estate, crown, market supplier, or captor. Ownership determines consent, return, leverage, dispute, inheritance, and recovery.

Equipment may include spear or lance, sword, seax, hand axe, shields where validated, mail, helmet, padding, and riding gear. No universal full plate, barding, identical lances, or modern uniform is authorized.

## 18. Equipment Progression and Degradation

Progression may improve mail coverage, helmets, saddlery, lances, shields, horse selection, maintenance, and visual coherence. It must remain chronology-compatible and may be uneven between households and files.

Weapons bend, chip, break, or disappear. Mail tears and corrodes. Straps and reins fail. Saddles damage horses. Hooves deteriorate. Shields split. Equipment readiness can collapse before rider headcount.

## 19. Recruitment Funnel and Assembly

Recruitment begins with qualified ownership and lawful authority, then protects essential household and regional duties, selects eligible cadres, assigns mounts, inspects equipment, gathers support, negotiates terms, conditions horses, forms files, practices signals, and confirms routes and return.

Failure at any stage produces delay, reduced frontage, mixed quality, political restriction, under-equipping, inadequate remounts, or cancellation. Money may accelerate some inputs but cannot manufacture trust, veterans, officers, horse temperament, or adaptation.

## 20. Assembly and Readiness State Machine

States include Unavailable, Proposed, Negotiating, Gathering Riders, Assigning Mounts, Conditioning, Integrating, Ready, Reduced, Officer Depleted, Mount Depleted, Remount Depleted, Equipment Delayed, Politically Blocked, Recovering, Declining, and Extinct.

The UI must show the strongest blocker and the affected layer. A nominally full unit can remain nonready because the owner relationship, officers, horses, tack, cohesion, routes, or service term is unresolved.

## 21. Assignment Firewall

Household Guard protects family, residence, treasury, archives, and authority. Escort protects people and goods. Patrol deters raids. Messenger service carries commands. Training cadres develop riders and horses. Field service joins armies. Recovery retains wounded people and animals.

One formation cannot perform these duties simultaneously. Reassignment creates delay, route, readiness, security, and political consequences. Fielding elite horsemen may weaken the exact region or household that produced them.

## 22. Service Terms and Duration

Service may derive from household duty, regional obligation, crown summons, paid extension, negotiated expedition, emergency defense, office, patronage, or political compulsion. Terms affect duration, reward, obedience, retention, demobilization, and grievance.

Long service increases fodder, equipment wear, remount use, household absence, rider fatigue, and political costs. Rotation is a strategic requirement rather than a free refresh.

## 23. Demobilization and Return

Demobilization accounts for riders, horses, wounded, captives, missing persons, equipment, arrears, loot, awards, disputes, and altered assignments. People and property return to the correct owners.

Safe return with paid retainers, recovered wounded, preserved standards, and accounted mounts can strengthen confidence. Unexplained losses, seized horses, unpaid service, or abandoned dependents can poison the next summons.

## 24. Officer Structure and Succession

Command includes the earl or delegated owner, household captain, mounted file leaders, standard group, messengers, guides, remount officers, and support supervisors. Functions include alignment, target confirmation, charge timing, flank security, pursuit restraint, rally, and owner protection.

Officer loss first damages local timing, turning, rally, and target selection. Recognized successors may preserve partial command. Rivalry or disputed succession can fragment the formation without causing a deterministic instant rout.

## 25. Signals, Standards, and Information

Standards, horns, calls, messengers, household markings, known routes, and officer positions carry orders. Dust, darkness, fog, hills, hedges, noise, speed, casualties, and mixed units create delay or misunderstanding.

A standard is a practical and political focus, not a magical aura. Its loss can cause confusion, shame, rivalry, concentration around recovery, or changes in household reputation.

## 26. Training Program

Training covers ordinary riding, route march, formation dressing, reserve posture, controlled acceleration, weapon presentation, countercharge, wheeling, disengagement, rally, pursuit restraint, owner extraction, horse care, and combined-arms timing.

Training quality depends on surviving officers, familiar files, suitable mounts, space, time, support staff, and repeated service. A building bonus cannot replace the program.

## 27. Experience Pathways

Countercharge veterans improve threat recognition and timing. Pursuit veterans improve restraint and capture. Escort veterans improve owner protection and route security. Campaign veterans improve pacing, remount use, camp discipline, and horse care.

Experience is contextual. It does not grant unexplained superiority in every terrain or mission, and replacement riders do not inherit the full experience of dead cadres.

## 28. Charge Geometry

Useful shock requires approach distance, suitable ground, coherent frontage, alignment, speed, weapon presentation, target visibility, serviceable mounts, officer control, acceptable fatigue, and a withdrawal or support route.

Wider formations threaten frontage but are harder to align. Deeper formations may add local mass but raise collision and congestion. Charge orders can yield clean, partial, fragmented, blunted, refused, countered, or overextended contact.

## 29. Countercharge Doctrine

Countercharge is the signature battlefield proposition. The unit meets hostile cavalry when it is exposed, tired, fragmented, committed against friendly troops, or threatening a flank.

Success depends on detection, space, alignment, horse condition, reach, timing, support, and rally. Earl's Horsemen do not automatically defeat Feudal Knights, horse archers, professional cavalry, or other noble horse.

## 30. Flank Security and Threat Deterrence

Flank security includes observation, deterrence, screening, interception, reserve positioning, and rapid reinforcement. The presence of a coherent comital reserve can change enemy choices without producing a hidden aura.

Deterring one threat may prevent commitment elsewhere. The player and AI must compare owner protection, enemy cavalry, infantry relief, pursuit opportunity, and escape routes.

## 31. Commander and Standard Protection

Protection includes close escort, reserve response, route opening, extraction, standard defense, messenger security, and preventing isolation. Owner proximity can improve shared purpose while narrowing tactical freedom.

A tempting charge may be rejected if it exposes the earl. Owner death, capture, injury, or disappearance can trigger succession, rally, fragmentation, rescue, ransom, or withdrawal behavior through explicit systems.

## 32. Disengagement and Rally

Mounted advantage collapses when the unit remains pinned in prolonged melee. Break contact requires space, functioning horses, signals, officers, protected routes, and friendly support.

Rally depends on recognizable standards, available ground, horse condition, enemy proximity, file survival, and route knowledge. A successful charge without recovery may be a strategic failure.

## 33. Pursuit, Capture, and Restraint

Pursuit converts real collapse into prisoners, horses, standards, baggage, intelligence, and strategic disruption. It also risks ambush, countercharge, exhaustion, scattered files, officer loss, and separation from supply.

Officers compare target value, enemy reserves, terrain, rally distance, owner safety, and remount condition. Pursuit is not an automatic casualty multiplier.

## 34. Morale, Loyalty, and Reputation

Morale reflects immediate confidence in officers, horses, equipment, purpose, support, and escape. Loyalty reflects relationships and obligations over time. Reputation records household memory of courage, competence, waste, reward, betrayal, and return.

The layers interact but must not collapse into one number. Strong loyalty may keep a unit in service without making an exhausted horse charge; high morale may not overcome disputed legal authority.

## 35. Fatigue and Horse-Rider Coupling

Horse and rider fatigue progress separately. A fresh rider on an exhausted mount cannot deliver reliable shock, while a fresh remount does not restore a wounded or exhausted rider.

Pace, distance, armor, heat, mud, slope, fear, collision, repeated acceleration, water, feed, and rest alter both layers. Training improves pacing, not physiology.

## 36. Veterinary and Medical Recovery

Rider wounds, horse wounds, lameness, hoof damage, saddle sores, exhaustion, disease, and equipment injury require different care. Farriers, grooms, shelter, feed, water, routes, medical support, and time shape outcomes.

Injured horses may recover, shift to limited duty, transfer, be sold, or die. Disabled riders may recover through household care, remain with the train, become captives, or create political and inheritance consequences.

## 37. Post-Battle Accounting

Track riders as fit, wounded, killed, missing, captured, dispersed, or deserted. Track horses as fit, worked, exhausted, injured, lame, missing, captured, killed, or unsuitable. Track officers, veterans, trainers, standards, tack, armor, and weapons separately.

Nominal headcount can recover before command depth, compatible horses, cohesion, reputation, household confidence, or political authorization returns.

## 38. Supply, Fodder, Water, and Radius

Mounted elite service needs food, large fodder and water flows, shelter, hoof care, replacement tack, veterinary materials, pack support, transport, pay or patronage, medicine, and secure routes.

Grazing can reduce carried fodder in suitable places but risks delay, dispersal, crop damage, conflict, and enemy denial. Operational radius depends on seasons, roads, staging, remounts, forage, security, and return.

## 39. War Weariness and Household Cost

Repeated summons remove high-value riders, heirs, clients, officers, horses, and working capacity from home institutions. Losses weaken estate administration, policing, communications, local defense, and succession stability.

Fair reward, meaningful defense, prestige, ransom, recovered equipment, and safe return may rebuild confidence. Recovery remains owner-specific rather than a generic cavalry timer.

## 40. Dismount and Horse Custody

Prebattle dismount is a conditional candidate preserving personnel, casualties, experience, owner relationships, and horse assignments. It requires eligible equipment, a coherent mission, horse security, AI planning, and routes.

Live dismount and remount remain prototype-required because animation, equipment transfer, horse custody, pathfinding, AI, casualty accounting, save, and replay are unresolved. Dismounted service does not automatically create Thegn Swordsmen or Spearmen.

## 41. Regional Variation

Frontier, wealthy, devastated, maritime, northern, southern, East Anglian, and continental-contact households may vary in horses, equipment, shields, officers, language, service duration, routes, and missions.

Variation stays within one card while owner, source, and tactical proposition remain shared. Separate cards require materially distinct source, ownership, access, campaign cost, or battlefield action.

## 42. Cultural Development and Cross-Adoption

Anglo-Saxon Continuity favors native household authority, Mounted Thegn cadres, regional command, comital legitimacy, and gradual improvement in horses, equipment, formation control, and support.

Continental lances, saddlery, armor, training, and horse practice may be adopted selectively without instantly transforming the unit into Feudal Knights. Anglo-Norman development may redirect some owners and riders while selected native institutions persist.

## 43. Coexistence, Conversion, and Reversion

Mounted Thegns, Shire Horsemen, Mounted Sergeants, Feudal Knights, and Royal Knightly Retinue may coexist when their sources and institutions exist. Automatic conversion is prohibited.

Selected cadre conversion, mixed new formation, equipment-only improvement, owner reorganization, and reversion are possible. Every path preserves casualties, ownership, experience, horse assignments, and source depletion.

## 44. Campaign AI Recruitment

AI recruits when a qualified owner exists, household security remains acceptable, S04 and S07 spare capacity is real, horses and remounts are healthy, routes and supply are viable, and a mounted command reserve is needed.

AI avoids recruitment when ordinary Mounted Thegns suffice, foot Thegn sources are more urgent, remounts are hollow, terrain is enclosed, or the source region is exposed. Difficulty improves decisions, not hidden bonuses.

## 45. Battle AI States

Battle AI observes under cover, protects the owner, secures flanks, prepares countercharge, commits under viable geometry, breaks contact before prolonged melee, rallies in safe space, pursues only broken targets, and recovers tired files.

It must not repeatedly charge prepared spears, chase bait through hostile terrain, ignore horse fatigue, abandon the earl, or treat noble status as immunity.

## 56. Weather and Ordered Seasonal Cycle

Rain lowers traction, damages roads and tack, slows recovery, and raises disease risk. Fog weakens identification and charge timing. Snow and ice reduce speed, endurance, and route access. Heat raises water demand and horse fatigue. Storms can block movement, communication, crossing, and maritime transport.

Winter emphasizes shelter, fodder, roads, and horse health. Spring brings mud, flooding, pasture recovery, and disease. Summer improves movement while raising heat and water costs. Autumn combines harvest, fodder storage, rain, route decline, and pressure to return home.

## 57. Siege, Settlement, Camp, and Sortie Use

Strong uses include outer screening, messenger duty, camp and commander protection, intercepting relief armies, pursuing sorties, and moving between threatened sectors outside walls. Conditional uses include dismounted guard or escort duty where authorized.

Poor uses include wall assault, mounted breach attack, narrow street shock, prolonged investment without fodder, and standing cavalry in exposed missile range.

## 69. Player Burden Control and Layered Disclosure

The design must remain deep without forcing the player to administer every horse, strap, hoof, retainer, groom, or feed ration individually. The player chooses owners, recruitment, assignments, service extensions, operating posture, commitment, disengagement, pursuit, rotation, and demobilization. The simulation resolves individual contributors into visible readiness layers and causal blockers.

The first campaign layer shows whether the formation is ready, reduced, recovering, or blocked and identifies the strongest reason. A second layer exposes riders, officers, service mounts, military mounts, remounts, equipment, support staff, household confidence, regional cooperation, supply, routes, and service terms. Detailed records remain available for designers, event resolution, loss accounting, and implementation validation.

Battle commands should remain recognizable Total War actions rather than administrative menus. Countercharge, owner protection, flank security, break contact, rally, and pursuit may be expressed through formations, contextual orders, AI postures, or a small number of readable abilities. Complexity belongs in consequences and state resolution, not button proliferation.

## 70. Emergence, Maturity, Decline, and Restoration States

Unavailable means Mounted Thegn service exists but no qualified comital formation has emerged. Proposed means an owner begins selecting cadres, mounts, officers, and support. Experimental means a small uneven body can perform limited owner protection and reserve duty. Introduction means a very scarce recruitable formation appears. Adoption means reliable service cycles and remount practices develop. Maturity means the owner sustains a finite established institution with deeper command and recovery. Strain means losses, politics, or supply reduce performance. Decline means the title or household survives while practical cavalry capacity contracts. Extinction means restoration requires rebuilding people, horses, officers, owner confidence, and institutions.

Restoration is never a timer-only process. A returning claimant, repaired estate, recovered standard, renewed pasture, imported mounts, surviving officers, political settlement, or successful regional defense may contribute, but no single event automatically recreates the formation. The player and AI must rebuild the causal chain.
