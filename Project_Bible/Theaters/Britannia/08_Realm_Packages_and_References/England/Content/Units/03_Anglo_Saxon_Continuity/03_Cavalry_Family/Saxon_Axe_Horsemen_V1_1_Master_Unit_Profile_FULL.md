## 2. One-Screen Designer Card

- **Class:** Selected High-era medium axe-and-shield cavalry.
- **Source:** S03 qualified regional riders intersected with S07 horses, pairings, remounts, tack, handlers, and mounted practice; conditional S04 sponsorship and S08 workshop support.
- **Best use:** protected flank pressure, countering light cavalry, attacking exposed missiles, exploiting infantry-created disorder, compact reserve counterattack, and protected pursuit.
- **Worst use:** braced spears or pikes, coherent knightly charges, dense missiles, deep woods, streets, marshes, blocked crossings, and prolonged stationary melee.
- **Identity:** a native continuity reform, not Mounted Huscarls, foot axemen on horses, or inferior Feudal Knights.
- **Cost:** riders, horses, mail, shields, axes, officers, workshops, local patrol coverage, household security, and slow paired recovery.

## 13. Political and Strategic Consequence

Fielding the unit concentrates regional mounted power. A strong body can protect routes, deter raiders, support local authority, and increase battlefield confidence. Removing the same riders and horses can weaken patrols, messengers, estates, household security, market movement, and rapid response.

Victory can strengthen reputation and institutional confidence. Waste, arrears, horse seizure, abandoned wounded, failed breakoff, disproportionate casualties, or unauthorized raiding can create grievance, resistance, compensation demands, and future recruitment decline.

## 28. Cavalry Axe Geometry

The axe has less reach than a spear or lance and therefore demands timing, shielding, horse control, target disorder, and an exit plan. Its compact head supports powerful cuts and hooking actions in short mounted contact.

```yaml
best_geometry:
  - exposed or turning targets
  - disrupted light cavalry
  - missile troops caught during movement
  - fixed infantry flanks
  - pursuit of armored remnants
worst_geometry:
  - frontal long-weapon reception
  - coherent lance charge
  - deep packed melee
  - obstacles preventing lateral exit
```

Any armor-piercing effect remains contextual and tested rather than a universal bonus.

## 29. Shielded Mounted Approach

The shield helps the rider survive scattered missiles, glancing contact, close pressure, and withdrawal. It also occupies the shield arm and affects balance, visibility, rein control, and axe delivery.

```yaml
benefits:

  - stronger contact against light mounted opponents
costs:

  - control burden
```

## 32. Anti-Light-Cavalry Engagement Cycle

Saxon Axe Horsemen can challenge lighter cavalry through protection, confidence, and compact contact, but may be outmaneuvered.

```text
Deny Enemy Harassment Lane
-> Force Turn Or Commitment
-> Close Under Shield
-> Strike During Disordered Contact
-> Avoid Extended Chase
-> Break And Reform
```

Faster opponents retain counterplay through feints, missiles, terrain, spacing, and refusal.

## 33. Counterattack and Reserve Doctrine

As a regional reserve, the unit waits behind the main line until an enemy flank, cavalry body, missile wing, or breakthrough becomes vulnerable. Premature commitment removes local mounted protection and exposes the army to later threats.

Reserve posture preserves horses, officers, cohesion, and decision time. A successful counterattack must still define a rally point and post-contact mission.

## 40. Fixed Melee and Anti-Cavalry Boundary

The unit is not a universal anti-cavalry solution. In prolonged melee, short reach, horse crowding, fatigue, shield-arm load, mixed protection, officer disruption, and inability to break contact become decisive.

Prepared long weapons, superior horses, lance reach, deeper armor, professional drill, and fresh formations can defeat the unit despite its axe identity.

## 41. Morale, Discipline, Loyalty, and Reputation

Morale reflects confidence in riders, horses, officers, equipment, support, mission, infantry anchors, and escape routes. Discipline governs contact, property, prisoners, breakoff, pursuit, rally, and return. Loyalty remains owner- and service-dependent.

Reputation records effective support, courage, restraint, horse care, property treatment, wounded recovery, cowardice, waste, theft, and reckless pursuit.

## 42. Rider and Horse Fatigue

Rider and horse fatigue progress separately. Mail, shields, axes, acceleration, turning, contact, heat, cold, mud, hills, hunger, thirst, poor saddle fit, fear, wounds, and repeated pursuit impose cumulative burdens.

A fresh horse cannot restore an exhausted rider. A protected rider on an exhausted horse cannot perform the unit's contact cycle.

## 48. Weather Interaction

Rain reduces traction, metal and leather condition, route quality, visibility, and signals. Fog harms target identification and timing. Snow and ice harm pace and endurance. Heat increases water and fatigue burdens. Wind, darkness, dust, and storms degrade control, communication, and horse confidence.

## 49. Settlement, Camp, Siege, and Convoy Use

Useful assignments include outer patrol, convoy escort, relief-column contact, foraging protection, camp reserve, anti-sortie warning, road response, command reinforcement, pursuit after collapse, and withdrawal cover.

Poor assignments include walls, ladders, breaches, dense streets, gate crushes, siege-engine operation, and fixed horse melee. Dismounted emergency use remains conditional.

## 50. Raiding and Property Boundary

A controlled raid requires authority, military objective, target identification, time and force limits, route and return plan, horse and supply review, prisoner policy, property rules, and friendly or neutral boundaries.

The card grants no automatic devastation, food, horses, loot, intelligence, or political immunity. Unauthorized seizure can undermine the very regional support that produces the unit.

## 65. Direct Cavalry-Family Acceptance Criteria

The unit advances only if tests show all of the following:

1. Shire Horsemen retain a cheaper local-service reason to exist.
2. Mounted Sergeants remain better at professional screening, escort, disengagement, and campaign reliability.
3. Saxon Axe Horsemen are better in suitable protected compact contact.
4. Earl's Horsemen retain command, owner, countercharge, horse, officer, and political superiority.
5. Feudal Knights retain decisive formed-shock superiority.
6. Infantry-created opportunities materially improve Saxon Axe Horsemen behavior.
7. Braced spears, pikes, concentrated missiles, terrain, fatigue, and failed exit routes remain strong counters.
8. Regional and household sources visibly constrain recruitment and recovery.
9. Achieved profiles produce meaningful variation without additional cards.
10. AI can contact, break off, reform, and refuse unsuitable fights.
