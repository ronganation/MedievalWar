## 5. Definitive Player-Facing Proposition

King's Men are the crown's most concentrated mounted field reserve, chosen for ruler and standard protection, decisive controlled shock, countercharge against a dangerous mounted threat, and disciplined reform after commitment. They are not a generic top-tier cavalry upgrade. Broader Feudal Knights supply more attainable heavy-cavalry frontage. Royal Knightly Retinue must own a different chronological or duty proposition if retained. Dismounted King's Men commit the linked royal source to foot service. The player should want King's Men intensely while still having reasons to recruit and preserve every neighboring family.

It describes heavily protected cavalry with an effective charge. Those facts remain external comparator evidence only. ## 8. Naming Architecture

King's Men remains the working player-facing label because it is readable and signals crown ownership. It does not assert a universally attested formal regiment. Search aliases should include King's Men, Kings Men, Royal Household Knights, and approved localized forms. Final terminology should preserve ownership and institutional meaning rather than biological or ethnic identity.

## 9. England Special-Case Boundary

England's culture development is a campaign-specific response to an unsettled historical transformation near the opening. It is not a required template for other factions. Other realms may express uniqueness through confederation, dynastic integration, frontier reform, royal centralization, religion, commerce, regional autonomy, or diplomatic coalition. King's Men use the England family decision without turning it into a universal system.

## 10. Shared Late-Capability Principle

Native Continuity, Anglo-Norman English development, and an integrated English outcome may reach comparable royal mounted capability through different institutions. The routes may share the King's Men card while differing in first emergence, personnel mix, horse depth, officers, equipment maturity, preparation time, political cost, visual profile, and recovery. Shared capability does not mean identical causality.

## 23. Warhorse and Remount Capacity

Warhorse capacity is distinct from personnel capacity. A rider may be available while suitable mounts are not. Primary mounts, remounts, young stock, recovering horses, captured horses, borrowed horses, and ordinary service animals must not be collapsed into one number. Horse recovery depends on breeding or acquisition, training, rider matching, fodder, health, handlers, routes, and time.

## 24. Officer and Training Capacity

Mounted officers, bannerets, file leaders, instructors, messengers, and household commanders are scarce shared inputs. Assigning them to King's Men can slow formation of Dismounted King's Men, Royal Knightly Retinue, or other royal bodies. One functioning training cadre cannot produce several mature elite units simultaneously merely because the treasury can pay.

## 25. Equipment Capacity

Lances, swords, shields, armor, helmets, saddles, tack, spare straps, horse protection, tents, tools, medical supplies, and repair labor are separately owned inputs. Concentrating the best equipment in King's Men leaves less for other royal and knightly formations. Captured or imported gear requires inspection, fit, repair, and training.

## 26. Rolling Elite Availability Model

The royal mounted source recovers independently of general knightly, noble household, native royal, professional infantry, civic, and regional sources. Recruiting King's Men may block another royal card while leaving Men-at-Arms, Feudal Knights, Saxon Foot Guard, a regional elite, or a lower-cost formation available. This supports varied army construction without one universal elite cooldown.

## 39. Relationship to Feudal Knights

Feudal Knights use the broader mature knightly ecosystem and provide wider shock frontage. King's Men are crown-owned, rarer, more politically concentrated, and tied to command protection. The player should prefer Feudal Knights for scalable heavy-cavalry mass and King's Men for a decisive royal reserve. Neither invalidates the other.

## 40. Relationship to Dismounted King’s Men

Dismounted King's Men remain a separate card candidate for roster readability, different battlefield scale, equipment, formation access, terrain, and recruitment timing. Both draw from one accountable royal ecosystem. The foot card must be designed independently and cannot be this profile with horses removed.

## 41. Relationship to English Foot Knights

English Foot Knights are the broader knightly foot family. Dismounted King's Men must prove crown ownership, stronger political concentration, a distinct crisis role, and a narrower shared royal pool or become a royal profile within English Foot Knights. Mounted King's Men compete with both for selected people and equipment.

## 42. Relationship to Native Royal Elites

Housecarls, Huscarl Guard, Saxon Foot Guard, Royal Thegn Spearmen, Mounted Thegns, and other continuity-path elites retain their own source and weapon identities. A Native Continuity route may transfer eligible people only through explicit owner processes. King's Men never represent automatic cultural replacement.

## 43. Relationship to Mounted Sergeants and Civic Cavalry

Mounted Sergeants provide trained nonknightly field service at broader scale. Merchant-supported cavalry militia provide city-bound escort, pursuit, and local response. King's Men have a far narrower crown-owned shock and protection role. Using them for routine scouting, raiding, or patrol is strategically wasteful.

## 46. Equipment Chronology

The Stainless Steel comparator presents a heavily armored later endpoint. Medieval War should stage the unit through strong mail and padding, improved mail and reinforcement, transitional plate, and mature plate only when chronology, production, wealth, import, repair, and campaign development support each state. Horse armor and enclosed helmets likewise require specific later profiles.

## 47. Route Equipment Profiles

Native Continuity may retain more native household shield and command cues while selectively adopting later armor and horse practice. Anglo-Norman English development may reach formed lance, shield, armor, and horse-equipment consistency earlier through knightly institutions. Integrated development may blend equipment while initially suffering fitting, command, and precedence complexity. These are profile differences, not hidden bonuses.

## 48. Primary Weapon and Lance Doctrine

The mounted expression uses a lance or mounted spear for controlled shock and a sword or approved sidearm after impact. Lance effectiveness depends on construction, presentation, rider skill, horse speed, formation, target angle, ground, and command. A high charge identity does not imply automatic penetration or armor defeat.

## 49. Armor and Shield Interaction

Protection depends on rider armor, helmet, padding, shield orientation, horse equipment, target weapon, impact angle, speed, and fatigue. Shields may be lost or become difficult after lance contact. Heavier armor improves survival but raises heat, weight, care, cost, and remount burdens.

## 50. Horse Equipment and Protection

Saddles, bridles, stirrups, reins, tack, cloth, partial protection, and later barding require manufacture, fit, repair, transport, handlers, and chronology. Horse protection can improve survivability while reducing endurance and increasing campaign burden. It should vary visibly across maturity states.

## 53. Formation Geometry

The formation needs enough frontage to avoid horse collision, enough depth to sustain local mass, and enough interval for steering and withdrawal. Excessive compression harms rear-rank control and disengagement. Excessive spacing weakens local shock and mutual support. Exact geometry belongs to shared formation systems and battle-size calibration.

## 54. Formation Transition Quality

Elite training improves deployment, wheel, alignment, acceleration, charge commitment, disengagement, and reform. It does not permit instant turns, perfect spacing, obstacle immunity, or immediate recovery. Mixed remounts, casualties, fatigue, and broken ground can reduce an elite body to fragmented files.

## 55. Approach-Lane Ownership

The unit does not own empty space. Friendly infantry, missiles, cavalry, routing troops, bodies, stakes, ditches, fences, terrain, and debris can narrow or close the lane. UI and AI should distinguish clear, narrowing, obstructed, and unsafe-withdrawal lanes. Orders issued without sufficient space should be degraded or refused according to shared command rules.

## 57. Impact Resolution

Impact should resolve local speed, mass, lance presentation, target angle, target cohesion, target reach, terrain, obstacles, shields, armor, and supporting pressure. Shock may disrupt morale and formation without physically passing through the target. Elite quality improves preparation and control, not certainty.

## 58. Penetration and Overextension

Penetration is optional and dangerous. A successful opening may expose the unit to deeper ranks, flank attack, lost standard contact, blocked withdrawal, and separation from support. The AI should not pursue penetration automatically. The player should see whether the formation can exit before committing.

## 59. Melee Transition

After impact, some riders lose or discard lances, draw swords, struggle through crowding, protect wounded companions, or seek a route out. Horse injuries and infantry reach rapidly change the fight. King's Men can survive contact better than lesser cavalry but should not become elite infantry while mounted in a stationary mass.

## 60. Disengagement Contract

Disengagement requires a viable direction, enough horse condition, officer control, surviving cohesion, space, and limited enemy fixation. A blocked or late withdrawal can turn a successful impact into catastrophic attrition. Disengagement quality is a principal elite advantage and must be tested separately from charge damage.

## 61. Reform Contract

Reform requires standards, officers, space, time, rider-horse control, casualty separation, and a defensible route. A fragmented formation may rally in stages. Reformed does not mean fully rested or rearmed. Lost lances, wounded horses, and missing officers remain visible readiness defects.

## 63. Failed Charge Consequences

A failed charge can kill or wound horses, unhorse riders, break lances, lose shields, separate files, block withdrawal, kill officers, panic remounts, expose the ruler, and damage royal prestige. Recovery cannot be represented by fatigue alone. The player should understand whether lane, terrain, missiles, target readiness, command, or overextension caused failure.

## 64. Battlefield Rhythm

Remain in royal reserve, preserve a clean approach and exit lane, allow infantry and missiles to create a decisive opportunity, align against an exposed target, commit under officer control, evaluate penetration, disengage before fixation, reform around standards, return to command protection if necessary, and pursue only a confirmed rout.

## 65. Royal Protection Assignment

Guarding the ruler, standard, heir, or command group is a real assignment that limits offensive freedom. Detaching King's Men for shock exposes command. Keeping them close may forfeit a distant opportunity. No passive royal aura, invulnerability, or automatic reinforcement is authorized.

## 66. Countercharge Decision Contract

Countercharge is appropriate when hostile cavalry is committed, King's Men are aligned and fresh, friendly flanks are secure, and impact prevents a worse strike against command or infantry. It is inappropriate when prepared infantry can absorb the attack, ground is poor, the royal body is understrength, or preserving the ruler is strategically superior.

## 67. Pursuit Discipline

Pursuit is valuable only after a genuine break. It risks separation, ambush, exhausted horses, captured officers, loss of standards, and abandonment of royal protection. Officers and formation memory improve recall without guaranteeing it. False rout and hidden reserve scenarios are mandatory AI tests.

## 68. Morale

Morale derives from loyalty, status, reputation, officers, equipment, royal purpose, and surviving standards. Disputed legitimacy, ruler loss, failed charge, officer casualties, encirclement, horse loss, exhaustion, and support collapse remain break risks. Rational surrender and ransom are permitted.

## 69. Cohesion and Veteran Memory

Familiar riders, trained horses, officers, standards, spacing, visibility, and repeated practice sustain cohesion. The formation remembers successful charges, failed commitments, rulers protected, standards lost, officers killed, and disciplined withdrawals through its people and history. Rebuilt headcount does not restore this memory automatically.

## 70. Command Casualty and Succession

Officer loss triggers local succession among recognized subordinates. A successor may preserve withdrawal and reform while reducing offensive control. Repeated losses may fragment the formation into files that seek the standard, protect the ruler, withdraw, or continue locally. One officer death cannot cause deterministic army collapse.

## 71. Fatigue and Horse Condition

Acceleration, armor, heat, mud, slopes, snow, long movement, water and fodder shortage, fear, wounds, and repeated charges degrade rider and horse. Elite training improves pacing, watering, rest, and withdrawal discipline but cannot erase physiology. Rider fatigue and horse condition must be separate readiness layers.

## 72. Combat Matchups

Favorable targets include exposed missile troops, disordered infantry, open flanks, routing elites, and poorly aligned cavalry. Unfavorable targets include prepared spears, pikes, bills, polearms, stakes, ditches, concentrated missiles, woods, marsh, walls, streets, bridges, and fresh supported countercharges. Even a favorable matchup fails without space and command.

## 73. Counterplay

Opponents can deny lanes, anchor flanks, maintain reach, use obstacles, shoot the approach, force poor ground, exhaust horses, threaten the ruler elsewhere, countercharge, bait pursuit, attack fodder and remount routes, and refuse decisive targets. Counterplay must remain visible enough for both player and AI to plan around it.

## 74. Combined Arms Role

Spears protect the reserve and receive enemy cavalry. Missiles disrupt targets and suppress hostile shooters. Scouts identify lanes and threats. Broader knights provide frontage. Infantry fixes enemies and holds objectives. King's Men protect command and perform the decisive commitment. An army dominated by King's Men is unsupported by recruitment and tactical logic.

## 77. Terrain Reconciliation

Open ground enables shock but also exposes the formation. Hills must distinguish direction and grade. Sparse woodland is lane-dependent rather than automatically favorable. Urban and wall environments require the separate dismounted card or another eligible foot assignment. Offensive and defensive crossings are separated because reserve action behind a choke differs from crossing under fire.

## 78. Weather

Rain reduces traction, lance control, visibility, and tack reliability. Fog complicates identification and command. Snow and ice increase falls and route risk. Heat increases water demand and armor burden. Storms disrupt assembly, signals, supply, and horse confidence. Weather modifies inputs rather than applying one magical unit penalty.

## 79. Ordered Seasonal Cycle

Winter stresses shelter, fodder, roads, and horse health. Spring adds mud and flooding. Summer offers the best general campaign window while increasing heat and water risk. Autumn links service to harvest, rain, route decline, and return obligations. Campaign Season advances one ordered step per strategic turn.

## 80. Supply and Logistics

King's Men require high-quality food, water, fodder, shelter, remounts, handlers, farriers, tack, hoof and veterinary care, lances, swords, armor repair, transport, servants or support train where appropriate, medicine, secure routes, records, and royal administration. Prestige raises expectation and grievance risk rather than removing logistics.

## 81. Horse and Rider Casualty Model

Rider and horse outcomes are separate. Either may be killed, wounded, captured, missing, separated, exhausted, or recovered. A surviving rider needs a compatible mount and equipment. A surviving horse needs a compatible rider, tack, handler care, and conditioning. Unhorsed riders do not automatically become Dismounted King's Men.

## 82. Casualty Quality

Royal officers, bannerets, veteran riders, standards, armor owners, trained warhorses, remount handlers, and trusted retainers have disproportionate value. Equal headcount loss can produce very different military and political damage. Battle results must identify the quality and ownership of losses.

## 83. Layered Recovery

Personnel survival, wounds, officers, horses, remounts, equipment, cohesion, household trust, legitimacy, training capacity, supply, and routes recover independently. A formation can return to nominal size while remaining horse-depleted, officer-depleted, equipment-mixed, or cohesion-recovering.

## 85. Prisoners, Ransom, and Diplomacy

Captured King's Men may affect ransom, exchange, court prestige, negotiations, succession, household relationships, and military availability through their proper systems. Status does not guarantee honorable treatment or release. Captured horses and equipment remain separately owned and recoverable.

## 86. Strategic Assignments

Potential assignments include royal escort, command protection, deterrence, rapid response, route security, diplomatic or ceremonial presence with political meaning, and decisive army reserve. Every assignment consumes availability. The same formation cannot guard court, escort the ruler, and charge on a distant battlefield simultaneously.

## 87. Campaign AI Recruitment

AI should form King's Men only when crown legitimacy, personnel, horses, officers, equipment, supply, politics, and a real mounted-elite need justify concentration. It should prefer broader knights or other units when they solve the task more economically. It must preserve critical royal-security capacity and avoid exhausting the family after one loss.

## 88. AI Strategic Conservation Thresholds

AI conservation increases when the ruler is exposed, the royal pool is depleted, remounts are scarce, the army is far from support, the enemy has prepared reach, withdrawal lanes are poor, or the battle is low value. It decreases when ruler rescue, a decisive objective, enemy elite collapse, or strategic breakthrough justifies risk. Exact thresholds remain tuning data.

## 89. Battle AI

AI should preserve lanes, coordinate with infantry and missiles, choose exposed high-value targets, countercharge selectively, abort unsafe approaches, disengage before fixation, reform behind support, recall pursuit, and return to command protection when conditions change. Difficulty improves timing and evaluation, not hidden stats.
