## 1. Copy-First Additive Authority Boundary

This profile is built after full review of the complete 1,171-line Saxon Huscarl Guard authority. It carries forward S05 ownership, household assignments, finite pools, guard politics, axe and shield states, formation transitions, terrain, weather, siege, supply, casualties, AI, presentation, legacy comparison, and nonmutation boundaries.

The parent remains a persistent High Era guard. Royal Huscarl Guard is a narrower late specialization, not a pasted replacement, automatic conversion, cosmetic rename, or statistical upgrade.

## 2. Current Verdict

Royal Huscarl Guard is the late royal specialization of a surviving Saxon Huscarl Guard institution. It concentrates the crown’s most trusted S05 veterans, officers, standards, equipment, privileges, and succession duties into an exceptionally scarce guard body.

Its battlefield identity is royal survival, command-post security, last-reserve crisis action, breach recovery, ruler extraction, controlled axe counterattack, and preservation of dynastic continuity.

## 3. One-Screen Designer Card

Class: late elite royal household axe guard. Availability: exceptional, ruler-bound, S05-limited, institution-dependent, politically visible, and extremely slow to rebuild.

Best use: ruler and heir protection, royal standard, final reserve, breach sealing or retaking, palace and hall defense, succession crisis, and extraction. Worst use: routine frontage, garrison filler, pursuit, unsupported missile approach, bridges, ladders, and attritional assault.

## 4. Historical and Counterfactual Boundary

Household warriors, royal guards, oath relationships, axes, swords, spears, shields, mail, standards, pay, estates, and personal lord protection are historically grounded foundations.

A continuing late Royal Huscarl Guard is an alternate-development synthesis. It is never presented as inevitable history, an unchanged 1062 survival, or a universal English regiment.

## 5. Distinct Unit Survival Test

The card survives only if direct royal ownership, succession duty, command continuity, royal extraction, privilege, equipment concentration, last-reserve doctrine, and political loss produce choices unavailable to Saxon Huscarl Guard.

If those differences collapse into better armor and morale, Royal Huscarl Guard becomes a royal owner profile or late equipment state instead of a separate unit.

## 6. Parent and Grandparent Persistence

Ordinary Housecarls may persist where qualifying S05 households survive. Saxon Huscarl Guard persists as the broader reformed royal and great-household guard.

Royal Huscarl Guard consumes a selected crown-centered subset. It does not erase or freely convert either parent layer.

## 7. Royal Owner Qualification

A late crown, palace, or high-tier building is insufficient. Qualification requires a recognized ruler or lawful regency, functioning royal household, surviving Saxon Huscarl institution, trusted veterans, officers, standards, privileges, equipment, records, pay, estates, workshops, routes, and political confidence.

Minority, captivity, deposition, disputed succession, bankruptcy, civil war, household purge, lost archives, unpaid service, or depleted veterans can restrict or fracture the guard.

## 8. Crown, Dynasty, Household, and Realm

The guard serves a person, dynasty, household, office, and realm in overlapping but nonidentical ways. A lawful ruler may lack household trust; a popular heir may lack formal command; a regent may control the palace without owning every oath.

These tensions affect muster, cohesion, officer succession, claimant splits, rescue priorities, and whether the guard defends a site, person, standard, treasury, archive, or withdrawal route.

## 9. Royal Privilege and Institutional Bargain

Service may be sustained through pay, estates, gifts, office, legal privilege, household rank, pensions, rations, equipment, family protection, honor, and access to the ruler.

Privileges create cost and political visibility. Attempts to revoke, confiscate, bypass, or favor rivals can reduce confidence, divide officers, or turn a loyal battlefield body into a succession risk.

## 10. S05 Source Ownership

S05 owns royal and qualifying great-household elites, trusted veterans, guard officers, prolonged-service tradition, and competing security assignments. Royal Huscarl Guard takes the narrowest crown-centered expression.

Population, castles, wealth, loyalty scores, and cultural percentages inform conditions but cannot create S05 directly.

## 11. No-Double-Counting Firewall

One guard cannot defend palace, escort heir, train replacements, garrison fortress, and fight in a distant army simultaneously. One officer, standard, axe, mail shirt, shield, privilege, room, cart, or obligation cannot serve several active formations.

Conversion transfers accountable people and property. It never leaves the full parent body behind while creating the successor.

## 12. Personnel Composition

The institution may include ruler guard, household captain, veteran axe files, sword-and-shield files, standard guards, messengers, gate officers, selected replacements, armorers, servants, medical attendants, and supply staff.

Not every courtier, noble, Housecarl, or armored warrior qualifies. Trust, service history, command acceptance, combat ability, equipment, health, and an available assignment remain necessary.

## 13. Veteran Selection and Successor Pipeline

Veteran service provides practical knowledge of ruler protection, formation recovery, missile approach, breach action, officer signals, and controlled withdrawal.

Replacement pipelines can train new members, but headcount returns before trust, reputation, file memory, or command depth. Family connections may influence entry without guaranteeing competence.

## 14. Assignments and Security Institution

Assignments include ruler person guard, heir or regent escort, royal standard, palace and hall defense, treasury and archive security, fortress guard, council security, royal progress, field reserve, training cadre, recovery, and active guard formation.

Concentrating one assignment creates explicit gaps elsewhere. The field unit represents real personnel drawn from a larger security institution, not free detachments.

## 15. Political and Security Opportunity Cost

Fielding the guard can expose the ruler’s residence, heir, spouse, treasury, records, hostages, councils, gates, and internal communications. Keeping it home sacrifices battlefield crisis power.

The player and AI must weigh visible threats, legitimacy, substitute guards, distance, army quality, rival claimants, and campaign purpose.

## 16. Recruitment Funnel

The process confirms royal authority, protects essential assignments, identifies veterans and compatible replacements, reviews oaths and privileges, inspects axes, shields, armor, standards, and clothing, assigns officers, integrates files, trains royal missions, and confirms pay, routes, supply, medicine, and return.

Failure can yield a reduced detachment, delayed formation, political block, equipment block, officer gap, ceremonial body without field readiness, or no unit.

## 17. Assembly and Readiness States

States include Unavailable, Proposed, Selecting, Negotiating, Equipping, Integrating, Ready, Reduced Detachment, Officer Depleted, Veteran Depleted, Equipment Depleted, Politically Blocked, Security Retained, Recovering, Declining, and Extinct.

The UI identifies the strongest blocker rather than hiding depth behind a generic cooldown.

## 18. Service Terms, Pay, and Demobilization

Royal household duty may be continuous while field service remains bounded by mission, security, health, supply, politics, and rotation. Gifts, arrears, victory rewards, confiscation, privilege, and pension influence future response.

Demobilization returns people, equipment, captives, wounded, standards, records, honors, grievances, and security duties to the correct owner. Poor accounting can damage the next muster.

## 19. Succession Crisis Architecture

On ruler death, capture, deposition, incapacity, or disputed inheritance, guards may follow the recognized heir, regent, household captain, claimant, office, oath, or immediate security duty.

Possible outcomes include cohesion, split, negotiation, sanctuary, extraction, palace defense, standard preservation, surrender, or defection. No universal scripted reaction is authorized.

## 20. Officer Structure and Succession of Command

Command includes ruler or delegate, guard captain, axe and shield file leaders, standard group, messengers, gate and reserve officers, and recognized replacements.

Officer loss first damages recognition, transition, target choice, relief, rally, and withdrawal. A legitimate successor may preserve action; disputed succession may divide it.

## 21. Standards, Signals, and Royal Recognition

Standards, banners, household devices, horns, calls, messengers, known posts, escort formations, and officer positions carry information.

Smoke, darkness, walls, crowds, noise, casualties, false messages, claimant symbols, and broken sightlines create uncertainty. Standards are practical and political anchors, not magical morale auras.

## 22. Equipment Ownership

Axes, swords, shields, spears, mail, helmets, late reinforced protection, clothing, standards, tents, carts, and stores may be personal, office-bound, household, estate, royal, inherited, gifted, captured, or recovered property.

Ownership controls return, recall, leverage, inheritance, dispute, repair, and confiscation. Royal issue does not erase older claims.

## 23. Primary Axe Profile

The two-handed Dane-axe tradition remains a principal identity where chronology, files, and equipment support it. Resolution requires approach, weapon space, committed strike, contact, recovery, mutual support, and reform.

Reach and impact pressure armor and shields comparatively, but no tag guarantees penetration. Crowding, missiles, fatigue, interrupted arcs, reach weapons, flank attack, and poor footing remain counters.

## 24. Sword-and-Shield Guard Profile

A committed sword-and-shield profile supports missile-exposed approach, bodyguard proximity, cramped halls, stairs, gates, streets, sustained defense, and Shieldwall anchoring.

The profile sacrifices two-handed rupture power and requires equipment, transition time, training, and file organization. It should be a prebattle or committed formation state before per-soldier switching.

## 25. Spears and Auxiliary Guard Weapons

Spears, javelins, hand axes, swords, and seaxes may remain carried or duty-specific equipment.

They do not convert the unit into Royal Thegn Spearmen, skirmishers, or a universal weapons toolbox. Every state needs explicit equipment, training, animation, and tactical value.

## 26. Armor and Late Equipment Progression

Mature mail, helmets, padding, shields, sidearms, reinforced pieces, gloves, footwear, and officer equipment may improve through chronology, craftsmen, wealth, contact, policy, and recovery.

Continuity lies in institution, ownership, axes, guard purpose, files, and command, not frozen 1062 clothing. Full plate and universal barding-like spectacle are not mandatory identities.

## 27. Equipment Degradation and Recovery

Axe heads chip, hafts split, swords bend, shields crack, mail tears, helmets dent, straps fail, clothing rots, standards burn, and carts break.

Recovery depends on battlefield control, search, ownership, transport, craftsmen, stores, routes, and time. New equipment does not restore dead veterans or lost trust.

## 28. Training Program

Training covers court and hall security, route march, guard spacing, axe arcs, shield states, reserve posture, breach containment, ruler extraction, standard recovery, controlled counterattack, relief, withdrawal, casualty handling, and mixed-unit coordination.

Buildings support training but do not substitute for veteran instructors, compatible files, equipment, time, pay, and real command relationships.

## 29. Formation Access

Strong candidates include March Column, Normal Line, Defensive Line, Reserve Posture, Shock Line, Assault Order, Close Order, Open Order, All-Round Guard, Breach Containment, and Orderly Withdrawal.

Shieldwall and advanced shieldwall depend on sword-and-shield equipment and mature practice. Spear Brace is not a default. Formation access never guarantees readiness or proficiency.

## 30. Formation Transition Quality

Every mode has entry, maintenance, turning, relief, recovery, and exit. Heavy equipment, long axes, casualties, rubble, fatigue, smoke, and officer loss slow transitions.

Elite veterans improve reliability, not instantaneous switching. Failed transitions create crossed arcs, gaps, collisions, exposed standards, or delayed counterattack.

## 31. Royal Guard Doctrine

The signature doctrine protects the ruler, heir, standard, command post, palace route, or decisive reserve and refuses low-value distraction.

Proximity strengthens purpose and communication but limits pursuit and tactical freedom. Detached service increases maneuver while weakening royal security elsewhere.

## 32. Last-Reserve Crisis Doctrine

Royal Huscarl Guard should normally remain preserved until a genuine threat endangers command continuity, a critical breach, an army flank, or the withdrawal route.

The AI must not hoard the guard through irreversible defeat, but routine offensive use erases its identity and wastes the source.

## 33. Controlled Counterattack

Counterattack requires a clear lane, fixed or disordered target, officer confirmation, usable weapon spacing, flank support, acceptable fatigue, and a stopping point.

Outcomes include clean counterstroke, partial commitment, blocked lane, fragmented contact, overextension, and successful containment without pursuit.

## 34. Breach Containment and Retaking

Containment seals a gate, breach, hall, stair, court, or command route after another body absorbs first contact. Retaking requires suppression, access, local superiority, and a viable reform space.

Fire, missiles, rubble, civilians, doors, smoke, bodies, and blocked exits remain severe. Royal guards are too valuable for unsupported first-wave assault.

## 35. Ruler Extraction and Sanctuary

Extraction opens and holds a route, identifies the ruler or heir, secures standards and messengers, covers movement, and prevents pursuit. Sanctuary may involve a hall, keep, ship, camp, religious site, or allied formation where owner systems permit.

Success can preserve dynastic continuity despite battlefield defeat. Failure may lead to capture, death, claimant advantage, or guard annihilation without automatically collapsing every army.

## 36. Standard Recovery and Command Continuity

A lost or endangered royal standard can trigger a bounded recovery effort when information, distance, enemy pressure, and owner duty make it rational.

Recovery may restore orientation and legitimacy; reckless attempts may destroy remaining officers. The unit must not receive omniscient knowledge of the standard’s location.

## 37. Morale, Loyalty, Privilege, and Duty

Morale is immediate battlefield confidence. Loyalty reflects relationships and oaths. Privilege shapes institutional bargains. Duty defines the protected person or office. Reputation records remembered conduct.

These layers can diverge. A loyal guard can break when exhausted and surrounded; a privileged guard can resist a claimant; a fearful guard can comply while remaining politically brittle.

## 38. Near-Death Resistance and Rational Surrender

Exceptional resistance may occur when ruler, heir, standard, escape route, or household survival creates a credible duty and cohesion remains.

Surrender may be rational to preserve high-status lives, facilitate ransom, avoid extermination, or follow a captured owner. Neither behavior is automatic.

## 39. Reputation and Battlefield Recognition

Ordered royal guards may steady nearby troops or pressure inexperienced enemies when identity is visible or credibly reported.

There is no terror aura. Veteran opponents, concealment, false standards, distance, tactical advantage, and prior failure reduce the effect.

## 40. Cohesion and File Integrity

Cohesion depends on trusted officers, shared service, stable files, weapon spacing, shields where active, known signals, protected flanks, and a clear duty.

Mixed replacements, smoke, rubble, pursuit, weapon-state changes, casualties, betrayal, and owner loss degrade file integrity. Recovery is strong but never instant.

## 41. Fatigue and Endurance

Mail, late reinforcement, two-handed strikes, climbing, heat, mud, repeated counterattacks, standing guard, sleepless crisis duty, and casualty evacuation accumulate fatigue.

Exhaustion reduces strike recovery, defense, transition quality, command response, and morale. Elite pacing cannot abolish physiology.

## 42. Casualty Quality and Institutional Loss

Track fit, wounded, killed, missing, captured, dispersed, and deserted members, plus officers, veterans, standard guards, instructors, messengers, and equipment owners.

Equal headcount losses have unequal effects. Losing the guard captain or veteran core may be worse than losing more replacement files.

## 43. Wounded, Captives, Ransom, and Hostages

Wounded return depends on evacuation, household medicine, shelter, supply, route, season, and security. High-status captives may enter ransom, exchange, hostage, claimant, intelligence, office, or inheritance systems.

Loyalty reduces casual defection without making every captive unbreakable. Rescue and ransom preserve veteran quality at financial and political cost.

## 44. Post-Battle Accounting

Account for people, officers, files, standards, axes, shields, armor, stores, carts, prisoners, recovered property, honors, arrears, privileges, grievances, and assignments.

Nominal headcount may recover before veteran quality, command, cohesion, equipment, guard institution, or household confidence.

## 45. Supply, Pay, and Maintenance

The guard consumes high-quality food, pay or patronage, shelter, mail repair, axe heads and hafts, shields, sidearms, clothing, footwear, medicine, transport, and secure routes.

Prestige raises expectations and political consequences rather than removing logistics. Out-of-supply states damage equipment, health, cohesion, loyalty, and operational duration.

## 46. War Weariness and Political Memory

Repeated low-value use, unpaid promises, abandonment, disastrous assaults, ruler loss, or catastrophic casualties reduce guard confidence and household willingness.

Meaningful defense, fair reward, recovered comrades, preserved standards, and successful extraction may strengthen the institution without creating infinite readiness.

## 47. Court Absence and Coup Risk

Sending the guard away removes trusted force from the palace, household, treasury, archives, heir, councils, and gates.

Risk depends on legitimacy, substitutes, intelligence, fortifications, rival claimants, distance, and current unrest. Absence does not automatically cause a coup, but it must be a real strategic trade.

## 48. Palace, Hall, Gate, and Inner-Court Defense

Royal Huscarl Guard excels in ruler-centered compressed defense where short routes, known posts, officers, reserves, and protected withdrawal matter.

Long axes may be awkward in doors, stairs, and dense interiors, making shield and sidearm states important. Fire, smoke, collapse, missile angles, and civilians remain counters.

## 49. Siege Defense

Strong roles include command-post guard, gate counterattack, inner court, breach retake, tower or stair reinforcement, standard protection, and evacuation route.

The guard should not hold every wall section or replace ordinary garrisons. Its scarcity demands concentration at politically and tactically decisive sites.

## 50. Siege Offense

Strong roles begin after access is secured: breach exploitation, gatehouse seizure, command-point capture, specialist protection, and resistance to counterattack.

Poor roles include ladders, unsupported escalade, unsuppressed breach entry, engineering labor, and repeated attritional assault.

## 51. Field Battle and Combined Arms

Spears receive cavalry, missiles suppress shooters, light troops screen, ordinary Housecarls and Thegns broaden elite frontage, and cavalry protects flanks or pursuit.

Royal Huscarl Guard protects command, contains crisis, and delivers a decisive local counterattack inside a diverse army rather than an elite-only doom stack.

## 52. Terrain Matrix Control

Terrain values remain close to Saxon Huscarl Guard because the successor shares the same personnel lineage, axes, armor, and physical constraints.

Differences arise from owner concentration, equipment maintenance, command, security missions, and recovery rather than invented environmental immunity.

## 53. Weather and Seasonal Cycle

Rain worsens grip, footing, mail, shields, and routes. Fog weakens identity and signals. Snow and ice burden traction and shelter. Heat accelerates fatigue and water use. Storms interrupt communication and crossings.

Winter emphasizes court security and stores; spring brings mud; summer opens campaigning but stresses heat and distant duty; autumn combines harvest, rain, routes, and return expectations.

## 86. Royal Guard Detachment Scale and Concentration
