## 3. One-Screen Designer Card

Class: late elite royal S04 sword-and-shield guard. Availability: very scarce, owner-bound, institution-dependent, politically conditioned, and slow to restore.

## 32. Royal Shieldwall Doctrine

The guard’s Shieldwall is designed for politically decisive short frontage, command protection, and controlled relief rather than cheap mass coverage.

It should enter with reserve depth, maintain recognized files, rotate damaged shields where possible, and preserve an exit or counterattack lane. Flanking, fire, missiles, fatigue, and broken ground can still defeat it.

## 33. Frontage Relief Doctrine

Relief requires a receiving formation, protected lane, reserve depth, signals, officers, enough space, and a clear responsibility transfer.

Outcomes include clean relief, partial overlap, delayed entry, blocked lane, exposed gap, or collapse during transition. Relief is not a hidden morale refill.

## 34. Royal Guard and Command-Post Doctrine

The unit protects a ruler, heir, standard, household, office group, council, palace route, or decisive command sector while refusing low-value distraction.

Proximity improves purpose and communication but limits pursuit and freedom. Detached use increases utility while weakening royal and office security elsewhere.

## 35. Controlled Counterattack Doctrine

A counterattack follows enemy disorder, fatigue, failed assault, compressed frontage, exposed flank, or a prepared relief opportunity.

The guard advances only far enough to restore the protected position, secure a route, rescue a person, or stabilize the front. Deep pursuit erases the doctrine.

## 36. Breach Containment and Retaking

Containment holds a gate, breach, stair, hall, court, tower access, or command route after another formation absorbs initial contact. Retaking requires suppression, access, local superiority, officers, and reform space.

Fire, smoke, rubble, bodies, doors, civilians, overhead missiles, and blocked exits remain dangerous. The guard is not disposable first-wave assault infantry.

## 37. Ruler, Heir, and Office Extraction

Extraction identifies the protected person, opens a shielded lane, secures standards and messengers, covers movement, and prevents local pursuit.

Success may preserve government despite battlefield defeat. Failure may cause capture, death, office collapse, claimant advantage, or guard loss, but never automatic universal army collapse.

## 38. Standard and Archive Recovery

## 85. Guard Detachment Scale and Royal Concentration

Royal Saxon Guard may appear as a full field formation, reduced ruler escort, council or office guard, gate relief body, procession security detachment, or emergency household concentration.
