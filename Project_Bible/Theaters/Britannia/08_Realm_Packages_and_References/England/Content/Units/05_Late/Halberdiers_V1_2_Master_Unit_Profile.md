## 3. Designer One-Screen Summary

**Source:** selected civic guards, paid professionals, household specialists, or external contractors with verified combined-head polearms, officers, sidearms, armor, workshops, storage, training, and a bounded owner.  
**Role:** guard frontage, breach stabilization, pike-flank support, siege-work protection, and controlled counterattack.  
**Strengths:** flexible thrust, cut, hook, and leverage actions at closer range than pikes; strong owner-specific guard use; useful pressure against committed protected targets.  
**Weaknesses:** uncertain English domestic chronology, scarce specialists, missiles, flanks, entanglement, compressed reach, costly weapons and workshops, and severe overlap risk.  
**Profiles:** experimental civic files, professional or contract company, and exceptional city body.  
**Boundary:** domestic England-wide recruitment remains blocked until historical, owner, and cross-realm gates pass.

## 4. Positive Player Identity

Halberdiers are selected later polearm infantry trained around combined-head weapons for a narrow guard and counterattack role. Halberdiers can stabilize a breach, protect a gate or important frontage, assist a pike formation at closer range, and strike committed protected opponents. Halberdiers are not stronger Billmen, shorter pikes, armored Men-at-Arms, or foot knights with a different weapon.

## 5. Historical and Naming Boundary

The legacy card explicitly describes English guild-funded urban Halberdiers. The current external evidence supports a later European halberd tradition but does not by itself establish the exact English formation, owner, or High Era chronology. “Halberdiers” remains the readable working name. Final terminology may identify a related combined-head polearm, imported company, or owner-specific guard. The complete gameplay architecture survives a later historically grounded rename.

## 6. Opening and Chronology Firewall

No Halberdier formation is available in 1062. Contact with later European weapons or troops may generate Encountered, Studied, or Contract Access states. Domestic recruitment requires stronger proof of English adoption, specialist people, workshops, officers, and owners. Date, culture, guild, city level, or treasury value cannot complete the gate alone.

## 7. Single-Family Structure

Halberd Militia maps to Experimental Civic Halberd Files. Halberd Men at Arms maps to Professional or Contract Halberd Company. City Halberdiers maps to Exceptional City Halberdiers. These profiles share the same family rules and can qualify or downgrade dynamically. None receives a free personnel or equipment pool. This preserves the visual-reference vocabulary while preventing a shallow three-card ladder.

## 8. England and Scotland Firewall

The England and Scotland legacy Halberdier cards are too similar to justify parallel palette-and-statistic units. V1.2 requires future cross-realm comparison of chronology, weapon form, owner, people, workshops, tactical use, and opportunity cost. If Scotland has the stronger domestic tradition, England may retain contract access or limited owner-specific adoption. If both pass, their institutions and battlefield identities must differ materially.

## 9. Relationship to Billmen

Billmen remain the broad mature English bill formation for general line support, flexible pressure, and controlled counterattack. Halberdiers are later, scarcer, more owner-specific, more guard- and breach-centered, and more dependent on verified combined-head weapons and sidearms. If weapon geometry, owner, service, and AI mission do not differ, V1.2 requires integration into Billmen rather than a duplicate card.

## 10. Relationship to Heavy Billmen

Heavy Billmen retain selected veteran sources, stronger protection, disciplined reserve, and sustained pressure. Halberdiers may respond more flexibly near breaches and pike flanks but should not equal Heavy Billmen in prolonged hard contact. Halberdiers have greater entanglement and owner-dependence, while Heavy Billmen have greater personnel, armor, and recovery cost.

## 11. Relationship to Burgh Pikemen

Burgh Pikemen control deep frontal reach and area denial. Halberdiers use shorter combined-head weapons, require less depth, turn more readily, and operate closer to breaches or supporting infantry. Halberdiers may protect pike flanks or engage enemies inside pike points where joint drill exists. They do not inherit pike-wall behavior or frontal charge immunity.

## 12. Relationship to Men-at-Arms

Men-at-Arms remain professional armored melee infantry with broader weapon and close-combat identity. A Halberdier professional profile must prove selected combined-head specialists, guard or breach service, and a bounded owner or contract. Armor and pay alone do not create Halberdiers. Shared professionals, armor, sidearms, and officers require exclusive assignment.

## 13. Relationship to City Guard

City Guard provide standing urban security, patrol, command continuity, and immediate response. Halberdiers provide a specialist polearm frontage when a gate, arsenal, institution, breach, or important person requires it. Experimental Civic Files and Exceptional City Halberdiers remove people from ordinary City Guard or other verified pools. Routine city status does not create specialists.

## 14. Relationship to Foot Knights and Royal Poleaxe Infantry

English Foot Knights and Dismounted King’s Men retain knightly, royal, household, ransom, status, and poleaxe identities. Halberdiers do not inherit those features through armor or weapon resemblance. A household Halberdier profile must use nonknightly specialists unless an explicit owner-authorized source proves otherwise, and every transfer must reduce the donor pool.

## 21. Armor Shields and Sidearms

The legacy mail and no-shield direction remains evidence. Actual protection varies by owner, date, fit, and competition. Two-handed use restricts shield employment but does not guarantee heavy armor. Sidearms are required for compressed contact, interiors, broken shafts, and pursuit, but must be issued and trained. Experimental files show mixed protection; professional and city profiles may improve consistency without becoming Heavy Billmen or Foot Knights.

## 22. Workshop Storage and Transport Contract

Readiness needs compatible heads, sound shafts, fittings, maintenance tools, armor repair, sidearms, dry storage, issue records, qualified craftspeople, replacement capacity, and transport. Imported contract weapons may create repair incompatibility. A guild or building supplies capacity only where actual workshops, people, materials, and ownership exist. A broken supply chain can downgrade a profile without deleting the unit.

## 23. Finite Pools and No Double Counting

One person cannot simultaneously serve as Halberdier, Billman, Heavy Billman, Burgh Pikeman, City Guard, Bill Militia, Men-at-Arms, Foot Knight, or household guard. One weapon, armor set, sidearm, officer, workshop slot, store, contract, drill location, or cart cannot support several active bodies. Profile qualification rearranges existing resources and never creates new ones.

## 24. Recruitment Contract

## 39. Guard Frontage Doctrine

Halberdiers protect a selected gate, arsenal, command post, civic building, siege work, bridge exit, broad street, or important person through point threat and flexible close actions. Guard frontage needs weapon recovery room, clear authority, flank support, sidearms, and relief routes. Routine patrol and generalized settlement security remain City Guard work.

## 40. Breach Stabilization Doctrine

Halberdiers may remain behind a breach or gate and counterattack after attackers become compressed or disordered. The formation should not lead unsupported ladder or rubble assaults. Strong use needs prepared ground, missile or shield support, clear lanes, and an exit. Fire, high ground, missiles, flank routes, and congestion can turn the position into a trap.

## 41. Pike-Flank Support Doctrine

Where evidence and joint training permit, Halberdiers protect the flanks or rear of a pike-like body, attack opponents who enter inside pike points, and assist turning or withdrawal. Separate people, command compatibility, and owner cooperation are required. Mixed deployment can fail through interference, entanglement, and conflicting orders.

## 42. Controlled Counterattack Doctrine

The body counterattacks a committed enemy from a protected guard or reserve position, exploits a stalled assault, or restores a breach. It should preserve formation, avoid long pursuit, and return to the assigned frontage. Timing, officers, working distance, target disorder, support, and withdrawal determine success. The legacy charge value remains evidence only.

## 43. Thrust Cut Hook and Leverage

Thrusting controls entry and targets gaps. Cutting pressures exposed targets and equipment where space exists. Hooking attempts to disturb shields, weapons, straps, limbs, reins, or exposed gear. Leverage may widen a local gap. Exact actions depend on weapon form. Failed actions can entangle the weapon, overextend the user, expose a flank, or surrender working distance.

## 44. Anti-Cavalry Boundary

Halberdiers threaten cavalry mainly in guarded, confined, stalled, or supported contact. Shorter reach provides less frontal denial than pikes but may permit more flexible action after contact closes. Fresh cavalry with space, missile preparation, flank access, or repeated charges remains dangerous. Hooks do not guarantee rider removal.

## 45. Anti-Protection Boundary

Points, edges, hooks, mass, and leverage can pressure protected opponents, but results depend on weapon construction, working range, angle, target movement, armor and shield geometry, hit location, user skill, fatigue, officers, and support. The legacy armor-piercing tag remains target-selection evidence, not an armor-ignoring rule.

## 46. Inside-Reach and Entanglement Response

When enemies cross the weapon head, files may shorten grip where plausible, draw sidearms, rotate with relief personnel, receive shield-bearing support, or withdraw. These transitions consume time and cohesion. Combined heads can catch friendly or enemy equipment. Narrow geometry, crowds, rubble, and blocked exits increase entanglement and collapse risk.

## 47. Battlefield Rhythm

Approach under protection, assume a bounded guard or reserve frontage, maintain point threat, use controlled combined-head actions, preserve flanks and rear, counterattack only after enemy commitment, stabilize rather than pursue, rotate under fatigue, and withdraw before entanglement or compression destroys the specialist formation. This rhythm separates Halberdiers from broad Billmen and deep pikes.

## 48. Combined Arms

Pikes provide deep frontal denial. Billmen provide broad flexible pressure. Sword-and-shield troops protect cramped spaces and relieve compressed files. Missiles disrupt threats. Heavy Billmen deliver sustained reserve pressure. Cavalry exploits enemies fixed at the frontage. Halberdiers add a narrower guard, breach, pike-support, and counterattack layer and should fail when isolated.

## 50. Counterplay

Opponents can use missiles, artillery, longer weapons, shielded advance, feints, flanking, rear attack, compressed assault, smoke, fire, officer targeting, fatigue, and terrain. Strategically, opponents can disrupt imported weapons, contractors, workshops, owner trust, civic finance, and specialist replacement. A bypassed guard frontage loses value even if the unit survives.

## 51. Morale Cohesion Fatigue and Command

Confidence comes from selected people, clear guard purpose, trusted officers, reliable weapons, sidearms, owner support, prepared geometry, and relief. Failed hooks, missile losses, contract disputes, officer casualties, pike separation, flank penetration, entanglement, and blocked withdrawal degrade cohesion. Repeated two-handed actions, armor, heat, rubble, and static guard duty create fatigue.

## 58. Campaign AI Recruitment

Production AI recruitment remains blocked until history and ownership pass. In a sandbox, AI selects the correct profile by source and mission. Experimental civic files appear only for qualified local trials. Professional or contract companies appear for paid guard, siege, or field needs. Exceptional City Halberdiers appear only when local security remains covered. AI refuses when bills, pikes, City Guard, or Men-at-Arms solve the mission better.

## 59. Battle AI

AI preserves the guard mission, selects broad-enough geometry, protects flanks, coordinates pikes and shield troops, times controlled counterattacks, avoids extended pursuit, responds to entanglement and inside-reach pressure, and withdraws before the frontage becomes a trap. Profile maturity improves reliability but does not change the unit into general-purpose elite infantry.
