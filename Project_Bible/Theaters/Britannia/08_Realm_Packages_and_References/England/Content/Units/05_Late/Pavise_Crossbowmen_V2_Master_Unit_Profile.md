## 3. Designer One-Screen Summary

**Core identity:** a professional crossbow company organized to deploy large pavises and sustain deliberate fire from prepared positions.  
**Battle role:** protected reload, lane denial, siege support, defense of gates and crossings, suppression of protected targets, and support of infantry holding ground.  
**Strategic cost:** specialist crews, assistants, crossbows, spanning devices, bolt lots, pavises, carts, workshops, pay, transport, and prolonged preparation.  
**Strengths:** endurance under frontal missile pressure, deliberate fire, defensive staying power, and strong prepared-position utility.  
**Weaknesses:** slow deployment and displacement, vulnerable flanks and rear, congestion, high-angle fire, fire and smoke, equipment loss, ammunition dependence, cavalry pressure, and poor emergency melee.  
**Not the role:** mobile skirmisher, universal anti-armor answer, free wall bonus, or automatic upgrade from every Crossbow Sergeant.

## 5. Historical and Design Boundary

The broad functions of professional crossbow service, large protective shields, prepared firing positions, town and siege support, assistants, workshops, and transported equipment are retained as the historical foundation. One uniform English national company, exact crew ratios, fixed pavise dimensions, universal mail, and a single standardized drill remain gameplay abstractions or unresolved. Final naming, chronology, institutional examples, and English distribution require focused review. Continental examples may inform the design but do not automatically create domestic English capacity.

## 6. Progression Placement

Crossbow Sergeants remain the mobile professional crossbow line. Pavise Crossbowmen are a narrower parallel development requiring deeper equipment and deployment institutions. Existing Sergeants may supply qualified individuals only through owner-authorized reassignment. The transition consumes personnel and equipment and is not a free upgrade. Both cards may coexist because they solve different tactical and campaign problems.

## 10. Personnel and Crew Model

The formation may include shooters, shield assistants, file leaders, captains, range observers, bolt handlers, crossbow maintainers, pavise repairers, cart personnel, messengers, and medical support. Assistants are not assumed to exist at one universal ratio. Some pavises may be managed by shooters, some by dedicated assistants, and some through mixed files. The active configuration depends on pavise weight, spanning method, terrain, transport, maturity, and tactical assignment.

## 12. Finite Pool and No-Double-Counting Firewall

One person cannot simultaneously act as shooter, assistant, garrison specialist, siege crew, workshop labor, trainer, or another active unit assignment unless the shared assignment owner explicitly releases and transfers that person. One crossbow, spanning device, bolt lot, pavise, prop, cart, mail shirt, workshop slot, or contract cannot support several active formations. Rendered soldiers and shields never create free campaign assets.

## 13. Recruitment Contract

Recruitment requires a lawful owner, eligible personnel, officers, maintained crossbows, compatible spanning equipment, bolt stocks, pavises, props, tools, workshops, armor where available, training grounds, pay or lawful service, carts or other transport where required, routes, stores, quarters, medicine, and a viable assignment. Failure produces delay, reduced frontage, fewer deployed pavises, local-only service, lower equipment grade, contract-only access, or an explicit block.

Confirm the institution and assignment, protect essential garrison and workshop duties, select shooters and assistants, allocate officers, inspect crossbows and spanning devices, match bolt lots, inspect pavises and props, allocate carts and tools, fit protective equipment, drill deployment and fire control, verify infantry support and withdrawal routes, then authorize readiness. ## 22. Penetration and Damage Boundary

No bolt receives guaranteed armor penetration. Resolution considers crossbow capability, bolt construction, range, velocity, impact angle, target motion, armor material and geometry, shield or pavise interception, local hit location, and projectile condition. The player-facing anti-protected-target role expresses deliberate pressure and threat selection, not a universal armor-deletion bonus.

## 23. Protected Reload Doctrine

The company fires from a prepared frontage, withdraws behind cover or lowers exposure during spanning and loading, returns to an aiming state, and releases under officer control. Protection is directional and state-dependent. A shooter exposed above, beside, behind, or between pavises remains vulnerable. Poor spacing, broken props, uneven soil, fatigue, panic, and assistant loss create gaps.

## 24. Deployment Procedure

Select ground, inspect slope and soil, secure the frontage, unload or unsling pavises, assign shields and crews, orient toward the expected threat, plant or prop the shields, establish overlap or controlled gaps, preserve passages, align files, place ammunition and tools, confirm infantry support, mark withdrawal lanes, and verify fire control. Deployment consumes time and can be interrupted.

## 25. Coverage Geometry

Coverage depends on shield dimensions, orientation, spacing, ground height, shooter posture, target elevation, projectile path, formation depth, gaps, damage, and current action. Pavises protect sectors rather than granting a flat defensive statistic. High-angle fire, enfilade, rear attack, elevated attackers, and local breaches can bypass or reduce the screen.

## 26. Fire Control

Officers manage target priority, range confidence, shot timing, ammunition rates, local volleys, independent fire, cease-fire, repositioning, and withdrawal. The formation favors deliberate target selection over fictional perfect synchronization. Suppression and threat can matter even where casualties are limited. Friendly troops, pavises, walls, smoke, and terrain may block a shot.

## 28. Mobility, Collapse, and Displacement

The unit moves slowly when carrying shields and equipment and more efficiently when using carts and secure routes. A prepared line cannot redeploy instantly. Collapse requires orders, crew access, recovery of bolts and tools, release of props, lifting or loading, and preservation of lanes. Under pressure, the player or AI may abandon damaged or heavy pavises to save personnel, accepting equipment and political cost.

## 29. Battlefield Rhythm

Advance under protection, select a defensible firing site, deploy with supporting infantry, establish deliberate fire, manage ammunition and exposure, refuse unsupported pursuit, and displace before flanking or melee pressure closes the escape route. The core skill is preserving a useful protected position without becoming trapped by the equipment that enables it.

## 30. Combined Arms

Spears or other holding infantry protect flanks and stop cavalry. Engineers and labor assist prepared sites and siege movement where separately available. Scouts and officers provide target information. Other missile troops can exploit different trajectories and firing rhythms. Pavise Crossbowmen do not substitute for a melee line, mobile skirmishers, artillery, or fortification crews.

## 31. Counterplay

Opponents can flank or enfilade the screen, use higher ground or high-angle missiles, send infantry through gaps, force displacement, attack assistants, ignite pavises or carts where conditions permit, use smoke, exploit night or poor visibility, deny stable soil, cut ammunition routes, threaten the rear, or attack before deployment. Heavy cover improves survival only while the position remains coherent and relevant.

## 32. Melee Emergency Defense

Sidearms, shield mass, discipline, and nearby support may permit brief local resistance, but the company is not a melee specialist. Deployed pavises can obstruct both attacker and defender. Collapse under direct pressure risks congestion, lost crossbows, abandoned shields, and blocked retreat. Successful emergency defense should depend on support and escape rather than hidden elite melee statistics.

## 33. Morale, Cohesion, and Command

Morale derives from trained crews, reliable officers, working equipment, protected frontage, ammunition confidence, prepared withdrawal, supporting infantry, pay, and institutional reputation. Flanking, burning shields, assistant loss, mechanism failures, ammunition exhaustion, breached cover, cart loss, and blocked retreat can cause rapid degradation. Elite labeling grants no immunity.

## 34. Fatigue and Labor

Carrying pavises, unloading carts, planting props, spanning crossbows, moving bolts, repairing equipment, standing under fire, and collapsing a position create separate labor burdens. Mud, heat, cold, steep slopes, hunger, wet strings or mechanisms where applicable, and repeated displacement compound fatigue. Assistants reduce some burdens but consume finite personnel and supply.

## 35. Siege Defense

Strong uses include prepared courtyards, gates, wall walks with sufficient space, towers, breaches held from behind, inner defenses, and controlled firing lanes. Wall use is not automatically ideal: parapets may already provide cover, heavy pavises consume space, wind and elevation matter, and stairs or narrow walks complicate transport. The company should deploy where the shield system adds real value.

## 36. Siege Offense

The company can support approaches, trenches, siege works, gate pressure, breached perimeters, and suppression of defenders from prepared ground. Offensive deployment requires transport, labor, secure setup time, ammunition access, and protection from sorties. Ladders, narrow breaches, unstable rubble, and rapid assaults are poor uses.

## 37. Urban and Site Defense

Squares, yards, broad streets, gates, market approaches, and prepared barricades may support useful fronts. Narrow alleys, crowds, fire, rubble, sharp corners, and blocked withdrawal create severe risks. Urban familiarity improves site selection and logistics, not projectile power or automatic defense.

## 38. Field Role

In field battles the unit anchors a deliberate missile position, supports a holding line, denies a lane or crossing, and pressures protected targets. The unit performs poorly when forced into continuous skirmishing, long pursuit, rapid flank rotation, or unsupported advance. Open ground helps line of sight but exposes the flanks and increases the need for escort.

## 39. Crossings and Chokepoints

On defense, secure banks and bridge exits can provide valuable lanes if the company has room to deploy behind a holding force. On offense, water, mud, congestion, carts, exposed approaches, and restricted frontage make deployment difficult. A choke point is beneficial only when the company controls the approach and retains ammunition and withdrawal access.

## 43. Recovery and Abandonment

Battlefield control, transport, time, weather, enemy pressure, and owner claims determine whether crossbows, spanning devices, bolts, pavises, props, tools, carts, armor, and records are recovered. Heavy shields may be abandoned to save crews. Captured equipment can be reused only after compatibility, repair, ownership, and training checks. Money cannot instantly restore trained files or matched equipment.

## 44. Experience and Retraining

Experience improves ground selection, crew coordination, deployment speed, exposure discipline, target judgment, ammunition control, equipment care, collapse timing, and withdrawal. Replacement shooters, assistants, or equipment may reduce achieved proficiency until retraining occurs. Veterans do not bypass physical deployment or supply requirements.

## 47. Campaign AI

AI recruits only when the company has a distinct source, sufficient equipment, a real defensive or siege need, transport, ammunition, and supporting infantry. AI avoids recruitment when Crossbow Sergeants provide better mobility, when pavise or assistant pools are depleted, when campaign routes are unsuitable, or when the army cannot protect a prepared line.

## 48. Battle AI

AI selects a defensible frontage, preserves flanks, deploys before effective contact, protects carts and ammunition, controls fire against valuable or protected targets, refuses unsupported advance, monitors gaps and elevated threats, and begins displacement before encirclement. AI may abandon equipment when crew survival and withdrawal outweigh recovery value.
