## 1. Controlling Verdict

Heavy Bill Militia do not pass as a separate recruitable unit at this gate. The concept is preserved as an exceptional-owner equipment and readiness profile inside Bill Militia. A future exact source or stronger historical and institutional evidence may reopen the card, but the current candidate cannot demonstrate a personnel source, service cycle, AI mission, or battlefield rhythm sufficiently distinct from Bill Militia, Heavy Billmen, and City Guard. Creating a full card now would produce a statistics-and-armor tier rather than a new strategic decision.

## 2. Why This File Comes Next

The remaining-candidate inventory required the Heavy Bill Militia collision test only after completion of Bill Militia. That dependency is now satisfied. This gate performs the required comparison before any new profile is drafted. It follows the project rule that attractive visual-reference names are candidates, not automatic roster admissions.

## 3. Evidence Boundary

The current enterprise search surfaced England roster and recruitment authorities, professional and civic comparators, and the broader Heavy Bill Militia candidate context. No exact dedicated Heavy Bill Militia source file was surfaced. The gate therefore does not invent legacy values, buildings, equipment, chronology, events, or headcounts. The decision is based on the candidate name and its collision with completed unit identities, not an unsupported historical reconstruction.

Those features belong to different personnel and institutional sources.

## 8. Strongest Preserved Identity

The useful concept is an **Exceptional Armored Bill Militia** profile. It represents a wealthy or strategically important locality that can equip part of its ordinary Bill Militia more consistently with helmets, padding, selected mail, better bills, dependable sidearms, stronger workshop priority, and additional officers. The formation remains militia: locally anchored, politically negotiated, seasonally constrained, slower to mobilize, and expensive to detach from home security.

## 10. Owner Gate

The profile requires an exceptional civic, regional, lordly, or institutional owner with demonstrably deeper armor, weapon, workshop, storage, officer, and financial capacity than an ordinary Bill Militia owner. Wealth alone is insufficient. The owner must also preserve local watch, labor, and emergency coverage while concentrating the heavier contingent.

## 11. Personnel Rule

The profile draws from the existing Bill Militia personnel pool. It does not create new people. Selection may prioritize veterans, wealthier equipage holders, paid local specialists, or personnel with armor experience, but every selected person is removed from ordinary Bill Militia or another verified local assignment. The profile cannot recruit professional Heavy Billmen through a militia interface.

## 12. Equipment Rule

The profile may improve consistency of bills, helmets, padding, selected mail, sidearms, and fittings. It may receive workshop and storage priority. It does not automatically receive partial plate, elite weapons, or uniform heavy armor. Equipment remains owner-, date-, fit-, and supply-dependent. The same armor and bill instances cannot support City Guard, Men-at-Arms, Billmen, or Heavy Billmen simultaneously.

## 13. Service-Cycle Firewall

The formation remains subject to local militia obligations, geographic restrictions, seasonal labor pressure, negotiated field service, and demobilization. If service becomes standing, professional, prolonged, and broadly deployable, the profile has crossed into Billmen, Heavy Billmen, or City Guard territory and must be reassigned rather than retaining the militia label.

## 14. Battlefield Role

The profile provides a more resilient local reserve than ordinary Bill Militia. It can reinforce a stressed gate, survive limited missile or melee pressure better, make a guarded counterattack, recover a breached local frontage, and protect withdrawal. It does not gain Heavy Billmen sustained-pressure doctrine. It should remain less cohesive, less enduring, less deployable, and less officer-rich than professional heavy formations.

## 15. Battlefield Failure States

The heavier profile still fails through flanking, prolonged missiles, pike reach, compression, inside-reach fighting, officer loss, mixed equipment, blocked withdrawal, extended service, and local political breakdown. Armor can delay failure while increasing fatigue, heat, cost, repair demand, and withdrawal burden. A premium local formation may become a costly trap if committed like professional Heavy Billmen.

## 16. AI Use Contract

AI may choose the profile when an exceptional locality faces a high-value defensive threat and ordinary Bill Militia need greater endurance. AI must not recruit a separate unit from a new pool. AI should avoid the profile when equipment would better support City Guard or professional infantry, when local labor and security are already strained, or when distant campaign service is expected.

## 17. Campaign Opportunity Cost

The profile consumes better armor, helmets, sidearms, bills, officers, workshop priority, storage, pay, and politically reliable militia personnel. These resources are unavailable to ordinary Bill Militia, City Guard, Billmen, Men-at-Arms, and household formations. Concentrating the profile may reduce the number of ordinary local defenders even as it improves one contingent.

## 20. Upgrade and Downgrade Logic

The profile is not a permanent linear upgrade. A base Bill Militia contingent may qualify when the owner concentrates sufficient people, equipment, officers, and support. The contingent downgrades when armor is lost, workshops fail, officers disappear, service terms expire, or local needs force redistribution. Surviving veterans may later transfer into Billmen or Heavy Billmen only through their separate recruitment gates.

## 21. UI Contract

The recruitment and unit UI should display the parent Bill Militia body, selected personnel, armor and bill allocation, officers, workshop priority, local-security impact, service limits, profile readiness, and downgrade conditions. “Heavy Bill Militia” may appear as a familiar alias or visual-reference label, but the UI should make clear that the capability is an exceptional profile rather than a free-standing national pool.

## 22. Visual Identity

The profile should look like selected Bill Militia with greater equipment consistency, stronger helmets and padding, some fitted mail where supported, better-maintained bills, clearer officers, and dependable sidearms. The visual must remain locally varied and recognizably militia. It must not become a palette copy of Heavy Billmen, City Guard, Men-at-Arms, or Foot Knights.

## 23. Animation and Audio Boundary

The profile reuses the Bill Militia action family with proficiency and equipment-state differences. Additional presentation may show more confident guard posture, improved recovery, armor movement, sidearm access, officer signals, and organized downgrade or demobilization. No unique animation should imply automatic penetration or charge stopping. Shared bill, formation, armor, fatigue, and collision systems remain authoritative.
