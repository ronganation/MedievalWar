## 1. Purpose and Modern Medieval II Principle

This gate separates place-owned regional units from England-owned cultural, political, professional, household, and royal axe formations. It preserves the Medieval II and Stainless Steel strength of discovering units through conquered geography while modernizing access through finite people, cooperation, officers, equipment, buildings, service, recovery, and AI. A settlement can expose a regional recruitment opportunity without becoming an unlimited unit dispenser.

## 4. Regional Recruitment Contract

The shared regional framework states that geographically bounded military communities and institutions may remain distinct under independence, alliance, vassalage, occupation, conquest, or integration, and that possession alone does not create cooperation or service. This gate applies the same rule to axe infantry. Every regional card must pass territorial access, controller relationship, and infrastructure-readiness gates before recruitment.

## 7. Wessex Axemen Decision

Wessex Axemen remain a separate regional unit. Their independent value comes from a bounded Wessex source, controller-sensitive access, local service, settlement and route defense, regional recovery, and local-security cost rather than faction exclusivity or a small stat variation. England may begin with easier access where opening relationships and institutions exist. Any later controller can qualify through the same three-threshold gate.

## 8. Saxon Axemen Decision

Saxon Axemen should remain available as a controlling candidate while the source map is clarified. “Saxon” may describe a broad cultural-regional service tradition spanning several local sources, an England political continuity path, or a gameplay umbrella. V1 cannot yet be reclassified as purely regional or purely factional from the surfaced snippet. The next full review must identify actual source regions, controller rules, and whether one shared definition or several regional cards best preserve the concept.

## 9. Mailed Saxon Axemen Decision

The current profile explicitly describes Mailed Saxon Axemen as an improved persistent expression of Saxon Axemen and records the content type as an improved profile or recruitable branch pending survival. The default disposition is therefore a dynamic equipment, veteran-cadre, and readiness profile within Saxon Axemen. A separate card survives only if it gains a distinct personnel source, owner, service cycle, AI mission, and recovery system.

## 10. Nottingham Axemen Decision

Nottingham Axemen are presumed regional because the name is geographic, but no dedicated source file surfaced in the current exact search. The name is preserved as a regional candidate rather than fabricated into a full unit. Research must determine the actual geographic source, chronology, settlement network, personnel tradition, equipment, battlefield role, and whether the concept remains distinct from Saxon Axemen and Wessex Axemen.

## 11. English Axemen Decision

English Axemen cannot be treated as an automatic upgrade from Nottingham or Saxon Axemen. The name suggests wider political or military integration, but a separate card needs a new institutional source and player decision. Possible directions include a professionalized realm formation, a hybrid Anglo-Norman adoption path, or an umbrella maturity profile. These are suggestions for later testing, not verified source facts.

## 12. Royal Axemen Decision

Royal Axemen belong behind a Crown and elite collision gate. The candidate must be compared with Housecarls, Saxon Huscarl Guard, Royal Huscarl Guard, Royal Saxon Guard, Thegn formations, Dismounted King’s Men, and any royal poleaxe or bill guards. A Crown label, heavier armor, or improved morale is insufficient. The unit requires a separate nonduplicative personnel source, weapon discipline, service contract, and royal battlefield assignment.

## 13. Regional Versus Factional Boundary

Regional units become available because the controller holds or accesses the source geography and qualifies locally. Factional units become available because the realm, culture, political path, household, civic institution, or professional system creates them. A unit can appear in England's roster while remaining regional. Roster presentation does not transfer constitutional ownership.

## 16. Settlement and Building Rule

Regional axe recruitment may appear in eligible settlements alongside the controller's core roster. Buildings can support records, assembly, training, storage, equipment repair, officer development, supply, and recovery. They cannot create regional people, loyalty, identity, or inexhaustible pools. Capturing a developed settlement may preserve infrastructure while cooperation and personnel remain unresolved.

## 17. Mixed Army Rule

A controller may combine faction-core troops, household forces, regional contingents, and mercenaries in one army where each source independently qualifies. A distant faction controlling Wessex or Nottingham can eventually recruit local axemen beside its own troops. The regional unit retains its name, provenance, officers, equipment history, service conditions, casualties, and replenishment source.

## 18. No Double Counting

Regional personnel cannot simultaneously serve Wessex, Saxon, Nottingham, royal, household, mercenary, and settlement-defense pools. Axe equipment, shields, armor, officers, workshops, halls, carts, and obligations remain finite. A regional source spanning several settlements uses one shared depletion and recovery model unless evidence establishes truly separate communities.

## 19. Household and Huscarl Firewall

Regional axe infantry are not cheaper Housecarls. Housecarls and later royal guards use household, status, patronage, continuity, and elite-service systems. Regional axemen use place, community, local obligation, controller relationship, and bounded pool systems. Transfers require release from the donor source and should create visible political and defensive consequences.

## 20. Battlefield Differentiation Standard

Separate regional axe cards require more than geographic labels. Each must differ through source, service, formation, equipment, terrain use, tactical rhythm, AI mission, loss consequences, and recovery. Wessex Axemen already emphasize southern settlement and route defense plus short counterattacks. Nottingham Axemen, if retained, must establish a different regional proposition rather than using northern colors and altered values.

## 21. Modern Medieval II Selective Inheritance

The design keeps the readable Medieval II and Stainless Steel experience of discovering regional troops in conquered lands. The modernization adds controller-access states, finite DEI-style population-informed pools, local officers, real equipment, building support, political cooperation, service limits, recovery, mixed-army AI, and persistent formation history. Regional recruitment remains rewarding without becoming immediate, universal, or disposable.

## 23. AI Requirements

Campaign AI must evaluate access, people, cooperation, equipment, officers, buildings, local-security cost, distance, service duration, recovery, and army role before selecting regional axes. AI should use Wessex Axemen near appropriate regional defense and avoid draining the pool for disposable distant warfare. AI must not unlock regional cards globally after conquering one settlement.

## 24. UI Requirements

The recruitment UI should show core and regional units together while clearly labeling the regional source. Each regional card must show current controller-access state, eligible settlements, cooperation, people, equipment, buildings, officers, preparation, service bounds, local-defense loss, pool recovery, and the strongest blocker. Controller culture affects relationships only through explicit systems, not hidden eligibility.
