## Core Character Thesis

Áed Ua Ruairc begins in January 1062 as King of Bréifne and the strongest plausible alternative claimant to the Connacht overkingship, not as an Ulster ruler or generic northern rebel. His claim is grounded in a regional kingdom, an earlier Ua Ruairc overkingship under his father Art Uallach, and the unresolved political division between Bréifne and the restored Síl Muiredaig line.

His central tension is opportunity versus overreach. Western Connacht instability and succession losses may create a real path to provincial restoration, but premature war can unite Connacht against Bréifne, expose the eastern frontier to Mide, or make Áed dependent on a stronger southern patron. The campaign should reward patience, home security, and political conversion rather than a scheduled 1067 victory.

## Historical Identity

Áed Ua Ruairc was a son of Art Uallach Ua Ruairc and belonged to the Uí Briúin Bréifne. Art Uallach ruled Connacht after Tadg in Eich Gil's death and before Áed in Gai Bernaig restored Síl Muiredaig authority in 1046. The Ua Ruairc political tradition therefore represents a prior provincial kingship, not merely local resistance.

Áed begins as ruler of Bréifne, a legitimate regional polity within the northern Connacht and central frontier world. His historical defeat and killing of Áed in Gai Bernaig in 1067, subsequent Connacht kingship, eastward activity, and death in 1087 are later events that remain conditional.

## Opening Strategic Function

Áed's opening function is to preserve Bréifne as an independent, militarily credible power and to keep a viable alternative claim available if Connacht's current order weakens. His existence prevents western crisis or succession collapse from resolving automatically within the Síl Muiredaig line.

He must monitor rather than mechanically attack. The best opening action may be preparation, diplomacy, internal consolidation, frontier defense, or support for selected Connacht factions. A claim pursued before Bréifne is secure may destroy the base that makes the candidacy credible.
