## Core Character Thesis

Conchobar Ua Máelshechlainn begins in January 1062 as the long-ruling King of Mide, not a ceremonial custodian of Tara prestige. His power rests on territorial control, central routes, military survival, dynastic consolidation, and a long record of suppressing rivals. Tara and Southern Uí Néill memory strengthen his political language, but they do not replace armies, government, regional acceptance, or succession capacity.

His central tension is that the methods that preserved his reign may now threaten its continuity. Blinding, killing, coercion, and disputed sacred conduct may have weakened immediate rivals while creating collateral grievances, ecclesiastical distrust, and assassination risk. Externally, he must contain Diarmait's Leinster-Dublin system and Bréifne pressure without allowing defensive war to expose the dynasty from within.

## Historical Identity

Conchobar was a grandson of Máel Sechnaill mac Domnaill and a member of Clann Cholmáin within the Southern Uí Néill tradition. He became King of Mide in 1030 and had ruled for more than three decades by the campaign opening. His reign was active, military, and coercive, involving suppression of close and collateral kin, competition with Leinster and Dublin, and engagement with central ecclesiastical institutions.

His family included sons Murchad and Máel Sechnaill Bán and a daughter, Ben-Midi, subject to Dynasty and identity authority. His later victory over Diarmait at Odba in 1072 and murder by a kinsman in 1073 are future history and remain conditional.

Conchobar is a **principal active ruler** and **Mide realm anchor** because his choices affect:

- control of central routes between eastern, western, northern, and southern powers;
- survival of Mide against Leinster-Dublin and Bréifne pressure;
- Tara-associated memory and wider-kingship reception;

## Opening Strategic Function

Conchobar's opening function is to preserve Mide as an active central power rather than a passive source of ceremonial prestige. He must defend routes and frontiers, coordinate military and regional authority, manage sons and collateral kin, negotiate with religious institutions, and prevent stronger neighbors from dividing or subordinating the kingdom.

He also acts as a strategic blocker. Diarmait's wider system, Bréifne expansion, Connacht movement, and Munster intervention all become more difficult if Mide remains coherent. This function depends on actual forces, government, communications, institutions, and regional cooperation owned elsewhere.
