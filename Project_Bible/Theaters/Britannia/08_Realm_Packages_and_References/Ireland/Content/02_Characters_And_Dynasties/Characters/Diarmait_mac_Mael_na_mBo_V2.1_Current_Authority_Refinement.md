## Core Character Thesis

Diarmait mac Máel na mBó begins in January 1062 as the strongest active political architect in Ireland: ruler of Leinster, effective overlord of Dublin, sponsor of Toirdelbach Ua Briain, and a king claiming wider leadership with substantial but contested recognition. His strength comes not from owning Ireland, but from connecting a Leinster dynastic base, Dublin's maritime power, capable delegation through Murchad, and alliances that project influence beyond direct control.

His central tension is structural. The same system that gives him exceptional reach depends heavily on continued control of Dublin, the survival and effectiveness of Murchad, the manageability of allied claimants, and Diarmait's own ability to convert military pressure into submissions, guarantees, and durable political arrangements. Expansion may produce wider recognition, or it may create an overextended network that fragments when its active leader or operational heir is removed.

## Historical Identity

Diarmait belonged to the Uí Cheinnselaig of south-eastern Leinster. He restored and expanded his dynasty's position within Leinster, displaced Echmarcach mac Ragnaill from Dublin in 1052, and used Dublin's fleet, commercial resources, military networks, and Irish Sea connections to support wider political action. His son Murchad mac Diarmata became a principal lieutenant and the opening ruler or governor of Dublin under Diarmait's supremacy.

By January 1062, Diarmait's influence extends beyond Leinster and Dublin through war, patronage, dynastic connection, coalition building, and support for Toirdelbach Ua Briain against Donnchad mac Briain. Contemporary and near-contemporary practice supports treating him as a ruler pursuing or receiving wider Irish royal recognition, but not as an uncontested sovereign whose commands bind every provincial king.

## Final Design Summary

Diarmait mac Máel na mBó begins in January 1062 as Ireland's strongest active political architect: a Leinster ruler whose effective control of Dublin, capable son, military record, and claimant alliances give him reach beyond any single provincial kingship.
