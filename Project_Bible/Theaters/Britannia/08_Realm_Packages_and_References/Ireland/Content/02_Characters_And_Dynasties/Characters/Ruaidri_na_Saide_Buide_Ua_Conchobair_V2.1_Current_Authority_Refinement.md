## Core Character Thesis

Ruaidrí na Saide Buide begins in January 1062 as a protected child or young adolescent within Áed in Gai Bernaig's household, not as an active ruler or guaranteed heir of Connacht. His opening value is dynastic survival: if Áed's restoration fails, older brothers die, or Bréifne captures the political initiative, Ruaidrí may preserve one route by which the Síl Muiredaig or Uí Conchobair line can survive, negotiate, or later return.

His central tension is protection versus political capture. A guardian, foster household, church institution, or foreign sponsor may keep him alive while controlling his residence, correspondence, claim evidence, education, and public presentation. The campaign must determine whether protection becomes preparation, exile, hostage leverage, client kingship, religious withdrawal, or independent adult agency.

## Historical Identity

Ruaidrí na Saide Buide was a son of Áed in Gai Bernaig and Caillech Caimgein. He belonged to Síl Muiredaig within the wider Uí Briúin Aí and Uí Conchobair context. Tadg in Eich Gil was his paternal grandfather and functions in the 1062 scenario only as a historical legacy figure.

Ruaidrí later became an overking of Connacht after prolonged competition, captivity, submission, warfare, family conflict, and political displacement. He was not the later twelfth-century High King Ruaidrí mac Tairrdelbach. His later reign, capture by Toirdelbach, conflict with Áed Ua Ruairc, blinding, descendants, and death in 1118 belong to conditional futures, not opening truth.
