## Core Dynasty Thesis

The Uí Briúin Bréifne begin in January 1062 as the ruling dynasty of a real regional kingdom and the strongest alternative dynastic route to the Connacht overkingship. They are not a minor rebel house inside a settled Connacht, an Ulster dynasty, or an internal branch of the Uí Conchobair. Bréifne gives Áed Ua Ruairc a defensible political base, functioning government, military credibility, kin networks, and a remembered precedent for provincial rule through Art Uallach Ua Ruairc.

The dynasty's central problem is timing. It can preserve a latent claim and wait for western or succession weakness, activate the claim under favorable conditions, negotiate divided authority, or remain a strong Bréifne dynasty without provincial rule. A premature campaign may unite Connacht, expose Bréifne to Mide, divide Ua Ruairc kin, or produce dependence on a southern patron. Victory in battle is not enough. The claim must be converted into recognition, title possession, government, western settlement, institutional cooperation, and succession capacity.

## Historical Foundation

The Uí Briúin Bréifne formed a major dynastic and regional division within the wider Uí Briúin political world. Their Bréifne base supported an independent regional kingship and a credible route into Connacht's provincial kingship.

After Tadg in Eich Gil died in 1030, Art Uallach Ua Ruairc held the Connacht overkingship until 1046. Áed in Gai Bernaig then restored Síl Muiredaig authority. That restoration displaced an Ua Ruairc provincial order but did not erase the dynasty's regional base, its remembered possession, or its future claim relevance.

Áed Ua Ruairc begins as King of Bréifne and the leading living representative of the alternative Connacht route. His opening strength comes from Bréifne, not from possession of Connacht or predetermined western support.

## Opening Strategic Function

The dynasty's opening function is to preserve Bréifne as an independent, governable base while maintaining a credible alternative Connacht claim. It prevents the western crisis or Uí Conchobair succession from resolving automatically within one dynasty.

Dynasty strategy should make the timing question explicit:

1. Is Bréifne secure enough to sustain an external campaign?
2. Is Connacht weak enough that intervention can gain real support?
3. Can Mide and other frontiers be neutralized or contained?
4. Can military success become recognized government rather than temporary destruction?
5. Can external aid be accepted without permanent political capture?

## Home-Base and Overextension Rule

Bréifne is the dynasty's source of credibility. A Connacht campaign should not proceed when home government, succession, kin cooperation, frontier defense, supply, or command continuity is collapsing.

The preferred sequence is preservation, opportunity assessment, claim activation, provincial settlement, consolidation, then any eastern initiative.

## Final Design Summary

The Uí Briúin Bréifne begin with less provincial territory than Áed in Gai Bernaig but with something strategically decisive: a real kingdom from which to wait, bargain, threaten, and restore. Art Uallach supplies remembered Connacht possession. Áed Ua Ruairc supplies current leadership and a valid claim. Bréifne supplies the government and military base that make the claim credible.

The dynasty succeeds when it preserves Bréifne, activates the claim only under favorable conditions, converts any victory into recognized government, settles western and Síl Muiredaig actors, and avoids permanent patron capture. It fails when remembered legitimacy is mistaken for possession, western opportunity is mistaken for loyalty, or the pursuit of Connacht destroys the regional kingdom that made restoration possible.
