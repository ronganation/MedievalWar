### Core Dynasty Thesis
  
The Uí Ceinnselaig begin in January 1062 as the restored ruling dynasty of Leinster and the dynastic center of the strongest connected political system in Ireland. Their power is real but highly concentrated. Diarmait mac Máel na mBó supplies the political architecture, the Leinster kingship, wider alliances, and the dynasty's current island-wide reach. Murchad mac Diarmata supplies operational government in Dublin, military execution, maritime coordination, and the clearest immediate succession route. The next generation remains insufficiently prepared to replace both men.  
The dynasty's central problem is not immediate expansion. It is whether one generation's restoration can become a transferable Leinster-Dublin order without confusing Diarmait's personal authority, Murchad's office, Dublin's distinct institutions, and collateral dynastic rights with dynasty-owned property. A stable transition requires more than Murchad's survival. It requires title recognition, household continuity, valid delegation, local Dublin cooperation, prepared substitutes, branch settlement, and government that functions after either principal actor is removed.

### Historical Foundation
  
The Uí Ceinnselaig claimed descent from Énnae Cennsalach and belonged to the wider Laigin royal world. The dynasty had supplied early kings of Leinster before centuries of Uí Dúnlainge predominance. Diarmait's recovery of the Leinster kingship therefore constitutes restoration rather than uninterrupted hereditary monarchy.  
Diarmait's seizure of Dublin in 1052 expanded the political system beyond the dynasty's south-eastern heartland. Dublin supplied access to trade, maritime transport, military forces, revenue, intelligence, and diplomacy, but these capacities remained institutionally and politically distinct from the dynasty. Murchad's active role in Dublin made the combined system operational without making the city, its communities, or its offices hereditary Uí Ceinnselaig property.

### Final Design Summary
  
The Uí Ceinnselaig begin near the height of their restored power because Diarmait links Leinster to Dublin and Murchad makes that system operational. The dynasty does not personally own the city, fleet, office, title, local communities, or people that create its reach. Its campaign is about converting extraordinary personal leadership into transferable government, protected households, recognized succession, reviewed delegation, and a workable branch settlement.  
The dynasty succeeds when Leinster and Dublin remain strategically connected after the removal of either principal adult, without collapsing distinct offices and communities into hereditary property. It fails when current strength is mistaken for durable capacity, when younger descendants are treated as ready-made replacements, or when too many systems remain concentrated in two men.
