## 1. Unit Verdict

**Lucht Tighe | Household Warriors** are the dependable fighting core of an Irish ruler's host: shielded household infantry who can hold a dangerous short front, punish lighter troops that press too far, receive wavering contingents, and withdraw around the ruler or standard before the household is destroyed.

This is not merely a bodyguard standing behind the army. It is a real combat unit with a clear Medieval II battlefield purchase:

- firmer and better equipped than Ceithern;
- smaller and more expensive than provincial spear contingents;
- capable of winning a short, disciplined infantry fight;
- reliable enough to anchor the ruler's immediate battle sector;
- dangerous against light infantry, raiders, and disrupted spearmen;
- vulnerable when isolated against sustained heavy infantry, mass missiles, or cavalry;
- politically costly to lose because its warriors, officers, and equipment come from a finite household.

The unit's controlling sentence is:

> **Hold the household front, counter the exposed enemy, rally the host around the standard, and return before the ruler's military core is spent.**

The unit's distinct purchase is therefore not passive protection. It is **bounded household superiority**: a compact, dependable formation that wins one important local infantry contest and then preserves enough strength to keep the Irish host politically and militarily coherent.

### 5.2 What the player is purchasing

- A compact formation with higher cohesion and command responsiveness than ordinary local contingents.
- A dependable reserve that can stabilize a threatened line without becoming a full heavy-infantry wall.
- A formation that remains useful during ambushes, raids, withdrawals, and broken-ground battles because its core job is protection and reorganization.
- A political asset whose casualties matter beyond the battle because the formation draws from a finite household provider.

### 5.3 What the player is not purchasing

- Free morale for the whole army.
- A magical Host Cohesion aura.
- Universal immunity to fear, fatigue, missiles, cavalry, or defeat.
- A unit that automatically fights better merely because a ruler is nearby.
- A bodyguard that can be detached while leaving the ruler fully protected elsewhere.

## 5A. Battlefield Role in Plain Terms

The Lucht Tighe should feel immediately stronger than common Irish foot without becoming a late-game armored elite.

### Against light infantry

The household warriors should win sustained frontal contact against most light raiders and skirmisher infantry once they close. Better shields, tighter leadership, and more reliable close-order fighting let them drive back troops that depend on mobility or disorder.

### Against ordinary spear infantry

The result should depend on position and timing. Lucht Tighe can break or displace an ordinary spear formation through a controlled counterattack, local numerical support, or flank pressure, but should not casually grind through a fresh deep spear line from the front.

### Against heavy infantry

The household may delay, contain, or exploit a disrupted heavy formation. It should lose an extended unsupported contest against dedicated later heavy infantry such as mature armored household warriors or Galloglaich.

### Against cavalry

Held spears, shields, and cohesion provide respectable local resistance. The unit is not a dedicated anti-cavalry wall. Open flanks, disorder, pursuit, and fatigue expose it to mounted attack.

### Under missiles

Shields and discipline offer more protection than Ceithern receive, but the small household pool makes every casualty expensive. Prolonged exposure should be strategically unacceptable even when the formation remains tactically steady.

## 9. Battlefield Behavior

### 9.1 Hold

The formation establishes a compact shielded front over a limited frontage. Hold is strongest near the household standard, commander, or defined rally sector. It should resist local pressure and protect lighter friendly infantry, but it cannot cover the frontage of a mass spear levy.

### 9.2 Counter

When an enemy becomes disordered, overextended, exposed, or fixed by another formation, the household advances in a short controlled counterattack. The aim is to break the immediate threat, not to begin an uncontrolled pursuit. This is the unit's principal offensive value.

### 9.3 Rally

Friendly troops that physically reach the household sector may reform behind or beside it. Officers, signals, space, and an intact standard improve the process. Rally does not restore casualties, erase fatigue, or automatically recover routed troops.

### 9.4 Return

After the local objective is achieved, the household breaks contact, reforms around its surviving leaders and standard, and returns to the protected command sector. A player who refuses to return converts an elite limited asset into an expensive ordinary melee unit and risks destroying the household's future capacity.

## 26. Thirty-Second Total War Read

- **Unit:** Lucht Tighe
- **Support name:** Household Warriors
- **Class:** Household spear-and-sword infantry
- **Signature:** Hold, Counter, Rally, Return
- **Battle promise:** Establish one compact household front, defeat light or disordered enemies through a controlled counterattack, receive friendly troops around the standard, and return before the ruler's finite military core is spent.
- **Best use:** Command-sector defense, local counterattack, receiving Ceithern after scatter, protecting a withdrawal, securing a narrow objective, and preventing one local collapse.
- **Strong against:** Light infantry, raiders caught in contact, exhausted skirmishers, fragmented spear troops, exposed crews, and enemies already fixed or disordered.
- **Conditional against:** Ordinary spearmen, cavalry approaching a prepared spear front, and better-protected infantry whose formation has already been disrupted.
- **Weak against:** Dedicated heavy infantry, prolonged crossbow or bow pressure, heavy cavalry on an open flank, encirclement, and any fight that prevents Return.
- **Campaign bargain:** Every fielded card removes trusted warriors, officers, shields, weapons, and protection capacity from a real household.
- **Flavor:** **The wider host may assemble for a season. The household must still exist when the season ends.**

### 29.1 Rank responsibilities

- Front files present spear and shield, absorb contact, and define the protected facing.
- Middle files preserve depth, replace losses, close passages, and support limited counterattack.
- Rear files protect officers, standard, wounded personnel, and the route of Return.
- Rear depth does not generate free attacks.

### 29.2 Frontage ceiling

Lucht Tighe are strong over a short front. Extending the formation reduces depth, officer reach, passage control, and the ability to Return. They cannot replace provincial frontage merely by being better warriors.

## 42. Enemy Counterplay

The opposing player can defeat or neutralize Lucht Tighe by:

- refusing the short front and attacking elsewhere;
- suppressing the compact formation with arrows or crossbows;
- presenting fresh heavy infantry after the household counterattacks;
- threatening the Return route with cavalry;
- forcing repeated turns or passage openings;
- separating the unit from ruler, standard, or supporting host;
- offering a low-value target that draws the household into pursuit;
- striking the residence, principal, or another household obligation at campaign level.
