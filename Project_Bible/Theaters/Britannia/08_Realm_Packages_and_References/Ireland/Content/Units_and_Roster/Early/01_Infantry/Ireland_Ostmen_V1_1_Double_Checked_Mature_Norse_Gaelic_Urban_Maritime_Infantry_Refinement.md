### 0A. Historical Identity and Naming Firewall

`Ostmen` or `Austmen` means East-men and was used for Norse or Norse-Gaelic inhabitants of Irish Sea communities. The term describes people and community identity, not one universally standardized regiment. Norse-Gaelic communities were culturally blended and historically associated with Irish Sea towns and maritime networks, including Dublin and other coastal centers. This profile converts that social and political context into a bounded game unit without treating Ostmen as generic Vikings or granting every coastal settlement a free Norse roster.

## 1. Direct Unit Verdict

**Ostmen are Norse-Gaelic urban and maritime infantry supplied by a valid town, ruler, or coastal community relationship to secure gates, quays, ship approaches, landing places, streets, stores, and the immediate military interests of their provider.**

Their signature is:

> **Muster, Secure, Repel, Return.**

Their strength comes from compact defensive organization, mixed spear-and-axe equipment, shield use, maritime familiarity, and access to an urban or trade-supported provider. Their limitation is equally important: they are not a universal Irish line unit and should not be drawn freely away from the town, port, ships, households, workshops, and commercial systems that sustain them.

## 2. One-Screen Total War Card

- **Unit:** Ostmen
- **Support name:** Norse-Gaelic Townsmen
- **Class:** Urban and maritime spear-axe infantry
- **Signature:** Muster, Secure, Repel, Return
- **Battle promise:** Muster from a valid Norse-Gaelic town or coastal provider, secure a compact urban or maritime objective, repel raids and assaulting light or medium infantry, then return before prolonged inland campaigning exhausts the provider and strips the town of its defenders, crews, guards, and skilled population.
- **Best use:** Gates, quays, streets, storehouses, markets, landing sites, beachheads, river approaches, ship protection, compact camp sectors, and coastal relief.
- **Strong against:** Raiders, exposed light infantry, disordered assault troops, isolated crews, enemies crossing narrow gates or gangways, and light cavalry in restricted urban terrain.
- **Conditional against:** Comparable spearmen, medium infantry, household retainers, and mounted troops compressed by streets or shore obstacles.
- **Weak against:** Sustained archery, heavy infantry, enveloping field armies, prolonged inland pursuit, cavalry in open ground, siege engines, and campaigns that separate the formation from maritime or urban support.
- **Campaign bargain:** Ostmen provide a better organized compact defensive body, but fielding them subtracts from town defense, ship crews, merchant protection, workshops, warehouses, and political confidence. Their service depends on the provider's relationship with the receiving ruler and on credible return, reward, loss, and autonomy terms.
- **Player instruction:** Muster from a real provider. Secure one defined access point. Repel short assault waves. Hand off or withdraw when the wider mission moves inland.
- **Flavor:** **The town's walls, quays, ships, stores, and fighting households are one system. Pull too hard on one part and the rest weakens.**

## 5. Irish Infantry Collision Firewall

### 5.1 Ceithern

Ceithern own mobile harassment, ambush, selective close action, scattering, and regrouping. Ostmen are more compact, more tied to defended access points, and generally less able to disperse across broken inland ground. Ostmen should defeat Ceithern in compact prepared contact but lose operational reach and flexibility.

### 5.2 Tuath Spearmen

Tuath Spearmen own locally gathered field frontage under a ruler or community obligation. Ostmen own town, gate, port, quay, landing, and maritime-provider defense. Both may use spears and shields, but provider, terrain, mission, service cost, axe distribution, and return obligation remain different.

### 5.3 Lucht Tighe

Lucht Tighe own ruler protection, household professionalism, rally, and crisis intervention. Ostmen defend the interests and infrastructure of an urban or maritime provider. An Ostmen contingent may guard an allied ruler but does not become the ruler's household automatically.

### 5.4 Later Armored Household Warriors

Ostmen may be comparatively well supplied through trade and craft access, but they do not own the High Era household-heavy ceiling.

### 5.5 Later Galloglaich

Galloglaich are later external or settled heavy infantry companies with a different chronology, provider structure, attendant burden, contract identity, and heavy battlefield role. Ostmen are not proto-Galloglaich.

## 23. Combined-Arms Integration

### Ceithern

Ceithern scout, harass, and disrupt outside compact objectives. Ostmen secure the port, gate, quay, camp, or landing point into which Ceithern can withdraw. Ostmen should not follow every Ceithern pursuit inland.

### Tuath Spearmen

Tuath Spearmen create broader local field frontage. Ostmen hold tighter urban and maritime positions with a more specialized provider and stronger spear-axe counterattack option.

### Lucht Tighe

Lucht Tighe protect the ruler and preserve household command. Ostmen protect the urban or maritime provider and may refuse unrelated household service.

### Royal Mounted Retainers

Mounted retainers cover approaches and pursue enemies beyond the gate or landing site. Ostmen maintain the objective and open controlled lanes.

### Ships and crews

Ships transport or support the contingent only when actual vessel and crew packets exist. Ostmen do not create naval capacity by themselves.

### 53. Enemy Counterplay

The opponent can defeat or neutralize Ostmen by:

- attacking several separated access points;
- using sustained missiles or siege engines;
- setting fires or creating smoke and debris;
- cutting maritime relief or return;
- forcing the contingent into open-field pursuit;
- pinning spear files while attacking lanes or objective rear;
- provoking axe files beyond the protected frontage;
- blockading the provider until equipment, crew, political, and food pressure accumulate;
- threatening the town while the contingent remains inland.
