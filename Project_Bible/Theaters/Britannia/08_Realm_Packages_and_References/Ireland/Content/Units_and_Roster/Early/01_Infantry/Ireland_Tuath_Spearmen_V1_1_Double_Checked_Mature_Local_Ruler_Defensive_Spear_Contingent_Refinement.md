## 1. Direct Unit Verdict

**Tuath Spearmen are locally provided spear-and-shield contingents gathered under a valid ruler or community obligation to create the coherent defensive frontage that Ceithern deliberately do not provide.**

Their signature is:

> **Gather, Brace, Receive, Release.**

Their battlefield purchase is not greater mobility, elite household morale, or heavier equipment. Their purchase is the ability to turn a politically assembled body of local fighters into a temporary but recognizable receiving line around a route, ford, camp, ruler, settlement approach, or supporting host.

## 2. One-Screen Total War Card

- **Unit:** Tuath Spearmen
- **Support name:** Provincial Spearmen
- **Class:** Local-ruler defensive spear-and-shield infantry
- **Signature:** Gather, Brace, Receive, Release
- **Battle promise:** Assemble a coherent frontage from a valid local provider, receive a mounted or infantry approach at a chosen point, preserve enough order for the wider Irish host to maneuver or regroup, then release from formation before isolation and fatigue destroy the contingent.
- **Best use:** Fords, roads, camp shoulders, settlement approaches, narrow valleys, baggage protection, receiving cavalry in restricted ground, and giving Ceithern a physical rally line.
- **Strong against:** Light cavalry forced into frontal contact, raiders, scattered infantry, exposed mounted pursuit, and attacks compressed by terrain.
- **Conditional against:** Medium cavalry, comparable spearmen, and formed infantry when ground, shields, leadership, support, and fatigue favor the contingent.
- **Weak against:** Sustained archery or crossbows, flank attack, heavy infantry, elite household troops, Galloglaich, prolonged encirclement, and open-ground isolation.
- **Campaign bargain:** A ruler gains a necessary defensive line by removing real people, shields, spears, leaders, and supply from local defense, work, transport, and subordinate obligations. The contingent is broader than a household retinue but less dependable and less permanent.
- **Player instruction:** Choose the place first. Gather before contact. Brace only when the approach is real. Receive the threat with support nearby. Release once the protected mission is complete.
- **Flavor:** **The host may be assembled from many rulers, but somebody must give the scattered companies a place to return to.**

## 5. Collision Firewall

### 5.1 Ceithern

Ceithern own route harassment, short-range missiles, ambush participation, scattering, and regrouping. Tuath Spearmen own the physical frontage to which Ceithern can return. Spear-equipped Ceithern remain light and mobile; Tuath Spearmen sacrifice mobility to form and maintain a receiving line.

### 5.2 Lucht Tighe Household Warriors

Lucht Tighe own ruler protection, professional household cohesion, crisis intervention, and high political replacement cost. Tuath Spearmen are broader local contingents whose first loyalty and recovery remain tied to the immediate provider. They cannot inherit household discipline merely by standing near a king.

### 5.3 Ostmen Candidate

Ostmen should own Norse-Gaelic urban, port, maritime, or trade-linked infantry. Tuath Spearmen are inland and regional only where a valid provider exists. Town ownership does not merge the pools.

### 5.4 Later Armored Household Warriors

Later household development may produce a heavier, better equipped guard core. Tuath Spearmen remain a provider-dependent frontage rather than progressing automatically into elite heavy infantry.

### 5.5 Later Galloglaich

Galloglaich provide a contracted or settled heavy anchor capable of enduring and breaking severe contact. Tuath Spearmen provide broader but shallower receiving frontage. They are not an early Galloglaich substitute.

## 9. Frontage Integrity

**Frontage Integrity** is the contingent's decisive battlefield resource. It represents enough people, shields, spear points, spacing, officers, confidence, and protected shoulders remaining to receive an approach as one useful formation.

```yaml
FrontageIntegrity:
  supported_by:
    - Completed gathering
    - Clear frontage and mission
    - Shields and serviceable spears
    - Captains and file leaders
    - Protected shoulders
    - Ground compatible with formation width
    - Nearby relief or receiving host
    - Credible service obligation

  spent_by:
    - Bracing
    - Missile exposure
    - Receiving charge or infantry pressure
    - Redressing gaps
    - Rotation of tired files
    - Extended holding

  broken_by:
    - Flank attack
    - Penetration of gaps
    - Captain loss
    - Heavy missile pressure
    - Prolonged melee
    - Several-direction attack
    - Ceithern or allies passing through without lanes
    - Isolation after the mission ends

  restored_by:
    - Real separation
    - Officer-led redress
    - Relief by another formation
    - Shortening the frontage
    - Retreat to prepared ground
    - Rest and equipment repair

  not_restored_by:
    - Ability cooldown
    - Province name
    - Irish identity
    - Standing still without protected conditions
```

## 13. Release Contract

A successful local contingent must know when its defensive mission is complete.

```yaml
Release_means:
  - Leave brace before isolation
  - Open planned lanes for friendly movement
  - Withdraw toward relief or provider route
  - Return fighters to local obligations after service

Release_requires:
  - Command signal
  - Physical exit
  - Enemy contact sufficiently reduced or handed off
  - Surviving file leaders
  - Receiving or relief formation where needed

Release_failure:
  - Formation remains too long
  - Fatigue and missiles accumulate
  - Provider contingent feels abandoned
  - Frontage collapses after strategic mission already succeeded
```

Release is not rout. It is disciplined termination of a temporary frontage mission.

## 20. Combined-Arms Integration

### Ceithern

```text
Ceithern harass, scatter, and draw pursuit.
Tuath Spearmen receive the pursuit at a chosen point.
Ceithern regroup behind or beside the frontage.
The spearmen release before becoming isolated.
```

No hidden proximity bonus is required.

### Lucht Tighe

Lucht Tighe preserve the ruler and intervene when the frontage fails. Tuath Spearmen reduce the number of routine threats that reach the household core. Household troops do not donate professional discipline automatically.

### Royal Mounted Retainers

Mounted retainers warn of approach, cover shoulders, and pursue a genuine break. The spearmen provide a stable point but should not obstruct cavalry passage.

### Ostmen

Ostmen may defend gates, ports, or maritime approaches with different equipment and provider relationships. Tuath Spearmen cover local field and route frontage.

### Later Galloglaich

Galloglaich can anchor severe contact and counterattack. Tuath Spearmen cover more broadly but cannot match their heavy endurance.

## 37. Final Strong First-Draft Verdict

**Tuath Spearmen pass as the next strong Irish Early Era unit candidate.** They survive beside Ceithern and Lucht Tighe because they purchase a different military function: a temporary local-ruler frontage capable of receiving pressure, protecting a route or host sector, and giving lighter Irish troops somewhere physical to regroup.

```text
Ceithern harass, close, scatter, and regroup.
Tuath Spearmen gather, brace, receive, and release.
Lucht Tighe guard the ruler, rally the host, and intervene in crisis.
```

Their strength comes from position, preparation, shoulders, relief, and provider commitment. Their weakness comes from missiles, flanking, fatigue, prolonged fighting, uneven equipment, and the political limits of temporary service.

The unit does not claim that `Tuath Spearmen` is an attested standardized title. The working name keeps the card readable while the professional-guard term `Cliathairi` remains quarantined from misuse and collision with Lucht Tighe.
