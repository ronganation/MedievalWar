Gwent Raiders are a proposed fast Welsh regional mercenary unit whose battlefield value comes from **Strike and Scatter**. They move through a screened or neglected route, attack a soft or poorly protected objective in close combat, loot or disrupt where the mission allows, and disengage before heavy infantry or cavalry traps them.

They are neither Irish Kerns with Welsh colors nor lighter Caterans. Kerns are missile-led and preserve distance. Caterans use javelin threat before a conditional pounce. Gwent Raiders are melee-led raiders who rely on speed, target selection, surprise from real deployment conditions, and short contact rather than sustained line fighting. The unit must remain valuable even when it does not rack up kills: forcing escorts to remain behind, pulling reserves toward a threatened camp, interrupting supplies, or aborting safely after discovering protection can all be successful use.

## 4. Player-Facing Card

### Short Description

Fast Welsh raiders who overwhelm exposed troops and vulnerable objectives before scattering ahead of a heavier response.

### Encyclopedia Description

Gwent Raiders are lightly equipped Welsh fighting bands available for temporary military service only when a real source, representative, route, agreement, and finite capacity permit. Armed with approved light melee weapons and limited protection, they move quickly through screened routes and broken country to strike baggage, camps, exposed missile troops, isolated crews, and already weakened infantry.

Their strength lies in choosing where not to fight as much as where to attack. They can create disruption, force escorts away from the main line, punish an exposed rear, and pursue a routed light unit. They cannot hold against disciplined heavy infantry, receive cavalry safely, or remain in prolonged melee. A raiding force that loses its leader, cohesion, route, or timing can be trapped and destroyed.

### Strengths

- fast movement and repositioning
- strong attacks against soft or isolated targets
- camp, baggage, route, and rear-area disruption
- useful pursuit against already broken light troops
- conditional use of lanes, hedges, wooded margins, and broken approaches
- lower equipment burden than heavy mercenaries
- memorable Welsh regional identity

### Weaknesses

- light protection
- poor sustained melee
- weak against formed heavy infantry and spears
- vulnerable to cavalry and superior missiles
- modest staying power
- unreliable impact against prepared targets
- dependent on approach, escape, leadership, and mission discipline
- replacement remains source-bound

### Player Guidance

Do not place Gwent Raiders in the center. Keep them behind terrain, a flank screen, or the army's outer edge. Select an exposed target, confirm a withdrawal route, attack briefly, and disengage before reserves respond. Their best victory is often disruption and survival, not holding the captured ground.

## 18. Target Selection, Counterplay, and Failure Modes

### Preferred Targets

- exposed archers, crossbowmen, and slingers
- isolated crews and support personnel
- baggage, livestock, supply points, and camp edges where the mission authorizes them
- damaged, tired, routing, or disordered light infantry
- marching or turning troops with poor immediate support
- small objectives whose disruption matters more than occupation

## 22. Campaign Role, Price Tier, and Strategic Value

Gwent Raiders occupy a lower-cost, average-quality, high-utility raiding tier. Their value lies between generic light infantry and more specialized mercenaries. They should be easier to contract and sustain than Galloglaich, but they remain finite and regionally sourced.

```yaml
campaign_value:
  - ThreatensRoutesCampsBaggageLivestockAndSoftObjectives
  - ForcesEnemyEscortAndReserveAllocation
  - AddsPursuitAndRearAreaReach
  - SupportsRaidingWithoutReplacingLineInfantry
  - CreatesPoliticalConsequencesIfMisused
cost_direction:
  relative_hiring_burden: LowToMedium
  relative_upkeep_or_reward_burden: LowToMedium
  relative_supply_burden: Low
  relative_replacement_burden: MediumBecauseSourceAndLeadersRemainFinite
  exact_values: Unresolved
spam_prevention:
  - ExactRegionalSource
  - LocalDefenseAndLaborCompetition
  - MissionAndRouteRequirements
  - LeadershipAndGuideLimits
  - PoliticalAndDisciplineConsequences
  - WeaknessInFormalBattle
```

A low material burden does not mean no campaign cost. Losing experienced local leaders, guides, and fighters can damage the source more than replacing simple weapons. The lower contract tier must therefore come from lighter equipment and narrower battlefield capacity, not from treating the source as disposable or infinitely renewable.

The unit should normally be cheaper and more available than Galloglaich, but not necessarily cheaper than every levy or domestic formation. Exact price depends on source access, distance, mission risk, guide requirements, loot terms, political consequences, and expected return.
