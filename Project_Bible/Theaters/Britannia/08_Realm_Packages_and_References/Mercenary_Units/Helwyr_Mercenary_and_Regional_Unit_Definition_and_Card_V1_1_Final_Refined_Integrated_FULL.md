Helwyr are a proposed lower Welsh bow layer whose battlefield value is **Stalk, Harass, Withdraw**. They use screened woodland or rough-ground routes to approach within a useful shorter range, release a brief and selective harassment sequence against exposed troops, then withdraw or displace before cavalry, superior missiles, or formed infantry fix them in place.

Their identity does not depend on being inaccurate, untrained, or disposable. They are practiced regional archers with useful field judgment. They remain distinct from Welsh Longbowmen because they do not own mature heavy-bow formation fire, broad long-range approach shaping, organized counter-missile frontage, stakes, or the higher conditioning and craft burden of the heavy-bow family.

## 4. Player-Facing Card

### Short Description

Light Welsh woodland archers who stalk through screened ground, harass exposed enemies at useful range, and withdraw before heavier troops can close.

### Encyclopedia Description

Helwyr are practiced Welsh bow users drawn from validated woodland, hunting, guiding, and regional military networks. Their strength lies in fieldcraft, mobility, target judgment, and the ability to use screened routes rather than in the heavy bows, organized long-range frontage, or sustained missile pressure of mature Welsh Longbowmen.

They are most effective against exposed skirmishers, scouts, light infantry, crews, and troops moving through broken or restricted ground. A well-handled Helwyr body can support an ambush, watch a flank, harass an approach, and slip through known routes before cavalry or superior missile troops respond. If held in place, forced into open ground, or ordered to fight a long missile duel, its advantage collapses quickly.

### Strengths

- woodland and rough-ground movement through validated routes
- shorter-range harassment against exposed targets
- conditional ambush participation
- observation and flank-screen value
- lower sustainment burden than mature heavy-bow formations
- useful withdrawal harassment
- strong Welsh regional identity

### Weaknesses

- shorter reach than Welsh Longbowmen
- weak pressure against armor and shields
- light protection
- vulnerable to cavalry and superior missiles
- poor sustained formation fire
- limited melee value
- detection rises after firing or repeated use of one position
- replacement and route knowledge remain source-bound

### Player Guidance

Use Helwyr to observe and threaten a flank, not to trade volleys with a formal archer line. Approach through real screened ground, choose exposed targets, fire briefly, and move before the enemy closes the route. Keep Welsh Longbowmen for protected long-range pressure and use Helwyr for mobility, ambush participation, and local disruption.
