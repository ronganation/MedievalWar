## 1. Strong Unit Verdict

**Galloway Spear Warbands are spear-led regional contact infantry that make disengagement expensive. They close with ordinary foot, establish a working spear front, and keep pressure on an enemy's facing, movement, and reinforcement choices while the wider host makes its decision.**

Their signature is **Close and Bind**. The player buys a committed infantry engagement rather than a missile opening, a concealed axe raid, a broad static spear frontage, or a later organized ground-taking drive. Their best result is an enemy body that cannot turn, redeploy, or help another sector without paying for disengagement.

They must be useful in a normal infantry fight. They are not permitted to become expendable troops whose only function is dying while better units win. A coherent Warband can defeat weak ordinary foot, pressure peers, or hold a manageable enemy in place. The risk is that the same commitment also makes the Warband harder to recover when the battle turns against it.

## 2. One-Screen Recruitment Card

- **Class:** Regional spear infantry with an active contact role.
- **Best at:** Engaging ordinary foot and making that engagement matter to the rest of the battle.
- **Useful against:** Weak musters, light raiders caught without an exit, ordinary regional infantry, and enemy screens that need to move elsewhere.
- **Conditional against:** Peer shield infantry, tired better troops, and a shortened enemy frontage with support nearby.
- **Poor against:** Fresh armored professionals, prepared long-spear or pike fronts, concentrated missiles, and cavalry reaching an exposed side.
- **Campaign bargain:** Regional fighting capacity and leaders committed to a real field engagement; more demanding than a casual muster, less protected and less institutionally sustained than maintained armored foot.
- **Player instruction:** Engage something worth holding, keep enough spear frontage working, and arrange the release before the fight consumes the band.
- **Flavor:** **Bring the spears to bear. Make the enemy stay.**

## 3. Player-Facing Description

These fighting bands bring the spear into a close, purposeful infantry contest. They gather around recognized leaders, advance as a usable body, and keep their weapons directed into the enemy rather than spending their first opportunity on a throwing exchange.

Their pressure gives the controlling army time and space. An enemy that turns away risks the spears; an enemy that stays cannot reinforce another threatened position. Against weaker foot the Warband may win the contest itself. Against a stronger formation, continued contact becomes a bargain that must be judged before too many leaders, shields, and spear-bearing files are lost.

The band is not a miniature Spear Host and not a later heavy company. Its strength is committed regional fighting effort. Its weakness is the cost of getting that effort back out of the battle.

## 4. Why This Candidate, and Why Not Another Clone

The V0.3 Scottish working foundation retains Galloway Spear Warband among unbuilt Early candidates under an overlap test. The professional field-infantry lane now has a supplied Scottish Armored Foot profile, and the conversation has completed Cateran Bowmen and Highland Warband drafts. This is a selected next retained candidate, not a claim that an updated live index mandates this exact order.

The proposed separation has three material parts: no normal javelin phase; a spear-dominant equipment and contact package; and a player commitment centered on occupying an enemy body rather than taking and consolidating a succession of objectives. Different recruitment qualification and recovery must support that difference.

If these distinctions cannot survive ordinary battles, merge the concept into an existing regional family. A regional adjective is not enough to justify a card.

## 10. Source Expressions and Emergency Assembly

A locally assembled body may have easier return and stronger recognition between contributors but less long-service support. A lord-supported contribution may have better assembly and equipment access but narrower political availability. A returning veteran body may have better release judgment while remaining understrength or weapon-poor.

These are expressions of one card, not three clone units. They cannot combine all advantages into a best-of-sources loadout. No expression grants hidden attack power.

Ordinary recruitment does not require an invasion alarm. Emergency overdraw can add available people but cannot manufacture practiced spear contact or release control. Hasty additions may support local defense separately instead of increasing the Warband's effective fighting frontage.

## 11. Availability, Cost, and Service Bargain

The intended price is above a low-readiness local contribution when equipment and field preparation are comparable. Relative cost against Javelinmen depends on their actual source and support. This is not an elite household purchase, but neither should it be disposable low-cost assault mass.

The player buys a useful contact commitment without an ammunition burden. The campaign cost is personnel exposure, spear and shield wear, leader dependence, and recovery after close fighting. A healthy source should support one reasonable short service without a crippling economic penalty.

Exact unit size, price, upkeep, recruitment time, pool size, and recovery rate remain calibration values. No arbitrary global one-unit cap is imposed. Multiple cards require multiple valid commitments, not repeated access to the same contributors.

## 13. Internal Files and Weapon Continuity

Front files establish spear contact. Supporting files preserve usable depth, replace disrupted fighters through real space, and keep leaders connected. Rear files are part of the same formation, not a free detached reserve unit.

A spear needs handling room and a clear direction. Congestion reduces useful attacks. Broken shafts, dropped weapons, damaged shields, and drawn sidearms change the remaining package. A compressed band may continue fighting but loses part of the reach that justified its commitment.

Sidearms allow survival and limited continued contact. They do not turn the unit into Mormaer's Swordsmen or Cateran Axemen once the spear front collapses.

## 15. Signature: Close and Bind

```text
Gather a usable spear front
  -> close against an enemy worth engaging
     -> establish contact without losing the supporting files
        -> keep pressure on that enemy's ability to move or turn
           -> exploit a real local advantage or receive relief
              -> release, rally, and preserve the surviving body
```

Binding is not a magical root, taunt, or compulsory duel. The enemy can withdraw, turn, or disengage. The cost follows actual weapon contact, facing, space, fatigue, and pursuit opportunity. A mobile enemy that leaves before contact has not been bound.

The Warband may win its own fight. Holding an enemy in place is a meaningful success only if the wider battle benefits or the local contest itself matters.

## 15A. Three Forms of Binding

```yaml
FacingBind:
  effect: EnemyMustKeepUsefulFacingTowardTheSpearThreatOrExposeAWithdrawalTurnOrFlank
  limit: DoesNotPreventMovementOrders
SpaceBind:
  effect: ContactFrontAndPursuitRiskMakeImmediateRedeploymentCostly
  limit: RequiresRealFrontageAndProximity
ReserveBind:
  effect: EnemyMayNeedNearbySupportToPreventLocalLossOrCoverDisengagement
  limit: OpponentMayAcceptLossYieldGroundOrRefuseReinforcement
not_binding:
  - MagicalRoot
  - Taunt
  - ForcedDuel
  - EnemyCommandLockout
  - AutomaticSlowAura
```

The unit's effect is strongest when the enemy has somewhere else it wants to be. If the opponent is content to remain in the fight, the Warband is simply conducting an infantry contest and must win or survive it through actual combat conditions.

## 15B. Contact-Control Sequence

```text
Deliver a connected spear front
  -> establish enough reach and pressure to occupy enemy attention
     -> keep supporting files and leaders connected
        -> read whether the enemy holds, turns, reinforces, or yields
           -> exploit a genuine local advantage or preserve the useful contact
              -> release before the spear body becomes pinned beyond recovery
```

A missed approach creates no bind. A fragmented front creates several small fights. A compressed front loses much of the reach pressure. Binding is an outcome of successful formation work, not a button pressed before contact.

## 16. The Commitment Economy

The key resource is **useful contact time purchased without losing the ability to release**. A short engagement can be excellent if it prevents an enemy reserve from reaching the decisive sector. A long engagement can be wasteful if it occupies an irrelevant enemy while the host loses elsewhere.

The player decides whether to approach sooner, narrow the front, continue pressure, accept a local retreat, or seek relief. Staying longer is not automatically bravery or competence. Releasing early is not automatically failure.

No visible countdown or mandatory new resource bar is required. Ordinary formation, condition, leader, and route feedback should explain the bargain.

## 17. Approach and Contact Geometry

A useful approach delivers enough spear files together. A narrow path may shelter movement but create piecemeal contact. A broad route may establish a stronger engagement while exposing more fighters to missiles.

Contact frontage is the part physically able to threaten the enemy. Working depth sustains that frontage. Excess depth can become congestion rather than additional power. An enemy that shortens its line may deny the Warband the broad contact it wanted.

A release route must remain usable behind or beside the card. Friendly troops blocking that route can turn a manageable fight into a costly pin. The unit receives no friendly collision immunity.

## 21. Three Failure Stories

**The unanswered approach:** the enemy refuses early contact and shoots the advancing band. Spears cannot answer missile fire. Continuing because the unit is an assault card wastes the formation before its useful phase.

**The irrelevant engagement:** the Warband holds an enemy body that the opponent was content to leave there. The real enemy reserve attacks elsewhere. Contact succeeded physically but failed strategically.

**The late release:** the host begins to withdraw while the Warband remains compressed against fresh infantry. Its leaders cannot reconnect the front, and friendly traffic blocks the return. The unit's own commitment becomes the enemy's advantage.

## 22. Counterplay and Fairness

Refuse low-value contact, shorten frontage, use missiles during approach, protect the threatened body with a reserve, and threaten the Warband's side or return route. A controlled retreat may make the Warband overextend if it pursues without redressing.

The enemy must remain free to make these choices. No fear aura, regional damage multiplier, automatic shield destruction, compulsory enemy facing, or hidden stamina reserve is granted. A competent peer can contest the spears directly rather than solve every fight through a scripted counter.

## 24. Combined Arms

The Spear Host gives the battle width while the Warband commits one enemy body. Forest Bowmen can exploit an exposed target before contact and must respect friendly arcs afterward. Galwegian Javelinmen may pressure a neighboring enemy or cast before the Warband closes, but cannot shoot safely through its back.

Caterans can exploit the side of an occupied opponent. March Riders protect a threatened route or intercept light pursuers. Mormaer's Horsemen and feudal knights need a real mounted approach rather than a path through friendly spear files.

Scottish Armored Foot can relieve a damaging engagement. Highland Warband can take over a later organized ground-taking task. Neither becomes a mandatory escort for the regional spear card to function.

## 25. Independent Use and Whole-Battle Employment

The Warband can contest a secondary field objective, reinforce regional defense, counterattack ordinary foot already engaged, or protect a host shoulder without a perfect combined-arms script. It should not need an already routing opponent to be worth recruiting.

Opening: gather and choose a useful opponent rather than rush the nearest unit. Main battle: establish contact while keeping support and release visible. Late battle: decide whether holding the enemy still matters, whether a weak opponent can be beaten, or whether survivors should leave.

A depleted band may defend a smaller approach. It does not regain lost spear frontage because the battle is nearly over.

## 26. Terrain and Settlement Use

Rolling ground, open approaches, broad field boundaries, and sparse woodland can support a usable spear front. Dense woods, steep rubble, narrow gates, and severe broken ground reduce connection and weapon room. The unit receives no generic regional terrain immunity.

It may defend a broad lane, camp approach, or crossing exit. It is not a specialist ladder, breach, deep-street, or amphibious assault unit. A crossing attack into prepared long weapons is a poor mission. Existing obstacles can help; this card does not create barricades.

## 26B. Weather, Season, and Regional Service

Rain changes footing, shield handling, routes, and spear control. Fog may shorten approach exposure while damaging identification and shared timing. Snow and cold increase shelter and supply burdens. Heat shortens useful contact time. Seasonal service competes with local defense, food production, transport, and return obligations. No weather immunity is granted.

## 27. Command, Morale, and Fatigue

Recognized leaders connect contributing groups and explain when to close, halt, continue, or release. Leader loss may produce uneven action before rout. Confidence depends on support, equipment, manageable opposition, and a route back, not a permanent regional personality.

Approach, spear handling, shield carriage, contact, redressing, and withdrawal consume endurance. Long contact can leave a band unable to exploit a victory. Morale cannot replace missing leaders, weapons, or usable frontage. No compulsory last stand or attack order is built into the unit.

## 28. Service Geography and Political Access

Local and nearby service may be easier to assemble and recover. Longer campaigns remain possible where food, command, terms, and return are supported. No arbitrary tiny operating radius is imposed.

Political control does not automatically transfer every contributor's service. A changed relationship may close recruitment or replacement while survivors remain accounted. The profile does not create a Galloway mini-faction, autonomous diplomacy, automatic betrayal, or a universal allegiance rule.

## 29. Supply and Material Wear

Food, footwear, clothing, spear shafts, heads, shields, sidearms, repair, and transport sustain the band. Absence of ammunition is a practical advantage, not exemption from supply. Successful close fighting may damage the very equipment that made the unit useful.

Compatible replacement matters. Spare shafts do not replace lost leaders; returning people do not restore shield coverage. Exact weather effects belong to shared systems and are not invented here.

## 32. After-Battle Accounting and Veterans

Separate killed, wounded, missing, captured, dispersed, and returned people. Recover equipment only when ground control, time, condition, and compatibility permit. Missing contributors are not instantly replaced from the same pool.

Veterans improve target judgment, contact timing, and release recognition. They cannot occupy absent frontage. Rebuilt bands recover mass before collective familiarity. Recruitment and replacement may close separately; surviving veterans do not disappear when a pool closes.

Options include continued narrow service, compatible replacement, local defense, return for recovery, or dissolution with people, equipment, wounds, and history accounted. Demobilization is not erasure.
