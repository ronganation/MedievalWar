## Immediate Source Preservation Layer

### Overview

The source Scotland AI governs the Kingdom of Scotland during the prototype campaign and frames the opening as late 1061 or early 1062. It describes Scotland as stable, independent, regionally diverse, dynastically secure, and partially consolidated. It asks the AI to balance royal authority, regional cooperation, dynastic stability, religious legitimacy, internal development, and external security, while treating Scotland as a political ecosystem rather than a single ruler.

### Core Philosophy

The source presents Scotland as not fully centralized, with a strong Crown, influential regions, an important Church, and a secure dynasty. Its controlling strategic preference is consolidation rather than aggressive expansion, with the greatest opportunities lying inside Scotland.

### Strategic Objectives

The source priority order is preserved as provenance:

- Preserve Scotland.
- Protect Malcolm III.
- Maintain dynastic stability.
- Increase royal authority.
- Maintain Church relations.
- Integrate regional interests.
- Secure borders.
- Pursue opportunities when safe.

### Primary Centers of Power

The source identifies the Crown, House Dunkeld, House Moray, the Church, and Historical Memory as major evaluation surfaces. It associates Malcolm III with royal authority, House Dunkeld with legitimacy and continuity, House Moray with regional and historical importance, the Bishop of St Andrews with religious legitimacy and mediation, and Macbeth with historical memory and Moray precedent.

### Historical Momentum Evaluation

The source tracks royal consolidation, regional authority, Scottish stability, northern connections, and Anglo-Scottish relations. It recommends strengthening authority through cooperation, avoiding unnecessary conflict, maintaining legitimacy, using diplomacy, monitoring England, and exploiting opportunities carefully.

### Character Priorities

The source gives fixed priority and protection levels to Malcolm III, Ingibjorg Finnsdottir, Donald III, Duncan II, the Bishop of St Andrews, Máel Muire mac Donnchada, Máel Snechtai mac Lulaich, and Gille Coemgáin of Moray. These records are retained as legacy strategic intent, not current active rankings.

### Succession Evaluation

The source states Malcolm III as dynasty head, Duncan II as primary heir, Donald III as secondary successor, and succession stability as high. These claims are retained only as superseded provenance.

### Military Strategy

The source favors defensive readiness, royal security, regional stability, border security, internal stability, and regional defense, while avoiding reckless foreign adventures.

### Diplomatic Strategy

The source favors stability, diplomacy, strategic alliances, beneficial relationships, and preserved independence, with England, northern territories, and religious authorities as possible diplomatic areas.

### Civil Conflict Prevention

The source treats succession instability, regional dissatisfaction, Church opposition, and dynastic rivalry as warning signs, with negotiation, mediation, legitimacy work, and tension reduction as preferred responses.

### Character Development Integration

The source identifies Duncan II and Máel Snechtai mac Lulaich as development targets with military, administrative, and court development paths. This is retained as legacy intent but is no longer an AI assignment.

### Dynasty Integration

The source evaluates House Dunkeld health, House Moray influence, succession stability, prestige, and legitimacy, and states that strong dynasties contribute to a stable kingdom.

### Realm Governance Integration

The source prefers cooperation, then integration, then consolidation, while warning that rapid centralization may produce regional resistance.

### Narrative Arc Integration

The source defines Scottish Consolidation, House Dunkeld, House Moray, Succession, and Church Authority arcs. V2 preserves these as non-scripted strategy families rather than fixed goals.

### AI Personality

The source characterizes Scotland AI as pragmatic, stable, legitimacy-conscious, regionally aware, and long-term focused, preferring the accumulation of strength over unnecessary risk.

### Success and Failure Conditions

The source calls preservation of House Dunkeld, stability, successor development, regional integration, Church relations, and stronger royal authority successes. It calls dynastic collapse, succession crisis, regional fragmentation, loss of legitimacy, and civil war failures. V2 converts these into review dimensions rather than universal victory conditions.

### Notes

The source frames Scotland as beginning in transition rather than crisis, with transformation rather than mere survival as its challenge. It assigns strengthening goals to House Dunkeld, regional-preservation goals to House Moray, stability goals to the Church, and pressure generation to Historical Momentum. V2 retains the transition thesis while removing universal actor assumptions.

## 2. Concise Verdict

The source contains a strong Scotland-specific strategic thesis: build durable capacity through consolidation, cooperation, regional accommodation, dynastic continuity, defensive security, and institutional development. Its defects are structural rather than thematic. It converts qualitative importance into fixed priority rankings, treats the Crown, dynasties, Moray, and the Church too often as unitary actors, assigns succession positions unsupported by the current owner stack, treats <Person>Duncan II</Person> as a future heir rather than a protected YoungChild, and retains the historically misidentified Gille project node.

V2 preserves the strategic identity while rebuilding the AI as an owner-separated coordination authority. It reads authorized state, generates multiple alternatives, applies hard gates, evaluates feasibility and reversibility, requests owner commands, records deterministic reasoning, and reviews validated outcomes. - Effective royal direction.

## 12. Strategic Objective Hierarchy

- Preserve lawful kingship and minimum government.
- Protect authoritative records and valid owner state.
- Preserve protected persons without assigning adult authority.

### Tier Two: Defensive Security

- Identify credible threats.
- Verify command, forces, routes, sites, communication, Supply, and recovery.
- Protect civilians, households, institutions, and strategic access.
- Avoid unsupported concentration and overextension.

### Tier Three: Regional and Institutional Cooperation

- Negotiate issue-specific cooperation.
- Preserve Moray plurality.
- Maintain person, office, institution, region, dynasty, and household separation.
- Avoid destroying capacity before replacement exists.

### Tier Four: Continuity Preparation

- Review adult service options.
- Protect <Person>Duncan II</Person> through separate owner roles.
- Investigate succession, claim, recognition, and installation without preselection.
- Maintain handback, sunset, and rollback.

### Tier Five: Development and Recovery

- Improve law, records, offices, communication, sites, routes, property, markets, transport, Supply, and institutional capacity.
- Reserve civilian, household, and recovery capacity.

### Tier Six: Strategic Opportunity

- Consider diplomacy, contact, trade, frontier action, or military opportunity only after hard gates pass.
