## Document Status and Scope

This profile creates a bounded character-authoring source for Duncan II using the approved Scotland profile pattern, the Scotland preparation artifacts, the project interpretation already used for Malcolm III and Ingibjorg Finnsdottir, and external historical references supplied or verified for this task.

Duncan begins as a child. The profile therefore emphasizes identity, dependency, formation environment, household placement, education, protection, political visibility, cross-realm exposure, and future flexibility. It does not assign adult agency merely because Duncan later became king.

Later events, including the historically reported 1072 hostage transfer, Anglo-Norman formation, military career, 1094 kingship, and death, remain conditional future paths rather than opening facts.

## Core Character Thesis

Duncan II begins as a dynastically important child whose value lies in future possibility and whose danger lies in adults treating that possibility as present authority. Duncan can become a focus for protection, education, recognition, household competition, cross-realm diplomacy, and succession planning long before Duncan can understand or control those systems.

Duncan's central design problem is not what political strategy Duncan chooses at opening. The opening problem is what kind of person and future actor Duncan becomes under the choices of caregivers, protectors, educators, households, rulers, and foreign powers. Every adult who claims to secure Duncan may also shape Duncan's loyalties, language, political habits, evidence access, and later acceptance.

## Player-Facing Strategic Summary

Duncan is a long-horizon investment and vulnerability. Protecting Duncan can widen House Dunkeld's future options, but visibility may turn Duncan into a political instrument. Concealment can improve immediate safety while weakening future recognition. Education can build readiness while allowing the educating household to shape identity. A foreign guarantee can prevent violence while becoming hostage dependence.

The player should never issue adult commands through Duncan. The player instead shapes the environment around Duncan and accepts that Duncan's later choices may reflect, resist, or reinterpret that formation.

## Historical Identity

Duncan II, also rendered Donnchad mac Máel Coluim or Donnchadh mac Mhaoil Chaluim in modernized Gaelic forms, is represented as a son of Malcolm III. The project interpretation follows the Orkneyinga tradition in treating Ingibjorg Finnsdottir as Duncan's mother, while preserving that parentage and the status of Malcolm and Ingibjorg's union as historically contested in later sources.

Modern reference works place Duncan's birth around 1060 or, more cautiously, before 1072. For the late 1061 or January 1062 campaign opening, Duncan must therefore be represented as a very young child if active in the scenario. The exact birth date and age remain unresolved.

Historical sources and later reference works associate Duncan with a transfer into William I's keeping in 1072, long residence in the Anglo-Norman world, release or altered status after William I's death in 1087, and a later bid for Scottish kingship culminating in a short reign and death in 1094. None of those later states exists at opening.

## Source-Grounded Historical Outline

### Opening-relevant evidence

- Duncan is a son of Malcolm III.
- The project may use Ingibjorg Finnsdottir as Duncan's mother, with uncertainty preserved.
- Duncan was born before the documented 1072 hostage event and is commonly estimated to have been born around 1060.
- Duncan is therefore a child in the project opening context.
- Duncan later became politically significant enough to be used as a high-value guarantee and later to pursue Scottish kingship.

### Later historical evidence, conditional for simulation

- The Annals of Ulster record that Norman or “French” forces carried away a son of the king of Scotland as a hostage in 1072; modern reconstructions usually identify that child as Duncan.
- Duncan spent a substantial part of later formation in the Anglo-Norman political environment.
- Later references associate Duncan with knighthood and military service.
- Duncan later received English or Anglo-Norman backing in a bid against Donald III.
- Duncan held the Scottish kingship briefly in 1094 and died on 12 November 1094.

These later events establish plausible development possibilities. They do not establish opening personality, allegiance, claim activation, or destiny.

## Historical Uncertainty

### Parentage and status

The project interpretation treats Ingibjorg Finnsdottir as Duncan's mother. The Orkneyinga tradition supports that relationship, while some English and Scottish narrative traditions either omit Ingibjorg or characterize Duncan in ways that later served succession arguments. The profile must preserve the distinction among genealogy, marital recognition, legitimacy narratives, claims, and succession.

### Birth date and exact age

Duncan's exact birth date is unknown. “Circa 1060” is a reconstruction, not a certified date. The opening state may safely classify Duncan as a young child only if the project fixes an age band rather than inventing an exact birthday.

### Hostage identity

The 1072 annalistic statement describes a king's son taken as hostage but does not name Duncan in the cited wording. Duncan is the common and plausible identification, but the future event should retain evidentiary confidence rather than claim direct naming.

### Later formation

Later residence in William I's and then the Anglo-Norman world is historically significant, but the precise daily custody, education, household sequence, languages, mentors, emotional effects, and degree of coercion are not fully supplied by the current source set.

### Later kingship

Duncan's 1094 reign demonstrates one possible successful political activation. It does not prove that Duncan begins with an active claim, recognized heir status, English alignment, military readiness, or an inevitable path to kingship.

No single `GuardianId` or relationship may own every function. The following roles must remain separate and externally assigned if used:

Responsible for legal protection or representation within the owning authority. Guardianship does not automatically grant custody, education, estate administration, or political alignment.

Controls daily placement and access. Custody does not automatically create guardianship, claim authority, educational control, or affection.

### Educator or formation authority

Shapes learning, language, ritual, conduct, literacy, martial exposure, and political expectations. Education does not create custody, succession authority, or loyalty.

### Political protector

Uses influence or force to prevent transfer, isolation, coercion, or premature activation. Protection may create dependency or factional interpretation.

### Estate or resource administrator

Manages any property, revenue, or support assigned to Duncan. No estate is created here.

### Dynasty representative

Speaks for House Dunkeld interests concerning Duncan only through valid dynasty authority. Dynasty representation does not create personal consent.

### Ecclesiastical or ceremonial sponsor

May support baptismal, educational, moral, ceremonial, evidentiary, or recognition functions. No specific cleric or institution is assigned.

## Formation Narrative

Duncan begins before the historically famous part of Duncan's formation. The opening character is not yet the Anglo-Norman-trained adult who later returns with external support. Duncan begins at the point when household decisions can still determine whether that later identity emerges at all.

A stable Scottish and northern household may give Duncan language, kinship knowledge, trust in local political forms, and familiarity with Ingibjorg's wider family world. A court-centered education may tie Duncan more closely to Malcolm's project of consolidation. A protected but hidden childhood may reduce immediate danger while leaving Duncan without public familiarity or adult relationships.

A future hostage transfer would be a profound formation transition. Residence in an Anglo-Norman court could expose Duncan to military training, aristocratic customs, languages, law, patronage, and external political expectations. It could also produce isolation from Scotland, dependency on foreign patrons, uncertainty about belonging, and later difficulty translating external support into domestic acceptance.

The simulation should not assume one predictable response to displacement. Duncan may adapt, assimilate, resist, combine identities, distrust all patrons, or use cross-cultural formation creatively. Childhood experience creates pressures and capabilities, not destiny.

## Hostage and Cross-Realm Formation Interface

The historically reported 1072 transfer is one of Duncan's most important possible future transitions, but it must not be a date-triggered script.

A valid hostage or guarantee event requires:

- a conflict or settlement capable of producing the demand;
- identified sender and receiving authority;
- a diplomatic purpose;
- coercion and consent treatment;
- custody and access terms;
- safety guarantees;

- education and religious practice terms;
- communication with family;
- duration and review;

Being a hostage does not mean Duncan becomes personal property. A receiving ruler may control physical custody while educators, household officers, clergy, and political patrons shape different aspects of life.

## Operational Interfaces and Consequences

### Daily care

Duncan requires stable food, shelter, health care, attendants, supervision, and safe routines. Major political importance cannot compensate for failed daily care.

### Household access

Who can see Duncan affects information, attachment, influence, and risk. Restricted access may improve security while enabling one custodian to monopolize formation.

### Travel

Travel requires custody authority, escort, route security, seasonal planning, supplies, destination, and contingency. Moving Duncan for ceremony, safety, diplomacy, or education should have distinct costs.

### Communication

Messages about Duncan may be delayed, curated, or weaponized. When Duncan is too young to communicate independently, adults may claim to speak for Duncan.

### Education

Education consumes time, trusted personnel, language access, texts or oral instruction, safe practice, and continuity. Competing educational programs may create divided expectations rather than universal readiness.

### Protection

Visible guards can deter abduction while signaling political importance. Concealed protection can reduce attention while weakening public status. Political protection may conflict with the child's daily welfare.

### Replacement capacity

No one adult should own every underage function. Removing one protector should not automatically remove custody, education, household continuity, and estate administration. The campaign should reward distributed safeguards with clear accountability.

## Conditional Development and Transformation

### Scottish Court Formation

**Conditions:** stable placement near Malcolm's court, safe access, suitable educators, and continuing Scottish relationships.

**Duncan-specific development:** familiarity with royal ceremony, Scottish political expectations, household actors, and regional diversity.

**Possible consequence:** stronger domestic recognition, with risk of overpoliticized childhood.

### Northern and Orcadian Formation

**Conditions:** active connection through Ingibjorg, Paul, Erlend, or approved northern households.

**Duncan-specific development:** familiarity with maritime, Norse-Gaelic, and cross-realm kinship contexts.

**Possible consequence:** broader northern connection without automatic Orcadian support.

### Anglo-Norman Hostage Formation

**Conditions:** a valid future treaty or coercive settlement produces transfer.

**Duncan-specific development:** adaptation to an external court, possible language and military-cultural exposure, new patrons, and distance from Scotland.

**Possible consequence:** strong cross-cultural readiness accompanied by domestic acceptance risk and dependence on foreign support.

### Protected but Politically Hidden Path

**Conditions:** high threat and deliberate reduction of visibility.

**Duncan-specific development:** greater immediate safety, narrow social formation, and dependence on a small household.

**Possible consequence:** survival with weaker recognition and fewer independent relationships.

### Public Dynastic Child Path

**Conditions:** ceremonial presentation, broad access, and consensus that visibility improves continuity.

**Duncan-specific development:** familiarity with public expectation and early political scrutiny.

**Possible consequence:** stronger recognition and increased factional use.

### Balanced Development Path

**Conditions:** separate caregivers, educators, protectors, and dynasty representatives cooperate without one actor monopolizing Duncan.

**Duncan-specific development:** broader attachments, better information exposure, and reduced capture by one political narrative.

**Possible consequence:** resilient later agency, with coordination costs among adults.

### Failure: Captured Future

**Conditions:** one household, ruler, or faction controls custody, education, access, and political narrative.

**Possible consequence:** Duncan survives but develops under severe dependency and later struggles to act independently.

### Failure: Symbol Before Person

**Conditions:** adults publicly mobilize Duncan's name before age, evidence, recognition, and readiness permit meaningful participation.

**Possible consequence:** factional danger, reduced safety, distorted education, and future resentment or dependence.

### Failure: Neglected Dynast

**Conditions:** Duncan is protected physically but receives weak education, inconsistent access, and no preparation.

**Possible consequence:** survival without readiness, leaving others to rule through or around Duncan.

### Later Adult Activation Path

**Conditions:** Duncan reaches adulthood, develops relevant readiness, possesses valid relationships and resources, and a separate Claim or Succession Authority establishes a lawful or politically viable route.

**Possible consequence:** service, candidacy, settlement, military leadership, kingship, exile, or refusal. The historical 1094 path is one branch, not a forced result.

## Person-Centered Development Dilemmas

These hooks focus on Duncan's welfare and formation even when adults make the formal decisions.

### A Child Shown to the Realm

Malcolm considers presenting Duncan publicly. Visibility may improve recognition, but the ceremony turns a child into a political signal. The choice changes who gains access and what expectations begin forming around Duncan.

### Ingibjorg's Household or the Royal Court

Adults dispute where Duncan should live. One placement offers maternal and northern continuity; another offers proximity to Malcolm's government. Duncan cannot decide the constitutional question, but attachment, language, routine, and later trust will reflect the outcome.

### Donald as Protector

A crisis makes Donald the most available adult dynast. Giving Donald a protection role may strengthen continuity while allowing others to interpret Donald and Duncan as competing branches or a joint alternative center.

### The Foreign Guarantee

A settlement with an external ruler demands a royal child as security. Sending Duncan may avoid immediate war. Duncan's safety, education, identity, and future relationships become dependent on terms Duncan cannot negotiate.

### The Educator's Language

Caregivers disagree over the languages, customs, and political narratives Duncan should learn first. The decision affects future access without determining permanent identity.

### A Claim Spoken Over the Cradle

Supporters describe Duncan as the future king before a Claim or Succession Authority exists. Protectors must choose whether to silence the language, preserve evidence privately, seek review, or accept the safety risks of public recognition.

### News from Orkney

Paul or Erlend offers protection, education, or household connection. The opportunity may broaden Duncan's family world while increasing distance from Malcolm's court and creating expectations of future alignment.

### The Child Returns Changed

After a future period away, Duncan returns with new language, habits, patrons, and expectations. Scottish actors must decide whether difference signals valuable experience or foreign capture. Duncan must later decide which parts of each formation to retain.

## Final Design Summary

Duncan II is Scotland's protected-underage benchmark: a child whose later historical significance must not be used to grant adult opening agency. Duncan's campaign value lies in the interaction among safety, household placement, education, recognition, cultural formation, foreign leverage, and future readiness.

The design succeeds when Duncan can emerge into several plausible adult identities shaped by experience without becoming a passive token or predetermined king. The design fails if Duncan is treated as a small adult claimant, if every underage responsibility is bundled into one guardian, or if the 1072 hostage transfer and 1094 kingship activate merely because historical dates arrive.
