## 1. Purpose and design decision

**One Scotland, two compatible High-era strategic emphases: Crown and Ordered Realm; Western Sea and Norse-Gaelic Integration.**

The design question is not whether Scotland remains Scottish or becomes Norse. It is how a continuing Scottish Realm coordinates its many military sources and how far it incorporates western and island relationships into its strategic reach.

The Crown can order the hosts of the land. It can also make the western sea-road an instrument of Scottish power. These developments can coexist, compete over specific resources, and reinforce one another when the player actually supports the combination.

The intended result is faction variation within Scotland, not two replacement factions. A different Scottish campaign should feel different because of its geography, political agreements, institutions, service networks, and choices. It should not need a permanent cultural switch or a universal national combat modifier to do so.

## 2. Faction identity and Modern Medieval II expression

**Many hosts, one battle plan. Land and sea, one kingdom.**

Scotland remains an infantry-centered kingdom assembled from common hosts, regional fighting traditions, magnate followings, royal service, practical mounted capacity, and scarce maintained or knightly formations. The western portfolio adds a different way of assembling and supporting that army, not a foreign roster pasted onto it.

The campaign spine remains recognizable: settlements, finite recruitment pools, army composition, strategic movement, diplomacy, service obligations, recovery, and decisive real-time battles. Population informs capacity without requiring the player to administer individual households. Research and decisions unlock capabilities; they do not create contributors, weapons, horses, vessels, crews, officers, or cooperation.

The player should understand a choice through its military payoff, visible opportunity cost, and political consequences. Detailed source accounting supports that choice behind the interface. It must not become a second game of filling forms before an army can sail.

## 3. Historical foundation and uncertainty

The historical inspiration is a Scotland shaped by both institutional change and longstanding northern and western maritime connections. The consulted Scandinavian Scotland overview describes Norse-held or influenced territories including the Northern Isles, Hebrides, Clyde islands, Caithness, and Sutherland, and Norse-Gaelic influence extending into Argyll and Galloway. It also warns that early documentation is weak and competing reconstructions exist. [H1]

The consulted Davidian Revolution overview associates David I's reign with burgh foundations, changes to government, feudal relationships, and immigrant knightly participation. It explicitly presents institutional change as modification of an existing Gaelic setting rather than complete erasure. [H2]

These observations support the broad creative direction. They do not verify a single Scottish western policy, a uniform sea-levy institution, exact regional boundaries, a particular unit's equipment, or the proposed mechanics. Nor do they make the Northern Isles, Hebrides, Galloway, and mainland western lordships one politically interchangeable source.

The authority does not schedule historical conquests, treaties, invasions, or dynastic outcomes as guaranteed events. Exact historical interfaces require their own evidence and current world-state treatment.

An emphasis is an accumulated pattern of supported choices, not necessarily a visible numerical meter. The interface can describe Scotland as Crown-led, western-maritime-led, or broadly integrated without making that description a magical source of benefits.

Specific agreements may conflict. One harbor's available capacity, one lord's service, one veteran cadre, or one treasury commitment cannot be spent twice. Such conflicts should be local and intelligible. A decision concerning one western source must not close all royal development across the kingdom.

England's scenario-specific cultural question is not copied. Scotland's identity is the integration of different military and political channels within one persistent Realm.

## 5. Shared Early roots and High-era opportunity

The shared foundation includes common hosts and local obligations, Highland and western sources, forest and March service, Galwegian contributions, magnate followings, uneven equipment, and limited coordinated reach. Existing High-era units retain their settled High placement; this account does not move maintained armored foot or royal spear retainers back into Early.

High is the principal era of strategic development because institutions and service networks can become more coherent and support larger ambitions. It is not an absolute prohibition on earlier success. A strong Early Scotland that already has actual ships, agreements, crews, routes, and support can attempt an appropriate expedition.

Conversely, reaching High does not supply those inputs. An isolated or politically divided High Scotland may remain maritime-limited. Both cases should be possible without cheating or contradictory era rules.

## 6. Crown and Ordered Realm: player promise

**“Make the many hosts answer to a more coordinated Crown.”**

The player invests in common-host leadership, reliable assembly, maintained equipment, repair, royal receiving authority, castle support, burgh connections, and predictable campaign service. The payoff is the ability to sustain an army's plan across movement, contact, relief, and recovery.

This portfolio should support deliberate inland campaigning and the concentration of scarce reliable forces. It is not simply a defensive route. Ordered hosts can carry a front forward; maintained foot can sustain pressure; royal formations can restore a critical seam; knights can deliver a decisive mounted blow.

The defining reward is predictability under supported conditions. It is not free obedience, uniform equipment, unlimited feudal troops, or superiority in every isolated unit duel.

## 7. Crown portfolio: capabilities and burdens

| Development area | Proposed military payoff | Real burden or limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Common-host coordination | More dependable assembly, movement, halting, and restoration | Leaders, practice, service time, equipment compatibility |
| Maintained foot | Better sustained hard-sector work and relief | Mail, repair, trained cadres, supply, expensive losses |
| Royal service | More reliable critical-point intervention | Scarce receiving capacity and competing royal duties |
| Castle and burgh support | Stores, repair, reception, records, and local support where present | Institutions, labor, finance, and geographic coverage |
| Knightly networks | Scarce formed mounted impact | Riders, suitable horses, equipment, feed, and recovery |

Political cost arises when particular powers lose privileges, officers displace existing arrangements, or service demands exceed accepted obligations. It must be tied to affected actors and agreements. Centralization does not automatically create a universal inland public-order bonus or penalty.

## 8. Crown portfolio: existing unit relationships

- **Ordered Spear Host:** coordination, section leadership, supported assembly, and equipment maintenance can improve qualified access and recovery. Advance the Hedge and Wall of Ash remain controlled by the unit profile.
- **Scottish Armored Foot:** maintained-service relationships and repair networks can support the Iron Shoulder. They do not create mail or a veteran company by decree.
- **King's Spear Retainers:** royal receiving authority, leadership continuity, and supported deployment can strengthen availability. Royal selection remains finite.
- **Scottish Feudal Knights:** actual knightly land or household service, horses, practice, and support remain required. A royal reform is not a knight generator.
- **Selkirk Forest Bowmen:** supported service, ammunition, leaders, and recovery can benefit developed field archery. The portfolio does not create a new bow type or universal accuracy bonus.

These are integration directions, not edits already applied to the profiles. None of these units becomes exclusive to a pure Crown build merely to exaggerate the contrast.

## 9. Western Sea and Norse-Gaelic Integration: player promise

**“Make the western sea-road part of Scotland's military system.”**

The player cultivates qualified island and coastal relationships, negotiates service, develops maritime assembly and reception, supports vessels and crews, and learns to sustain forces beyond an inland march route.

The payoff is geographic reach: coastal intervention, island campaigning, landing and extraction, supply and relief, and the ability to threaten a different approach. The player may use the sea to support an inland war rather than pursue an overseas conquest.

This is not the primitive or disorderly alternative to a civilized Crown. It is a different form of organization with its own authorities, obligations, expertise, and logistical strengths. Norse-Gaelic is historical inspiration and source context, not a universal bravery, aggression, or loyalty statistic.

## 10. Western portfolio: capabilities and burdens

| Development area | Proposed military payoff | Real burden or limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Island and coastal agreements | Qualified access, reception, guides, or contingents | Negotiation, privileges, service limits, rival claims |
| Maritime assembly | More reliable concentration at suitable departure points | Actual people, crews, vessels, provisions, and time |
| Coastal transport | Move around some inland constraints | Capacity, weather, interception, navigation, access |
| Landing organization | Better preparation and reformation | Vulnerable transit, surf, congestion, enemy opposition |
| Western recovery | Supported return, repair, and source continuity | Available facilities, accepted service, losses, routes |
| Sea-road campaigning | Reach distant objectives and create new fronts | Supply, reception, reserves, return, and political exposure |

A maritime network may be excellent along its supported coast and poor deep inland. That difference should arise from the actual supply and replacement network, not an arbitrary penalty attached to every western soldier.

## 11. Western portfolio: existing unit relationships

- **Highland Warband:** suitable western sources may gain supported coastal assembly, transport, and return. The unit remains organized regional ground-taking infantry, not an automatic Norse axe guard.
- **Highland Bow Retainers:** particular qualified western or island sources may support retained missile service. Bows, guard files, and the normal mission remain as defined in the profile.
- **Galwegian Warband:** southwestern maritime relationships may support coastal deployment and return. The unit does not automatically become a ship crew or duplicate a future Isles heavy-foot family.
- **Cateran families:** particular routes and coastal missions can gain support. Not every Cateran source is maritime-qualified.
- **March Riders:** little direct benefit is assumed. Where transport is proposed, horses and their support consume real capacity. Frontier service remains a different military strength.

A unit's name or cultural association does not certify sailing skill. Existing native ownership remains unchanged, including Galwegian Javelinmen and Galwegian Warband. Galloway Spear Warband remains the separately governed Britannia regional profile.

## 12. Mixed development and integrated Scotland

A Crown-led Scotland can buy or negotiate maritime reach. A western-led Scotland can develop ordered hosts, royal service, and maintained infantry. The integrated kingdom is a legitimate aspiration, not a forbidden compromise.

It is demanding because the player must support both sets of actual commitments. Some investments reinforce one another: reliable stores can help an expedition; a cooperative harbor can support royal transport; a successful return can improve future willingness. Other investments compete for finance, leaders, labor, repair, or available service.

There is no blanket hybrid surcharge. Slower progress is an expected consequence where the same scarce resources are divided, not a mandatory penalty if the player has genuinely expanded capacity. A wealthy, well-governed Scotland can become broadly capable without being granted costless maximum benefits.

## 13. Decision design and selective exclusivity

The following are authored decision templates, not historical events or activated buttons:

| Decision template | Potential gain | Specific tradeoff |
|---|---|---|
| Recognize a western service compact | Defined transport or contingent access | Privileges, obligations, duration, recall, or political concessions |
| Extend royal equipment support | More reliable repair and service readiness | Treasury, materials, workshops, and competing priorities |
| Reserve coastal capacity for an expedition | Credible transport and return support | Less capacity for another task during the reservation |
| Integrate a receiving command | Better coordination for agreed service | Appointments, accepted authority, and practice |
| Renew or revise a compact | Restore or expand valid rights | New terms, grievances, or changed political conditions |

Exclusivity applies only where two promises cannot both be honored. It can end through renegotiation, expiry, replacement capacity, or a changed world state. It is not a permanent prohibition on the other development portfolio.

## 14. Political autonomy, diplomacy, and rival powers

The western sea is a political network, not an empty movement corridor. Sources can have their own rulers, obligations, allies, rivals, and competing claims. Scotland's relationship with one source does not grant rights over all western or northern territories.

Recognized access, alliance, negotiated contribution, lordship, occupation, and integration must remain distinct. Territorial possession does not instantly create cooperation. Nor does cultural affinity transfer ownership of people, ships, equipment, or replacement capacity.

A maritime emphasis may expose Scotland to Norwegian, island, and other regional political competition. It can also create opportunities for cooperation. No permanent war with Norway or inherited ethnic hostility is required. The player's expedition can be directed elsewhere, or the sea-road can remain a defensive and commercial support network.

## 16. Finite recruitment, crews, and conservation

Use finite recruitment and recovery capacity informed by actual sources. Avoid both unlimited recruitment spam and a direct population-to-army subtraction model imposed by this authority.

One qualified person, equipment lot, horse, vessel, or crew assignment cannot be committed to incompatible simultaneous uses. Where sources do not actually overlap, do not invent a shared pool merely because two units are culturally related.

Protected minimums include essential local defense, survival and production, repair, transport, and existing military obligations. One healthy bounded use need not devastate a source. Repeated extraction, long absence, casualties, lost equipment, blocked return, and political conflict can narrow future access.

A ship crew can provide a landing party only when vessel operation, custody, and return remain viable or the player knowingly accepts the restriction. The same full crew cannot be counted ashore and aboard at once.

## 17. Vessels, ports, routes, and transport

Birlinns, galleys, and other suitable vessels are candidate components of a later Scottish maritime portfolio, not ships created by this file. Vessel capabilities, chronology, crew requirements, transport loads, and naval combat belong to their eventual profiles and shared naval systems.

An operation requires suitable vessels, available crews, accepted service, embarkation access, reception or a feasible landing, provisions, repair, and a return or alternative recovery arrangement. Ports, beaches, lochs, firths, river mouths, and open-sea routes are not interchangeable.

The Western portfolio can improve access and organization around those inputs. It does not grant unrestricted inland river navigation, automatic blockade bypass, or teleportation between coasts. Portage is conditional on a suitable vessel, feasible route, labor, time, security, and system support.

## 18. Landing, coastal intervention, and battlefield identity

A landing is an operation with exposure and recovery, not an amphibious immunity trait. Troops disembark through actual space, move equipment, establish facing, and reform. Enemy missiles, cavalry, infantry, weather, terrain, and congestion can disrupt that process.

Experience and preparation can improve execution relative to an unprepared operation. They cannot nullify surf, mud, shallow water, fatigue, or hostile contact. A coastal force needs useful employment after landing and a plan if the intended shore is denied.

Coastal intervention can mean relief, reinforcement, evacuation, a raid, seizure of a route, or opening another front. It need not mean a decisive beach assault. The military reward is choosing where and when to appear with support, not winning automatically once ashore.

## 19. Service closure, return, and recovery

When an agreement ends or access closes, deployed formations and vessels persist as actual campaign assets. Determine whether they complete agreed service, lose replacement access, receive recall, negotiate continued service, return, recover elsewhere, or dissolve through the owning systems.

Record people, leaders, equipment, ships, crews, prisoners, missing personnel, and losses at the level the shared systems support. A victorious expedition does not instantly restore a damaged source. A timely withdrawal may preserve more future capability than a costly conquest.

Rebuilding requires compatible replacements, repair, leadership, practice, and accepted support. Political trust can be affected by honoring return terms, losses, unpaid obligations, or conduct, but this authority does not invent a global loyalty score or guaranteed defection script.

## 20. Existing roster and battlefield interlock

The portfolio operates through the established army, not outside it. Common spears provide width; Ordered Spear Host carries a connected front; Armored Foot sustains a hard sector; royal retainers restore the critical point; regional and magnate infantry exploit a local opportunity; missile families shape exposure; practical cavalry screens; knights deliver scarce impact.

A western expedition still needs those complementary roles. Transporting an army does not remove its battlefield weaknesses. A Crown-led inland army still benefits from coastal supply or an evacuation option where available.

No passive synergy aura is granted for choosing the right mixture. The advantage comes from actual routes, frontage, supply, support, timing, and command. Detailed unit profiles remain controlling for their own equipment, power contracts, and battle loops.

## 21. Hebridean or Western Isles retainer candidate

Preserve **Hebridean Sea Retainers** and **Western Isles Retainers** as alternative working names for one possible future family, not two admitted units.

The proposed purchase is selected western maritime contact infantry: supported deployment followed by a short, meaningful heavy-foot commitment. Equipment might include strong shield use or a selective heavy-weapon expression, meaningful mail among selected followers, and source-appropriate axes, spears, and sidearms. No universal two-handed axe package is certified.

Admission requires a valid retained-service source, defensible chronology and equipment, a distinct campaign burden, and a battlefield role not already adequately owned by Highland Warband, Scottish Armored Foot, or Galwegian Warband. The unit must remain useful when no landing is taking place.

Compare it with Galloglaich and Galloway Spear Warband as well. Similar weapons or a shared broad combat category are allowed. Duplicating the same normal purchase with a maritime adjective is not enough. Do not import galloglass backward, claim a direct lineage without evidence, or turn crews into elite infantry by default.

## 23. Optional objectives, milestones, and achievements

Milestones give the player reasons to attempt interesting campaigns without prescribing every route. They are optional authored challenges, not normal-victory prerequisites or evidence of historical intent.

Distinguish three presentation layers: a campaign objective proposes a current task; a milestone recognizes a meaningful campaign result; an achievement may recognize an outcome across playthroughs if a later platform and tracking design supports it. This authority does not implement an achievement service.

Recognition should focus on an outcome and necessary integrity conditions, not on choosing a named emphasis. A western-led Scotland may find a maritime challenge easier, but a Crown-led or mixed Scotland can qualify through genuine preparation. Players may discover solutions the designer did not anticipate.

## 25. Reverse the Sea-Road: campaign design

The central adventure is to turn a route of exposure into a route of Scottish agency. Scotland can survive pressure, establish maritime relationships, assemble real reach, and carry war toward island or Scandinavian centers when the campaign permits.

The objective is not collective revenge against a people. It does not require a fixed Norwegian enemy, an ethnic combat bonus, or a scripted historical grievance. It recognizes the player's reversal of strategic vulnerability through supported action.

A western emphasis may reduce practical barriers through relationships and coastal support. A Crown emphasis may supply finance, maintained forces, and sustained logistics. A mixed approach may combine them. None is automatically superior in every world state.

Merely entering a destination with a disposable unit should not satisfy a milestone intended to recognize sustained reach. Equally, the designer should not force total conquest if a bounded expedition, negotiated foothold, or another supported outcome better fits the chosen challenge. The exact predicate must be settled in a later objective-design pass.

## 26. Scenario scope and connected Theaters

Treat a Theater as a geographic and content context, not an assumed separate multiplayer server. Whether Britannia and Scandinavian destinations are connected depends on the actual campaign scenario and authorized travel systems.

If a destination and supported travel exist, the objective can be eligible. If the map omits the destination or travel is unavailable, hide or clearly mark the optional objective unavailable. It must never block ordinary victory.

A Britannia-only campaign can still use western development for island operations, coastal defense, relief, and local sea-road campaigning. Do not invent off-map conquest, naval transit, or Scandinavian ownership to make an achievement possible. Scenario designers may author a separate local milestone without claiming it is the same overseas accomplishment.

## 27. Early success, campaign pacing, and alternate starts

A strong Early Scotland may pursue maritime reach if it actually qualifies. Do not hold a feasible player adventure behind an arbitrary High label. High remains the main development focus because the design expects broader coordination and sustained support to become important there.

Core capabilities must become available while they can still affect the campaign. Avoid long chains that deliver Scotland's identity only after the outcome is already decided. A failed western lane must not block the core land roster; a delayed Crown lane must not delete existing regional strengths.

Additional campaign starts remain a separate scenario decision. This authority preserves the possibility of different initial conditions without selecting another date, calibrating era duration, or requiring the base game to include every start. The Early start continues to support the full developmental arc.

## 28. Late-era convergence and persistent differences

Late development can share technologies and weapon families without erasing earlier strategic choices. Do not create separate Norse-Gaelic firearms or mutually exclusive technological universes merely to preserve a branch diagram.

Crown-oriented development may leave stronger centralized supply, burgh and royal support, maintained formations, and siege or garrison organization. Western-oriented development may leave stronger ports, island relationships, coastal reception, maritime assembly, and intervention support.

Pikes, artillery, later missile systems, professional or retained service, naval development, and more standardized supply remain under their own historical and system authorities. Their eventual access can reflect the player's institutions and geography. This is a proposed convergence model, not a claim that all historical armies became identical.

## 29. Campaign AI and strategic choice

AI evaluates threats, geography, current relationships, source health, available finance, repair, ships, crews, military missions, and recovery. It can emphasize Crown development, western integration, a mixed program, or postponement.

A maritime opportunity is valuable only if the AI can support departure, reception, useful service, and return or a defensible alternative. Do not send the entire army overseas to complete optional flavor while the homeland collapses. Conversely, do not make the AI permanently passive when a feasible intervention would materially help its war.

The AI obeys the same access and conservation rules as the player. It cannot create ships, ignore local retention, duplicate a crew, teleport a recalled force, or receive free cooperation because a campaign plan requests it.

## 30. Player feedback and faction presentation

Use faction selection, history panels, campaign introductions, decision text, optional objectives, and encyclopedia entries to express the identity. Music and visual direction can support land-and-sea contrast, but no actual score or asset is commissioned by this file.

Suggested authored faction line: **Order the hosts of the land. Bind the roads of the western sea. A strong Scotland may learn to do both.**

A decision should communicate its main benefit, affected source or relationship, actual cost, missing input, and persistence consequence. For example, a coastal commitment may be blocked by insufficient crew rather than a vague missing technology. Show the decisive blocker without exposing every internal record by default.

Flavor never conceals a bonus. Removing the epithet or objective title must not change the underlying rules. Scotland's adventure comes from supported choices and their outcomes, not a secret national modifier.

## 31. Modularity and modding intent

The design should be data-oriented where later implementation supports it: development portfolios, relationship conditions, objective predicates, scenario eligibility, presentation text, and candidate unit links should be separable from hard-coded ethnic branches.

A future scenario or total conversion can reuse the pattern without copying Scotland's historical sources. This is an implementation intention, not a verified modding API or promise that a specific editor already exists.

The base game must provide a coherent and enjoyable Scottish campaign without requiring mods to repair its balance or complete its identity. Modding should enable alternate starts, settings, objectives, and creative extensions, not excuse unfinished core systems.
