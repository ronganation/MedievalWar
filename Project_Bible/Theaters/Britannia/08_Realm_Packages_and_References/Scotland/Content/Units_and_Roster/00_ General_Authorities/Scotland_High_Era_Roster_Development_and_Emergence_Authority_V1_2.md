## 2. Scotland's High-era military promise

**Many hosts, one battle plan.** Scotland should gain the ability to assemble an increasingly formidable army from unequal sources without losing the regional variety and infantry-centered character that make the faction memorable.

The common spear front creates space and time. Selected royal and magnate troops preserve or overturn the important local fight. Highland formations deliver regional fighting weight. Forest and Cateran archers shape exposure in different ways. Practical horsemen screen and pursue; knights deliver scarce decisive shock. Burghs, castles, and western maritime service add capabilities that do not need to descend from an existing Early card.

The High army should feel more capable, not merely more restricted. Its rewards include a spear line that can reposition deliberately, regional foot that can win ground rather than only raid, armored infantry that can carry sustained fighting, and credible mounted punishment when the enemy mishandles its front.

Its enduring costs are finite recruitment, uneven equipment, competing service obligations, limited quality horses, and recovery that depends on real leaders and source relationships. These costs must support interesting decisions, not bury the player under household accounting.

## 3. Creative development principles

```yaml
development_modes:
  persistence: ExistingFamilyRemainsAUsefulRecruitmentChoice
  maturation: ExistingFamilyImprovesWithoutANewCard
  progression: NewFamilyOffersAMateriallyDifferentBattlefieldChoice
  convergence: QualifiedInputsCanSupportOneLaterFamilyWithoutDuplication
  emergence: NewCapabilityCanAppearWithoutAnEarlyParent
  retirement: FamilyCanCloseToNewRecruitmentWhileSurvivorsPersist
  package_only: VisualOrEquipmentVariationDoesNotRequireAnotherCard
```

Progression is not an experience-star conversion. Experienced soldiers may qualify for another family, but institutional access, equipment, leaders, service terms, and training must exist. A technology can unlock the ability to organize a formation; it cannot create the people, horses, weapons, or officers.

Creativity is encouraged. A distinct unit can be justified by an especially strong weapon identity, formation behavior, recruitment source, or campaign bargain without requiring a completely unprecedented historical institution. The test is whether the complete combination produces a different normal player choice. Do not demand absolute uniqueness in every attribute.

## 5. Common-host progression: the central new unit

### Ordered Spear Host

**Proposed player promise:** carry a broad spear front through deliberate movement, halt, reception, redressing, and renewed use without losing the connection between its parts.

The Early Scottish Spear Host remains cheaper and broadly available. Ordered Spear Host pays more in training, leaders, service preparation, and recovery for better coordinated movement and frontage restoration. It must be visibly useful in an ordinary field battle, not only during an ideal relief transaction.

```yaml
OrderedSpearHost:
  design_status: NextNewUnitCandidate
  primary_role: CoordinatedCommonSpearFront
  useful_actions:
    - AdvanceAndHaltWithConnectedFiles
    - ReceiveOrdinaryPressure
    - RestoreDamagedFacingAndFrontage
    - SupportNeighboringHostSections
    - ConductMeasuredForwardPressure
  weaknesses:
    - MissilePreparationAndCrossfire
    - HeavyCloseAssaultAfterCompression
    - SeveralDirectionPressure
    - DifficultGroundAndRepeatedTurning
    - LossOfLeadersAndWorkingDepth
  not_a_standing_professional_army: true
  not_a_pike_card: true
```

Do not make coordination an invisible blanket bonus. Show it through order response, fewer isolated files, more coherent halts, and the ability to recover a usable front. Exact timings and numerical advantages remain for later testing.

### Schiltron Spearmen

A later specialization within the planned High development, not a synonym for all Scottish spear infantry. Their reward is stronger dense frontal resistance and a formation the enemy must solve through missiles, maneuver, terrain, or coordinated pressure. Their costs include constrained turning, exposure, fatigue, and danger when isolated.

Schiltron is a formation/doctrine concept, not automatically proof of a separate troop population. A separate card is justified only if qualification, training, service, and normal battlefield use differ enough from Ordered Spear Host. Otherwise, use a formation qualification within that family. Royal Schiltron Guard remains an optional selected-service idea, not a promised endpoint.

## 7. Highland and magnate development

Highland Warband's reward is a connected regional fighting body capable of entering an infantry contest, winning bounded ground, and holding long enough for consolidation or relief. Its weapon mixture should remain readable and organized, not grant every soldier every weapon advantage.

Cateran Axemen retain their lighter route-led attack and breakaway identity. Hill and Glen Muster retains accessible rough-ground support and obstruction. Neither must disappear because Highland Warband exists.

Seasoned Caterans can be an improved expression of Cateran Axemen rather than a separate card. A new card needs a changed operational role, source, or service burden. Highland Retainers can offer more dependable selected regional service, but must not duplicate Mormaer's Swordsmen.

Mormaer's Swordsmen and Mormaer's Horsemen persist as magnate-facing families. Better equipment and longer service can mature them. Do not invent a third generic retinue card for symmetry. Foot and mounted assignments may compete where actual people overlap, but do not assume all swordsmen are dismounted horsemen.

## 8. Foot archers and skirmishers

Forest Bowmen remain the dedicated bow line. Cateran Bowmen remain the mobile hybrid whose rear-guard work costs shooting output. Galwegian Javelinmen remain Scotland-owned close-cast and conditional-press troops.

A High missile roster should become more capable without becoming an imitation of English mass longbows. Candidate rewards include better fire discipline, stronger fieldcraft, selected protection, more dependable ammunition support, and a rare archer retinue able to survive brief close pressure.

missile_candidates:

Crossbows supplement the existing archery families. They do not replace every bowman. An expensive hybrid cannot retain full dedicated shooting output and full assault-infantry power simultaneously.

## 10. Cavalry development

March Riders remain useful practical cavalry. Border Horse is a proposed mature frontier-service development: longer operational use, stronger interception and pursuit judgment, and better repeated response. It must not simply add armor and charge power until it becomes a cheap knight.

Mormaer's Horsemen remains the selected magnate contact body. Scottish Feudal Knights remains the formed lance-shock family. Their different roles can coexist within the same army.

Armored Border Lances and Royal Lances are optional later candidates. The former must preserve a medium-cavalry mobility bargain; the latter needs crown-specific selection, service, command, and recovery. Neither is an obligatory top-tier card.

Horse condition, rider qualification, tack, remounts, and collective practice remain separate constraints. Surviving riders without fit mounts do not preserve full mounted strength.

## 11. Northern, western, Isles, and Norse-Gaelic emergence

The general strategic foundation is now established by the Crown and Western Sea authority. Scotland develops through one persistent Realm with two compatible emphases: Crown and Ordered Realm, and Western Sea and Norse-Gaelic Integration. These are not replacement factions, permanent cultural forks, or exclusive technological universes. They may coexist, compete over particular finite commitments, and reinforce one another when the campaign genuinely supports both.

The roster consequence is broader than a single Isles unit. Northern and western development may affect access, assembly, transport, reception, landing, supply, return, recovery, and the political conditions under which existing or future formations can serve. Maritime qualification is never inferred from a regional name alone. A unit remains controlled by its own profile for equipment, battlefield role, recruitment, recovery, and power contract.

#### 11.3 Roster relationships under the western and northern direction

- **Highland Warband** may gain qualified coastal assembly, transport, reception, and return where a real western source supports them. It does not become a Norse axe guard or default ship crew.
- **Highland Bow Retainers** may receive support from particular western or island retained-service relationships. Their missile mission and close-security bargain remain controlled by their profile.
- **Galwegian Javelinmen and any admitted Galwegian Warband** may interact with southwestern maritime routes without becoming interchangeable with the Theater-owned Galloway Spear Warband.
- **Cateran families** may use qualified coastal routes or missions where their actual source supports them. Cateran identity alone does not prove maritime service.
- **Ordered Spear Host, Scottish Armored Foot, King’s Spear Retainers, and Scottish Feudal Knights** can participate in an expedition when transport, reception, supply, and return exist. Maritime movement does not erase their ordinary battlefield costs.
- **March Riders and other mounted forces** receive no automatic maritime benefit. Horses, handlers, feed, embarkation space, landing conditions, and recovery consume real capacity.

No generic maritime paragraph should be appended to every unit profile. A profile changes only when a concrete access, source, campaign interaction, presentation need, or battlefield consequence is authorized.

## 12. Burgh, castle, and siege emergence

The rejected Town Watch draft remains rejected. It is not restored as a compulsory ancestor. Burgh development should begin with a more flavorful civic military source and a useful campaign bargain, not with a bland guard card that must exist to fill a tree.

Possible burgh outcomes include civic spearmen with field-service limits, maintained crossbowmen, or selected guild-linked companies where research supports them. Burgh Militia and Burgh Spearmen are working concepts, not two guaranteed cards. One strong family is better than several near-identical urban tiers.

Castle service may support garrisons, relief forces, stores, repairs, engineering labor, siege equipment, blockade, assault, and destruction of fortifications. Not every capability needs a recruitable battlefield card. A specialist card must contain a distinct body with an actual battle function and recruitment source.

Pike, artillery, and larger royal naval developments retain later design space. No exact date or universal unlock is assigned by this authority.

## 13. Recruitment and development transactions

Use finite recruitment pools informed by population and military sources. Do not directly subtract a universal manpower number from settlement population as the entire recruitment system. A unit requires qualified contributors, leaders, equipment, support, and a valid service relationship.

```yaml
development_transaction:
  - IdentifyFamilyAndMilitaryNeed
  - ConfirmSourceAndPoliticalAccess
  - ProtectEssentialLocalAndExistingMilitaryDuties
  - QualifyPeopleLeadersEquipmentAndSupport
  - ReserveInputsWithoutDoubleCounting
  - AssembleAndTrainForTheActualRole
  - AdmitOnlyAServiceableFormation
  - RecordLossesReturnAndRecovery
failure:
  before_commitment: ReleaseReservationsWithoutCreatingAGhostUnit
  after_commitment: AccountActualLossAndUnfinishedService
```

Buildings, decisions, and research can expand organization, storage, training, or access. They cannot independently create eligible people, horses, bows, ships, officers, or cooperation. Normal supported recruitment should remain practical and understandable; emergency overdraw adds risks without becoming the routine optimum.

## 14. Recovery, persistence, and conversion

Recover personnel, leaders, equipment, cohesion, and source cooperation separately. Full headcount is not full quality. Understrength veterans may retain judgment and file familiarity while lacking frontage or striking mass. Rebuilt companies restore bodies before collective timing.

When a family progresses, distinguish individual qualification, formation conversion, and new recruitment. Do not convert every existing unit instantly. Compatible survivors can retain experience; incompatible equipment must be repaired, reassigned, stored, or replaced rather than disappearing.

When recruitment closes, survivors can persist under restricted replacement, local duty, recall, recovery, or accounted dissolution. No calendar transition deletes armies or resets source losses. Mercenary service follows the Britannia contract system; it does not duplicate native recruitment capacity.

## 16. Visual identity and battlefield readability

The High army should show increasing helmets, mail, maintained shields, command markers, and equipment consistency without becoming uniformly armored. Common hosts retain variation. Royal and magnate bodies show concentrated quality. Regional assault and missile units retain different silhouettes and handling.

Use clothing, shield forms, weapon length, armor density, banners, formation depth, and animation to distinguish families. Do not rely on universal blue-and-white clothing, standardized modern tartan, giant swords, or a single Highland costume.

A unit's major weapon must read at Total War battle distance. Mixed weapons should be organized by meaningful files or roles. Decorative variety must not obscure whether the player is buying a spear front, sword body, axe assault group, or missile band.

## 17. Proposed army styles and comparative tests

These are composition patterns, not exact unit-count prescriptions.

**Coordinated host:** common spear mass, Ordered Spear Host at the working front, dedicated bow support, practical riders, and one scarce decision body. It should win through frontage and timing, not elite saturation.

**Royal field army:** armored foot and royal retainers support common troops, with limited knightly shock. Its strength is reliability; its weakness is concentrated cost and slow recovery.

**Highland-centered host:** Highland Warband, Caterans, mobile bowmen, and a spear anchor. It should pressure difficult ground and flanks but suffer against concentrated missiles and prepared heavy fronts.

**Western maritime expedition:** qualified Isles land and ship service with a real transport and return plan. Its distinct value must come from campaign reach and coastal operations as well as any land combat profile.

**Frontier army:** practical and mature border cavalry, archers, common spears, and a restrained household reserve. It should intercept and pursue without becoming a knightly steamroller.

Test each against an equal-budget mixed army, not only a favorable isolated duel. A successful unit should remain useful outside one perfect combination and still have readable counters.
