## 1. Strong Unit Verdict

**March Riders are Scotland's proposed practical outer-flank horse: spear-and-shield riders who watch the approaches to an infantry host, threaten light raiders, intercept exposed skirmishers, and pursue broken troops only while they can still return to protect the army.**

Their signature is **Watch, Turn, Return**. They make the enemy respect the ground outside the spear front. Their most important action may be turning back from an attractive pursuit because another threat is approaching the bowmen.

This is real light-contact cavalry, not messengers disguised as a battle card. They can attack vulnerable troops and contest a short local mounted action. They cannot break a prepared spear front, defeat fresh heavy cavalry by nationality, or keep fighting indefinitely after their horses tire.

## 2. One-Screen Total War Card

- **Name:** March Riders.
- **Class:** Light spear-and-shield cavalry.
- **Promise:** Keep light enemies away from Scotland's vulnerable wings and punish exposed troops without spending the whole mounted arm on one charge.
- **Best use:** Open shoulders of the Spear Host, approaches to Forest Bowmen, retreat lanes, exposed missiles, and controlled pursuit.
- **Strengths:** Mounted reach, useful local contact, quick changes of task when ordered in time, and pursuit of already broken foot.
- **Weaknesses:** Prepared spears, heavier cavalry, concentrated missiles, bad ground, horse fatigue, and blocked turning space.
- **Campaign bargain:** More practical than selected household cavalry, but still dependent on suitable horses, trained riders, tack, feed, and recovery.
- **Instruction:** Guard the way back before taking the way forward.
- **Flavor:** **Beyond the spears, keep the road home open.**

## 3. Player-Facing Description

March Riders give an infantry-centered Scottish army a mobile answer to danger beyond its spear points. They ride with practical spears, shields, and limited protection, using position and timely movement rather than the weight of an armored knightly charge. Exposed archers and broken infantry can be worthwhile targets; fresh spearmen and stronger cavalry usually are not.

A rider chasing too far cannot protect the host. Their value comes from choosing the right interception, ending contact before it becomes a grind, and returning with horses fit enough for another task. They are neither expendable scouts nor a replacement for the kingdom's heavier mounted followings.

## 4. Scotland's Mounted Identity

The current Scottish portfolio has a strong foot-centered shape: spear frontage, selected royal foot, regional defense, close raiders, bow pressure, and shielded javelin support. March Riders supply the mobile protection that lets those different infantry contributions work together.

The proposed Scottish expression is an outward-facing screen tied to the safety of the host: riders watch the open wing, turn a threat away from the archers, then return rather than behaving as an independent heavy striking arm. This is a design emphasis, not a claim that other medieval cavalry could not perform the same tasks.

Do not make Scotland uniformly Highland, uniformly mounted-raiding, or incapable of meaningful cavalry. Equally, do not solve every infantry weakness by giving its common riders the combat ceiling of household men-at-arms.

## 9. Campaign Bargain

The intended cost sits below selected household cavalry, not below all infantry. Horses make even lightly protected riders a material investment. They should be practical enough for an ordinary army to field where the source supports it, without becoming an unlimited disposable cavalry pool.

Repeated losses remove riders, mounts, leaders, and tack. Source recovery can be limited by horse condition even when rider headcount is restored. The player sees the pool, cost, recruitment time, recovery, and the decisive shortage, not an individual horse-property ledger.

## 15. Battlefield Loop

### Watch

Take a position from which an actual approach can be observed while remaining within useful response distance of the host. A hidden enemy is not known merely because riders are nearby.

### Position

Move to the threatened wing or a useful interception line. Preserve turning room and avoid exhausting the horses before contact.

### Threaten or Intercept

A threat may force light enemies to turn away without a charge. Attack only when target protection, support, and return space make contact worthwhile.

### Break Away

End a short action before heavier opposition fixes the riders. Contact can prevent clean withdrawal; this is not a guaranteed hit-and-run ability.

### Recall and Reform

Stop pursuit, turn with real space and time, and return to a recognizable rally. A formation scattered among fleeing troops is not ready for another immediate charge.

## 16. The Screen-versus-Pursuit Choice

The central resource is **mounted response capacity still available when the next threat appears**.

A tempting exposed archer line may draw the riders away from Scotland's own bowmen. Pursuing broken foot may improve the victory but leave a wing unguarded. Holding a reserve can be the better choice even when it produces fewer immediate casualties.

The player repeatedly chooses:

- Cover a wider approach or concentrate for contact.
- Threaten without charging or commit to a short interception.
- Chase a broken target or return before enemy reserves arrive.
- Use fresh horses now or preserve them for withdrawal protection.

This is the unit's Scottish army role, not an exclusive national mechanic.

## 17. Contact and Charge Limits

March Riders can make a useful charge into exposed, weak, or already disordered foot. Their impact depends on actual speed, order, ground, target facing, weapons, and horse condition under the shared battle model.

They are poor frontal attackers against prepared spears and fresh heavy infantry. A charge into a shielded target with nearby support can become a losing melee even if the first contact causes casualties. No universal charge-cycle damage bonus is added to compensate.

A second attack requires separation, turning room, reforming, and sufficient remaining condition. Repeated charges are possible opportunities, not a free loop.

## 21. Strengths and Weaknesses

### Strengths

- Useful mounted response beyond an infantry front.
- Can threaten exposed missiles and light raiders.
- Provides pursuit that foot cannot easily perform.
- Can preserve friendly withdrawal routes.
- Useful as a wing reserve even without charging.
- Lower proposed protection burden than selected heavy cavalry.

### Weaknesses

- Poor against prepared spears and heavier cavalry.
- Vulnerable to concentrated missiles.
- Horses impose feed, care, and recovery costs.
- Bad ground and tight turning space reduce the card's value.
- Pursuit can remove protection from the host.
- Light equipment does not grant immunity to fatigue or contact losses.

## 22. Enemy Counterplay

Keep spears near missiles, anchor a flank, and retain a mounted or infantry reserve. Use exposed troops as bait only where the actual enemy behavior permits it; the AI does not gain hidden knowledge of player orders. Force the riders to choose between two approaches rather than chasing their screen without purpose.

A light cavalry opponent can threaten the return route. Heavy cavalry can deny open ground without needing to catch every rider. Missile troops can punish a stationary screen or a slow reform. The enemy need not rout for the riders' threat to matter, and the riders need not charge to perform useful work.

## 23. Combined-Arms Geometry

The Spear Host gives riders a protected shoulder and a place to return, not a magical passage through dense infantry. Forest Bowmen need the riders to warn of and contest an approach while keeping the bow lane clear. ## 24. Roster Separation

**Royal or magnate household cavalry candidates:** stronger selected mounted service, protection, command responsibilities, or contact ceiling. March Riders do not become household troops by experience alone.

**Scoto-Norman or other heavier mounted candidates:** a distinct source and equipment development, not a generic upgrade button attached to these riders. Their exact profile remains outside this deliverable.

**Mounted messengers and scouts:** service functions may share people or horses, but a battle card needs mounted combat qualification. The same personnel cannot perform separated assignments simultaneously.

**Mounted infantry:** traveling on horses and fighting on foot is a different primary promise. March Riders must justify mounted contact rather than using transport speed to disguise an infantry card.

**Later border horse or reivers:** potential later research direction, not a package of later names, armor, weapons, or institutions projected backward.

**Foreign practical horsemen:** broad role overlap is normal across realms. Scottish distinction must come from supported source, army integration, development, and presentation, not invented ethnic bonuses.

## 25. Terrain, Crossings, and Weather

Open shoulders, broad valleys, and usable tracks can support movement and contact. Broken ground may conceal an approach but also disrupt mounted order. Dense woods, marshes, steep slopes, narrow streets, and crowded crossings are not cavalry playgrounds.

At a ford, the riders may screen an approach or exploit an enemy already across and disordered. They should not lead a frontal crossing into prepared spears. A bridge can remove turning and escape space.

Weather effects remain shared-system proposals. No exact mud, snow, wind, or regional endurance modifier is invented. Local knowledge can inform route choice only where actual knowledge exists; it does not remove physical ground constraints.

## 26. Siege and Settlement Roles

Useful roles include camp perimeter response, watching a relief-force approach, intercepting exposed sorties, protecting supply movement, and pursuing broken troops after a safe exit opens. The unit does not operate siege engines or become a lead breach assault formation.

In a settlement, wide approaches may permit short mounted action. Tight streets and gates can trap the riders. Dismounting, if supported, is a deliberate reduction in mobility and mounted capability rather than a free second full-strength infantry card.

## 28. Campaign Uses and Information Boundary

Proposed campaign uses include escort, counter-raid response, route watching, screening an army's movement, and protecting a retreat. These are roles for existing campaign systems, not new actions activated by this profile.

## 29. Sustainment and Competing Work

Food, horse feed, water, shelter, equipment repair, and care determine whether the mounted arm remains useful on a long campaign. Light cavalry can be less armored without being low-maintenance.

Repeated fast actions may exhaust mounts before riders are incapable of fighting. A formation with healthy people but poor horses needs a different recovery response from one with fit horses but missing riders.

Where actual sources overlap, military use can compete with transport, escort, household needs, and local defense. Do not impose a universal agricultural penalty merely because horses exist in the unit. Summarize the dominant real constraint through pool and readiness behavior.

## 31. Veteran and Rebuilt Behavior

Understrength veterans can retain better threat judgment, pacing, recall, and reforming while covering less ground and delivering less contact mass. A veteran rider on an unfamiliar replacement mount is not automatically at full effectiveness.

A rebuilt unit can regain riders and horses before restoring collective turning, pursuit control, and response timing. Horse quality and condition should remain visible enough to explain why a numerically restored formation cannot yet perform its normal role.

No amount of experience creates heavy protection, a larger mount supply, or a household service relationship.

## 33. Visual Identity

The distance read is a practical mounted spear-and-shield group with limited protection, visible turning space, and no horse armor. Equipment variation should remain coherent enough that the card does not look like a random assortment of mounted civilians.

Use source-appropriate clothing, restrained identity markers, practical tack, and a few better-equipped leaders. Exact shield forms, helmets, garments, and horse conformation require material-culture review. Do not use modern breeds as shorthand for medieval capability.

Avoid standardized tartan, universal clan heraldry, late reiver helmets, firearms, heavy barding, identical tournament lances, fantasy fur uniforms, or an entire unit presented as mounted noble knights.

## 34. Animation and Audio

Animations should show observation, measured movement, concentration from a screen, short spear contact, costly disengagement, a real turning arc, pursuit, recall, reforming, horse fatigue, and rider survival after mount loss where supported.

A recall order is visible through movement and regrouping, not a teleport. Spears do not lengthen for the charge. Horses do not pivot in place at full speed or pass through dense infantry without consequence.

Proposed intent calls: Watch the wing. Keep the road. Riders ahead. Leave the spears. Turn them off. Back to the host. Enough pursuit. Gather the horses.

These are localization concepts, not reconstructed historical commands. Avoid caricatured accents or a lawless-raider personality assigned to every regional rider.

## 44C. Mounted Response Economy

The unit's decisive resource is **mounted response capacity still available when the next threat develops**.

### Coverage or concentration

A wide screen observes and threatens more ground but cannot strike every point with full mass. A concentrated group responds harder but leaves more of the wing unwatched.

### Threat or contact

The riders may turn away light enemies simply by occupying a credible interception line. Contact spends horse condition, formation order, and time.

### Intercept or preserve

A vulnerable target can be punished now. Holding the riders fresh may protect Forest Bowmen or a withdrawing Galwegian formation later.

### Pursue or return

Pursuit converts enemy disorder into losses but steadily abandons screening coverage and rally connection.

## 44H. Roster Collision Refinement

### Household cavalry

Household and selected mounted troops should own better protection, command reliability, stronger close contact, and politically narrower service. March Riders own practical screening and response capacity.

### Heavier Scoto-Norman mounted troops

A heavier mounted family requires distinct people, horses, equipment, institutions, and combat emphasis. March Riders are not a cheap prerequisite that automatically transforms into knights.

### Messengers and scouts

Information and communication duties may share source capacity, but a battle card requires mounted combat readiness. Scouting does not grant omniscience, and courier service does not prove spear contact skill.

### Mounted infantry

Transport to battle and fighting on foot is a separate promise. Dismounting remains a constrained contingency, not the normal identity.

### Later border horse

Later frontier institutions may become a genuine development branch only when chronology and source support them. They do not retroactively define the Early card.
