## 1. Strong Unit Verdict

**Mormaer's Horsemen are Scotland's proposed selected magnate cavalry: a compact spear-and-shield following with better protection and more deliberate mounted contact than March Riders, committed to a chosen flank or weakened front when the host has made an opening worth spending expensive horses and experienced retainers on.**

Their signature is **Commit the Following**. They are the cavalry the player keeps in hand until the target, approach, and support justify a real attack. Unlike March Riders, they are purchased primarily to deliver useful mounted combat, not to cover the widest possible screen.

They can make a consequential charge and continue a short coherent fight against suitable opposition. They are not a disposable cycle-charge tool, an unbreakable noble guard, or a full late knightly formation projected backward. Prepared spears, stronger cavalry, concentrated missiles, and entanglement remain dangerous.

## 2. One-Screen Total War Card

- **Name:** Mormaer's Horsemen.
- **Class:** Selected medium cavalry.
- **Promise:** Deliver a supported mounted blow that lighter screening riders cannot reliably sustain.
- **Best use:** A fixed enemy flank, a weak advancing wing, a local counterattack, or an exposed target whose supporting reserve has been checked.
- **Strengths:** Better proposed protection, concentrated mounted contact, cohesion within a selected following, and a useful short melee after impact.
- **Weaknesses:** Prepared spears, stronger heavy cavalry, costly horse losses, crowded ground, missiles during formation, and overcommitment.
- **Campaign bargain:** A finite magnate contribution with higher cost and slower recovery than ordinary practical riders.
- **Instruction:** Make the host create the opening; spend the following only when the ground and target justify it.
- **Flavor:** **Held in hand until the host has made the moment.**

## 3. Player-Facing Description

A mormaer's mounted following brings selected riders, suitable horses, shields, spears, and stronger protection to the Scottish host. This proposed formation is built for a deliberate attack rather than a wide screen: it gathers, chooses a route, and strikes where the enemy is already occupied or locally vulnerable.

Its better equipment buys a stronger chance of surviving contact, not permission to charge anything. A fresh spear front or an uncommitted heavy mounted reserve can ruin the attack. Preserve the following after the first success; experienced riders and fit combat horses are harder to replace than a momentary patch of ground.

## 4. Scottish Identity and the Player Choice

Scotland's roster should contain more than a spear mass and a Highland stereotype. The selected provincial following adds a political and military layer: the king's host can draw on powerful local military households without every good fighter becoming a royal guard or an imported knight.

March Riders ask whether to spend response capacity on a chase. Mormaer's Horsemen ask whether this is the moment to spend the army's stronger mounted contact. The two may share broad cavalry tasks, but their normal army position and preferred commitment differ.

The following is proposed as a stronger mounted arm within an infantry-centered army, not a mandatory one-card bodyguard. It should be useful enough to fight, rare enough to value, and costly enough that throwing it into a prepared front is a meaningful mistake.

Repeated losses damage more than headcount. Losing experienced leaders, suitable mounts, and costly protection can slow recovery even after new people arrive. A player may prefer common riders for broad security and reserve this formation for the decisive local task.

## 16. Battlefield Loop

### Hold in Hand

Keep the following in a position from which it can reach a useful fight without exhausting its mounts or blocking friendly missiles. A reserve that never has a plausible route is not well positioned.

### Choose the Approach

Prefer a target occupied by the host or a weak enemy wing. Check the reserve behind the target, not only the nearest exposed figures.

### Form for Contact

Gather order and enough frontage. A narrow gap may deliver only part of the unit and turn a strong card into a weak local attack.

### Commit

Attack for a bounded purpose: turn a wing, break exposed light troops, stop a weak advance, or exploit a genuine opening.

### Assess the Fight

Continue briefly if the target and support justify it. Refuse the prolonged grind when fresh spears, heavy cavalry, or a closed route changes the bargain.

### Separate and Reform

Disengagement requires actual space, horse condition, and control. Re-form before another full attack. Losses and fatigue persist.

## 17. Commitment Economy

The decisive resource is **a coherent selected mounted following still available at the right moment**.

- **Reserve or early attack:** early contact may secure an advantage but removes the answer to a later crisis.
- **Flank or direct counterattack:** a longer flank route may offer a better target while arriving too late to save the front.
- **Continue or separate:** better protection buys some contact endurance, not unlimited staying power.
- **Pursue or preserve:** pursuit may finish a threat but scatter the following and consume the horses needed for the next battle.

The player should feel the formation's strength when the commitment is right. It must not be so hedged by conditions that it becomes an expensive spectator. Against suitable exposed troops, a formed attack should be a real and satisfying combat option.

## 19. Short Melee and Separation

This card should be more capable of a short coherent fight than March Riders, but it remains cavalry with a finite mounted source. The intended distinction is meaningful contact and local control, not a blanket attack increase in every situation.

When a target collapses, a bounded pursuit may be appropriate. When it holds and receives support, the following should seek separation while a route remains. A withdrawal order cannot rescue riders already surrounded or deeply entangled.

No automatic cycle-charge multiplier is added. A second attack requires a real interval to separate, turn, gather order, and recover enough condition.

## 21. Strengths, Weaknesses, and Counterplay

Strengths are concentrated mounted contact, stronger proposed protection than practical riders, useful short melee, and a valuable reserve role. These arise from equipment, practice, leadership, and actual order, not a noble or Gaelic courage modifier.

Weaknesses are prepared spears, heavier mounted opposition, concentrated missiles, bad turning ground, high replacement burden, and overcommitment. Enemy counterplay includes refusing an exposed flank, keeping a reserve, narrowing the approach, and forcing the following to commit before the decisive opportunity appears.

An enemy can threaten two points and make the player choose. A false opportunity is dangerous because the following cannot be in reserve and committed elsewhere simultaneously. Neither side receives perfect information about hidden reserves.

## 22. Combined Arms

The Spear Host fixes a front and discourages direct mounted interference. Forest Bowmen can force an enemy to move or turn. Galwegian Javelinmen add close disruption. Cateran Axemen punish a different exposed side. March Riders protect the approach and keep lesser threats away while the stronger following forms.

The following then delivers a deliberate mounted action where those real positions create an opening. There is no automatic combined-arms bonus.

## 24. Other Roster Boundaries

Royal mounted retainers, if separately justified, must own a crown-specific source and duty rather than simply better numbers. This following does not become royal merely because it joins the king's army.

Heavier Scoto-Norman cavalry, if developed, must have a distinct source, equipment, institution, or combat emphasis. The presence of that family must not automatically erase Gaelic-facing magnate cavalry or create a binary cultural fork.

A Mormaer's Retinue infantry proposal would require its own battlefield purpose and source accounting. This document does not create one by dismounting every rider into an elite foot card.

Foreign noble cavalry are useful coverage comparators, not templates whose people, horses, institutions, and values can be transferred wholesale. No universal cross-realm ranking is asserted.

## 25. Terrain, Weather, and Crossings

The legacy card's Highland bonuses are not adopted. Open ground with a usable approach and separation route can be better than a steep slope. Sparse cover may help approach but disrupt formation. Dense woods, marshes, crowded streets, and steep broken ground can deny the mounted promise.

A crossing can compress the formation and expose it to missiles. The following may counterattack troops that have crossed and lost order; it should not receive a special ability to charge through a defended bridge.

Weather affects actual ground, visibility, supply, and horse condition through shared systems. No inherited winter immunity, low winter penalty, or fixed snow bonus is granted.

## 26. Siege and Settlement Use

Useful roles include reserve against sorties, relief-field fighting, escort of critical movement, and pursuit after an enemy exits into usable ground. The following does not operate siege equipment or lead a packed breach assault by default.

Broad settlement approaches may permit mounted contact. Narrow gates and streets can trap valuable horses. Better armor does not make a mounted formation a suitable universal urban assault unit.

## 34. Visual Identity

The distance read is a compact, better-equipped mounted following: spears and shields dominate, helmets and stronger protection are more visible than among common riders, and a restrained source banner helps identify the group.

Mail is an important proposed visual direction inherited from the legacy concept, but its exact coverage is unverified. Do not depict every rider in identical equipment. A few richer figures can signal leadership without implying every soldier is a provincial ruler.

Avoid standardized tartan, universal clan badges, late plate armor, tournament lances, horse barding, fantasy fur uniforms, giant axes, and a generic Viking cavalry costume. Norse-Gaelic influence in the old card is a research lead, not a universal costume package.

## 35. Animation and Audio

Show the following gathering order, aligning for contact, using mounted spears, entering a short fight, separating under pressure, and reforming. The better contact ceiling should be visible in coherence and equipment, not superhuman attack animations.

Horses need real turning space. Riders cannot teleport out of melee or pass through dense spears. Sidearm use must match carried equipment. Horse loss and rider survival remain conditional on shared animation support.

Proposed intent calls: Hold the following. Make room to form. Their wing is fixed. Spears forward. Stay together. Enough ground. Back to the banner. Gather before the next charge.

These are localization concepts, not reconstructed historical commands. Avoid caricatured accents and automatic aristocratic arrogance as a unit personality.

## 44C. Commitment Economy Refinement

The decisive resource is **a coherent selected mounted following still uncommitted when a worthwhile local opening appears**.

### Hold or spend

Holding preserves the answer to an unknown threat. Committing may solve the current fight while leaving no comparable reserve elsewhere.

### Full contact or partial contact

A narrow route may deliver only part of the formation. Sending the front files alone can waste quality without creating the mass expected from the card.

### Continue or separate

Better armor and order allow a short useful fight. They do not transform cavalry into unrestricted line infantry.

### Pursue or reform

A short pursuit may complete the local result. Reforming earlier preserves cohesion, horses, and availability for another commitment.
