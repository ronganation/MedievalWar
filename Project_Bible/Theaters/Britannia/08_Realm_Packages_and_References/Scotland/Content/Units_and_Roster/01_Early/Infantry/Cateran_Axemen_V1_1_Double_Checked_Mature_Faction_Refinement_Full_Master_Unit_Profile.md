## 2. Direct Unit Verdict

**Cateran Axemen are Scotland's practiced rough-country raiders and flank infantry: lightly protected, axe-led fighting bands able to approach through difficult ground, conceal their movement, strike an exposed side or rear, overwhelm isolated light troops, and disengage along prepared routes before cavalry or disciplined heavy infantry can fix them.**

They are not emergency musters with better attack statistics. They are not berserkers, great-axe shock troops, Galloglaich, or universal Highland infantry. Their battlefield value comes from practiced movement, route selection, target judgment, local violence at the right point, and knowing when to leave.

## 3. One-Screen Total War Card

- **Unit:** Cateran Axemen
- **Class:** Practiced regional raiding and flank infantry
- **Battle promise:** Reach the battle's weak side through terrain, hit an exposed or already engaged target with axes and shields, and withdraw before the enemy reorganizes.
- **Best use:** Woods, hills, folds in the ground, settlement edges, camp approaches, concealed flank routes, ambushes, pursuit of broken foot, and raids against poorly protected rear positions.
- **Strong against:** Isolated archers and skirmishers, tired light infantry, crews, baggage defenders, fragmented musters, and ordinary infantry already fixed from another direction.
- **Competitive against:** Peer light and medium infantry when Caterans gain surprise, flank access, favorable terrain, or a short violent contact.
- **Weak against:** Prepared spear fronts, heavy shield infantry, elite household foot, cavalry in open ground, prolonged missile exposure, and any fight where withdrawal routes close.
- **Campaign bargain:** Gain a practiced regional raiding arm with strong operational uses, but draw from finite people whose local service, seasonal activity, leaders, weapons, and political relationships cannot be assumed.
- **Player instruction:** Do not announce them on the main line. Find the route, choose a vulnerable target, strike for a bounded purpose, and leave before the enemy's heavier troops arrive.
- **Flavor line:** **Take the hidden way. Fall upon the weak side. Be gone before the answer comes.**

## 4. Player-Facing Description

Cateran Axemen are lightly equipped but practiced fighting bands accustomed to movement, raiding, and sudden close action in rough country. One-handed axes dominate the unit's silhouette, supported by limited shields, knives, short spears, and a minority of swords where actual wealth and service support them. Their power comes from approach and target selection rather than armor or formation depth.

On the battlefield they move around the Spear Host's fixed front, threaten missiles and rear positions, attack troops already committed elsewhere, and punish enemies moving carelessly through broken terrain. If forced to stand under arrows, receive cavalry in open country, or grind against ordered heavy infantry, their advantages disappear quickly.

## 6. Historical and Creative Boundary

The external results explicitly describe “cateran” as deriving from Gaelic *ceatharn*, meaning warrior, usually lightly armed; as a term originally applied to bands of fighting men of Scottish Highland clans; and later associated in Lowland usage with Highland cattle raiding. The Cateran Ecomuseum states that the earliest documentary evidence for Cateran raids is in the mid- to late-13th century. Those statements support the broad light-warrior and raiding identity, but create a serious chronology hold for an Early Era 1062 opening card. citeturn11search285turn11search286

The design therefore distinguishes between:

- broader Gaelic light fighting-band concepts that may have earlier roots;
- explicit documented Cateran raiding evidence beginning later;
- the recognizable Total War name and gameplay family;
- any exact 1062 opening claim, which remains unauthorized.

## 14. Five-Part Battle Loop

### Scout the Way

The band identifies terrain, route, target, rally, supporting troops, and possible enemy response.

### Close Unseen

Sections use cover, folds, woods, settlement edges, or enemy distraction to reduce exposure. Concealment is conditional, not invisibility.

### Choose the Victim

The player selects isolated missiles, a committed flank, crews, baggage defenders, broken sections, or another target worth the risk.

### Strike Hard

Axe-led sections deliver short close pressure while shielded and spear-bearing fighters secure edges and the route.

### Break Away

The band disengages by known paths, passes behind the Scottish line, scatters toward rally points, or transitions into short pursuit only if the enemy is broken and mounted response is controlled.

## 17. Strengths, Weaknesses, and Counterplay

### Strengths

- Strong rough-country approach and route selection.
- Practiced ambush and flank entry.
- Effective short attack against exposed light and medium targets.
- Useful against missiles, crews, baggage, and fragmented troops.
- Better offensive confidence and disengagement than Hill and Glen Muster.
- Good campaign raiding and rear-security threat.
- Distinctive Scottish axe-led identity without heavy armor.

### Weaknesses

- Limited armor and shield coverage.
- Poor frontal performance against prepared formations.
- Vulnerable to cavalry outside protective terrain.
- Quickly loses advantage in extended combat.
- Missile exposure if revealed early.
- Expensive loss of practiced leaders and route knowledge.
- Political and source complications around raiding service.
- Depends on a viable exit.

### Enemy counterplay

- Screen flanks and rear positions.
- Use scouts to reveal routes.
- Keep cavalry reserve near missiles and baggage.
- Refuse pursuit into rough ground.
- Reface disciplined shield infantry before contact.
- Block rally and withdrawal routes.
- Force Caterans to fight on open ground.
- Let expendable screens absorb the first strike while heavy troops respond.

- Hills and folds with protected approaches.
- Settlement edges and rear lanes.
- Camps, baggage areas, and missile positions.

- Open plains under cavalry observation.
- Long frontal advances.
- Dense missile kill zones.

- Raiding exposed local capacity.
- Ambush support.
- Route denial and rear-area threat.
- Screening rough approaches.
- Harassing supply and movement where systems support it.
- Pursuing already defeated foot.
- Defending home rough country against small incursions.

## 26. Visual Identity

**Distance read:** loose, purposeful axe-led bands moving in sections, with limited shields and spears but no heavy uniform block.

**Primary silhouette:** one-handed axes, practical small shields among some files, short spears around edges, knives and occasional swords, hoods and short cloaks, weathered wool, boots or practical footwear, limited helmets, and rare mail among leaders or wealthier veterans.

### Visual principles

- More coherent and better armed than Hill and Glen Muster.
- Lighter and less protected than Galloglaich or household infantry.
- Axes visible enough to define the card.
- Shields meaningful but incomplete.
- Regional color and pattern variation without standardized tartan.
- Leaders readable through posture, equipment, and rally position rather than fantasy costume.

### Avoid

- Universal great axes.
- Two-handed shock-infantry silhouette.
- Horned helmets.
- Naked torsos.
- Blue face paint.
- Standard kilts projected across chronology.
- Uniform clan badges.
- Universal mail.
- Berserker animations.
- Bandit caricature.

## 27. Animation Direction

- Scouts and leaders checking routes.
- Sections moving low through folds, woods, and settlement edges.
- Shields concentrated toward exposed direction.
- Axes carried ready without constant overhead posing.
- Short spear files securing edges.

- Hard one-handed axe attacks with realistic recovery.

## 38C. Target-Access Economy

The Caterans' central resource is **contact time against a valuable vulnerable target before the enemy can answer**.

### Route or speed

A protected route takes longer but preserves surprise. A direct approach reaches the target sooner but increases detection and missile exposure.

### Valuable target or safe target

Missiles, crews, baggage, and engaged flanks offer high payoff but may be guarded. A weaker peripheral target is safer but may not justify committing the band.

### Continue or break away

An apparently successful strike can become a loss if Caterans stay after the target is solved. Withdrawal timing is part of their offensive skill.

## 38F. Full Collision Refinement

### Hill and Glen Muster

The Muster owns accessible regional screening, warning, obstruction, and local fallback. Caterans own practiced offensive approach, target selection, and disengagement. A veteran Muster is not automatically a Cateran band.

### Highland Warband

Highland Warband must own more organized assault, greater capacity to remain in contact, and stronger field cohesion. Caterans should remain superior at route exploitation and breaking away.

### Galwegian Javelinmen

Galwegians own shielded ranged disruption and immediate assault transition. Caterans own concealed approach and axe-led flank contact. Thrown weapons remain excluded from this normal card.

### Galloglaich

Galloglaich own heavy professional axe combat, armor, and sustained melee. Caterans own mobility, concealment, target choice, and timely withdrawal.

### Mormaer's Retinue

The retinue owns selected magnate household service and reliable political attachment. Caterans may serve or contract under lords, but the entire family is not a household formation.
