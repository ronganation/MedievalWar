## 1. Direct Unit Verdict

**Hearth Spearmen are Scotland's bottom-rung organized spear infantry: local groups assembled around familiar leaders to establish one shallow, understandable front, deny an easy route, and buy useful time without consuming the field host.**

Their signature is **Keep the Way**. The unit chooses one road, camp entrance, field edge, crossing approach, baggage lane, or withdrawal passage and makes that approach require a real answer. Success is measured in three ways:

1. **Time bought:** the enemy must deploy, shoot, turn, or fight instead of moving freely.
2. **Ground kept:** the assigned route remains usable to Scotland or denied to the enemy while the mission matters.
3. **People preserved:** the local body withdraws or demobilizes with enough leaders, contributors, and equipment to remain a living source.

The card is intentionally humble, but not empty. A Hearth Spear formation that holds the wrong place, blocks the retreat route, or dies after its task is complete has been misused. A formation that forces cavalry to turn, prevents raiders reaching the baggage, or keeps one lane open long enough for better troops to reform has earned its place in the army.

## 2. Refined One-Screen Total War Card

- **Unit:** Hearth Spearmen.
- **Class:** Lowest organized local spear infantry.
- **Battle promise:** Turn one secondary route from free enemy movement into a timed, directional problem.
- **Best use:** Road approaches, camp entries, baggage lanes, field edges, withdrawal passages, captured ground, missile protection, and quiet flanks supported by better troops.
- **Strong against:** Unformed civilians, improvised foot, exposed crews, light raiders forced into the points, and cavalry probes approaching an intact prepared front.
- **Competitive against:** Peer bottom-rung levies when leadership, preparation, width, fatigue, and shields favor the Hearth line.
- **Weak against:** Organized field spears, trained sword or axe infantry, concentrated missiles, open flanks, several-direction attacks, repeated turning, and long campaigning.

## 3. Player-facing description

Not every Scottish spear belongs in the main host. Local contributors can gather around a recognized leader with enough usable weapons to hold a road, defend stores, or stand behind the fighting line. Their shields are uneven and their practice limited, but a simple front is still a real obstacle when the enemy must approach its points.

Hearth Spearmen free Scotland's better troops for harder work. Set them where the task is clear, protect their sides, and let their numbers and reach buy time. They may turn back light raiders or withstand another levy, but trained infantry, concentrated arrows, and a flank attack can expose their limitations quickly.

They are cheap to field, not cheap to lose. Survivors must return to households, work, and local defense. A useful stand ends with people coming home, not with a heroic label covering an avoidable slaughter.

## 4. Naming decision

**Hearth Spearmen** is selected as a creative English gameplay designation. Hearth evokes home and local contribution without requiring a particular parish boundary, chartered burgh, clan system, landholding category, or later administrative arrangement. Spearmen keeps the battlefield promise immediately legible.

The name does not establish a historical hearth levy, tax, legal quota, permanent corps, or universal obligation. No translated Gaelic title is invented.

Alternatives considered:

- **Township Spear Levy:** clear but more administrative and dependent on settlement terminology.
- **Parish Spear Levy:** retained planning provenance, not an approved universal recruitment institution.
- **Alba Spear Levy:** more overt faction branding, but broader and less descriptive of the local role.
- **Hill and Glen Levy:** rejected because it blurs the existing Muster.
- **Hearthguard:** rejected because it suggests selected household warriors rather than cheap contributors.

The working name may change after owner and linguistic review. A name change alone must not create another unit.

## 6. The low-tier power contract

The unit must be weak enough to preserve the ladder and useful enough to recruit voluntarily.

**Intended favorable circumstances:** a fresh local body, an intelligible front, adequate spear coverage, a manageable enemy, and protected flanks. Against improvised foot or light raiders forced into reach, it should offer meaningful resistance. Against a peer levy, the result depends on numbers, ground, fatigue, shields, and leadership.

**Intended unfavorable circumstances:** trained field spears, fresh sword-and-shield infantry, practiced axemen, concentrated missiles, open-flank cavalry, or prolonged pressure. Better troops should beat the levy for understandable reasons rather than arbitrary tier immunity.

No exact victory ratio is assigned. This is a testable power target, not a validated matchup result.

## 7. Why recruit them instead of another Spear Host?

Because the army does not need every sector to win a major battle. One cheap spear body can cover the baggage route while the Host holds the decisive front. It can occupy captured ground after the stronger unit moves on. It can protect a fallback assembly point or add a visible threat to a low-demand approach.

The economic bargain must survive normal play. Recruitment and upkeep should be lower than the Spear Host, while field readiness, collective control, endurance, and reliable mounted reception are clearly inferior. If price differences are too small, the levy has no purpose. If the levy performs the Host's job at a fraction of its cost, the design has failed.

## 8. Recruitment source and availability

The proposed source is eligible local contributors released for a recognized defensive or short-service need. It may include rural settlement, estate, or community sources where historical and political review supports them. Residence or poverty alone does not establish military eligibility.

A recruitable card requires available people, sufficient spears, basic leadership, an accepted task, assembly, and support. If the source cannot meet that minimum, it does not produce a spear card. Do not automatically create a second mixed-peasant unit as a consolation output.

Use finite recruitment pools informed by population and source health. Do not subtract army headcount directly from a settlement population counter. Normal recruitment should be straightforward; severe losses, repeated extraction, source damage, or conflicting duties create the meaningful constraints.

## 9. Civilian cost without administrative punishment

Cash cost can be low while the social cost remains real. Mobilization draws people away from work, transport, household support, and local security where those duties actually overlap. The player should see the principal consequence through existing recruitment, supply, settlement, and recovery systems.

Do not impose a bespoke agricultural penalty for every recruit, invent a fixed harvest calendar, or make ordinary use so punitive that the card is never worthwhile. Pressure should escalate with long absence, repeated mobilization, devastation, and casualties. Seasonal behavior must use the project's distinct campaign-season track rather than treating the historical clock as an annual farming calendar.

## 11. Visual character

The unit should look assembled from neighboring groups rather than issued from a uniform store: repaired shields, ordinary shafts, practical wool and linen clothing where period review supports them, restrained cloaks or wraps, and a few better-equipped leaders. Repeated small groupings make the body readable without identical dress.

Use a modest rally reference and visible leaders, not national banners on every file. No standardized tartan, modern clan badges, universal blue, fantasy face paint, later Highland weapons, or caricatured poverty. The visual contrast with the Spear Host is shallower organization and less consistent field equipment, not foolishness.

## 12. Signature: Keep the Way

```text
Gather by local group
  -> choose one way
     -> dress the points
        -> check or delay
           -> yield by group when the task is complete
              -> rally and return
```

The signature rewards a comprehensible assignment and preparation. It does not improve attack because the unit is near its home settlement. Familiar ground may help identify routes only where the simulation represents that information; it does not reveal hidden enemies.

The player uses familiar move, face, halt, attack, formation, and withdrawal controls. There is no mandatory six-button sequence.

## 13. Three practical deployments

**Keep the Road:** a modest front covers a route, camp entry, or store approach. It is cheap and useful, but vulnerable if bypassed.

**Second Line:** the levy remains behind stronger infantry to occupy ground, protect a fallback, or receive a disordered light enemy. It does not magically restore the morale of troops passing nearby.

**Last Useful Stand:** the levy holds briefly while archers, wounded, or baggage withdraw. The objective is time and a surviving exit, not automatic death to the last soldier.

These are mission descriptions, not stat stances. Changing mission requires movement, facing, leadership, and time.

## 14. Spear geometry and mounted threats

A shallow front with enough points can discourage a careless mounted probe. It is not a reliable answer to every cavalry charge. Moving, turning, gaps, uneven footing, missile losses, and weak leadership sharply reduce reception.

Light riders should often refuse a prepared front and look for another route. Heavy or well-supported cavalry can exploit disruption or an open flank. The levy should not receive the Spear Host's deeper prepared-denial package, mature schiltron behavior, or an invisible anti-large field.

A longer weapon matters only where it can be handled. Rear files do not attack through allies, and a dense crowd does not create unlimited spear output.

## 15. Ordinary infantry contact

The first reach contest may be useful. The longer fight is the danger. Once an opponent enters the points, the levy has uneven sidearms, thin protection, limited depth, and weak collective control. It can contest another low-grade body but should not grind down trained infantry by cheap numerical efficiency alone.

A short supported advance against an already disordered enemy is permitted. A general offensive assault is not its normal purpose. Refusing an attack is a legitimate decision, not a failure to use the unit.

## 16. Delay and withdrawal outcomes

```yaml
UsefulDelay: EnemySpendsTimeWhileFriendlyTaskCompletes
CheckedProbe: LightThreatRefusesOrLosesMomentum
CostlyHold: ApproachRemainsClosedAtMeaningfulLoss
PrematureFlight: PositionLostBeforeUsefulTimeIsBought
PinnedLevy: EnemyContactPreventsCleanDeparture
OrderedFallback: BodyLeavesThroughRealSpaceWithLeaders
FragmentedReturn: GroupsEscapeAndRallySeparately
Overrun: CoherentSpearBodyLost
```

A retreat order does not guarantee escape. Withdrawal needs a route, warning, leadership, and some separation. Supporting infantry or missiles may create that separation. If the player blocks the exit with friendly troops or baggage, the levy cannot phase through the obstruction.

## 17. Readiness and degradation

A hasty levy can appear with poor spacing and incomplete shields. A serviceable body meets the normal card promise. A prepared body has its best facing and simple spear relation. A worn body is slower and less confident. A depleted body may still guard a short point but cannot claim its original frontage.

Distinguish spear shortage, shield shortage, leader loss, fatigue, and casualties. More people do not solve every shortage. An experienced survivor group may rally better than fresh replacements but still lacks the bodies to cover a broad road.

## 18. Leadership and morale

Confidence comes from recognizable leaders, familiar contributors, support, a clear task, and a visible route back. It is not a national bravery modifier. Leadership loss should reduce facing, response, and rally before automatically forcing a rout.

The levy can surrender, disperse, withdraw, or continue locally depending on circumstances. Neither cowardice nor heroic immunity is a default social characteristic. A sensible defense ends when further resistance no longer protects anything worthwhile.

## 19. Combined arms and independent utility

With the Spear Host, the levy holds secondary ground so the main line remains concentrated. With Forest Bowmen, it can protect a modest approach while arrows disrupt the enemy. With the Muster, it covers the clearer route while the mixed light foot works the rough edge. With Caterans, it should not be expected to pin elite infantry long enough for a perfect flank attack. With Mormaer's Swordsmen, it can occupy ground after the selected body wins the local decision.

It also works without those combinations: camp defense, a roadblock, captured-ground custody, or a rearward assembly point. It must not require an elaborate combined-arms script to justify a cheap card.

## 20. Terrain, settlements, and siege limits

Best ground is simple, constrained, and understandable: a road approach, field edge, camp entrance, broad lane, or supported crossing approach. Dense woods and severe broken ground favor the Muster's handling rather than this spear body.

The levy may defend an existing gate or obstacle but creates neither walls nor barricades by appearing. It is not a revived Town Watch, charter-dependent civic institution, ladder-assault unit, siege crew, or universal free garrison. A siege assignment still debits the same people and equipment from other service.

## 21. Casualties, recovery, and demobilization

Record killed, wounded, missing, captured, dispersed, and returned contributors distinctly where shared systems permit. Survivors do not disappear when recruitment closes. Equipment recovery requires actual control and usable condition.

After service, release surviving people and equipment through the source's valid arrangements. Wounds, missing leaders, damaged shields, and disrupted households can delay the next muster. Money alone does not recreate community capacity.

A veteran levy may retain its name and history, improve simple facing and withdrawal, or return home. Experience stars do not automatically convert it into the Spear Host, a professional company, or a household unit.

## 22. Early-to-High persistence

The older Parish Spear Levy concept supplied a proposed path toward provincial and burgh spears. This profile preserves that as a research lead, not an automatic legal or institutional ladder. Rural contribution does not become civic membership merely because an era changes.

Possible outcomes include a better-supported same-family levy, qualification of some contributors for the common field host, restricted replacement of veteran survivors, or retirement where no distinct role remains. A later provincial or burgh card needs its own source, service, equipment, and battlefield reason.

Do not manufacture a High counterpart simply to fill a folder. The low-cost local-defense role may remain useful beside more advanced units.

## 23. Campaign and battle AI

Campaign AI recruits for low-demand defense, emergency depth, or release of better troops. It should not fill an expeditionary army with levies merely because they are cheap. Source health, available weapons, support, and the actual mission matter.

Battle AI chooses a manageable approach, protects flanks, sets before contact, refuses pointless pursuit, and withdraws before encirclement when the task permits. It should not spin toward every mounted feint, march through friendly formations, or attack a prepared professional front unsupported.

Enemy AI should exploit the levy's limits through missiles, bypass, trained infantry, and flank threats rather than endlessly charging its best-facing points.

## 24. UI, animation, and sound

The card should communicate cheap local spear defense immediately. Show only decisive readiness blockers and battlefield warnings: still forming, flank exposed, shields thin, leader lost, exit blocked, or too worn for another stand.

Animation should show uneven but understandable dressing, directional shields, hesitant turns, short spear work, compression, local sidearm use, and a costly withdrawal. No synchronized fantasy bracing or effortless wheels.

Suggested English design calls: To the marker. Face the road. Keep the points. Hold for them. Back to the host. Do not follow. These are authored intent lines, not reconstructed medieval speech.

## 25. Autoresolve and calibration

Autoresolve should weigh readiness, spear coverage, shield coverage, facing, support, terrain, missiles, leadership, enemy quality, and escape. The name Hearth must not grant an automatic home-defense multiplier.

Unit size, costs, upkeep, pool capacity, replenishment, armor, morale, attack, defense, charge, speed, weapon length, and formation depth remain uncalibrated. Comparative targets are lower cost and lower field reliability than the Spear Host; less rough-ground utility than the Muster; much less offensive power than Caterans or magnate swordsmen.

## 26I. Whole-Battle Employment

**Opening:** assign the low-demand approach, baggage route, or rearward passage. Avoid unnecessary missile exposure and repeated redeployment.

**Main battle:** keep the way, check light threats, hold captured ground, or form the passage through which missiles, wounded, baggage, and disordered troops can move.

**After the task:** release, withdraw, or shorten the line. Do not convert a successful delay into a prestige melee.

**Late battle:** cover a final withdrawal, occupy abandoned ground, protect prisoners or stores, or refuse an exposed pursuit. A fresh levy can still matter late because better formations may be exhausted.

## 26O. Physical Relief and Passage Transaction

Hearth Spearmen can cover a withdrawal or hand a route to stronger troops, but relief must occur through real space.

```text

  -> shorten or split the local front
     -> open a passage
        -> move missiles, wounded, baggage, or relieved troops through
           -> receiving formation faces the threat

```

A blocked passage, enemy pressure, panicked traffic, or premature withdrawal can collapse the transaction. Hearth Spearmen do not grant friendly units collision immunity or instant safety.

## 26P. Five Representative Battle Stories

1. **Baggage road:** light riders probe the rear. The Hearth line dresses toward the road, forces the riders to turn, and remains in place rather than pursuing.
2. **Archer withdrawal:** Forest Bowmen pass through an opened lane. Hearth groups close behind them, delay ordinary foot, and yield once the bowmen reform.
3. **Captured ridge approach:** Mormaer's Swordsmen win the local decision. Hearth Spearmen occupy the access so the selected swordsmen can return to their standard.
4. **Broken-ground mismatch:** the levy follows raiders into woods, loses its simple frontage, and is outperformed by troops suited to dispersed fighting.
5. **Empty victory:** the levy defeats a peer body but remains in contact after the route is safe; avoidable losses damage the source and turn tactical success into campaign failure.
