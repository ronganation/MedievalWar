## 1. Final Unit Verdict

**Mormaer's Swordsmen are Scotland's scarce magnate-facing sword-and-shield decision infantry: selected household fighters and recognized associates who remain behind the broad host until a short front has been fixed, compressed, or exposed, then enter under their own standard to win the close contest, stop on the required ground, and return as a coherent following rather than dissolve into pursuit.**

The card's power is not simply superior melee statistics. Its power is the ability to deliver a more reliable, protected sword fight at the precise point where Scottish spear mass, regional pressure, terrain, or enemy fatigue has already neutralized reach. Its cost is that the same magnate-facing body cannot simultaneously protect the household, reinforce another wing, supply overlapping mounted service, or be cheaply regenerated after a bloody success.

## 2. One-Screen Total War Card

- **Unit:** Mormaer's Swordsmen
- **Class:** Selected sword-and-shield infantry
- **Battle promise:** Win a short, important close fight after the Scottish host has fixed or compressed the enemy, then secure the result without wasting the following in a long grind.
- **Best use:** Behind a Spear Host shoulder, beside a threatened seam, at a ridge crest, gate approach, road, camp edge, or against ordinary infantry already committed and unable to exploit spear reach.
- **Strong against:** Ordinary melee troops, compressed or disrupted spear infantry, exposed missile troops reached through a safe lane, and local breakthroughs lacking fresh support.
- **Competitive against:** Peer shielded swordsmen when the mormaer's body enters coherently with better leadership, freshness, protection, and a bounded objective.
- **Weak against:** Prepared deep spears, fresh elite household foot, later armored professionals, sustained missiles, several-direction pressure, and cavalry against an exposed flank.
- **Campaign bargain:** Magnate-facing leaders, sword-and-shield equipment, selected fighters, political cooperation, and household continuity are concentrated into a scarce decision unit.
- **Player instruction:** Keep them behind the host. Commit them only when swords can decide the ground. Halt on the objective and pull them back before victory becomes attrition.
- **Flavor line:** **When the spears have made the press, the mormaer's swords decide it.**

## 3. Final Player-Facing Description

A mormaer's following may place some of its best-equipped and most trusted fighters on foot. These swordsmen gather around familiar leaders and a recognizable standard, carrying shields and close weapons suited to the compressed fight that follows once spear reach and broad frontage no longer decide the issue.

They are not a cheap front line and not a berserk assault band. Keep them behind Scotland's broader infantry, then send them through a real lane against an enemy already fixed, tired, turned, or crowded. Their shields help them survive the entry; their swords and collective practice help them work the short front; their leaders must stop the advance once the useful ground is won.

Every success spends something valuable. If the swordsmen pursue too far, remain in ordinary attrition, or lose their leaders and standard, the army may win the patch of ground while the magnate loses the following that made future decisions possible.

## 12. Signature Battle Architecture: Watch, Enter, Decide, Return

### Watch

Maintain a compact body behind or beside the important sector. Preserve shields, leaders, standard, and fresh files while reading where ordinary troops are becoming compressed, penetrated, or exhausted.

### Enter

Use a real passage. The first files must reach useful contact together, followed by enough supporting depth to prevent the unit from becoming a handful of isolated wealthy fighters.

### Decide

Fight for a named short objective: the shoulder of a spear line, a ridge crown, a road mouth, a gate interior, the edge of a camp, a standard position, or the ground needed to extract another formation.

### Return

Stop the advance, restore facing, pass the sustained mission to appropriate troops, and withdraw toward the standard while the company still possesses leaders and common control.

## 16. Matchups and Enemy Counterplay

Ordinary shield infantry, disrupted spear troops, tired attackers, and exposed missiles are suitable targets under favorable conditions. Fresh deep spears should be fixed or turned rather than attacked as if short weapons erase reach.

Enemies can stretch the swordsmen, concentrate missiles, attack a flank, retain fresh reserves, deny relief space, or draw them away from the objective. Cavalry remains dangerous against unprepared or exposed foot. Shields and selected mail reduce some exposure; they do not replace formation, support, or preparation.

## 17. Scottish Combined Arms

The Spear Host holds width and occupies enemy weapons. Swordsmen enter beside the fixed contact, not through friendly spear points. Bowmen can encourage facing changes and suppress exposed troops. Javelinmen can pressure a selected sector before the swordsmen close, with a clear cease-fire and movement handoff.

Cateran Axemen can create a different offensive threat while swordsmen secure the gained position. March Riders screen the outside; Mormaer's Horsemen exploit a mounted opening only if a real route exists. None of these relationships grants a passive composition bonus.

## 18. Three Useful Battle Applications

**The ridge shoulder:** common spears occupy the center while swordsmen attack an already engaged shoulder. After gaining the crest, they turn outward and reconnect rather than chasing downhill.

**The settlement approach:** swordsmen contest a gate approach or short street after access is opened. Crowding, missiles, and fresh defenders remain serious risks. This is not an automatic wall-assault bonus.

**The failing wing:** swordsmen counterattack an enemy already committed into the Scottish line. The goal is to restore ground and order, not pursue the attacker so far that the wing becomes exposed again.

These are proposed gameplay applications, not reconstructions of named historical battles.

## 19. Morale, Leadership, and Fatigue

Familiar leaders, household bonds, maintained equipment, and a clear local task support cohesion. They do not grant fanaticism or unbreakable morale. Loss of leaders, heavy casualties, encirclement, failed relief, or collapse of neighboring troops can break the body.

Close shield fighting, repeated advances, difficult ground, heat, and prolonged contact consume endurance. Veterans pace and judge the fight better; they do not ignore fatigue. Freshness must matter against an equally equipped opponent.

## 20. Campaign Service and Political Cost

The same trusted fighters may be needed for household security, escort, estate defense, local leadership, mounted assignments, or succession-sensitive duties. Only actual overlap creates competition; do not invent a universal penalty across unrelated sources.

Service duration, geography, command, support, and return depend on the actual relationship. No universal fixed service term is assigned. Prolonged mobilization or destructive losses may affect future cooperation through the political system, not random mid-battle desertion.

## 22. Losses, Capture, and Recovery

Track dead, wounded, missing, captured, and returned people separately from damaged shields, lost weapons, mail, leaders, and standards. Capture is not automatically death. Ransom or release requires the shared political and prisoner systems, not an automatic refund.

Recovery needs eligible replacements, service access, equipment, leaders, and collective practice. Money can support those inputs but cannot replace them. An understrength veteran body may fight more coherently than a newly rebuilt full-strength company, while still lacking its frontage and mass.

## 23. Terrain, Siege, and Transport

Firm ground and controlled approaches favor the intended close fight. Broken terrain can restrict cavalry without granting the swordsmen magical Highland bonuses. Woods, steep slopes, mud, streets, and breaches also disrupt their own formation and relief.

They can contest a gate, camp, short street, or accessible strongpoint when supported. Ladders and breaches remain costly; household status grants no siege immunity. Coastal transport requires actual lift, landing, supplies, and reassembly. No island or maritime ownership is assigned merely because Scotland has coasts.

## 27AG. Representative Deployments

1. **Host shoulder:** the Spear Host fixes the enemy; swordsmen enter beside the points and defeat the compressed flank rather than moving through friendly bodies.
2. **Ridge crown:** swordsmen counterattack the final short rise, halt at the crest, turn outward, and accept relief instead of pursuing downhill.
3. **Gate interior:** another formation controls the outer approach; swordsmen defeat troops already inside the confined objective and then guard the interior until site-duty infantry arrives.
4. **Failing center:** swordsmen seal a penetrated seam, recover the standard line, and withdraw once ordinary frontage is restored.
5. **Magnate extraction:** the company forms around the standard and wounded leadership, trades offensive output for a protected withdrawal, and does not receive a free morale aura.
6. **Independent defense:** without a perfect combined-arms setup, the swordsmen hold a short road or camp front against ordinary melee troops but remain vulnerable to missiles, bypass, and fresh reserves.

## 27AJ. V1.3 Final Thesis

**Mormaer's Swordsmen are not Scotland's generic elite infantry and not a weaker imitation of a royal household guard. They are a magnate's finite sword-and-shield decision body. Held fresh behind the host, they enter one short close fight with enough protection, leadership, and common practice to defeat ordinary foot, secure the precise ground that matters, and return to their standard. Their battlefield strength is real; their campaign weakness is that every casualty, broken shield, lost sword, dead leader, and failed return damages the household capacity that made the intervention possible.**
