## 1. Direct Unit Verdict

**Scottish Spear Host is Scotland's broad Early Era field-spear formation: a regionally varied body of ordinary contributors whose long spear frontage can deny a mounted approach, hold a useful piece of ground, and keep the enemy fixed long enough for Scotland's archers, javelinmen, Caterans, riders, or household reserve to decide the battle.**

It is not a peasant mob with unusually high anti-cavalry statistics. It is not a mature Wars of Independence schiltron. It is not a pike company projected backward. Its strength comes from achieving enough shared facing, depth, spear clearance, leadership, and confidence to make many individually modest soldiers dangerous together.

## 2. One-Screen Total War Card

- **Unit:** Scottish Spear Host
- **Class:** Broad common-host long-spear infantry
- **Battle promise:** Build a dangerous frontal spear relation, blunt mounted pressure, bind ordinary infantry, and give Scotland's more aggressive arms time and space to act.
- **Best use:** Firm or gently uneven ground, protected flanks, broad defensive sectors, missile protection, road approaches, camp edges, and battles where another Scottish unit can exploit a fixed enemy.
- **Strong against:** Cavalry committed into an intact prepared front, loose mounted probes, emergency levies, and ordinary infantry forced to approach through working spear reach.
- **Competitive against:** Peer common spears and ordinary infantry when frontage, depth, leadership, support, fatigue, and ground favor the host.
- **Weak against:** Missile concentration, crossfire, repeated turning, flank or rear attacks, heavy close infantry inside the spear points, broken ground that parts the files, and unsupported advance.
- **Campaign bargain:** Scotland gains broad affordable spear frontage by drawing real contributors, leaders, long weapons, shields, equipment, time, and local capacity from qualifying sources.
- **Player instruction:** Set the host before impact, keep its flanks intelligible, let it bind the enemy, and win elsewhere. Do not chase with a wall of long spears.
- **Flavor line:** **Hold the ground. Set the spears. Let the host become the wall.**

## 3. Player-Facing Description

Scottish Spear Hosts gather ordinary field contributors into broad formations built around long spears, mixed shields, and local leadership. Their equipment is uneven and their order is not the product of a standing professional army, but a host that finds its frontage before contact can make a mounted approach brutally expensive and force ordinary infantry to fight through reach, depth, and mutual support.

Their value is collective. A correctly faced host holds the dangerous front while archers shoot, Galwegian javelinmen disrupt, Caterans attack a flank, March riders threaten exposed troops, or scarce household forces wait for the decisive moment. Once the host turns repeatedly, loses its leaders, stretches past its neighbors, or allows heavy infantry inside the spear points, the same long weapons become awkward and the formation can unravel quickly.

## 6. Historical and Creative Boundary

Public comparative material supports several broad foundations for the design: poorly armored spear and bow infantry formed much of Scottish military strength; long spears were prominent; small shields, axes, knives, javelins, and bows could appear; and mounted heavily armored forces remained comparatively limited. The public reference also describes variation in clothing and protection rather than uniform presentation.

This profile does **not** treat those broad descriptions as proof of:

- one national equipment standard;
- a fixed twelve-foot spear for every soldier;
- a perfected 1062 schiltron;
- exact regional quotas;
- exact unit sizes, pools, costs, morale, or statistics;
- one universal Scottish dress;

## 17. Five-Part Battle Loop

### Gather

The host assembles from compatible contributors, finds its leaders, establishes banners or rally markers, and enters the field as a body rather than a collection of small groups.

### Set

The player assigns a manageable sector, faces the principal threat, protects or understands the flanks, chooses suitable width and depth, and halts in time for the spears to become useful.

### Receive

The host contests an approach through actual reach, depth, footing, courage, and formation order. Mounted troops committed frontally face the greatest danger. Ordinary infantry may be checked, slowed, or bound rather than instantly destroyed.

### Bind

Once contact develops, the host's purpose is to keep the enemy occupied and facing the spear front. Binding creates the opportunity for bows, javelins, Caterans, riders, retainers, or knights to act elsewhere.

### Redress

The host shortens damaged frontage, restores facing, lets another formation relieve or support it, covers withdrawal, or halts an advance before the long-spear formation becomes a disordered chase.

## 20. Infantry Combat Contract

The host is useful against ordinary infantry but is not elite close-combat foot.

- Long reach can check an approach and inflict losses before close contact.
- Broad frontage can bind a larger portion of the enemy line.
- Supporting depth helps replace losses and preserve confidence.
- Shields provide partial directional protection where present.
- Once heavy or aggressive infantry gets inside the spear points, mixed sidearms, uneven protection, and limited professional training become serious weaknesses.
- The host can make a short supported advance, but should not perform a deep attack while preserving full anti-cavalry readiness.

## 22. Strengths, Weaknesses, and Counterplay

### Strengths

- Broad and visually distinctive Scottish spear frontage.
- Serious frontal mounted deterrence when prepared.
- Useful against ordinary infantry at first contact.
- Protects archers, javelin troops, and reserves.
- Creates time and position for regional assault units.
- More field-capable than emergency or parish levies.
- Available broadly enough to serve as Scotland's infantry backbone.

### Weaknesses

- Slow to turn and redress.
- Vulnerable to crossfire and sustained missiles.
- Long weapons struggle in cramped close contact.
- Uneven armor and shield provision.
- Less reliable than royal retainers or professional men-at-arms.
- Poor pursuit and weak independent flanking ability.
- Source and cadre losses can degrade replacement quality sharply.
- Difficult ground helps defensively only when it does not break the formation itself.

### Enemy counterplay

- Refuse the prepared front.
- Shoot the host until gaps or hesitation appear.
- Threaten two directions and force turning.
- Fix the front and attack the flank.
- Send heavy close infantry after missile disruption.
- Draw the host into an unsupported advance.
- Crowd it in woods, streets, steep ground, mud, or crossings before it sets.
- Block withdrawal and relief passages.

## 44B. Frontage Economy and Exact Player Choice

The host's battlefield resource is not a hidden national bonus. It is **usable frontage maintained for long enough to matter**.

The card creates three recurring choices:

### Width or control

A wider host covers more ground and protects more missiles, but increases gaps, leader reach, turning time, and flank risk. A shorter front surrenders coverage to preserve control.

### Set or move

A set host creates the strongest mounted denial and approach control. Movement preserves initiative but gives up part of that preparation. The player should not receive full anti-cavalry benefit while wheeling or advancing.

### Hold or redress

Remaining in contact preserves the army's fixed point but increases fatigue, equipment loss, and the risk of an enemy getting inside the spears. Redressing protects the formation but may release pressure on the enemy.

## 44F. Adjacent-Family Separation Refinement

### Parish Spear Levy

Parish Spear Levy owns the cheapest and least reliable spear presence. It can occupy low-demand frontage and reinforce numbers but lacks the Scottish Spear Host's normal field assembly, leader depth, Set quality, and redress control.

### King's Spear Retainers

King's Spear Retainers own royal responsiveness, stronger leaders, better equipment concentration, and crisis steadiness. They should hold a narrower critical point better, but they do not replace the host's attainable breadth.

### Galloway and Galwegian families

Galwegian troops own aggressive disruption and assault. If a Galloway spear expression survives separately, it must close and attack more readily than the common host and accept weaker broad mounted denial or formation endurance.

### Scoto-Norman Men-at-Arms

Men-at-arms own professional protection, close-combat flexibility, and extended service. The host owns width, reach, and lower institutional burden.

### Ordered Spear Host

Ordered Spear Host must visibly improve facing, signals, movement control, section coordination, and campaign endurance. It does not survive as a separate successor if it is merely the same host with higher morale.

### Schiltron Spearmen

Schiltron Spearmen own mature dense doctrine, stronger collective shock denial, and more deliberate formation behavior. They also accept sharper movement, missile, flank, isolation, and terrain liabilities. The Early host must never quietly receive that full package.
