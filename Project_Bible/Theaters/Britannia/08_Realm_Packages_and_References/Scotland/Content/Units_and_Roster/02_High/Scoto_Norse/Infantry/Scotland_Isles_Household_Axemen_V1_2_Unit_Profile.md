This profile proposes a flagship High Era unit for Scotland's **Lords of the Western Sea** development path. It represents scarce armored or partly armored household warriors maintained by powerful Norse-Gaelic lords and kindreds of the western seaways. It does not create a universal ethnic troop type, a generic Viking survival, or an unlimited Scottish axe infantry pool.

The unit is designed to be exciting enough to justify the path: a compact body of experienced retainers that converts a fixed or disordered enemy line into a localized breakthrough. Its power comes from cohesion, patronage, personal equipment, and practiced service rather than frenzy or ahistorical superhuman strength.

## 2. Historical and Cultural Grounding

The concept draws from the Norse-Gaelic military world of the Hebrides, western Scotland, Man, Galloway, and the wider Irish Sea, where Scandinavian settlement and Gaelic integration produced distinct lordships, maritime networks, and mixed cultural traditions. The western seaways remained politically contested rather than becoming a uniform Scottish province.

The unit therefore represents a **campaign-adapted household infantry candidate**, not a claim that every western lord maintained an identical company under this exact title. The later Galloglaich tradition provides a useful developmental horizon, but it must not be projected backward as a complete and universal High Era institution.

## 7. Battlefield Role

### Primary role

**Decisive local counterattack infantry**

The axemen should enter combat where supporting troops have fixed the enemy, where terrain constrains cavalry, or where a flank has become exposed. They are not intended to absorb the full missile approach and entire first collision unsupported.

### Secondary roles

- shield-breaking pressure against ordinary or tired infantry;
- heavy reserve behind Isles Spear Levy or Scottish spear formations;
- assault through a breach after missile and spear preparation;
- commander and standard protection;
- difficult-ground attack when cavalry counteraction is limited;
- short shipboard or landing-place combat when transported, without replacing Birlinn Marines.

### Roles not owned

- broad main-line frontage;
- anti-cavalry wall;
- prolonged pursuit;
- independent missile-screen assault;
- cheap settlement garrison;
- stealth raiding;
- universal marine duty;
- routine frontal charge into fresh armored spears.

## 9. Formation Portfolio

### 9.1 Household Shielded Order

Approach and reserve posture using shields and supporting weapons where the represented equipment permits.

**Strengths**
- improved survival during a bounded approach;
- reliable local cohesion;
- strong protection of the lord, captain, or standard;
- good resistance to ordinary infantry pressure.

**Weaknesses**
- reduced axe density and offensive output;
- compact missile target;
- more limited frontage than mass Scottish spear formations;
- no special cavalry protection.

### 9.2 Axe Counterpress

The main offensive posture for entering a fixed melee.

**Strengths**
- high local anti-infantry pressure;
- strong against tired, compressed, or shield-dependent troops;
- can turn a wavering sector into a rout.

**Weaknesses**
- reduced shield coverage;
- vulnerable to arrows, crossbows, and cavalry;
- declining cohesion and efficiency if the commitment becomes prolonged;
- dangerous if the target disengages and exposes the axemen.

### 9.3 Retinue Wedge

A shallow concentrated body for a breach, gate, slope, or narrow local attack. This is not a fantastical cavalry-style wedge and does not grant automatic penetration.

**Strengths**
- concentrates experienced warriors behind a manageable front;
- useful for passing through an already opened breach;
- protects interior ranks during a short advance.

**Weaknesses**
- exposed flanks;
- severe losses if stalled;
- poor response to enveloping infantry;
- vulnerable to prepared missile lanes.

### 9.4 Guard the Standard

Defensive rally posture around a recognized command point.

**Strengths**
- strong morale retention;
- better local recovery and order;
- supports nearby compatible western troops qualitatively.

**Weaknesses**
- minimal offensive momentum;
- concentrated target;
- benefit collapses if command relationships or the standard are disrupted;
- no universal aura or unbreakable state.

## 11. Strengths

- powerful controlled counterattack against fixed infantry;
- high morale when command and lordship relationships remain intact;
- strong close combat against ordinary shielded infantry;
- better equipment and staying power than western levies;
- effective in breaches, slopes, broken ground, and constrained sectors;
- valuable defensive reserve;
- strong visual and mechanical identity for the Lords of the Western Sea path;
- capable of short maritime expeditions when transport is provided;
- meaningful late-battle exploitation against tired enemies.

## 12. Weaknesses

- scarce household recruitment and slow replacement pool;
- more limited frontage than mass Scottish spear formations;
- vulnerable during an unsupported approach;
- vulnerable to concentrated archery and crossbows;
- dependent on supporting spears against fresh heavy cavalry in open ground;
- lacks the spear depth required for major cavalry denial;
- efficiency declines when a short counterattack becomes a prolonged unsupported melee;
- lordship casualties carry political and campaign consequences;
- expensive personal equipment recovers slowly;
- cannot substitute for affordable Scottish line infantry.

## 13. Enemy Counterplay

- screen the target and shoot the axemen before commitment;
- disengage the fixed unit and expose them to missiles or cavalry;
- refuse a narrow contest and stretch the Scottish frontage;
- pin the supporting spears, then flank the axemen;
- use fresh armored infantry to absorb the initial local pressure;
- countercharge while they are in Axe Counterpress;
- force repeated redeployment and fatigue;
- threaten their lord, standard, transport, or home source at campaign level;
- raid western equipment and vessel capacity to slow recovery.

## 16. Campaign Bargain

### Recruit when

- the army already possesses enough spears or shield infantry to fix targets;
- the campaign includes breaches, rough western ground, ports, islands, or constrained sectors;
- a decisive infantry reserve is more valuable than additional frontage;
- the relevant western lordship is cooperative and can spare retainers;
- transport and return routes can be protected;
- the player can absorb slow recovery after losses.

### Do not recruit when

- broad cheap frontage is the priority;
- the army lacks anti-cavalry protection;
- the enemy can dominate the approach with missiles;
- household service would leave a western territory politically or militarily exposed;
- the campaign requires prolonged distant occupation without relief;

## 21. AI Direction

### Campaign AI

- recruit only when an eligible western provider, equipment, lordly relationship, and household capacity exist;
- prefer one decisive retinue body over filling an army with elite axemen;
- protect local household minimums and avoid exhausting every western source;
- pair the unit with spear frontage, skirmishers, and appropriate transport;
- account for political influence gained by the provider;
- preserve depleted survivors rather than repeatedly disbanding and recreating the company.

### Battle AI

- deploy behind or beside spear and shield formations;
- avoid leading the missile approach;
- commit against fixed, tired, compressed, or flank-exposed infantry;
- avoid fresh heavy cavalry and deep spear fronts;
- recover after a successful local break rather than pursuing indefinitely;
- use defensively near a captain, standard, breach, or constrained reserve sector;
- do not activate Axe Counterpress while isolated or under direct cavalry threat.

No exact AI weights, hidden immunities, or artificial stat bonuses are authorized.

## 35. Endure, Cleave, Break, Recover

### Endure

Approach or wait in the protected household posture. Shields and supporting weapons reduce exposure, but do not make the unit immune to missiles or cavalry. Endure preserves the decision rather than winning the fight by itself.

### Cleave

Commit the axe-bearing assault core into a target already fixed, tired, compressed, disordered, or flank-exposed. Cleave is a short concentration of local melee pressure, not a permanent attack mode or universal armor-piercing state.

### Break

Break the enemy's **local formation or commitment**, not automatically the entire unit or army. A successful break creates room to recover, widen a breach, or transfer pressure to supporting infantry.

### Recover

Halt exploitation, restore shielded order where equipment and space permit, accept relief, return behind the spear line, or protect the captain and standard. Recovery is part of mastery and survival.
