## 1. Controlling mounted thesis

**Traditional Scottish cavalry develops through parallel service channels, not one linear light-to-heavy upgrade ladder.**

Scotland's mounted portfolio supports an infantry-centered army through three distinct purchases:

- **March Riders** preserve mounted response capacity around the host.
- **Mormaer's Horsemen** preserve a selected magnate-mounted reserve for consequential short contact.
- **Scottish Feudal Knights** emerge through developed land-service and knightly household networks to deliver formed first-generation lance impact.
- **March Wardens** may emerge later through qualified frontier-service networks to control routes, intercept raids, deny withdrawal, and preserve operational frontier coverage.
- **Lowland Lancers** may emerge later through selected mature mounted-service convergence to control a decisive countercharge, separation, rally, and one earned renewed threat.

The High era does not delete the earlier families. Better equipment, longer service, or a later date does not automatically convert practical riders into magnate cavalry or magnate cavalry into knights. Each family survives only through a distinct source, battlefield decision, campaign burden, recovery pattern, and normal reason to recruit.

## 4. March Riders: practical mounted response

March Riders own the army's widest practical mounted-security role. Their central resource is mounted response capacity still available when the next threat appears. A screen can deter, intercept, protect missiles, cover withdrawal, or pursue, but every concentration or chase leaves another task less protected.

## 7. Current cavalry purchase separation

|---|---|---|
