## 1. Strong unit verdict

**Lowland Lancers are Scotland's proposed later selected armored cavalry: a compact body of experienced lancers assembled from mature knightly, landed, crown-aligned, and magnate service networks to deliver a controlled countercharge or decisive reserve commitment, remain coherent through a short protected fight, separate under command, and reform for one further consequential action when the battle permits.**

Their signature is **Gather, Break, Return**. Their decisive resource is **mounted initiative**: a coherent elite reserve still able to answer the enemy's major mounted commitment, seize the key lane, and recover enough controlled force to threaten another decision.

## 2. One-screen Total War card

- **Name:** Lowland Lancers.
- **Epithet:** **The Gathered Lance of the Realm**.
- **Era:** Later High or Late selected mounted development; exact emergence unresolved.
- **Signature:** **Gather, Break, Return**.
- **Class:** Selected armored lance cavalry.
- **Promise:** Preserve an elite mounted reserve, commit it at the battle's decisive point, and retain enough order to separate and reform after the first collision.
- **Best use:** Countercharging enemy heavy cavalry, striking a fixed wing, exploiting a real breach, or removing a high-value threat whose support and reserve have been checked.
- **Strengths:** Strong formed impact, high reserve discipline, decisive countercharge capability, substantial rider protection, useful short melee, controlled separation, and an earned second commitment.
- **Weaknesses:** Prepared spears or pikes, bad approach ground, concentrated missiles, horse exhaustion, deep entanglement, failed separation, and catastrophic replacement burden.
- **Campaign bargain:** Very narrow access to suitable riders, horses, armor, lances, commanders, and sustained support.
- **Instruction:** Preserve mounted initiative until the commitment changes the battle; strike hard, leave under command, and return only after real reform.
- **Flavor:** **The first charge opens the decision. Discipline decides whether the lances return.**

## 11A. Mounted initiative and reserve-control economy

The Lowland Lancers' unique strength is not simply superior charge damage. The family holds Scotland's strongest proposed concentration of **mounted initiative**: the ability to keep an elite lancer body coherent, move it toward the decisive sector, answer an enemy cavalry commitment, and retain enough command structure to choose between exploitation, separation, and recovery.

This economy gives the unit a strong battlefield identity. Scottish Feudal Knights primarily ask whether the player can create and spend a clean impact window. Poor use can spend the entire formation on the first action; excellent use can preserve a meaningful remainder without granting free cycle charging.

## 12. Signature: Gather, Break, Return

The signature combines three decisions:

### Gather

Preserve the selected mounted body in a position from which the unit can countercharge or reach the decisive sector. Gathering is not passive hoarding. The player must maintain a usable lane, protect horse condition, and judge whether the infantry can hold long enough.

### Break

Commit against a target that changes the battle: an enemy heavy cavalry attack, a fixed wing, an exposed reserve, or a breach whose exploitation matters. The reward is a strong local mounted decision, not a guaranteed rout.

### Return

Separate while command, space, horse condition, and formation still permit. Re-form around surviving leaders and standards. A renewed commitment is earned through actual recovery, not an instant cycle-charge reset.

## 13. Battlefield loop

1. **Preserve the reserve.** Keep the formation close enough to matter without wasting horse condition.
2. **Read the battle.** Identify which enemy commitment creates the highest-value response.
3. **Form the lance body.** Gather frontage, order, leaders, and an actual route.
4. **Commit or countercharge.** Strike for a bounded army-level purpose.
5. **Break through or check.** Exploit only while a useful local advantage survives.
6. **Separate under command.** Leave before fresh support closes the route.
7. **Reform and return.** Restore enough order, weapons, and horse condition for a second major action only when the real state supports it.

The unit's pleasure comes from performing this sequence visibly and powerfully. The family should not be so condition-heavy that the player never commits it. Against a suitable target on good ground, the charge or countercharge must be clearly worth the investment.

The family should be capable of meeting strong enemy cavalry without receiving a nationality bonus. Result depends on order, approach, support, equipment, horse condition, timing, frontage, and comparative force.

A prepared, timely countercharge against comparable cavalry already committed to another action should produce a clear advantage in testing. The Lancers should not be numerically weakened merely to preserve Scotland's infantry identity. Their limits are positional and strategic: committing badly, fighting prepared anti-cavalry troops, losing the command cadre, or remaining entangled should destroy the favorable bargain.

## 15. Matchups and counterplay

### Preferred opportunities
- Enemy heavy or shock cavalry committed into a readable lane.
- A fixed infantry flank with no prepared spear reception.
- A dangerous reserve whose removal changes the battle.
- An exposed command or missile body where the exit remains viable.
- A local breakthrough that can be exploited without abandoning the Scottish host.

### Conditional fights
- Peer elite cavalry, depending on order, support, ground, and condition.
- Ordinary infantry without prepared anti-cavalry reception.
- A tired or disordered enemy wing.
- A short protected melee after a clean impact.

### Poor assignments
- Prepared pikes or deep supported spear formations.
- Dense woods, marshes, steep broken ground, and narrow crossings.
- Concentrated missiles during formation or approach.
- Deep pursuit away from the Scottish army.
- Prolonged encircled melee.
- Repeated commitment after horse or command readiness has failed.

Enemy counterplay is readable: anchor flanks, retain a mounted reserve, deny approach lanes, force premature commitment, threaten separation, and target expensive horses and leaders.

## 35D. Final distinct power contract

- provide Scotland's most controlled later mounted countercharge against a major committed threat;
- redirect from reserve toward the battle's decisive sector more reliably than earlier Scottish knightly formations;

- remain useful in a short protected sidearm fight;
- separate under working command before entanglement destroys the formation;
