## 1. Strong unit verdict

**March Wardens are Scotland's proposed later frontier-service cavalry: selected riders, leaders, and local mounted elements organized for sustained border watch, rapid concentration, counter-raid interception, route denial, controlled pursuit, and the recovery of people, livestock, prisoners, or strategic movement before hostile riders can disperse.**

Their signature is **Find, Fix, Fold the Road**. Their campaign identity is **rare operational depth**: a shallow, source-bound pool that can repeatedly solve frontier problems only while riders, horses, leaders, guides, and warning relationships survive.

## 2. One-screen Total War card

- **Name:** March Wardens.
- **Alternative card name:** **Wardens' Horse** if historical and linguistic review finds the selected name too officer-facing.
- **Epithet:** **The Riders Who Close the Road**.
- **Era:** Later High or Late frontier-service development; exact emergence unresolved.
- **Signature:** **Find, Fix, Fold the Road**.
- **Class:** Selected frontier operational cavalry.
- **Promise:** Detect or read a hostile mounted movement, concentrate along the useful route, force a turn or short fight, and close the enemy's safest withdrawal before the raiders disperse.
- **Best use:** Raider hunting, counter-skirmisher action, route interception, relief movement, withdrawal denial, escort, controlled pursuit, and protecting an exposed army wing over a wider operating area.
- **Strengths:** Operational reach, route judgment, rapid local concentration, interception, pursuit control, repeated practical service, recovery of the frontier objective, and high veteran value inside a shallow pool.
- **Weaknesses:** Heavy shock cavalry, prepared spears, dense missile zones, prolonged melee, poor intelligence, divided assignments, exhausted horses, and terrain that blocks concentration.
- **Campaign bargain:** A rare source-bound pool produces a highly useful formation that is worth preserving. Losses remove scarce riders, proven horses, leaders, guides, and service relationships from future recruitment and recovery.
- **Instruction:** Do not chase the raiders' dust. Read the road they must use, turn the escape, and return with the frontier secured.
- **Flavor:** **Watch the crossings. Sound the riders. Close the road home.**

## 11. Signature: Find, Fix, Fold the Road

### Find

Use actual scouts, watch relationships, local reports, visible movement, and known routes to identify the likely frontier problem. The unit does not receive omniscience.

### Fix

Force the hostile riders to turn, slow, fight shortly, abandon the raid objective, or reveal the withdrawal route. Fixing may come from position and threat rather than immediate charge.

### Fold the Road

Concentrate across the safest enemy exit, crossing, pass, or relief route. The purpose is not always maximum casualties. Recovery of captives, animals, baggage, a threatened detachment, or control of movement can be the operational success.

## 15. Matchups and counterplay

### Strong opportunities

- Enemy raiders burdened by objectives, baggage, captives, livestock, or a predictable withdrawal.
- Light cavalry committed against missiles or a flank.
- Mounted skirmishers or scouts forced near a crossing or constrained route.
- Dispersed hostile riders before they can reconcentrate.
- Pursuit of a mounted threat whose return would renew the frontier danger.

### Conditional

- Comparable medium frontier cavalry.
- Exposed missiles or broken infantry.
- Escort and relief under time pressure.
- Short attacks on an occupied or disordered flank.

### Poor

- Fresh heavy shock cavalry in open formed contact.
- Prepared spears and pikes.
- Dense missile coverage.
- Prolonged melee.
- Unknown ground without warning or guides.
- Deep pursuit beyond relief, supply, and recall.

Enemy counterplay includes false trails, divided raids, reserve cavalry, concealed infantry support, damaged crossings, night or weather disruption through shared systems, attacks on guides and warning points, and forcing the Wardens to protect several routes at once.

## 32D. Mature power contract

When fully sourced and ready, March Wardens should:

- react across a wider operational frontage than ordinary host-linked cavalry;
- use warning and route knowledge to improve the chance of timely concentration without gaining omniscience;
- defeat or drive off lighter raiding groups when formation, horse condition, support, and local mass favor the Wardens;
- credibly contest comparable medium frontier cavalry;
- force a hostile mobile formation to turn, delay, disperse, or accept an unfavorable route;
- deny or threaten the safest withdrawal after the enemy has committed to a raid or exposed movement;
- perform controlled pursuit without automatically abandoning the frontier objective;
- preserve understrength veteran companies as valuable operational assets;
- make losses matter through the finite pool rather than through arbitrary rarity alone.

They should not:

- become weaker March Riders with a more expensive card;
- become cheap heavy cavalry;
- win against fresh knights through a raider-hunter tag;
- receive automatic ambush, tracking, or campaign movement bonuses detached from real systems;
- duplicate civic horsemen, mounted retainers, or crown sergeants merely because battlefield jobs overlap;
