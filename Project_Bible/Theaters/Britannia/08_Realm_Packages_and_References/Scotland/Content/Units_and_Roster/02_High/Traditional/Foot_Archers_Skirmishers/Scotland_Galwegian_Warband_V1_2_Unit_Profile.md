## 1. Direct Unit Thesis

**Galwegian Warbands are Scotland's developed shielded assault skirmishers: fighting bands that use a limited close cast to prepare an entry, then bring a connected shield-and-spear body into the short infantry contest behind it.**

Their signature is **Cast, Close, Carry Through**. Their distinctive purchase is not another volley. It is the ability to make a useful infantry contribution after the volley, without pretending that a missile opening guarantees a charge victory.

Early Javelinmen approach, cast, assess, and make a brief press or refuse. The High Warband can support a longer bounded follow-through against ordinary foot because its shields, retained weapons, supporting files, and leadership are organized for that commitment. It still lacks the breadth of a common spear host, the protection of maintained armored foot, and the assault ceiling of selected heavy infantry.

**Flavor:** The cast opens the question. The shields must answer it.

## 3. Why this card belongs in the missile folder

The opening missile decision remains central. The player chooses a casting edge, target, angle, and timing before deciding whether to commit the shield body. If normal play treats the unit as ordinary spear infantry that happens to throw once, the folder placement and separate-card case require reconsideration.

The unit must remain useful when a press is refused. Its remaining missiles, shields, and retained weapons can cover a withdrawal, check light foot, or guard a short approach. Refusal preserves a future decision rather than representing failure to activate the signature.

Conversely, exhausting missiles does not remove all value. A serviceable Warband can complete its assigned local fight or accept a reduced defensive mission. It does not gain better melee statistics because its ammunition is gone.

## 7. Separate-card bargain

Early Javelinmen should win on lower recruitment burden, lighter short-mission use, and preserving the option not to enter contact. Warbands should win when a local infantry result must be carried through after the cast. Neither should automatically replace the other.

The High unit pays for connected contact through greater assembly, equipment, leadership, and recovery demands. Its heavier commitment makes it harder to disengage than a lightly committed skirmisher. The reward is a real short infantry contest against ordinary or disrupted troops, not merely a few extra seconds before routing.

The distinction fails if both units receive the same targets, formation, commitment duration, and return behavior with only numerical differences. In that case revise the role or treat development within the existing family; do not preserve an empty tier for symmetry.

## 8. Three mission postures

### Cast from the Shoulder

Approach beside a steady friendly front and spend a limited cast on an exposed section. Preserve distance and the option to refuse. This posture offers the greatest target flexibility and lowest immediate melee commitment, but limited independent missile endurance.

### Shields Through the Opening

Close a connected shield-and-spear front against a target already weakened, occupied, or turned. Supporting files replace local losses and help keep the entry from becoming several separate fights. This is the strongest expression of the new card and the point at which release becomes costly.

### Hold the Gain, Bring Them Back

Shorten the frontage on a bounded objective, restore facing, and await relief or withdraw by connected groups. Holding too long spends the experienced core in ordinary attrition. A useful gain is not permission to pursue through fresh reserves.

Postures are ordinary formation and order choices, not mandatory new ability buttons. No posture creates armor, ammunition, morale immunity, or a safe disengagement.

## 14. Ammunition economy

The limited issue creates a preparation window, not battle-long missile superiority. A first cast may cause losses or attention changes without disorder. Another cast spends time, ammunition, and the opportunity to close before enemy support arrives.

Expended weapons remain expended. Dropped or recovered missiles require safety, time, condition, and compatibility. No rally, melee victory, or formation change refills the load. Ammunition shortage reduces the opening phase; it does not promote the unit into heavy infantry.

## 15. Shield and weapon handling

Casting needs arm space and exposes part of the formation. The whole body cannot throw at full output while maintaining an uninterrupted defensive wall. Leaders sequence the local cease-fire and entry so that throwing files do not collide with advancing retained weapons.

Shields protect the direction actually covered. A turn, compressed frontage, damaged shield, or several-direction attack changes that protection. Retained spears provide useful reach against ordinary foot, but not the density or length of a prepared common-host anti-cavalry front.

## 18. The commitment decision

A useful cast is not proof that a press is safe. Check target facing, protection, fatigue, support, fresh reserves, cavalry, and the friendly shoulder. Refuse when the target remains prepared or the return route is threatened.

Once contact begins, choose between maintaining useful pressure, taking the next short step, shortening the front, and seeking relief. The Warband can continue an ordinary infantry contest longer than the Early skirmisher, but it cannot indefinitely fight fresh replacements. The second gain is often more expensive than the first because shields, leaders, and working depth are already worn.

## 18A. Commitment Budget and Decision Windows

The Warband has a finite **commitment budget**. This is a design economy, not necessarily a visible meter. It represents the remaining combination of missiles, shield condition, working depth, leaders, fatigue margin, and release space needed to make another coordinated decision.

```yaml
commitment_budget_spent_by:
  - CastingAtPoorOrProtectedTargets
  - CrossingOpenGround
  - EnteringWithoutAConnectedFront
  - RemainingAfterTheFirstBoundedResult
  - TurningWhileInContact
  - FightingFreshReserves
  - ReopeningAClosedReleaseLane
commitment_budget_preserved_by:
  - HoldingFire
  - RefusingABadEntry
  - UsingAProtectedShoulder
  - ShorteningAfterTheGain
  - TimelyHandover
  - EarlyAccountedReturn
```

### Decision Window 1: Cast

The cast window closes as the enemy reaches cover, contact, or supporting formation. Waiting may improve target value or lose the chance entirely.

### Decision Window 2: Enter or Refuse

The entry window is short. An exposed enemy can recover, a fresh reserve can arrive, cavalry can take the route, or friendly frontage can close the gap.

### Decision Window 3: Carry Through or Stop

Once the first local result is achieved, continued pressure must be justified by the objective and remaining integrity. Pursuit is not a justification by itself.

### Decision Window 4: Handover or Return

A controlled end state requires actual space and another body capable of holding the result. Waiting for perfect safety commonly turns an Open release into a Contested or Closed one.

## 22. Independent utility

Without an ideal spear-host partner, the Warband can defend a short camp approach, counterattack a weak local incursion, harass a constrained crossing, or support withdrawal with remaining missiles. These are useful reduced missions, not excuses to grant broad frontage, cavalry brace, engineering, or professional garrison capabilities.

In settlements, broad lanes may support casting and a short counterattack. Narrow breaches and packed streets can remove both missile clearance and the working depth. Do not make the unit a first-wave breach specialist by default.

## 23. Terrain, weather, and fatigue

Ridges, field boundaries, and woodland edges can shorten exposure or create angles. Dense trees obstruct casts; steep or muddy approaches disrupt arrival; narrow paths can trap the return. Regional familiarity may improve route judgment under shared rules, never remove physical penalties.

Approach, throwing, shield carriage, contact, and withdrawal all spend endurance. Better protection can improve survival while increasing fatigue burden. Weather affects readiness and support through the shared simulation; no exact modifier is assigned here.

## 24. Leadership and morale

Recognized leaders control cast, cease, entry, halt, and return. Their loss can leave some groups pressing while others stop, even before the whole unit routs. A senior survivor may organize a simpler withdrawal without restoring the original collective timing.

Morale follows losses, support, leadership, fatigue, and escape. No regional bravery multiplier, compulsory charge, berserker state, or hidden betrayal roll defines the card. A disciplined refusal is compatible with an aggressive unit identity.

## 25. Layered recovery

Restore fit people, javelins, retained weapons, shields, protection, leaders, collective practice, and source access separately. The player should see the decisive capability blocker, not administer individual shafts or garments.

Shooter losses reduce the cast; contact-file losses reduce follow-through; leader losses damage the decision between them. Full headcount can coexist with a weak shield front or unreliable return. Understrength veterans retain judgment but cannot replace missing missile mass or contact frontage.

## 27. After-battle continuity

Account killed, wounded, missing, captured, dispersed, and returned people. Ground control, time, labor, and transport determine recoverable equipment. Victory does not restore missiles or erase wounds. Captured leaders cannot simultaneously appear in the replacement pool.

Political change does not delete a deployed formation. Foreign hire would require separate service terms and actual released capacity; this profile creates no mercenary company.

## 31. Battle AI

Approach with a clear cast and a protected shoulder. Assess actual target condition after the throw. Refuse against a prepared supported enemy, shorten a press when reserves appear, and seek relief before working depth collapses.

Do not automatically charge after the final javelin, throw through allies, chase beyond support, or turn the whole formation instantly while engaged. The AI must accept an early withdrawal when cavalry controls the exit. No hidden ammunition, fatigue, visibility, or access advantage is authorized.

## 32. Autoresolve

Evaluate remaining missiles, shields, retained weapons, leader condition, contact depth, enemy protection, support, cavalry threat, and retreat geometry. The regional label supplies no flat bonus. A depleted Warband must not receive the full value of both fresh skirmishers and fresh melee infantry.

Campaign raiding, scouting, transport, and political actions require their own shared authorities. This profile does not grant an independent strategic ability because its battlefield loop is aggressive.

## 33. Visual identity

## 36. Representative battle stories

**The shoulder intervention:** Ordered Spear Host occupies ordinary enemy foot. The Warband casts into a clear side, enters with shields connected, and takes the short road edge. Common spears assume the gain while the Warband returns worn but coherent.

**The refused invitation:** the cast causes casualties but the enemy remains ordered with fresh support. The Warband declines entry and retains enough missiles to cover a friendly withdrawal. No assault is needed for useful service.

**The reserve trap:** an exposed enemy front appears ready to yield. The Warband closes, then fresh armored infantry arrives. Early release preserves part of the band; continuing for a second gain destroys it.

**The cavalry shadow:** riders occupy the return route. The Warband abandons a profitable casting edge before throwing its last missiles. Enemy counterplay succeeds without a frontal charge.

**The wrong purchase:** several Warbands replace common hosts and archers. The army lacks cheap width and sustained fire. Flexible local fighters cannot compensate for missing army structure.

## 36A. Additional Failure and Success Stories

**The useful refusal after a good cast:** the Warband wounds and turns an enemy section, but a fresh reserve appears. The Band refuses the entry. The Scottish player gains time and facing pressure without spending the selected formation.

**The short counter-entry:** ordinary enemy foot pushes into a worn host shoulder. The Warband casts at close range, enters connected, restores the road edge, and hands the frontage to Ordered Spear Host. The result is control, not a dramatic rout.

**The overloaded equipment failure:** too many fighters carry an unrealistic complete missile-and-melee package. The animation and formation cannot present proper shields or retained weapons. The correct design response is fewer throwers or a revised role distribution, not hidden instant weapon swaps.

**The empty second victory:** the Warband clears the first target, pursues, and defeats scattered survivors. Fresh heavy foot then traps the worn body. The battle log shows kills; the campaign receives a damaged source and missing leaders.

**The independent short defense:** with no ideal host shoulder available, the Warband uses a camp approach, spends one cast, and holds briefly until baggage and wounded pass. It withdraws rather than becoming a permanent garrison line.
