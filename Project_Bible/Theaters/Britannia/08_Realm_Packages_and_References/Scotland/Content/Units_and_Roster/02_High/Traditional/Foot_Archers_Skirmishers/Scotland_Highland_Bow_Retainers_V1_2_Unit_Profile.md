## 1. Direct Unit Thesis

**Highland Bow Retainers are Scotland’s selected Highland missile followers: better-equipped bowmen trusted to hold an important wing, punish an exposed enemy, and remain dangerous when manageable foot reaches the position.**

Their signature is **Loose, Stand, Return**.

They are the closest Scottish High-era expression to the noble Highland archer fantasy: practiced shooters drawn from qualified retained followings, marked by stronger leaders, more consistent bows and protection, and a disciplined minority of axe-and-shield guard files. They do not become mounted nobles, heavy longbowmen, or universal household guards.

The player buys a bow unit that can risk a more important position than common or raiding archers. It can deliver a useful shoot, stand through a short bounded pressure window, then restore its bow line if the position remains recoverable. The unit’s glory is not an automatic melee transformation. The glory is that a carefully chosen stand can preserve the shooting mission instead of ending it.

## 2. One-Screen Total War Card

- **Name:** Highland Bow Retainers.
- **Epithet:** **The Chosen Bows of the Highlands.**
- **Class:** Selected retained bow-and-guard foot.
- **Signature:** **Loose, Stand, Return.**
- **Promise:** Strong supported archery from an important wing, with enough selected guard strength to resist a bounded close threat and restore the shooting line.
- **Best use:** Ridge shoulder, protected wing, camp edge, difficult approach, or a position whose loss would expose the host.
- **Strong against:** Exposed ordinary foot, unprotected shooters, disrupted attackers, and light infantry attempting a weak rush.
- **Conditional against:** Shielded ordinary foot from a useful angle, counter-missiles under protection, and manageable melee pressure with a real rally point.
- **Weak against:** Cavalry, fresh heavy assault infantry, prolonged melee, crossfire, blocked exits, and arrow exhaustion.
- **Campaign bargain:** Better equipment, retained leadership, stronger protection, and more reliable service at narrower availability and slower recovery.
- **Player instruction:** **Win the shoot. Stand only for a reason. Return to the bows before the guard is spent.**

## 5. The actual separate-card bargain

Early Cateran Bowmen buy route harassment and recoverable escape at lower retained-service burden. Bow Retainers buy a more supported wing assignment, stronger common leadership, and a more dependable transition from shooting to local cover and back. They pay for that through scarce qualified followers, maintenance, slower recovery, and fewer useful arrows per coin than dedicated archers.
If ordinary play shows only better armor, morale, or melee statistics, the separate card fails. A new name alone is not sufficient. The supported-wing mission must alter deployment, commitment, recovery, and campaign selection.

## 6B. Three Mission Postures

### High Ground Bow Line

Occupy an important protected rise, shoulder, or approach and use the full shooting body. This delivers the best arrow economy and command connection. It risks concentrating expensive followers under counter-fire or cavalry threat.

### Retained Wing

Operate beside a spear or armored-foot shoulder. Guard files protect the edge, leaders control cease-fire and target changes, and the formation can absorb limited disorder when friendly frontage shifts. Friendly bodies can still block arcs and exits.

### Stand and Return

When manageable foot threatens the position, selected guard files and nearby shooters make a bounded stand around the rally point. Success preserves enough space to withdraw or re-form the bow line. Failure pins the expensive unit in a fight it should not win.

## 10. Normal availability and campaign cost

The unit should be attainable within meaningful High-era play, not gated behind every Scottish institution. Ordered Hosts, knights, burgh crossbows, and Isles ships are not mandatory ancestors. Exact dates, recruitment time, cost, upkeep, and pool quantities remain uncalibrated.
The intended burden exceeds Early Cateran Bowmen because retained support and mixed qualification are costly. It is not a royal guard ceiling. A player should rationally choose cheaper hybrids for raids, dedicated archers for sustained shooting, and retainers for a pressured supported wing.

## 11. Emergency service and duration

Emergency expansion can add bodies but weakens bow compatibility, cover practice, leadership density, and return control. It does not create a second disposable card or preserve full performance at arbitrary size.
Local service is easier to sustain than prolonged distant campaigning. Longer service requires arrows, repair, shelter, accepted terms, and return support. Expiry or recall changes availability and replacement through campaign systems; it does not trigger an unexplained battlefield betrayal.

## 13. Ammunition and release rhythm

Finite compatible arrows govern endurance. A concentrated shoot spends stock and drawing endurance quickly; deliberate fire preserves later options. No automatic specialized ammunition modes are granted.
Local redistribution requires carriers, safety, and time. Resupply requires real compatible stores and authorized support. Returning behind spears, winning a fight, or switching weapons does not refill arrows. Arrow-poor retainers can preserve the body or perform a narrow cover task; they do not become free assault infantry.

## 14. Range and target economy

Useful targets are exposed ordinary infantry, unprotected shooters, turning formations, and manageable pursuers before contact. Heavy shields at poor angles, armor at ineffective range, dense foliage, and friendly melee without safe clearance are poor trades.
No universal range or penetration advantage over dedicated foreign archers is promised. At comparable burden, Selkirk Bowmen should retain the stronger dedicated shooting bargain. Retainers pay for another capability rather than receiving full specialist output in both roles.

## 14C. Arrow Economy and Guard Economy

The player manages two coupled resources through ordinary battlefield conditions:

- **shooting strength:** fit bow users, arrows, bows, spacing, and leaders;
- **guard cohesion:** uncommitted close-capable files, shields, rally control, and fatigue.

Spending guard cohesion preserves shooting strength only if the bow body actually escapes or re-forms. Spending shooting strength in the stand may preserve the position but shorten every later missile mission. Neither is a supernatural meter; both summarize real people, equipment, and geometry.

## 16. Cover commitment economy

Cover files are temporary allocations inside one unit. Every person covering the exit reduces shooting, movement flexibility, or the reserve available for the next problem. They cannot detach indefinitely or become another card.
The meaningful choices are arrows now versus safety later; early withdrawal versus local objective; a short cover check versus an unacceptable melee; and a second shoot versus preserving worn followers. Spending the entire body in close combat forfeits the normal missile promise.

## 17. Three Mission Postures

### 17.1 High Ground Bow Line

Occupy an important ridge shoulder, protected rise, camp edge, or constrained approach and use the largest useful shooting body. This posture produces the strongest missile output and target control. It also concentrates scarce retained followers under counter-fire and makes a lost exit especially costly.

### 17.2 Retained Wing

Operate beside Scottish Spear Host, Ordered Spear Host, Scottish Armored Foot, or another formation physically capable of protecting one shoulder. Guard files protect leaders, arrow carriers, and the exposed edge while the bow line changes targets or shortens. Friendly movement can still block the arc or consume the return lane.

### 17.3 Stand and Return

When manageable foot reaches the position, selected guard files and nearby shooters make one bounded stand around the rally point. The objective is to preserve the bow body, restore spacing, and recover another useful mission. The objective is not to win a prolonged melee.

## 18. Formation geometry

Proposed formations are route column, shallow shooting line, offset wing, compact passage-cover body, and rally group. These are configurations of one unit, not independent formations with separate pools.
Wide shooting order improves frontage but stretches leadership and exposes ends. Compact cover improves local support but reduces shooting and increases missile concentration. Narrow passages can prevent both bow-body escape and cover-file recovery. Rear files cannot strike or shoot through allies.

## 19. Five physical spaces

Inspect approach, shooting arc, friendly transit, cover frontage, and return route together. An excellent shooting edge with no return route is a trap. A friendly spear line can protect the retainers and block their arrows. A knightly charge may require the same gap the bow body needs.
The formation receives no collision immunity, automatic passage through allies, or teleporting rear guard. If the route closes, the player must accept another position, earlier withdrawal, or a costly local fight.

## 20. Equipment transition and contact

Bows must be lowered or secured before normal axe use. Shields must be available and handled rather than appearing on command. Transition can be uneven across files; leaders must prevent shooters and cover fighters from obstructing each other.
Against improvised or disrupted pursuers, fresh cover files should credibly check a short contact. Against trained fresh melee infantry, the aim is limited separation under support, not victory. Against heavy assault or cavalry, external protection and early departure are the proper answers.

## 23. Counterplay

Opponents can threaten both exits, send cavalry toward the rally rather than the bow line, force repeated shifts, shoot compact cover files, or commit fresh heavy foot after the cover is spent. Shielded screens can reduce arrow value while another body closes the route.
Poor counterplay includes feeding isolated weak pursuers into fresh cover or ignoring an exposed flank under repeated arrows. The unit should punish those mistakes without forcing enemies to chase or suffer automatic suppression.

## 24. Terrain, weather, and fatigue

Woodland edges, broken approaches, and ridges offer positioning choices but still obstruct arrows, movement, visibility, and cohesion. No bog immunity, permanent concealment while firing, or zero terrain penalties are granted.
Drawing, running, climbing, shield handling, and axe contact all consume endurance. Weather requires sensible preparation and maintenance through shared systems; exact penalties are uncalibrated. A long cover fight can preserve lives while leaving the formation unable to deliver another meaningful shoot.

## 25. Leadership and morale

Recognized leaders coordinate fire, cease, passage, cover release, and rally. Losses can cause late shooting or separated files without an immediate whole-unit rout. A surviving senior follower may organize a simpler retreat, not instantly restore trained timing.
Morale depends on threat, losses, support, leadership, and escape. Retained service is not unbreakable loyalty. No national courage modifier, clan stereotype, compulsory last stand, or hidden betrayal roll defines the unit.

## 26. Recovery layers

Restore personnel, bow conditioning, bows and strings, arrows, axes and shields, protection, leadership, cohesion, and source access separately. Full headcount without a working cover cadre or arrow supply is not full readiness.
Understrength veterans retain judgment and familiar timing but lose frontage and output. Rebuilt formations regain numbers before shared transitions. Recovery must identify the decisive blocker without forcing the player to manage a separate meter for every item.

## 27. After-battle accounting and reduced missions

Account survivors, wounds, missing people, capture, dispersal, leaders, equipment, and stores. Ground control, time, labor, and transport determine what can be recovered. Captured followers are not available replacements.
Arrow-poor units may cover a short protected passage; cover-poor units may perform a static shoot behind strong protection; leader-poor units should avoid complex shifts. A source can close recruitment while existing veterans persist with restricted replacement or return for recovery.

## 28. Service closure and continuity

When terms end, the mission disappears, or the source cannot support further duty, compare renewal, restricted service, return, recovery, and accounted dissolution. Deployed followers do not vanish because access closes.
Foreign hire is a separate future access expression, not automatic availability. This Scottish family does not create a mercenary company, captain, contract, or second population. Any such expression must share actual capacity and valid release.

## 29. Scottish army interlock

Common spears create time and width. Ordered Spear Host carries the hedge forward. Retainers shoot from an offset, then protect a manageable local passage when that front shifts. Selkirk Bowmen maintain the stronger dedicated shooting front; the retainers accept lower output for close-cover flexibility.
Highland Warband takes ground, Armored Foot carries the hard sector, royal retainers secure a critical seam, and practical cavalry contests interception. None grants a passive synergy bonus. Friendly movement can obstruct the very passage the retainers need.

## 30. Roster separation

**Early Cateran Bowmen:** lower-burden route harassment and recoverable displacement.
**Highland Bow Retainers:** supported hybrid wing service and bounded passage cover.
**Selkirk Forest Bowmen:** developed dedicated shooting and repeated fire contribution.
**Galwegian Javelinmen:** close cast, assessment, and bounded press or refusal.
**Cateran Axemen:** offensive axe-led raid and breakaway.
**Mormaer’s Swordsmen:** selected sword decision.
**Future crossbows:** different weapon tempo and institutional burden if justified.
Similarity to foreign hybrids is acceptable. Scotland’s expression must come from the whole roster and service bargain, not exclusive ownership of bows and axes.

## 31. Campaign AI

## 32. Battle AI

## 37. Representative Battle Stories

**The noble wing:** Bow Retainers occupy a ridge shoulder beside Scottish Armored Foot. They punish ordinary infantry approaching the hard sector. When the armored line advances, the retainers cease fire, shorten their frontage, and move to the new shoulder.

**The chosen stand:** disrupted light foot reaches the hill. Guard files step around the rally marker, check the rush, and allow the shooting line to re-form. The unit resumes with fewer arrows and tired guard files. The same action against fresh heavy swordsmen becomes a disaster.

**The cavalry shadow:** enemy riders never charge. They occupy the return route and force the retainers to abandon a profitable shoot early. The counter succeeds through geometry.

**The price of nobility:** the unit wins a long melee against ordinary foot but loses leaders, shields, bows, and selected followers. The battlefield success creates a campaign-recovery crisis.

**The wrong army:** several Bow Retainers are recruited instead of common spears and Selkirk archers. The army has expensive flexible wings but insufficient frontage and dedicated arrow economy. The roster punishes monoculture.

**The camp edge:** behind a defended camp approach, retainers shoot and check one weak incursion until spears arrive. They do not need a forest map to justify their identity.
