## 1. Strong Unit Verdict

**Highland Noble Swordsmen are Scotland's proposed rare Highland great-weapon decision infantry: selected noble-facing fighters, experienced retainers, household associates, and proven regional champions who assemble around recognized leaders, approach behind the protection of the broader host, enter a short frontage where enemy guard and order can be broken, and use powerful two-handed swords to open the local fight before exposure, fatigue, missiles, cavalry, or fresh reserves overcome their limited protection.**

Their signature is **Open the Guard**.

Their decisive resource is the **guard-breaking window**: a short period when frontage, target commitment, weapon reach, fighter spacing, leader control, and friendly protection align well enough for two-handed sword work to materially disrupt the enemy's local defense. Their finite company resource is **champion cohesion**: selected fighters still connected closely enough to approach, work great weapons, halt, and leave as one commanded body.

## 2. One-Screen Total War Card

- **Unit:** Highland Noble Swordsmen.
- **Short name:** Noble Swordsmen.
- **Faction epithet:** **The Clan Champions**.
- **Class:** Rare selected two-handed sword assault infantry.
- **Era:** Later High or Late development candidate; exact emergence unresolved.
- **Signature:** **Open the Guard**.
- **Promise:** Preserve a rare great-weapon body until friendly troops secure the approach, then send the swordsmen into one short infantry decision where reach, timing, and powerful two-handed blows can break defensive order.
- **Best use:** Against an occupied heavy-infantry shoulder, a compressed shielded formation, an exposed flank, a breached local seam, a gate interior after entry is secured, or a counterattack against heavy foot already committed elsewhere.
- **Strong against:** Ordinary infantry in restricted contact, tired or disordered shield troops, protected infantry whose guard has been fixed or turned, and local heavy-foot threats without fresh support.
- **Competitive against:** Peer assault infantry and armored foot when the Noble Swordsmen enter coherently, preserve weapon space, deny clean enemy refacing, and receive flank protection.
- **Weak against:** Concentrated missiles, cavalry with charge room, prepared spears and pikes frontally, encirclement, long grinding melee, several-direction pressure, and fresh reserves arriving after the first guard is opened.
- **Campaign bargain:** A shallow source-bound pool concentrates rare fighters, leaders, expensive weapons, protection, and collective practice into a high-value company that is slow to rebuild.
- **Instruction:** Protect the approach, choose the short front, open the guard, and stop before the champions are surrounded.
- **Flavor:** **When the shielded line will not yield, send the blades that need both hands.**

## 4. Historical and Chronology Boundary

The classic Scottish two-handed claymore is a late-medieval and early-modern weapon. The retrieved overview places the two-handed Highland form in use from the 15th through 17th centuries and explains that *claymore* derives from Gaelic *claidheamh mòr*, meaning great sword. The same term has also been used for a later basket-hilted sword, so weapon naming and assets must remain explicit.

## 11. Signature: Open the Guard

### Preserve the Champions

Hold the company behind the battle's broad frontage. Do not spend rare great-weapon fighters on ordinary screening, missile exposure, or cheap custody.

### Approach Under Cover

Use Scottish spears, shields, terrain, missiles, or another assault body to reduce the missile and cavalry danger. Protection must be physical and positional, not an aura.

### Read the Guard

Identify the enemy's weapon reach, shield orientation, reserves, local spacing, fatigue, and exit routes. A visually heavy target is not automatically a suitable target.

### Enter the Short Front

The formation needs enough width for useful swords to work, but not so much width that the rare body loses leader connection and flank protection.

### Open the Guard

Use weapon reach, leverage, timing, file cooperation, and powerful controlled blows to disrupt shields, weapon control, facing, and local order. This creates an opening; it does not guarantee armor penetration, rout, or dismemberment.

### Decide or Disengage

Exploit only until the local objective is won or the favorable bargain expires. Rally behind a secure line if fresh opposition, missiles, cavalry, or congestion closes the window.
