## 1. Direct Unit Verdict

**Highland Warbands are organized regional assault infantry: shielded fighting bands that gather enough connected spear-and-axe frontage to contest ordinary infantry, take a bounded piece of ground, and consolidate it before the attack becomes a destructive heavy-infantry grind.**

Their signature is **Gather and Drive**. Unlike the Muster, they are recruited to enter a real infantry contest. Unlike Cateran Axemen, they do not need an isolated victim or a hidden route to justify commitment. They can continue useful contact after the first collision, but they cannot match selected household foot or heavy professional axe troops in prolonged close fighting.

The defining satisfaction is taking a stubborn field edge or recovering a pressured shoulder with an organized regional body, then deciding whether to hold, hand over, or withdraw. The unit is neither a berserker charge nor a slightly armored Cateran card.

## 2. One-Screen Total War Card

- **Class:** Organized regional assault infantry.
- **Promise:** Put coherent regional fighting weight into a contested infantry sector and remain useful beyond the first rush.
- **Best use:** An occupied enemy shoulder, a field boundary, a broad settlement approach, a rolling flank, or a weakened friendly sector with room to form.
- **Useful opponents:** Local musters, ordinary light foot, isolated raiders, and peer regional infantry where support, condition, and frontage favor the Warband.
- **Conditional opponents:** Ordinary shield infantry, shortened spear formations, and tired better troops already committed elsewhere.
- **Poor assignments:** Fresh heavy household infantry, professional heavy axe troops, prepared long-spear fronts without support, concentrated missile exposure, and cavalry reaching an open flank.
- **Campaign bargain:** More demanding to assemble and recover than a Muster; broader regional fighting capacity rather than a scarce household intervention or a permanent professional company.
- **Instruction:** Gather enough force to matter, drive for one useful gain, and secure the result before pursuing another.
- **Flavor:** **Gather the bands. Take the ground. Keep enough strength to hold it.**

## 3. Player-Facing Description

A Highland Warband brings regional fighting groups together for a deliberate field task. Shields, ordinary spears, practical axes, recognizable leaders, and repeated collective service give the formation a stronger fighting front than a hurried muster. It advances to contest ground, not simply to frighten an enemy with a sudden rush.

Its strength is the connection between the first files and the groups behind them. Spears help establish contact; axes and other close weapons become useful where the fighting compresses. Leaders keep the groups directed toward the same objective. When that connection fails, the Warband becomes several smaller fights instead of one useful attack.

These troops can win a local infantry decision and stay long enough to secure it. They remain vulnerable to arrows on approach, mounted threats on an open side, and better-equipped infantry that turns a short drive into a prolonged contest.

## 5. Naming and Historical Honesty

Highland Warband is retained as a readable gameplay title. Highland describes the Scotland family's proposed regional-source identity to be researched, not Theater ownership, a universal ethnic military category, a fixed administrative boundary, a standardized clan system, or a national costume. Warband means an assembled fighting body with collective service, not an undisciplined crowd.

No independent historical source was verified during this drafting pass. Exact chronology, source geography, service obligations, shield coverage, weapon distribution, armor, and terminology remain research questions. This profile does not claim an attested regiment or formal Gather and Drive doctrine.

Alternative names for later review include Regional Warband, Highland Fighting Band, and Highland Shieldmen. None should replace the working title merely to disguise an unsupported equipment or source case. Highland Retainers remains a different selected-service destination, not an interchangeable name.

## 6. The Player Choice: Fighting Weight or Specialist Access

Recruit the Warband when the army needs a regional body that can enter and continue a manageable infantry fight. Recruit Cateran Axemen when access to an exposed target and a rapid breakaway matter more. Recruit the Muster when inexpensive screening and obstruction are enough.

The Warband pays for contact capability through greater shield and weapon requirements, more assembly practice, more connected frontage, and higher exposure when advancing as a body. It is less comfortable than Caterans on a narrow concealed route and less economical than the Muster for a low-demand screen.

Its assault must feel worthwhile against ordinary foot. Requiring perfect concealment, an already routing target, and a household escort for every attack would erase the reason to recruit it.

## 9. Protected Capacity and Reservation

Protect household support, food production and processing, essential craft, transport, local warning, defense, and recovery before calculating safe capacity. Debit only actual overlapping sources. Do not impose a blanket economic penalty on every region with a Highland label.

People and equipment already assigned to the Muster, Caterans, archers, Galwegians, household troops, cavalry, naval service, or support cannot satisfy another complete claim. A leader cannot command separated primary formations simultaneously without a validated arrangement.

## 10. Ordinary Recruitment and Emergency Overdraw

Ordinary recruitment does not require an invasion alarm. A healthy source may support planned field service without a major penalty from one reasonable use. Pressure rises with repeated extraction, long absence, equipment depletion, casualties, and home threats.

Emergency overdraw may add hasty contributors or enlarge a local defense, but cannot manufacture the practice that defines the Warband. Additional unintegrated people may remain a separate muster contribution rather than increasing the Warband's effective assault strength. The same family may deploy degraded; it does not receive a free emergency card.

The player sees finite pool, price, time, service readiness, recovery, and the principal blocker. Individual shield reservations stay beneath the familiar recruitment interface.

## 11. Campaign Bargain and Army Composition

The proposed cost is above Hill and Glen Muster and above cheap Hearth Spearmen. Relative cost against Caterans depends on equipment, source, and service, not a mandatory universal price ranking. Availability is narrower than ordinary local contribution but need not be household-rare.

Several Warbands may be justified where independent sources and missions support them. No arbitrary global one-card limit is imposed. An army made entirely of Warbands should lack the Spear Host's economical width and prepared mounted denial, dedicated archery, and mounted response.

At equal budget, more Warbands buy stronger regional contact but fewer cheap secondary-task bodies. More Musters buy coverage and terrain support but less ability to take contested ground. More selected household foot buy a sharper scarce decision, not a limitless replacement for regional fighting mass.

## 13. Internal Weapon Tasks

Spear-bearing files establish initial reach and help keep the approach connected. Shielded close files contest compressed contact. Axe-bearing fighters exploit the space actually available; they do not attack through friendly spear files. Leaders maintain direction and halt the drive before the groups separate.

This is one commanded card. Internal task groups are not detachable free units, a professional relief system, or simultaneous spear-and-axe output for every person. Weapon transitions require space and handling time. Lost spears, damaged shields, and drawn sidearms persist through the action.

## 15. Signature: Gather and Drive

```text
Gather the participating groups
  -> establish one connected attack front
     -> close through a usable approach
        -> drive for a bounded infantry objective
           -> secure the gain and restore facing
              -> hold briefly, hand over, or withdraw
```

The signature is a physical battle relationship, not an activated attack spell. The player commits enough coherent force to take a real position and must then stop the action from becoming several disconnected pursuits.

## 16. Approach, Contact, and Consolidation Geometry

An approach must deliver enough of the card to matter. A narrow path may conceal movement while allowing only the first group into contact. A broad route may deliver mass while exposing shields and leaders to missiles.

Contact frontage needs room for spear handling, shield orientation, close weapons, and groups behind the first line. Excessive depth creates congestion rather than unlimited attacks. A supporting group cannot replace a damaged front by teleporting through it.

Consolidation needs room to halt, turn toward the next threat, reconnect leaders, and receive relief. Capturing a ridge or lane is not secure if the Warband arrives facing the wrong direction with its return blocked.

## 17. The Drive Economy

The decisive resource is **connected fighting weight still available when the first gain must be secured**.

- Commit early to save a sector, or wait for a cleaner opportunity.
- Use a wider front to threaten more ground, or concentrate enough force to win one local contest.
- Continue pressure against a weakening target, or halt before fresh reserves arrive.
- Hold the gain with tired fighters, or hand it to cheaper troops and recover the Warband.

Winning the first collision but losing coherence across the captured ground is an incomplete success. Refusing an unsupported second objective is part of competent assault play.

## 20. Three Failure Stories

**Missile failure:** a broad approach gives the enemy time to shoot. Shield orientation protects only actual covered directions. Leader and shield losses can break the drive before close combat begins.

**Cavalry failure:** the Warband commits its front and leaves an open side. Ordinary spears may discourage a careless probe, but the card lacks the Host's prepared reception and cannot turn instantly while fighting.

**Close-contact failure:** the first enemy yields, drawing groups forward at different speeds. Fresh heavy infantry reaches the separated front. Better regional staying power delays collapse but cannot restore missing connection by itself.

## 21. Enemy Counterplay

Use missiles to make the approach expensive, narrow the usable frontage, keep a fresh reserve behind the threatened position, and attack the Warband's sides while its front is occupied. A controlled fallback may stretch the participating groups if they pursue carelessly.

A peer formation can simply remain ordered and contest the objective. The Warband receives no automatic terror, shield destruction, ethnic courage, charge victory, or forced enemy withdrawal. The enemy can concede low-value ground rather than fight the Warband on its preferred terms.

## 22. Combined Arms and Relief

The Spear Host fixes the broader front while the Warband contests one shoulder. Forest Bowmen pressure exposed targets before the drive and cease fire when allies enter unsafe arcs. Cateran Bowmen can draw a screen away but do not guarantee an opening. Galwegian Javelinmen may cast beside the action; their shorter press must not block the Warband's contact frontage.

Cateran Axemen threaten another exposed route while the Warband remains in the infantry contest. March Riders screen mounted approaches. Mormaer's Horsemen exploit a real opening without riding through the Warband. Mormaer's Swordsmen remain a selected close-decision body, not the Warband's mandatory escort.

Hearth Spearmen may occupy a modest captured approach after a physical handover. Relief requires receiving troops to arrive, face the threat, and open passage before the Warband yields. No collision immunity or morale aura is created.

## 24. Terrain, Crossings, and Settlements

Rolling ground, broad field edges, sparse woodland, and settlement approaches can support a connected regional attack. Severe broken ground, dense trees, bog, steep rubble, and narrow gates can split it into isolated groups. The Muster may be better at dispersed obstruction; Caterans may be better at narrow-route access.

A Warband may counterattack an enemy that has crossed and lost order. It should not lead an unsupported crossing into prepared spears. In settlements it can contest a broad lane or yard, but not become a universal breach, ladder, or deep-street assault unit. Existing obstacles matter; the unit creates no barricades.

## 25. Whole-Battle Employment

**Opening:** gather behind useful cover or support, choose a plausible objective, and preserve an approach wide enough for meaningful contact.

**Main battle:** reinforce a weakening shoulder, recover a field edge, or attack ordinary foot fixed by another arm. Do not spend the card on a low-value chase while the main sector needs coherent infantry.

**After the gain:** halt, shorten the front if needed, reconnect groups, and decide whether the objective still matters. Receive relief before fatigue and enemy reserves erase the advantage.

**Late battle:** a preserved Warband can secure a withdrawal passage, contest tired peer foot, or hold a recovered approach. A depleted one may guard a smaller task; it does not regain assault power because the battle is nearly over.

## 26. Independent Utility

The card remains useful without a perfect spear-host setup. It can contest a secondary field objective, reinforce a regional defense, counterattack a weak camp approach, or fight peer infantry from a connected position.

It is not entitled to win every such task. Its independent value is the ability to conduct a normal bounded infantry action, not immunity to unsupported combat. A mission demanding stealth, sustained archery, mounted pursuit, or heavy armor defeat should use another family.

## 27. Command, Morale, and Fatigue

Confidence follows recognizable leaders, connected groups, working shields and weapons, support, a comprehensible objective, and a route back. Loss of a group leader can create uneven movement before causing a rout. Different sections may halt, continue, or fall back at different times.

Approach, shield carriage, repeated close action, redressing, and withdrawal consume endurance. The Warband can remain in contact longer than the Muster or a raiding band in its intended mission, not indefinitely. A second drive needs remaining people, equipment, cohesion, and condition. No regional stamina exemption or compulsory last stand is granted.

## 28. Campaign Service and Political Relationships

A regional source may support local defense, nearby field service, or a longer campaign when actual arrangements exist. Local origin does not impose a tiny movement radius. Distance, absence, casualties, supply, and competing duties can narrow availability or replacement.

Joining Scotland's army does not transfer permanent ownership of all contributors. Changes of lordship, command, or succession may affect access without deleting survivors or duplicating a fresh band. No automatic obedience, hostility, betrayal, or loyalty trait is assigned from a regional label.

A later hired expression may reuse the family with a familiar finite mercenary bargain: higher coin cost, available band, limited replenishment, and clear service. It cannot draw the same live capacity twice or become a miniature faction.

## 29. Supply and Equipment Wear

Food, footwear, clothing, shield repair, spear shafts, axe heads, sidearms, transport, and leader support sustain the band. It has no normal ammunition burden, which is a campaign advantage over missile hybrids, but shields and close equipment may suffer heavily in successful assaults.

Weather effects remain shared-system questions. No exact rain, snow, or wind penalty is invented. Light equipment does not make the unit tireless; stronger protection is not free to carry. Summarize the dominant shortage rather than require individual weapon administration.

## 32. Veterans, Reconstitution, and Closure

Understrength veterans retain better approach judgment, halt timing, group recognition, and consolidation. They cannot occupy missing frontage or absorb absent losses. A rebuilt fuller band restores mass before restoring shared timing and confidence between groups.

New recruitment and replacement may close separately. Survivors can continue understrength, accept restricted compatible replacements, shift to local duty, return for recovery, or dissolve with people and equipment accounted. Closing the pool does not delete veteran history. Reopening requires restored source capacity and qualification, not a technology timer.
