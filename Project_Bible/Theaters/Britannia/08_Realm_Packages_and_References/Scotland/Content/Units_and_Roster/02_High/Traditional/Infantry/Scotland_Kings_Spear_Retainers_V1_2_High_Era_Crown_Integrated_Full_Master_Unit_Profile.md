## 1. Direct Unit Verdict

**King's Spear Retainers are Scotland's narrow royal-service spear-and-shield infantry: better led, more consistently protected, and more responsive than the broad Scottish Spear Host, with enough close-weapon depth to secure a critical point, reinforce a wavering frontage, or make one controlled counter-entry without becoming dismounted knights or generic elite swordsmen.**

Their strength is not a longer spear than every other Scot carries. Their strength is that more of the formation arrives equipped, recognizes the same command relationship, keeps a tighter front, and can continue fighting after the first spear contact becomes a shield-and-sidearm struggle.

## 2. One-Screen Total War Card

- **Unit:** King's Spear Retainers
- **Class:** High-era royal-service spear-and-shield infantry
- **Battle promise:** Hold the point that broad common frontage cannot be allowed to lose, then answer manageable close pressure with controlled shield-and-sidearm work.
- **Best use:** Beside a Scottish Spear Host, near command or a royal camp, at a threatened gap, on a short critical approach, or behind the main line as a reliable stabilizing reserve.
- **Strong against:** Ordinary infantry in disciplined frontal contact, cavalry committed into prepared spears, disrupted attackers, and local breakthroughs that remain recoverable.
- **Competitive against:** Trained spear-and-shield infantry and medium melee troops when leadership, protection, frontage, and support favor the retainers.
- **Weak against:** Elite household infantry, prolonged missile concentration, deep encirclement, repeated large-sector relief, heavy cavalry striking an unprepared flank, and battles requiring broad cheap frontage.
- **Campaign bargain:** Scotland gains a dependable royal infantry layer by concentrating trusted service, leaders, maintained spears, shields, helmets, selected mail, sidearms, and support into a small finite pool.
- **Player instruction:** Do not stretch them across the army. Place them where failure would matter, let the common host provide width, and commit the retainers before a local problem becomes a total collapse.
- **Flavor line:** **Where the king's line must not fail, the retainers take their place.**

### Immediate High-Era Read

- **Signature:** **Hold Fast for the Crown**.
- **Postures:** **Command-Seam Anchor**, **Pre-Collapse Reinforcement**, **Bounded Counter-Entry**.
- **Player decision:** Choose the point that matters, commit before it fails, and restore control before the Crown's reserve is spent.

The promise is dependable intervention, not absolute stability. The formation can start on the line or wait in reserve. The most important point is not always the largest fight: a seam, crossing, command approach, or withdrawal passage can decide whether the wider army remains functional.

Against manageable ordinary infantry, the player should see a firmer front and a useful local response. This is not merely a card that dies more slowly. It can secure ground, stop a local failure, or recover a bounded objective. It cannot rescue every collapse or hold a point after the whole position has become untenable.

## 3. Final Player-Facing Description

King's Spear Retainers are selected royal-service infantry equipped around spear and shield but prepared for the moment when a spear line becomes a close fight. Better leaders, denser helmet and mail provision, maintained shields, and more dependable sidearms allow them to hold a narrower critical sector with greater confidence than Scotland's common hosts.

They are most valuable beside the broad Scottish Spear Host. The host covers ground and fixes the enemy; the retainers secure the seam, reinforce a damaged section, protect a command approach, or step forward against an enemy already committed to the spears. Their royal relationship does not make them invulnerable. They remain finite foot soldiers whose service, equipment, leaders, and recovery cost Scotland more than common frontage.

### Weapon-handling rule

The weapon mix is organized by sections and serviceable equipment, not random individual bonuses. Spears remain dominant while the formation holds a front. Sidearms emerge when:

- the enemy gets inside effective spear reach;
- a controlled counter-entry begins;
- the formation enters cramped ground;
- shafts break or become unusable;
- a bounded relief requires closer work.

Sword-equipped retainers provide guarded close work where their actual weapons and handling permit. Axe-equipped retainers provide deliberate local pressure within shared weapon, shield, and armor rules. Neither label guarantees faster attacks or armor penetration. Neither sidearm transforms the entire unit into elite melee infantry.

## 14. Five-Part Battle Loop

### Attend

The retainers begin near the command relationship, royal camp, reserve, or critical frontage they are meant to support. They preserve order and are not spent merely because they are better equipped.

### Anchor

They establish a compact spear-and-shield front at the point where failure would compromise command, a flank seam, a route, a missile position, or the common host.

### Receive

Prepared spears meet cavalry or infantry approach. Better leaders and protection allow the formation to survive a manageable first contact more reliably than broad common troops.

### Close

If the enemy enters the spear front or becomes committed and disordered, selected sword and axe sections conduct controlled close work. Close is not a shock charge. It is a bounded transition that prevents a spear formation from becoming helpless after compression.

### Restore

The retainers re-establish the point, shorten frontage, hand the broad sector back to common spears, cover withdrawal, or return to reserve before royal-service infantry is consumed in ordinary attrition.

## 18. Close-Weapon Section Contract

### Sword sections

- Guarded close attacks and recovery governed by actual handling and contact space.
- Better fit for leaders, wealthier retainers, and soldiers expected to continue fighting after spear compression.
- No automatic armor penetration or superiority over axes.

### Axe sections

- Deliberate local blows against shields and protection under the shared damage model.
- Strong Scottish visual character without turning the formation into Highland axemen.
- Slightly greater exposure and recovery cost during committed swings.
- No universal shield breaking.

### Spear sections

- Preserve frontage, mounted deterrence, and approach control.
- Continue supporting sidearm sections instead of every soldier switching weapons at once.

Weapon sections describe equipment distributions and local tasks inside one card. They do not create detachable sword and axe companies. ## 19. Strengths, Weaknesses, and Counterplay

### Strengths

- Stronger leadership and order retention than common spear infantry.
- Better helmet, shield, sidearm, and selective mail concentration.
- Reliable critical-sector spear reception.
- Can operate as main-line anchor or reserve.
- Controlled transition into close combat.
- Better relief and redress behavior.
- Strong relationship with broad common hosts and missile troops.

### Weaknesses

- Narrow frontage.
- Scarce royal-service source.
- Expensive casualties and slow leader replacement.
- Below true elite household or professional heavy infantry.
- Vulnerable to concentrated missiles and encirclement.
- Counter-entry can consume the exact reserve the army needs later.
- Mixed sidearm sections demand leadership and space.
- Cannot economically hold every sector.

### Enemy counterplay

- Force the retainers to cover too much ground.
- Draw them away from the protected point.
- Shoot the compact formation.
- Attack another sector and make the royal reserve choose.
- Pin with ordinary infantry, then attack from a second direction.
- Commit fresh heavy infantry after the counter-entry.
- Threaten cavalry while sidearm sections are engaged.
- Block relief and withdrawal lanes.

## 39B. Critical-Point Economy and Player Choice

The unit's battlefield resource is **reliable attention concentrated on one important place**.

### Preserve or commit

Holding the retainers back protects Scotland's ability to answer a later crisis. Committing them early strengthens the present line but removes that option.

### Anchor or reinforce

Anchoring gives the formation its cleanest frontage and mission. Reinforcing a pinned ally may be essential, but mixed files and blocked weapons reduce its best expression.

### Receive or counter-enter

Remaining on spears preserves mounted denial. Counter-entering with sword and axe sections can recover ground, but it lowers readiness against fresh cavalry and may expose the mission point.

### Restore or remain

After success, the retainers can restore the point and return to reserve, or remain as the new front. Remaining is safer immediately but spends stamina and royal capacity in ordinary contact.

## 39F. Adjacent-Family Collision Refinement

### Scottish Spear Host

The Host owns width, attainable numbers, broad mounted denial, and enemy binding. The retainers own a shorter front, faster local response, better equipment concentration, and close-contact recovery.

### Scottish Armored Foot

Scottish Armored Foot own sustained protected sector fighting, maintained field service, and relief endurance through qualified castle and lordship networks where supported. Retainers own the royal mission, anchor timing, and critical-point economy.

### Mormaer's Retinue

A mormaer's retinue owns loyalty and service to its magnate, regional equipment expression, and local political availability. King's Spear Retainers require royal receiving authority and should not automatically absorb every magnate's best men.

### Scottish Feudal Knights and Conditional Royal Mounted Service

Scottish Feudal Knights own formed lance impact, counterblow, short post-impact fighting, and horse-dependent service. March Riders retain routine screening and pursuit. A separate royal mounted family remains conditional. Retainers do not become their dismounted fallback pool without deliberate reassignment, horse disposition, equipment compatibility, and a separate campaign decision.

### King's Foot Guard

The later guard can inherit crisis reserve, command protection, and converged royal armored-foot responsibilities only after a real institutional gate. It must not appear as King's Spear Retainers plus plate and higher statistics.
