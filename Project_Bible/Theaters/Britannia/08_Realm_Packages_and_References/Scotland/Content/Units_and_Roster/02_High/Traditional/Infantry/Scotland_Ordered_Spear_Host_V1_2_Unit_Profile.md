## 1. Direct Unit Thesis

**Ordered Spear Hosts are High-era Scottish common-field formations trained and supported to carry a connected spear-and-shield line onto important ground and settle it into a formidable directional hedge before impact.**

Their primary signature is **Advance the Hedge**. Their achieved formation condition is **Wall of Ash**. Their supporting doctrine is **Keep the Host Together**.

The unit's promise is physical and immediately readable:

```text
Dress the spear sections
  -> carry the hedge forward
     -> halt on the chosen line
        -> present the Wall of Ash
           -> receive, bind, or apply measured pressure
              -> shorten, hand over, withdraw, or recommit
```

This is not a professional Lowland regiment, an automatic burgh unit, a royal guard, or a mature schiltron. The formation remains a broad Scotland common-host development. It may draw strongly from southern, eastern, royal, baronial, burgh-adjacent, and veteran host relationships where those sources qualify, but no single geography or institution monopolizes the card without later owner and historical proof.

The unit is more ordered than the Early Scottish Spear Host, not simply stronger in every statistic. The Early host still buys cheaper width. The Ordered host buys the ability to move that common front toward danger, stop it in time, withstand the first directional contest, and retain enough organization to make another useful battlefield decision.


## 2. One-Screen Total War Card

- **Unit:** Ordered Spear Host
- **Class:** High-era coordinated common-host spear infantry
- **Battle promise:** Carry a broad Scottish spear-and-shield front onto useful ground and settle it into the Wall of Ash before the enemy's main pressure arrives.
- **Signature behavior:** **Advance the Hedge**
- **Achieved condition:** **Wall of Ash**
- **Supporting doctrine:** **Keep the Host Together**
- **Best use:** Deliberate main-line advance, directional anti-cavalry reception, protection of missile space, restoration of damaged frontage, and one renewed battle phase.
- **Strong against:** Frontal mounted attacks and ordinary infantry forced to solve a connected, halted spear front.
- **Competitive against:** Peer common infantry when the Ordered Host preserves its sections, leaders, shield direction, and working depth.
- **Weak against:** Crossfire, several-direction pressure, heavy infantry inside the spear reach, dense broken ground, repeated forced turns, and losses among section leaders.
- **Campaign bargain:** A more demanding and more useful High-era common formation, still broader and more attainable than royal or heavily maintained foot.
- **Player instruction:** Bring the hedge forward. Set it where the kingdom must stand.
- **Flavor:** **Many banners. One front.**

## 3. Player-Facing Description

Ordered Spear Hosts turn Scotland's varied common military sources into a visible High-era main line. Section leaders dress the files around shared markers. Long spears rise into safer carriage for movement. Shield-bearing files keep the formation's exposed face coherent. At the chosen position, the host halts, lowers its points, and forms the **Wall of Ash**.

The player recruits the unit not because every soldier is heavily armored, but because more of the front can move and settle together. A Scottish Spear Host can create affordable width. The Ordered Spear Host carries that width toward the part of the battlefield where it matters most.

When the Wall of Ash is established, frontal cavalry and ordinary infantry face a hedge of working spear points, directional shields, supporting depth, and leaders who can replace a damaged local file before the entire line opens. The formation can then bind the attack, preserve shooting space, or apply measured pressure into yielding infantry.

The unit remains mortal and physical. Repeated arrows kill leaders and expose sections. A turn can pull shields and spear points away from the true threat. Heavy foot inside the reach forces an inferior close fight. Broken terrain separates the local groups the card depends upon. Better order creates another decision; it does not erase casualties, fatigue, or bad ground.

## 5. Faction identity and adaptation contract

The unit makes **many hosts, one battle plan** visible in ordinary play. Scotland's High army gains a dependable common front without requiring every soldier to become a royal retainer, armored professional, or Highland assault specialist.

Preserve familiar Total War choices: recruitment pools, formation width, facing, movement, halt, attack, guard behavior, and withdrawal. The simulation can track more underneath, but the player should not approve individual spear reservations or issue separate orders to every section.

Draw selectively from the project's Modern Medieval II philosophy: finite pools, readable strengths and counters, meaningful veteran recovery, and clear formation states. No foreign civic institution or professional drill system is copied into Scotland merely because another faction has a strong spear profile.

## 6. Exact progression bargain

The Early Scottish Spear Host already sets, receives, binds, and redresses. Ordered Spear Host therefore cannot survive by merely claiming those same verbs. Its proposed difference is **repeatable field coordination under changing conditions**.

```yaml
Early_host:
  principal_purchase: AffordablePreparedWidthAndTime
  acceptable_limit: MorePreparationAndLessReliableRepeatedMovement
Ordered_host:
  principal_purchase: RepeatedUsefulMovementHaltAndFrontRestoration
  additional_burden: QualifiedSectionLeadersPracticeAndSupportedService
separate_card_fails_if:
  - OnlyMoraleArmorOrAttackImproves
  - EarlyHostPerformsTheSameRepeatedManeuverAtTheSameBurden
  - CoordinationHasNoVisibleEffectOutsideFlavorText
```

At equal budget, the Early host should still buy more accessible coverage. The Ordered host should deliver more useful formation work over a changing battle. At equal headcount, the difference should be most visible after movement, losses, and renewed commitment, not in a motionless spear duel alone.

## 7. Source constitution

Candidate sources are recognized Scottish common-field contributors with a sustained assembly relationship, compatible leaders, serviceable long weapons, and enough collective practice to meet the ordered qualification. Several providers can support one card if their service terms and command connection are compatible.

Neither Highland nor Lowland identity automatically qualifies or excludes a source. There is no universal national manpower bucket. Local duties, households, transport, warning, garrisons, naval service, archery, cavalry, and existing formations retain their actual claims.

People may overlap between source ecosystems, but only actual overlap is debited. Do not impose a blanket economic penalty on every Scottish region when one host is recruited.

## 9. Finite pools, replacement, and emergency access

New recruitment and replacement draw on related but separately evaluated capacity. A source may be unable to raise another host while still supporting limited replacement for survivors. It may also have people but lack enough long spears or leaders to restore the ordered promise.

Ordinary supported service should be practical. Repeated mobilization, long absence, casualties, and local threat raise pressure. Emergency overdraw can add people but not manufacture coordinated files. A hasty body that cannot meet ordered qualification is evaluated for an appropriate common-host expression, not granted this card at full quality.

## 10. Campaign economy and availability

Design target: attainable High-era backbone, not an ultra-rare elite. Several formations may serve where real sources support them. No arbitrary global one-card cap is proposed.

Recruitment and upkeep should exceed the Early host where the ordered package adds leaders, preparation, equipment maintenance, and service support. It remains below the concentrated institutional burden of selected royal or heavily maintained foot in its intended broad-front role. Exact costs are unresolved; no universal ranking against every regional specialist is imposed.

An all-Ordered-Host army spends too much on coordination while lacking missiles, pursuit, assault, and protected close-combat power. A mixed army can use cheaper Early hosts on low-demand ground and Ordered hosts where the battle is likely to move.

## 11. Equipment identity

Long infantry spears dominate. Their length, handling, and source variation require historical review; no exact measurement is certified. Shields, practical sidearms, helmets, padded protection where supported, and limited mail create a more serviceable package without making the entire formation armored foot.

```yaml

  shield_use: SourceAndHandlingCompatibleDirectionalCoverage
  sidearms: KnivesAxesAndLimitedSwordsWhereSupported

  - UniversalMailOrPlate
  - GreatswordOrHeavyAxeMajority
  - FreeMissileVolley
  - IdenticalNationalUniform
```

Better inspection reduces unusable equipment and mismatched handling. It does not produce identical weapons. A two-handed spear action cannot simultaneously gain the full protection of a shield held in an incompatible position.

## 12. Leaders, sections, and working depth

Recognized section leaders carry the practical identity. They maintain intervals, relay intent, prevent isolated advances, and help shorten damaged frontage. This is not modern drill or a professional countermarch.

The front files present weapons. Supporting depth sustains the front through real space. Reserve files provide local replacement or cover a withdrawal only when passage exists. Rear ranks do not all attack through friendly bodies. Excess depth produces congestion, not free damage.

Loss of leadership can leave headcount intact while destroying the unit's main purchase. A smaller veteran body may perform a simple controlled move better than a fuller newly rebuilt one, but cannot occupy missing ground.

## 14C. Order Reserve and Section Integrity

The unit's main resource is **order reserve**: the remaining ability of leaders and connected sections to complete another coordinated change without fragmenting. This is not a visible mana bar or passive national bonus. It is a summary of surviving leaders, fatigue, spacing, section connection, and formation memory.

A depleted formation may still stand, receive simply, or withdraw. What it loses first is the defining ability to perform another clean coordinated transition. The player should understand this through section-state feedback, not through an arcade ability meter unless later UI testing proves one necessary.

## 15. Movement-to-reception transition

The unit is most distinctive while moving between useful fronts. Spears must be carried safely, sections need enough room to follow, and the formation must settle before its best mounted denial returns.

A measured advance trades speed for connection. A hurried move may be necessary to save the battle, but spends that advantage. A wheel exposes the outside files and disrupts presentation. Shortening the front can restore control while surrendering ground to either side.

```yaml
transition_outcomes:
  CleanHalt: ConnectedSectionsSetBeforeContact
  UnevenHalt: SomeFilesReadyOthersStillMoving
  InterruptedHalt: EnemyReachesTheFormationDuringAdjustment
  OverextendedAdvance: NeighborsAndSectionsLoseConnection
  ControlledShortening: LessWidthButAUsableFront
  FailedRedress: LocalCorrectionCreatesAnotherGap
```

## 16. Anti-cavalry and infantry power

A settled, coherent long-spear front should be a serious frontal cavalry problem. That remains a core spear-family strength, not a unique supernatural benefit of this successor. Moving, turning, missile disruption, gaps, fatigue, and oblique approach reduce reception.

Against ordinary foot, reach and depth support a working contest and limited forward pressure. The host can advance into a favorable ordinary infantry fight without requiring a perfect flank. It is not an assault breaker. Heavy close infantry that passes the points exposes its uneven protection and limited sidearm training.

No cavalry deletion field, reflection damage, automatic knockback, taunt, rooting effect, or immunity is proposed. Enemy troops can refuse, withdraw, shoot, or concede ground.

## 17. Three recurring player decisions

**Breadth or connection:** cover more ground, or shorten enough to keep the sections working. Better leadership extends the useful envelope but does not remove its limit.

**Move or receive:** reposition before the enemy fixes the battle, or settle for stronger reception. The unit cannot have both at full strength simultaneously.

**Restore or exploit:** pause to rebuild the front after a local success, or accept the risks of pressing a weakening enemy. Pursuit is not automatically wrong, but a long chase is usually a poor use of this formation.

## 18. Counterplay and failure stories

**Missile preparation:** the enemy shoots while the host dresses or advances. Directional shields help where physically presented; exposed files and leaders still suffer. Repeated halts can become predictable targets.

**Two-direction pressure:** a mounted threat draws a turn while infantry holds the front. The Ordered host reacts more coherently than a hasty body, but cannot rotate through engaged soldiers or cover every direction.

**Reach collapse:** fresh armored infantry compresses the front after missile disruption. Leaders may preserve a shortened body or withdrawal passage, but cannot turn sidearms into professional heavy-foot capability.

A competent opponent can also yield low-value ground and force the host to decide whether the next move is worth its fatigue and exposure.

## 19. Combined arms and independent utility

Ordered hosts establish a moving-and-settling front for Forest Bowmen, protect the working space of Galwegian Javelinmen, and keep an enemy occupied while Highland Warband contests a shoulder. Cateran Axemen exploit a separate route rather than walking through the spear files.

Scottish Armored Foot can carry a harder sector. King's Spear Retainers stabilizes a critical point rather than serving as a mandatory escort. March Riders screen and pursue; Mormaer's Horsemen and Scottish Feudal Knights exploit actual opportunities without charging through friendly spears.

Independent uses include a deliberate advance onto unoccupied ground, protection of a retreating missile body, a crossing approach after deployment, and a modest counteradvance against ordinary foot. The unit remains useful when no perfect combined-arms flank exists.

## 20. Relief, disengagement, and handover

Relief requires a receiving formation, correct facing, space, and timing. The Ordered host can shorten, create passage, or withdraw behind a prepared body. It cannot phase through a friendly line or exchange places instantly while deeply engaged.

A clean handover preserves capability. A delayed one spends fatigue and casualties. A congested one can make both units worse. If the receiving unit cannot hold the threat, withdrawal may abandon the ground or force renewed contact.

Do not make relief mandatory after every fight. It is one valuable option among holding, restoring, advancing, and withdrawing.

## 21. Terrain, weather, and settlements

Firm fields, gentle slopes, broad approaches, and clear rear routes best support the unit. Rolling ground and sparse woodland can remain usable with shortened frontage. Dense trees, bog, steep rubble, narrow gates, and offensive crossing transit split the sections and obstruct long weapons.

No Scottish homeland bonus or terrain immunity is granted. Familiarity can inform route choice without removing physical penalties. Weather affects footing, fatigue, equipment, visibility, and supply through shared systems; no exact weather modifier is invented.

Broad settlement lanes may support spear work. Ladders, breach mouths, and cramped street fighting are not this unit's specialty.

## 22. Campaign service and source expressions

Community-led, lord-connected, and returning-veteran sources can qualify for the same card. They may differ in availability, equipment distribution, support, and service burden without creating three hidden combat tiers.

Longer or more distant service requires stronger supply and return arrangements. Local origin does not impose an arbitrary tiny movement radius. Political changes can restrict replacement or recall a formation without deleting survivors.

A hired expression, if later authorized, reuses a real released formation under Britannia's mercenary rules. It does not create another population or a mini-faction.

## 25. Persistence, conversion, and schiltron development

Early Scottish Spear Hosts remain recruitable where their economy and source breadth matter. Individual veterans may qualify for Ordered hosts; whole-formation conversion requires actual leaders, equipment, practice, and service. Experience stars alone do not authorize conversion.

A future schiltron capability may be a specialized card or a formation qualification. This draft leaves that decision open. Ordered hosts do not automatically gain dense all-round defense, and a separate schiltron card must prove a different normal purchase rather than merely receive more anti-cavalry statistics.

When recruitment closes, existing hosts persist under valid service and replacement terms. Return, local duty, recovery, or dissolution accounts for people and equipment. No era transition deletes veterans.

## 27. Battle AI and autoresolve

Battle AI should choose a useful threat axis, move before contact rather than after encirclement, halt in time, preserve missile and reserve lanes, and shorten a damaged front. It should ignore minor feints when turning would expose the main threat, but not remain stationary under unlimited fire.

No omniscient reserve detection is allowed. AI uses the same movement, equipment, fatigue, and recovery rules as the player. It should not repeatedly issue contradictory formation orders to chase tiny theoretical advantages.

Autoresolve should distinguish prepared reception, moving contact, usable frontage, leader condition, missile exposure, flank access, close-infantry quality, and retreat space. The word Ordered does not create a hidden victory multiplier.
