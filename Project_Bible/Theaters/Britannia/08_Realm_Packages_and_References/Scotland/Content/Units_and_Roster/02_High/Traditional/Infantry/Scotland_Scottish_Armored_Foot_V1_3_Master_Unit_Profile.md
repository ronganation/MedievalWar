## 1. Strong Unit Verdict

**Scottish Armored Foot are Scotland's maintained mail-and-shield field company: the formation assigned to bear the ordinary battle at the point where ordinary troops can no longer carry it. They dress a protected spear front, absorb the first pressure, feed experienced files into a compressed fight, move forward when the enemy begins to yield, and leave behind a usable Scottish position rather than a heap of exhausted victors.**

Their signature is **Bear the Battle**. This is not another emergency-reserve card. The player may deploy them directly into an important working sector, advance them against ordinary foot, or use them to take over a failing frontage. Their identity comes from the combination of protection, file depth, weapon continuity, repeated field service, and controlled relief.

The creative core is the **iron shoulder of the many-host army**. The Spear Host gives Scotland width; royal retainers safeguard the crown's decisive point; Mormaer's Swordsmen win one chosen close decision. Scottish Armored Foot carry the sustained burden between those moments. They must feel strong enough to bend ordinary infantry backward, durable enough to remain useful after the first contact, and expensive enough that grinding them away is a campaign mistake.

- **Class:** Maintained armored spear-and-shield infantry.
- **Promise:** A dependable sector that can fight longer than common host troops and continue usefully when the front compresses into close combat.
- **Weapons:** Serviceable infantry spear, shield, and a real close sidearm.
- **Protection:** Mail-centered equipment direction, helmets, and shields; exact coverage remains subject to period and source review.
- **Strengths:** Strong against ordinary infantry, protected frontal contact, useful prepared cavalry deterrence, reliable relief, and sustained campaign employment when supplied.
- **Weaknesses:** High cost, narrow recruitment, modest frontage per expenditure, fatigue in armor, crossfire, flank attacks, heavy assault specialists, and slow recovery after material losses.
- **Best assignment:** Take the sector that must keep working while Scotland's broader host and regional arms maneuver elsewhere.

- **Flavor:** **The host makes the line. These men keep it in service.**

## 3. Player-Facing Description

A broad Scottish host can hold much ground, but not every part of that front can endure the same pressure. These armored foot soldiers concentrate maintained weapons, shields, mail, and familiar fighting files into a smaller, more dependable body. Their value is the ability to accept a hard assignment and continue performing it after less prepared troops would need to withdraw.

Place them on an important approach or bring them forward to replace a tiring spear body. They receive with ordered points, fight at close range when the front compresses, and redress when space returns. They can advance against ordinary infantry with support, but their armor does not make them fast assault troops or protect an open flank.

They are expensive to keep and harder to rebuild than to hire. Losing experienced files, shields, mail, and leaders can leave the company present on the muster roll but unable to perform the sustained field duty for which it was raised.

## 5. Scottish Faction Fit

## 13. Signature: Bear the Battle

**Assign the sector:** identify the ground and the sustained task, not just the nearest enemy.

**Dress the front:** align shields and points, clear friendly routes, and preserve depth.

**Receive or advance:** hold a useful approach or move against ordinary foot with support.

**Work the contact:** maintain the spear front where possible and continue close fighting where necessary.

**Restore order:** use a real lull to recover facing, move casualties, replace damaged files, and reassess the mission.

**Continue or relieve:** retain the assignment only while enough physical capability remains; otherwise transfer or withdraw.

This is a familiar move, face, attack, halt, formation, and withdrawal loop. It does not require six unique ability buttons or a hidden service-capacity currency.

## 16. Frontal Mounted Threat

Prepared points, stable ground, intact shields, depth, and supporting neighbors give credible frontal resistance. The player must face the threat and stop moving in time. A formation turning, advancing in disorder, or fighting close cannot instantly receive cavalry as if it had always been prepared.

Cavalry can threaten the flank, force an early halt, attack a depleted body, or combine with missiles. Armor reduces some consequences but does not protect the formation from momentum, disorder, encirclement, or mount-and-rider contact under the shared combat rules. No universal anti-large damage multiplier is specified.

## 17. Offensive Use and Limits

This is not a passive unit. It should advance against ordinary infantry, recover a short ridge, press a contested road, or relieve a host body and continue the fight. Its measured attack trades explosive impact for better order and the ability to remain on the ground afterward.

Prepared deep long spears, fresh assault elites, defended breaches, and crossfire are poor frontal assignments. Use missiles, a second angle, terrain, or relief rather than expecting mail to solve every tactical problem. Against weaker troops, however, the player should see steady progress rather than a premium unit endlessly waiting for ideal conditions.

## 18. Relief Is a Physical Handoff

The relieved formation needs a route and time. Neighboring troops must protect the shoulders while the armored body takes the front. Mixed friendly bodies can obstruct spears, expose gaps, and lose facing.

```yaml
handoff_outcomes:
  CleanTakeover: OldBodyLeavesNewFrontEstablished
  ReinforceInPlace: BodiesSupportWithoutFullExchange
  PressuredTakeover: TemporaryDisorderAndPartialFrontage
  PartialRelief: SomeFilesRemainPinned
  FailedTakeover: GapCrowdingOrEnemyPenetration
  ExtractionOnly: GroundAbandonedToPreserveSurvivors
```
Relief does not restore fatigue, casualties, or equipment. The new body assumes the task with its own remaining depth. A second relief is possible only if enough capability and geometry survive.

### Relief Company: Operational Sequence and Abort Decision

The player selects a tired sector, not a teleport destination. The incoming company approaches behind a protected shoulder or through an actual interval. It establishes enough of its own front before the relieved troops attempt separation. Where the enemy pins those troops, relief may be partial or become reinforcement in place. There is no assumption that an entire unit can withdraw through an enemy-engaged front.

```text
Identify the tired sector and protect its shoulders
  -> approach through usable space
     -> establish an incoming front or adjacent supporting face
        -> signal and open the feasible withdrawal passage
           -> extract the files that can actually leave
              -> close the new frontage and restore facing
                 -> continue the mission or shorten the sector
```

Abort or narrow the handoff when enemy cavalry reaches the passage, fresh assault troops exploit the interval, friendly traffic blocks the lane, or the incoming company cannot establish a viable face. Preserving part of the old host while yielding ground is a legitimate result. Relief skill improves execution under the shared battle model; it is not a claim that a fixed historical rotation drill has been established.

**Measured Press** is equally bounded. Pressure creates an opportunity only when the enemy gives space or loses local order. If the enemy does not yield, the company continues an ordinary contest at real cost. Neither the name nor a successful posture transition supplies automatic pushback.

## 20. Combined Arms and Independent Use

Alongside the Spear Host, armored foot can occupy a difficult shoulder or replace a tired section, freeing common troops to extend the line. Forest Bowmen can shoot targets held in front; javelin troops need a clear casting and withdrawal lane. Caterans can exploit a flank while this body keeps the target engaged. Mormaer's Swordsmen can deliver the sharper close decision while armored foot assumes the sustained ground-holding afterward.

These are physical interactions, not passive faction buffs. The unit also works independently on a short front, camp approach, road, or siege-work defense. It must not require all Scottish families to be present before its ordinary field role functions.

### Many Hosts, One Battle Plan

The army relationship is **screen, width, anchor, blade, missiles, hammer**. March Riders screen and intercept. Common hosts buy frontage; Ordered Spear Host moves and settles the main hedge. Scottish Armored Foot carry the expensive working sector. Highland Warband, Caterans, and magnate swordsmen seek local infantry decisions. Missile families shape exposure and facing. Scottish Feudal Knights deliver scarce formed impact.

## 21. Terrain, Weather, and Missiles

Firm, usable ground supports points and replacement depth. Broken ground, trees, steep slopes, mud, rubble, and narrow streets can disrupt the company's own order. Scotland does not grant universal hill mastery to every troop.

Shields protect directionally and mail contributes under the shared damage model. Sustained missile concentration, crossfire, shooting during a turn, and fire against depleted shields remain effective counters. Weather affects visibility, ground, fatigue, supply, and equipment condition through shared systems, not a special Scottish winter immunity.

## 22. Siege and Garrison Service

Useful assignments include guarding siege works, resisting a sortie, holding an opened gate approach, defending a road into camp, and taking over a captured interior position. These tasks justify maintained service without turning the unit into a universal breach specialist.

Ladders, packed breaches, overhead missiles, and repeated assault waves can destroy expensive equipment and cadre. A field company cannot also count as a full garrison at its source. A player who removes it must accept the actual local security cost. No automatic siege-engine skill or free wall-defense bonus is assigned.

## 23. Leadership, Signals, and Morale

The company needs a recognized field leader, surviving experienced files, and signals that can communicate face, advance, halt, relieve, and withdraw. These are proposed functional requirements, not invented historical officer titles or a fixed establishment.

Leader loss should slow turning, redressing, and relief. A recognized second may continue; if common control fails, surviving files may hold locally, withdraw, surrender, or rally. Standards are visible references, not morale auras. High reliability is compatible with rational retreat and capture. Professional is not a synonym for unbreakable.

## 24. Campaign Support and Service Duration

The proposed advantage is longer useful service than short obligation mobilization when pay or maintenance, food, shelter, repair, command, and source consent remain available. No fixed service duration, wage, or universal contract is invented.

Longer service also accumulates wounds, illness, damaged shields, worn shafts, mail repair, fatigue, and leadership strain. A company can remain present while its field capability declines. Distant campaigning can make replacement and repair harder even when treasury funds remain high.

## 25. Casualties, Capture, and Material Recovery
