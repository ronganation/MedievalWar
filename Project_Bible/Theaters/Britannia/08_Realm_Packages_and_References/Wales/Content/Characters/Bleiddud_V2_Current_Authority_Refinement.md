## Core Character Thesis

Bleiddud begins the campaign as an identifiable Welsh ecclesiastical person who occupies a strategically important office at St Davids, while remaining distinct from the office, institution, place, property, jurisdiction, and wider Welsh religious life associated with that center.

The profile's central tension is whether Bleiddud can preserve religious continuity, institutional credibility, community protection, and political usefulness without allowing ecclesiastical office to become a universal source of legitimacy, an automatic political faction, or an extension of one ruler's government.

Bleiddud matters because St Davids is a major religious and cultural center at a moment when Wales combines broad political rule with uneven regional integration and rising frontier pressure. Bleiddud does not matter because the profile grants universal Church authority or direct control over every Welsh cleric.

## Historical Identity

Bleiddud, also recorded in variant forms such as Bedwd, is associated with the bishopric of St Davids, then commonly represented through names including Menevia or Mynyw, from approximately 1061 until death in 1071.

The surviving record is sparse.

The source tradition supports these bounded propositions:

- Bleiddud is a historical person;
- Bleiddud is active in the opening period;
- Bleiddud occupies the Bishop of St Davids office by January 1062;
- Bleiddud succeeds an earlier office holder traditionally identified as Joseph;
- Bleiddud remains associated with the office until death around 1071;
- Sulien later succeeds Bleiddud;
- later tradition associates Bleiddud with episcopal activity beyond St Davids, including a reported consecration connected to Llanelwy or St Asaph around 1070.

The record does not support a detailed biography, precise household, complete career, universal Welsh ecclesiastical supremacy, exact property portfolio, complete jurisdiction map, or fully reconstructed institutional hierarchy.
