## Core Dynasty Thesis

House Mathrafal begins not as the government of Powys or Gwynedd, but as a **latent adult continuity network** whose principal members are close maternal relatives of the living Welsh realm anchor.

The dynasty's opening importance comes from:

- adult dynastic redundancy;
- connection through Angharad ferch Maredudd ab Owain;
- regional relevance in Powys and central Wales;
- possible service beneath Gruffudd ap Llywelyn;
- potential usefulness if one-person rule becomes strained;
- the possibility that later crisis creates recognition, office, title, or government;
- the danger that useful adult alternatives become political poles before continuity is formally settled.

The central dynasty question is:

```text
Can a family with credible adult continuity
strengthen the living Welsh polity
without being transformed prematurely
into a rival government or inevitable successor dynasty?
```

The strongest opening genealogy centers on Angharad ferch Maredudd ab Owain.

Angharad was:

- daughter of Maredudd ab Owain;
- mother of Gruffudd ap Llywelyn through Llywelyn ap Seisyll;
- mother of Bleddyn ap Cynfyn and Rhiwallon ap Cynfyn through Cynfyn ap Gwerstan.

## Future Flexibility Portfolio

### Constructive routes

#### Continuity Without Usurpation

Bleddyn and Rhiwallon support the living Welsh polity and help reduce dependency on Gruffudd ap Llywelyn without demanding immediate rule.

#### Negotiated Post-Gruffudd Settlement

If the ruler dies, abdicates, disappears, or loses authority, the family participates in an evidence-driven settlement involving title, succession, recognition, Governance, regional elites, and external pressure.

#### Powysian Regional Stewardship

The family gains bounded regional responsibility without claiming all Wales or automatic control of Powys.

#### Shared Adult Leadership

Bleddyn and Rhiwallon receive different responsibilities and preserve independent judgment while cooperating where interests align.

#### Durable Powys Dynasty

Later members survive, develop, and create a durable Powys political tradition through campaign outcomes.

#### Welsh Coalition Partner

House Mathrafal becomes one part of a broader Welsh settlement rather than the sole national dynasty.

### Expansion routes

#### Gwynedd Co-Rule

A post-crisis settlement grants Bleddyn and Rhiwallon roles in Gwynedd. This requires title, recognition, Governance, and regional support.

#### Welsh Leadership

The family acquires wider leadership through service, military success, succession, coalition, or negotiated recognition.

#### Resistance Dynasty

External pressure drives the family toward long-term military and political resistance.

#### National Unification

The family attempts wider Welsh integration. Success requires more than title or victory; durable administration and consent matter.

### Alternate routes

#### Regional Dynasty Without National Ambition

House Mathrafal remains focused on Powys and surrounding regions.

#### Divided Adult Partnership

Bleddyn and Rhiwallon pursue different strategies while remaining family.

#### Service Dynasty

The family remains important through offices, commands, and marriages without holding a crown.

#### External Accommodation

The family accepts bounded relationships with English, Mercian, Norman, or other powers to preserve local autonomy.

#### House That Never Forms

Bleddyn and Rhiwallon die, separate, fail politically, or leave no durable later line. The later historical House Mathrafal never emerges in recognizable form.

### Failure and overreach routes

#### Premature Succession Bloc

Supporters organize around the family before the current order has failed, provoking repression, division, or external manipulation.

#### Brotherhood Collapse

Bleddyn and Rhiwallon diverge over strategy, recognition, command, or future succession.

#### Regional Capture

The family becomes dependent on one group of elites and loses broader flexibility.

#### Victory Without Government

Military success creates title or recognition, but administration, law, revenue, communication, and succession remain weak.

#### Dynastic Violence

Later descendants compete violently, weakening Powys and enabling external pressure.

#### External Clientage

One branch gains protection or title through an external power at the cost of autonomy or family cohesion.

#### Fragmented Powys

Later branches divide regional government, claims, resources, and identity.

#### Extinction or Displacement

The family loses principal members, fails to reproduce, is defeated, or is replaced by another dynasty.
