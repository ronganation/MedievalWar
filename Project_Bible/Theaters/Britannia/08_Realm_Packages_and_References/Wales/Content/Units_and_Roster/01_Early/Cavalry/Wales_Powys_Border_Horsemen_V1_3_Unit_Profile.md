## 1. Direct Unit Verdict

**Powys Border Horsemen are Wales's proposed regional frontier-response cavalry: experienced riders assembled through valid Powys-facing service relationships to watch contested approaches, identify and intercept raiders or enemy screens, protect march routes, and delay stronger mounted threats long enough for Welsh infantry, missiles, terrain, or reserves to take over.**

Their signature is **Watch, Intercept, Delay, Return**.

Their defining purchase is **time and route control on the dangerous outer battle**. They do not defeat a heavy threat by meeting it head-on. They detect its movement, move to a useful crossing or approach, force it to alter pace or direction, preserve contact without becoming fixed, and hand the threat to a stronger receiving force.

Their finite battle resource is **frontier control**, meaning enough current route knowledge, connected riders, conditioned horses, leaders, reports, interception space, and return lanes remain to choose where contact occurs and when it must end.

## 2. One-Screen Total War Card

- **Unit:** Powys Border Horsemen
- **Support name:** Riders of the Eastern Border
- **Class:** Regional frontier light-to-medium cavalry
- **Signature:** Watch, Intercept, Delay, Return
- **Battle promise:** Discover the dangerous movement, reach the useful approach first, force raiders or enemy cavalry to spend time and order, and withdraw before a stronger formation traps the border riders.
- **Best use:** Outer approaches, road and ford security, flank-delay sectors, counter-raid missions, contested deployment edges, and routes where a receiving spear or missile formation can finish the handoff.
- **Strong against:** Enemy scouts, exposed skirmishers, raiding parties without close support, fragmented light horse, baggage attackers, and routing enemies within a controlled pursuit envelope.
- **Conditional against:** Peer regional cavalry and heavier mounted threats that can be delayed through route choice, javelin pressure, terrain, friendly support, and disciplined refusal.
- **Weak against:** Fresh knights, dense medium cavalry, formed spears, prepared missile troops, prolonged melee, unfamiliar deep operations, blocked return lanes, and several simultaneous incursions.
- **Campaign bargain:** Powys gains a capable mounted frontier layer by concentrating actual riders, suitable horses, local route experience, officers, reports, tack, weapons, regional obligations, patrol coverage, and recovery support into a finite pool.
- **Player instruction:** Do not ask them to win the cavalry battle. Ask them to find the dangerous movement, meet it at the useful approach, delay it, and return before observation becomes entrapment.
- **Flavor:** **The border is held first by the riders who know which road the enemy must use.**

## 5. Wales Cavalry Collision Firewall

### Marchogion Ysgafn

Marchogion Ysgafn own broad army screening, skirmisher disruption, flank observation, withdrawal cover, and pursuit. Powys Border Horsemen own regional frontier response, contact confirmation, route interception, counter-raid action, and delay handoff. The broad light horse are more attainable and better suited to routine army screening. The Powys riders are more experienced in their bounded frontier mission but less economical as a generic screen.

### Mounted Raiders

Mounted Raiders, if retained, own deliberate predation: raid, scatter, and escape. Powys Border Horsemen own protection against that behavior. They watch routes, intercept incursion, preserve reports, and recover patrol control. They should not receive superior raid income merely because frontier riders understand raiding.

### Mounted Guides

Mounted Guides own pathfinding, dispatch, route knowledge support, and information transfer with low combat power. Border Horsemen can use guides, informants, patrol reports, and local relationships, but they are a combat-capable cavalry formation. They do not replace the guide function or gain perfect local knowledge by card name.

### Marchogion Uchelwyr

Marchogion Uchelwyr own noble mounted reserve, controlled flank charge, command support, pursuit, and dismounted flexibility. Powys Border Horsemen own earlier threat contact and delay. They should usually yield a hard cavalry decision to the noble riders rather than matching their charge or melee ceiling.

### Teulu Marchogion

Teulu Marchogion own princely protection and decisive household intervention. Powys Border Horsemen may warn, channel, and delay the enemy so the household can preserve its intervention window. They do not inherit household morale or command priority.

### Powys Border Spearmen candidate

A Powys spear family should own received contact, route blocking, settlement approach defense, and anti-mounted frontage. Border Horsemen own the moving outer layer. Their ideal outcome is to deliver a slowed or redirected threat into prepared infantry rather than to duplicate the spear line.

## 12. Battle and Operational Loop

### Watch

The horsemen establish a bounded observation responsibility around an approach, road, crossing, flank, or counter-raid corridor. Watch requires riders, position, reports, and a receiving authority. It is not automatic map vision.

### Confirm

They distinguish a real threat from distant movement, rumor, tracks, or a feint. Confirmation may require closer observation and therefore greater risk.

### Intercept

The riders move to a useful line where the enemy must slow, turn, reveal support, or accept harassment. Interception is successful when it changes the enemy's freedom and preserves Welsh response time, not only when casualties occur.

### Delay

The formation uses presence, movement, limited missile pressure where supported, false approaches, route occupation, and controlled refusal to consume enemy time and order. Delay ends before the enemy fixes the riders in sustained melee.

### Hand Off

The threat passes to Powys or Welsh spearmen, archers, terrain, settlement defenses, regional infantry, noble cavalry, or household reserve. A handoff requires a physical lane and current report.

### Return

The horsemen break contact, report changes, recover horses, restore patrol connection, and become available for the next incursion. Return is part of victory.

## 22. Combined-Arms Handoff

### Marchogion Ysgafn

Broad light horse may extend the army screen while Powys Border Horsemen concentrate on the specific reported incursion or route-security problem. When both are present, they must not duplicate the same riders, horses, leaders, or observation sector.

### Welsh and Powys spear infantry

Border Horsemen identify, slow, and route the mounted threat. Spearmen receive it on prepared ground. Failed passage can disorder both formations, so the cavalry must retire through a known lane rather than across the spear front.

### Saethwyr and Gwyr y Bwa

Archers can punish an enemy that slows, turns, or compresses during interception. Fire lanes and rider exits must be explicit. Border riders do not grant automatic accuracy or suppression.

### Helwyr and ambush forces

The horsemen may maintain contact, provide current reports, or encourage a threat toward prepared ground. Ambush success still requires concealment, timing, actual friendly forces, and enemy behavior. The mounted unit does not create an ambush by ability button.

### Marchogion Uchelwyr and Teulu Marchogion

Regional riders identify and delay the high-value mounted threat. Noble or household cavalry choose whether to countercharge, exploit, or protect command. Powys riders should not remain in the decisive melee merely because elite support arrives.

## 23. Terrain and Weather

Useful environments include watched roads, fords, valley approaches, open upland edges, hedge and wall networks with usable gaps, rolling ground, firm lowland routes, settlement approaches, and known crossings connected to a receiving force.

Difficult environments include deep woods, marsh, steep trackless slopes, deep mud, ice, heavy snow, darkness, smoke, unfamiliar route networks, dense streets, blocked gates, and terrain that closes the refusal lane.

Regional familiarity changes available information and route choice only when actual riders, reports, guides, or relationships support it. No universal Powys hill, border, or defensive bonus is granted.
