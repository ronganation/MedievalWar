## 1. Direct Unit Verdict

**Gwyr y Bwa are Wales's local bow contributors: community-raised archers and bow users who establish affordable defensive shooting from familiar approaches, add arrows to a Welsh line, and withdraw behind local spears before stronger enemies turn their limited cohesion into a liability.**

Their signature is **Gather, Loose, Fall Back**.

They are not trained Welsh Bowmen in cheaper clothing. They possess enough bow familiarity and local leadership to create a useful missile body, but they lack the Welsh Bowmen family's consistent target changes, repeated lane shifts, expeditionary support, and dependable field control.

## 2. One-Screen Total War Card

- **Unit:** Gwyr y Bwa
- **Support name:** Men of the Bow
- **Functional name:** Welsh Local Bowmen
- **Class:** Early local bow muster
- **Signature:** Gather, Loose, Fall Back
- **Promise:** Add affordable arrows to a protected Welsh position and leave before the enemy forces an extended field fight.
- **Best use:** Settlement approaches, low slopes, hedged lanes, camp edges, protected secondary fronts, and ground immediately behind Local Spearmen.
- **Useful targets:** Exposed light infantry, enemy skirmishers, raiders crossing a readable approach, disordered troops, and cavalry before contact when an actual spear refuge exists.
- **Poor assignments:** Sustained missile duels, repeated target changes, long-distance expeditions, heavy armored targets, unsupported open flanks, cavalry stopping, and melee.
- **Campaign bargain:** Fast bounded local bow support from finite people and equipment, purchased with uneven readiness, limited endurance, and real community opportunity cost.
- **Player instruction:** Give them one useful lane, one understandable mission, and one safe way home.
- **Flavor:** **Raise the bows. Make the road costly. Leave before the road closes.**

## 3. Player-Facing Description

Gwyr y Bwa gather bow users who can contribute more than scattered individual shooting but do not yet meet the practiced field standard of Welsh Bowmen. Some bring experience from hunting, guarding routes, local conflict, household service, or prior musters. None of those backgrounds automatically creates an archer regiment.

Their value is immediate and practical. Behind a spear line or along a settlement approach, even a modest volume of arrows can slow light troops, drive skirmishers from a position, or make an enemy advance less orderly. Their weakness appears when the mission changes. Rapid retasking, repeated movement, long service, and exposed counterfire reveal the difference between a local contribution and a trained field-archer family.

## 20. Failure Stories

**The cheap army trap:** the player recruits too many local bow bodies because the coin price is low. Bows, arrows, protected lanes, and spear refuge become insufficient, and the army collapses when pressed.

**The Welsh Bowmen order:** a commander treats Gwyr y Bwa like trained field archers and demands repeated target changes and shifts. Files lose coordination, arrows scatter across low-value targets, and the fallback begins too late.

**The closed lane:** a good settlement position becomes a trap when friendly spears block the only refuge. The bowmen stop firing but cannot pass through the closed frontage.
