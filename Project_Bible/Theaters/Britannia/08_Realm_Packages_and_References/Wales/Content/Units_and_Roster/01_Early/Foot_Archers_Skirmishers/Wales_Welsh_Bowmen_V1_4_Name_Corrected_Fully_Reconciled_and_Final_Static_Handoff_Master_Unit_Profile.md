## 1. Direct Unit Verdict

**Welsh Bowmen are Wales's trained domestic field archers: practiced bowmen who establish a useful firing position, deliver controlled close-to-medium-range pressure against exposed or maneuvering enemies, and shift before cavalry or committed infantry can trap them.**

Their signature is **Loose, Press, Shift**.

They are more coherent and dependable than an emergency local bow muster, broader and less specialized than Helwyr, less regionally concentrated than a Saethwyr Gwent candidate, less protected and politically narrow than princely archers, and not automatically a domestic copy of Welsh Longbow Mercenaries.

## 2. One-Screen Total War Card

- **Unit:** Welsh Bowmen
- **Support name:** Trained Welsh Field Archers
- **Class:** Trained domestic foot archers
- **Signature:** Loose, Press, Shift
- **Promise:** Put practiced Welsh arrows into exposed enemies from a useful close-to-medium lane, keep pressure through a short decisive window, and relocate before the enemy closes.
- **Best use:** Protected slopes, lanes, wood edges with real arcs, gaps beside Local Spearmen, settlement approaches, and flanks facing light or turning troops.
- **Good targets:** Exposed infantry, enemy missiles, disrupted formations, lightly protected cavalry before contact, horses presenting a usable angle, and troops forced to turn against Welsh pressure.
- **Conditional targets:** Shielded infantry from a side, moving medium cavalry, and better-protected troops at a range and angle where arrows still change behavior.
- **Poor assignments:** Prolonged frontal shooting against heavy shields and armor, stopping cavalry without a spear screen, firing blindly through woods, staying for melee, and trading stationary volleys with superior dedicated missile troops.
- **Campaign bargain:** Wales gains reliable common archery from finite practiced shooters, bows, strings, arrows, leaders, routes, and service support.
- **Instruction:** Find the useful angle, spend arrows while the target remains vulnerable, and shift before the enemy turns the firing position into a trap.
- **Flavor:** **Let the arrows bite, then leave them striking empty ground.**

## 3. Player-Facing Description

Welsh Bowmen bring trained domestic archery to the Welsh host. They are not hidden hunters and not a court guard. Their value comes from enough practiced shooters, compatible bows, usable arrows, leaders, and mutual signals to deliver controlled fire as part of a field army.

The player should enjoy placing them near a spear shoulder, punishing an enemy that turns uphill or enters a narrow approach, then shifting to another lane while the Welsh infantry protects the move. Their mobility is purposeful rather than effortless. Every relocation interrupts shooting, spends time, and risks losing the angle that made the arrows useful.

A successful Welsh Bowmen unit does not need to destroy its target. It may force shields toward the wrong threat, slow a cavalry approach, drive enemy missiles from a useful position, or create the disorder needed for Local Spearmen, javelinmen, raiders, or household troops to answer.

## 3B. The Player Choice

The recurring choice is not merely whether to fire. It is whether the current target justifies remaining visible.

- **Angle or shelter:** better arcs expose the formation; safer ground may produce poor shots.
- **Pressure or conservation:** continued fire can force a turn, withdrawal, or advance, but consumes arrows needed for the decisive window.
- **One more loose or shift:** another controlled release may complete the effect or close the escape route.
- **Concentrate or divide:** a short front puts more arrows into one target; a broader line watches more space but weakens command and target concentration.

Welsh Bowmen should reward active judgment without requiring a unique ability bar. Target behavior, ammunition, approach threats, firing arcs, spacing, and visible withdrawal routes carry the decision.

## 13. Signature: Loose, Press, Shift

**Loose** means the archers have a visible target, compatible arrows, enough settled shooters, and a real projectile path.

**Press** means they continue controlled fire while the target remains vulnerable enough to justify the ammunition and exposure. It does not mean standing immobile until ammunition is empty.

**Shift** means the formation ceases fire, moves through a valid route, and establishes another useful relation to the battle before countercontact arrives.

## 19. Three Failure Stories

**The beautiful useless ridge:** the position looks dramatic, but the spear line, trees, and ground block the target. The player wastes time and arrows on an angle that never truly existed.

**The final greedy volley:** Welsh Bowmen remain to fire once more after cavalry has begun the approach. The arrows cause some losses, but the shift route closes and practiced shooters are destroyed in melee.

**The empty quiver campaign:** repeated battles preserve headcount but consume arrows, strings, bows, and carriage. The card remains on the army list while its normal promise is unavailable.

## 21. Independent Use and Terrain

Useful independent assignments include camp-edge shooting, settlement-approach defense, protected flank fire, harassment of exposed light troops, and covering a planned withdrawal. They do not require a forest map.

## 23. Fatigue, Weather, and Equipment Wear

Shooting, running, climbing, repeated redeployment, and carrying bows and arrows should accumulate fatigue where shared systems support it. The card receives no stamina exemption.

Rain, mud, wind, cold, heat, and visibility matter only through shared validated systems. This profile promotes no exact modifier. Relevant effects may concern footing, visibility, route speed, string care, arrow condition, clothing, and fatigue without asserting that all shooting stops in poor weather.

## 25. Veterans and Rebuilt Units
