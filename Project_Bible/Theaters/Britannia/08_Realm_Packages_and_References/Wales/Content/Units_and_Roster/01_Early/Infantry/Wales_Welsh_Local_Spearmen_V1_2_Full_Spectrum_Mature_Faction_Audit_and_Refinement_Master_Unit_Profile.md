## 1. Direct Unit Verdict

Welsh Local Spearmen are the honest foundation of the Welsh domestic roster: locally raised spear-and-shield infantry who can establish useful frontage, protect bowmen and javelinmen, deny careless mounted approaches, and advance or recover across hills, woods, lanes, and uneven ground more effectively than their light protection suggests.

Their mature battlefield signature is **Muster, Guard, Answer**. The original **Gather, Guard, Counterthrust** language remains the internal mechanical loop: the unit gathers under a valid call, guards a practical piece of frontage, and answers a disrupted or overextended attacker with a short spear advance.

They are not designed to defeat every cavalry unit, hold a continental battle line without support, or become invisible hill supermen. Their value comes from affordable local access, terrain-compatible movement, enough spear order to make an approach dangerous, and a timely counterthrust after the enemy has been disrupted by ground, missiles, or another Welsh formation.

## 2. One-Screen Total War Card

**Unit:** Welsh Local Spearmen  
**Class:** Early-era domestic spear-and-shield infantry  
**Signature:** Muster, Guard, Answer  
**Battle promise:** Put an affordable Welsh spear body where the army needs honest frontage, protect lighter troops, and punish a careless approach after terrain or missiles have damaged enemy order.  
**Best use:** Lanes, low ridges, wooded margins, settlement approaches, protected flanks, in front of bowmen, or beside a stronger regional formation.  
**Strong against:** Light cavalry approaching prepared spears, scattered raiders, lightly protected infantry already disrupted, and attacks compressed by poor ground.  
**Competitive against:** Other ordinary local infantry when leadership, support, fatigue, frontage, and terrain are favorable.  
**Weak against:** Concentrated missiles, armored melee infantry, heavy cavalry striking an open flank, prolonged open-field grinding, isolation, and repeated unsupported charges.  
**Campaign bargain:** Wales gains broad local defensive frontage, but prolonged service removes labor from communities, strains food and household obligations, and produces weak replacement behavior far from home.  
**Player instruction:** Use them to make the enemy's approach costly, not to win the whole battle alone. Let archers, javelinmen, hill troops, and household forces create the decision.  
**Flavor line:** The spear line is raised from the land it guards.



## 2A. Player-Facing Description

Welsh Local Spearmen are raised from communities able to provide a usable body of spear-bearing infantry under recognized local leadership. Their equipment varies, but the card is not an unformed crowd. Once assembled and settled into frontage, they protect the working space needed by Welsh bowmen, javelinmen, scouts, and lighter fighting bands.

Their satisfaction comes from making ordinary troops matter. A mounted probe turns away, a pressured archer line escapes through a protected shoulder, or an enemy advance loses order against a lane and receives a short spear answer. The unit succeeds by preserving an army relationship, not merely by accumulating kills.

If left in a long armored melee, stretched across too much ground, or marched repeatedly away from its source, its bargain fails. Better Welsh formations exist precisely because local serviceable frontage is not the same thing as veteran regional order, household loyalty, or High-era institutional coordination.

## 5. Battlefield Constitution

### 5.1 Core verbs

**Gather** means the unit becomes useful only after local men, leaders, weapons, shields, provisions, and a legitimate call to service converge.  
**Guard** means it protects a space, approach, flank, missile body, or withdrawal route rather than seeking unsupported decisive combat.  
**Counterthrust** means it exploits short enemy disorder without pretending to be shock infantry.

### 5.2 Formation states

```yaml

    risk: VulnerableIfSurprisedBeforeFrontageForms
  guarded_front:

    risk: LimitedDepthAndOpenFlankExposure
  compressed_lane_defense:
    value: StrongerFrontageEconomyInNarrowGround
    risk: MissileConcentrationAndWithdrawalCongestion
  conditional_counterthrust:
    value: PunishesDisruptedOrOverextendedEnemy
    risk: RapidCohesionLossIfPursuitContinues
  emergency_all_round_guard:
    value: TemporarySurvivalAgainstMultipleApproaches
    risk: ReducedMovementWeakOffenseAndFastFatigue
  broken_local_cluster:
    value: IndividualResistanceAndTerrainEscapeRemainPossible
    risk: SpearDeterrenceAndMutualProtectionCollapse
```

The unit should turn, expand, compress, and recover more comfortably than a heavy ordered spear line, but it must not change formation instantly. Leaders, visible neighbors, fatigue, slope, obstacles, and incoming pressure determine whether local flexibility remains useful or becomes disorder.

### 5.3 Contact behavior

A prepared frontage can check light cavalry and make medium cavalry reconsider. Heavy cavalry remains dangerous if it attacks with support, strikes a flank, catches the unit moving, or follows missile preparation. Against ordinary infantry, the unit performs best when the enemy has already lost order. Against armored melee troops in sustained contact, Welsh Local Spearmen should deteriorate unless relieved by missiles, javelins, regional veterans, or household troops.



## 5A. The Answer Window

The mature card survives because it owns a small but consequential decision window. After receiving a manageable attack, checking a mounted probe, or benefiting from Welsh missile disruption, the formation can make a brief ordered spear answer. This is not a charge ability and not a permanent attack bonus.

```yaml
answer_window:
  opens_when:
    - EnemyFrontRankIsDisruptedOrHesitating
    - EnemyLightCavalryBreaksContactInPoorOrder
    - WelshMissilesOrJavelinsCreateVisibleLocalDisorder
    - AdjacentFriendlyFormationPinsTheTarget
    - LocalLeaderMaintainsEnoughFrontageControl
  player_purchase:
    - ShortForwardSpearPressure
    - RecoveryOfLostGround
    - ProtectionOfMissileWithdrawal
    - LocalReliefOfAnAdjacentWelshFormation
  closes_when:
    - UnitLeavesSupportingFrontage
    - EnemyReserveArrivesInGoodOrder
    - PursuitExtendsBeyondLocalCommand
    - FormationIsShotTurnedOrFatigued
    - HeavyInfantryReestablishesCloseOrder
  forbidden_expression:
    - MagicalTimedDamageBuff
    - UnstoppableSpearCharge
    - AutomaticEnemyMoraleCollapse
```

A successful Answer should often gain tenable ground, restore a screen, or force an enemy to reform. It does not need to produce high casualties to matter. This gives the basic Welsh spear card active play without stealing shock work from household or regional assault infantry.

### 8.1 Home and away behavior

Near a valid source, losses can be replaced gradually if the community, leadership, equipment, and political relationship remain intact. Far from home, reinforcement becomes slower and more fragile. Desertion, expired obligation, illness, route interruption, and pressure to return for local defense can reduce effective strength without treating every local soldier as disloyal.

## 9F. Failure Stories

**The open shoulder:** bowmen withdraw through an unprepared gap while cavalry threatens the same side. Friendly traffic ruins the spear front and the mounted threat reaches both units.

**The proud answer:** the spearmen repel a weak probe and pursue beyond their leaders and missile support. Fresh armored infantry receives the scattered advance and turns a local success into permanent source loss.

**The exhausted expedition:** repeated distant service restores nominal headcount with hasty replacements but not leaders, shields, or collective facing. The card looks full and performs below its promise because readiness was never rebuilt.

## 11D. Independent Use and Whole-Battle Employment

Independent assignments include settlement-lane defense, camp approach security, rear-guard frontage, escort of a local missile detachment, and holding a minor crossing against light opposition. Poor independent assignments include attacking fortified heavy infantry, chasing raiders across open ground, covering an entire wide flank alone, and acting as the army's only reserve.

In a full battle, several Local Spearmen may provide economical width, but an all-Local-Spearmen army should lack strong missile exploitation, mobile threat, selected crisis troops, and prolonged armored contact capability.

## 12. Player-Facing Strengths, Weaknesses, and Counters

### Strengths
- Affordable domestic spear frontage when a valid local source exists.
- Useful protection for Welsh bowmen, javelinmen, and withdrawing light infantry.
- Better formation recovery than heavier conventional infantry on manageable broken ground.
- Credible deterrence against careless light mounted attacks.
- Strong value in lanes, settlement approaches, wooded margins, and compressed fronts.
- Can punish disrupted ordinary infantry with a short controlled counterthrust.

### Weaknesses
- Light and uneven protection.
- Vulnerable to concentrated archery and missile preparation.
- Weak in prolonged melee against armored or professionally maintained infantry.
- Spear value collapses when flanked, moving, exhausted, or fragmented.
- Long and distant service harms cohesion, replenishment, and home-region economy.
- Broad availability must never become infinite availability.

### Enemy counterplay
Enemy players can pin the guarded front, shoot it, threaten the flank, force repeated turning, draw it into open pursuit, attack the supporting archers, or make the Welsh army fight far from familiar routes and replacement sources.

## 12B. Veterans, Rebuilt Units, and History Preservation

Veterans may improve facing judgment, refusal of pursuit, local relief timing, and recognition of the Answer window. Experience does not create missing spears, shields, people, or leaders.

A rebuilt body may regain headcount before regaining collective control. Preserve formation history where the systems support it. Do not delete experienced identity merely because recruitment closes temporarily, and do not grant full veteran performance to newly added contributors without integration.

## 12C. Campaign Reach, Supply, Weather, and Season

Longer service is possible when authority, food, route, equipment maintenance, compensation or obligation, leadership, and return support exist. “Local” defines the source, not an arbitrary tiny movement radius.

Rain, mud, cold, heat, poor visibility, and difficult footing matter only through shared validated systems. This profile invents no exact weather modifier. Relevant consequences may include slower assembly, worse footing, shield and clothing wear, route disruption, fatigue, and delayed return. The unit receives neither immunity nor a uniquely punitive Welsh weather rule.

## 16. Final Strong Direction

Welsh Local Spearmen should remain plain in name but not generic in play. They are the domestic spear frontage that lets the rest of the Welsh roster function. Their identity comes from local source, finite service, practical terrain handling, protection of missile troops, conditional cavalry deterrence, and the judgment to counterthrust briefly rather than grind forever.

They establish the Wales roster's first essential truth:

> Wales does not begin with a perfect national army. It begins with communities that can raise useful fighting bodies, princes who must coordinate them, and players who must turn ground, timing, missiles, and local obligation into battlefield power.
