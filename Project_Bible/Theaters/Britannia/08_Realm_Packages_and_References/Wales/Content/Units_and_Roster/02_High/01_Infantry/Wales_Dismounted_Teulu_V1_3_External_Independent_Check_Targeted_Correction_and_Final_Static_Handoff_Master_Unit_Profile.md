## 1. Direct Unit Verdict

**Dismounted Teulu are Wales's premier princely household foot anchor: a very small body of trusted, well-equipped retainers deliberately taken out of mounted, escort, security, and household service to preserve one decisive short front, contain an elite enemy commitment, and answer that commitment with one measured counterstroke before household strength is consumed.**

Their signature is **Anchor, Break, Preserve**.

Their defining purchase is not simply high armor or morale. It is **trusted control of one decisive household front**. They can establish a protected point, keep common purpose under a severe but bounded assault, and convert surviving order into a disciplined local counterstroke. Their finite battle resource is **household integrity**, meaning the surviving alignment of trusted people, recognized leaders, standard, shields, weapons, spacing, mission, and an available preservation route.

## 4. Why This Card Exists

Wales's roster foundation is intentionally uneven. Local spearmen provide affordable frontage and conditional cavalry deterrence. Meirionnydd Spearmen provide veteran regional long-spear reception, countermovement, and controlled advance. Saethwyr and Gwyr y Bwa provide domestic bow pressure at different quality and service bands. Uchelwyr Retainers provide a broader upper-status reserve able to support, commit, and adapt. None of those purchases should also carry the prince's premier household identity.

Dismounted Teulu therefore exist to answer one specific question:

**Which short front is important enough to remove the prince's trusted household from its other duties, expose it on foot, and risk dynastic military continuity in exchange for the highest Welsh control under elite pressure?**

## 5. Wales Roster Collision Firewall

### Welsh Local Spearmen

Welsh Local Spearmen own affordable local frontage, settlement defense, and conditional cavalry deterrence. Dismounted Teulu own one short high-value front at extraordinary opportunity cost. If the household becomes the economical main line, the design has failed.

### Meirionnydd Spearmen

Meirionnydd Spearmen own veteran regional long-spear reception, controlled advance, and counterthrust. Dismounted Teulu do not exceed them by carrying a magically better spear. They own stronger household cohesion, command protection, close-order endurance, and elite containment at far lower availability.

### Uchelwyr Retainers

Uchelwyr Retainers own **Support, Commit, Adapt** across threatened flanks, local-lord protection, archer shoulders, and changing reserve missions. Dismounted Teulu own **Anchor, Break, Preserve** around a decisive princely or household mission. Uchelwyr are broader, more available, more variable, and easier to redirect. Teulu are narrower, more cohesive, politically central, and substantially harder to replace.

### Teulu Spearmen

Teulu Spearmen should own personally attached household spear reception, princely protection, and anti-mounted short-front work. Dismounted Teulu own shielded heavy-infantry containment and measured close counterstroke. If both cards use the same weapon package, formation, target set, and battle loop, they should merge rather than survive as stat variants.

### Young Teulu Riders and Teulu Marchogion

Mounted Teulu expressions own escort, rapid response, mounted intervention, extraction, and pursuit according to their profiles. Dismounting them is not free. A foot commitment removes riders, leaders, equipment, and duty capacity from those mounted purposes until a real reassignment and recovery occurs.

### Gwyr y Faner and later organized Welsh foot

Later organized field infantry should own broader coordinated line service. Dismounted Teulu remain a household apex anchor, not the institutional endpoint of all Welsh heavy infantry.

## 6. Mature Comparator Reconciliation

### King's Spear Retainers

King's Spear Retainers demonstrate a mature critical-point purchase: reliable control of one short spear front, followed by controlled close work and restoration. Dismounted Teulu must sit above that royal-service band in scarcity, household trust, elite-contact tolerance, political cost, and replacement difficulty, while remaining less economical and less suited to repeated ordinary spear duties.

### Highland Noble Swordsmen

Highland Noble Swordsmen own a protected approach and a short great-weapon guard-breaking decision. Dismounted Teulu must never inherit that offensive rupture ceiling. Their offensive action is generated by surviving the anchor phase with household integrity, not by two-handed weapon reach or specialized armor-breaking.

### Swedish Huskarla

Swedish Huskarla own supported bounded rupture and breakaway with two-handed axes. Dismounted Teulu own shielded containment, command-facing security, and measured counterstroke. They should outlast Huskarla in a protected defensive mission but underperform them in immediate supported rupture against an already fixed target.

### Count's Household Knights

Count's Household Knights establish the correct household opportunity-cost principle: protection and decisive intervention cannot both be provided at full strength. Dismounted Teulu adopt the principle without importing Flemish people, institutions, horses, values, or availability. Every distant foot commitment spends princely protection, mounted readiness, or another household duty somewhere else.

## 13. Seven-Part Battle Loop

### Attend

The Teulu begin near the prince, household standard, reserve, or decisive front they are assigned to protect. Remaining uncommitted is valuable because it preserves choice and command security.

### Establish

They occupy a short front with known flanks, neighboring units, fallback, and relief. Establishing on a broad open frontage wastes the card.

### Receive

Shield relation, leadership, and household cohesion absorb a severe but bounded assault. Receive is strongest against a frontal commitment already shaped by Welsh spears, missiles, terrain, or a narrow approach.

### Contain

The Teulu prevent the enemy's local success from widening. They close manageable gaps, shorten around casualties, and deny immediate exploitation. Contain is not indefinite grinding.

### Read

Leaders judge whether the enemy is spent, disordered, compressed, unsupported, or renewed by fresh reserves. If the enemy remains fresh and deep, the counterstroke should be withheld.

### Counterstroke

Selected files advance only far enough to break, displace, or recover the assigned local objective. The action ends when the protected point is restored, not when an attractive pursuit appears.

### Preserve

The formation shortens, accepts relief, returns to guard, covers withdrawal, or leaves contact. Preservation protects future command, political, and mounted capacity.

### Casualty consequences

- **Light loss:** repair, treatment, short-term readiness decline, and reduced mounted or guard flexibility.
- **Serious loss:** shared household functions weaken; experienced leaders, equipment, and trust become limiting inputs.
- **Catastrophic loss:** the company closes for extended recovery; prince security, household prestige, political confidence, instruction, mounted capacity, and succession stability may all require owner-system resolution.
- **Capture:** may produce ransom, hostage, property, loyalty, and claimant consequences through their proper authorities.

## 22. Combined-Arms Integration

### Local Spearmen

Local Spearmen provide width and absorb ordinary pressure. They should not be ordered to overlap chaotically with the compact household front. A physical relief passage must exist.

### Meirionnydd Spearmen

Meirionnydd Spearmen can receive cavalry, control longer spear contact, and shape an enemy shoulder. Dismounted Teulu then contain the elite breakthrough or restore the critical seam. The Teulu must not steal the regional unit's spear identity.

### Saethwyr and Gwyr y Bwa

Archers pressure approach, flanks, horses, and unshielded targets. Fire must shift or cease when the Teulu counterstroke crosses the lane. The household can protect an archer shoulder, but cannot simultaneously pursue deeply and remain an archer guard.

### Uchelwyr Retainers

Uchelwyr screen secondary command needs, reinforce neighboring sectors, and accept handoff after the Teulu's decisive work. A mature army should rationally value both. Uchelwyr preserve flexibility; Dismounted Teulu preserve the decisive point.

### Mounted Teulu and limited cavalry

Mounted troops protect flanks, deny cavalry access, threaten enemy missiles, extract the prince, and exploit a successful local break. The household cannot provide full mounted and dismounted strength from the same people at once.

## 23. Terrain, Weather, and Siege Envelope

Good conditions include firm bounded ground, narrow approaches, defended slopes with usable footing, short reserve lanes, settlement gates after missile exposure is controlled, bridgeheads with protected flanks, and compact command positions.

Difficult conditions include deep mud, marsh, steep unstable slopes, dense woods that break shield relation, smoke, darkness, severe congestion, open missile ground, snow or ice that ruins footing, and streets too narrow for relief or counterstroke.

Welsh identity does not grant a flat hill or forest bonus. Terrain advantage must come from actual approach, concealment, footing, frontage, guides, neighboring troops, and withdrawal routes.

Siege tasks that fit include gate recapture, courtyard containment, breach defense after the exposed lip is secured, command-post defense, and withdrawal through a controlled corridor. Poor tasks include ladders under fire, first-wave breach assault, artillery operation, wall engineering, and unsupported deep street pursuit.

## 24. Counterplay and Matchups

Dismounted Teulu should defeat ordinary infantry in a short supported front and contest peer elite foot when mission, formation, and household integrity favor them. They should not automatically defeat every heavy unit.

Hard and soft counters include:

- sustained bow or crossbow concentration before contact;
- artillery or thrown weapons disrupting leaders and shield relation;
- fresh long-spear or pike frontage that denies the counterstroke;
- cavalry reaching an open flank or preservation route;
- several-direction pressure;
- alternating fresh enemy reserves;
- forcing the prince and objective apart;
- broad frontage that dilutes household control;
- fatigue and repeated recommitment;
- blocking relief with friendly or enemy congestion;
- winning elsewhere so the protected point loses operational value.

The enemy should beat the Teulu by solving the household mission, not merely by fielding a unit with one higher attack statistic.
