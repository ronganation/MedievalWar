## 1. Direct Unit Verdict

**Teulu Marchogion are Wales's signature princely household cavalry: a small, finite, comparatively well-equipped mounted household held near a valid prince or chieftain to protect command, counter sudden threats, and deliver one controlled intervention at the battle's decisive moment.**

Their signature is **Guard, Strike, Dismount**.

Their defining purchase is **household certainty under crisis**. They are not simply stronger Marchogion Uchelwyr. Noble cavalry seeks the favorable commitment; Teulu Marchogion remain available when the prince, command position, army cohesion, or decisive reserve window is directly threatened.

Their finite battle resource is **household integrity**, meaning enough trusted retainers, riders, horses, officers, equipment, command awareness, mutual confidence, and proximity remain to protect the prince and still execute a decisive response.

## 2. One-Screen Total War Card

- **Unit:** Teulu Marchogion
- **Support name:** Mounted Teulu
- **Class:** Elite princely household cavalry
- **Signature:** Guard, Strike, Dismount
- **Battle promise:** Stay close enough to protect command, preserve a clear intervention lane, then deliver one controlled countercharge, flank strike, rescue, or crisis response without sacrificing the household to routine cavalry work.
- **Best mounted use:** Command protection, elite mobile reserve, decisive flank intervention, countercharge, withdrawal protection, and controlled pursuit after the battle has truly broken.
- **Best dismounted use:** Final command-position defense, settlement or objective defense, constrained ground, and elite household infantry service when mounted mobility is no longer the greater need.
- **Strong against:** Exposed enemy elites, already engaged cavalry, disordered infantry, attackers threatening command, fragmented reserves, and routing enemies within recall range.
- **Conditional against:** Heavy cavalry when the Teulu countercharge from a protected position with support and a recoverable exit. They are not granted generic knight parity.
- **Weak against:** Prepared spears, concentrated missiles, isolated frontal commitment, prolonged heavy-cavalry melee, several separated crises, loss of the prince or leader, and pursuit that abandons command.
- **Campaign bargain:** Wales gains its most reliable household cavalry response by reserving real trusted retainers, horses, armor, weapons, officers, political relationships, household defense, and command service from other mounted, dismounted, escort, and character-linked assignments.
- **Player instruction:** Keep them close enough to matter. Do not spend the household winning a minor flank while the prince, command position, or army reserve remains vulnerable.
- **Flavor:** **The household does not chase the battle. It waits where the battle can still be saved.**

## 5. Wales Cavalry Collision Firewall

### Marchogion Ysgafn

Marchogion Ysgafn own affordable screening, skirmisher disruption, immediate observation, withdrawal cover, and routine pursuit. Teulu Marchogion should almost never perform those jobs while a lighter formation can do so.

### Powys Border Horsemen

Powys Border Horsemen own regional warning, route interception, counter-raiding, mounted delay, and return to patrol. Teulu Marchogion respond only when the reported threat reaches command or becomes strategically decisive.

### Marchogion Uchelwyr

Marchogion Uchelwyr own the main quality noble cavalry role: maneuver, choose the valuable target, commit, recover, or deploy dismounted. Teulu Marchogion own command protection and the battle-saving response. They are smaller, more cohesive, more politically central, and less available for independent flank work.

### Dismounted Teulu

Dismounted Teulu represent the household's elite foot expression. Mounted and dismounted household profiles must not duplicate one body of retainers. Exact ownership may later use mutually exclusive deployment, distinct validated sub-pools, or character-specific household packets, but no assumption is authorized here.

### Teulu y Tywysog candidate

A character bodyguard candidate may represent the prince's direct escort. It survives separately only if it is character-linked and smaller than the field Teulu Marchogion card. The same retainers and horses cannot populate both without an independent source packet.

## 11. Battle and Crisis Loop

### Guard

Remain close enough to protect command and preserve a usable response lane. Guarding is active positioning, not passive aura generation.

### Read the crisis

Distinguish a real command threat, flank collapse, enemy elite breakthrough, rescue requirement, or decisive opening from ordinary pressure better handled by the wider army.

### Strike

Execute a controlled charge, countercharge, rescue, or flank intervention. Household cohesion improves reliability, not invulnerability.

### Recover

Break contact, restore order, protect the prince or withdrawal, and return surviving capability to the command position.

### Dismount

When command defense, terrain, settlement fighting, or exhausted horses make foot service more valuable, deploy as elite household infantry without creating a second population.

## 19. Combined-Arms Integration

Marchogion Ysgafn maintain the broad screen. Powys Border Horsemen secure the outer battle. Marchogion Uchelwyr strike exposed flanks. Teulu Marchogion remain near command and answer the crisis that the other layers cannot safely resolve.

Spears fix threats and protect the household's return. Archers and skirmishers create disorder and reveal support. The Teulu should exploit a decisive opening or prevent collapse, not replace those formations.

## 20. Terrain, Siege, and Settlement Use

Mounted use favors firm open or rolling ground, protected approaches, clear countercharge lanes, and enough space to return to command. Mud, marsh, steep slopes, woods, streets, gates, smoke, darkness, and congestion reduce mounted reliability.

Dismounted use is more suitable for settlement interiors, command posts, objectives, breaches after organized entry, and constrained final defense. It does not create siege specialists or eliminate horse-accountability costs.
