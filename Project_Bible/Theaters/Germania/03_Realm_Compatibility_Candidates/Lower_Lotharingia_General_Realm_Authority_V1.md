## 4. Core campaign fantasy

**Hold the ducal dignity, build the ducal power.**

The player leads a prestigious but incomplete western Germania authority. The title creates expectations, visibility, rivals, and opportunities. It does not create obedience, money, records, roads, castles, troops, or institutional cooperation. Every reliable capability must come from a real base: direct property, staffed administration, recognized office, negotiated obligation, protected route, functioning institution, household capacity, military source, or enforceable agreement.

The Realm fantasy has four linked dimensions:

1. **Recognized dignity:** remain credible within the Holy Roman political order.
2. **Fragmented capacity:** turn scattered direct resources and relationships into dependable government.
3. **Contested continuity:** survive succession, restoration pressure, and cross-Theater dynastic claims.
4. **Brokered geography:** use rivers, crossings, fortresses, cities, and institutions without pretending they are automatically owned.

## 7. Realm structure: direct core, relationship field, and external interface

### 7.1 Direct core

The direct core consists only of assets and responsibilities that later evidence assigns directly to the Realm government or its valid holders. Candidate areas include Frederick's verified direct property, household-facing administration, ducal records and communication, bounded advocacy obligations, and the offices and resources required to exercise the ducal dignity.

```yaml

  exact_opening_settlement_or_capital: unresolved
  exact_direct_property_inventory: unresolved
  exact_treasury_and_force: unresolved
```

The general authority does not select a capital. A fixed capital must not be created merely because the campaign interface expects one. The later geography, government, and Opening State authorities must determine whether the opening position is centered on one principal site, an itinerary, or multiple administrative nodes.

### 7.2 Relationship field

The relationship field contains actors, places, and institutions with their own owners whose decisions materially shape Lower Lotharingian power. It includes counts, marches, abbeys, episcopal powers, urban communities, royal sites, routes, and fortresses.

Lower Lotharingia may negotiate with, protect, pressure, contest, recognize, serve alongside, or fight these actors. The Realm does not own them merely because they lie within a historical or geographic field associated with the duchy.

### 7.3 External interfaces

External interfaces connect the Realm to Upper Lotharingia, Flanders, Boulogne, Hainaut-Mons, Capetian France, Burgundy, Italia and Tuscany, and the royal Salian government. Interface state must be read from each owner rather than copied into Lower Lotharingia.

### 11.1 Direct administration

Direct administration should apply only where the Realm has evidence-supported machinery. It does not mean the duke personally owns or performs all work. Direct administration can be strong at one site and weak elsewhere.

### 11.2 Distributed arrangements

Regional, strategic-site, ecclesiastical-facing, household-facing, and cross-Realm arrangements may all be used where justified. None is automatic. The later governance authority must select only the arrangements needed for Lower Lotharingia rather than copying the full shared framework.

### 11.3 Performance, legitimacy, and cooperation

Administrative performance does not prove legitimacy. Legitimacy does not ensure performance. Cooperation does not equal loyalty. Orders fail when communication, competence, resources, jurisdiction, recognition, or enforcement are missing. The player should see the material cause of important failures.

## 20. Campaign phases and development channels

The following are flexible campaign channels, not locked cultural forks or guaranteed historical stages.

### 20.1 Stabilize the dignity

Preserve recognition, communications, direct assets, and the capacity to answer immediate threats. Failure may reduce the duchy to prestige without effective reach.

### 20.2 Build a dependable core

Strengthen verified direct holdings, records, fiscal collection, route protection, household-government interfaces, and strategic-site administration. This channel favors reliability over symbolic breadth.

### 20.3 Broker the network

Create durable terms with counts, institutions, cities, and external partners. This channel accepts distributed power in exchange for cooperation, access, service, and reduced administrative strain.

### 20.4 Resolve or survive restoration

Negotiate, defeat, absorb, coexist with, or be transformed by the Ardenne-Verdun restoration pathway. Restoration is not a mandatory war and does not guarantee one historical outcome.

### 20.5 Shape the western imperial frontier

Become a trusted royal intermediary, an autonomous broker, a western-aligned counterweight, or a contested frontier power. Each direction requires resources and relationships and may coexist with internal weakness.

## 21. Realm strengths and vulnerabilities

### 21.1 Strengths

- a recognized ducal dignity with high political relevance;
- central access to Germania, western Francia, and Italia-facing networks;
- rich diplomatic, ecclesiastical, urban, riverine, and dynastic interactions;
- several viable consolidation and alignment strategies;
- a strong named-character and succession portfolio;
- meaningful route and crossing leverage;
- potential to convert mediation and protection into durable influence.

### 21.2 Vulnerabilities

- unresolved and potentially narrow direct property base;
- a persistent gap between title and working capacity;
- powerful local actors that cannot be treated as subordinates;
- restoration and succession pressure;
- exposed routes and noncontiguous interests;
- dependence on negotiated service and external corridors;
- cross-Theater obligations that can divide attention and resources;
- risk that attempted centralization produces resistance faster than capacity.

## 23. Player-facing presentation

The Realm should communicate its situation through a small number of legible strategic surfaces:

- **Ducal standing:** recognition, unresolved challenges, and political-order relevance.
- **Working capacity:** direct holdings, staffing, records, routes, money, and available force.
- **Network map:** major actors, agreements, disputes, access, protection, and known obligations.
- **Restoration pressure:** claimant capability and opportunity without a hidden destiny meter.
- **External exposure:** commitments and vulnerabilities across western and Italia interfaces.

The player should not need to inspect every charter or household member each turn. Detailed provenance should be available when a major decision depends on it.
