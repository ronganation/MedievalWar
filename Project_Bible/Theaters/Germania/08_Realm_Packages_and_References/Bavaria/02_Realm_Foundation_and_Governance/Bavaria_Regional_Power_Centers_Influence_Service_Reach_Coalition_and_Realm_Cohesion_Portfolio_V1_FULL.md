## 9. Bavaria Regional-Political Thesis

Bavaria's political coherence should arise through a network of overlapping centers and relationships rather than a single capital, universal hierarchy, or binary loyal-rebellious map.

Regional strength may arise from:

- recognized office and lawful competence;
- household and patrimonial resources;
- institutional identity and jurisdiction;
- assemblies, petitions, records, messengers, and hosted administration;
- routes, crossings, markets, storage, hospitality, and information transfer;
- household density, labor, craft, care, and local service capacity;
- defensive position, warning, muster support, protected stores, and military access;
- mediation, brokerage, witness, protection, patronage, and dispute settlement;
- external recognition, agreements, and communication;
- historical memory, custom, privilege, accepted burden, and reciprocal service.

No single factor creates regional predominance.

## 26. Coalition Formation

Formation requires:

- a shared or compatible purpose;
- recognized participants and representatives;
- lawful or customary room to act;
- communication and assembly access;
- known contributions, benefits, risks, and opportunity costs;
- explicit property, institutional, and jurisdictional separation;
- decision, dissent, withdrawal, remedy, and dissolution procedures;
- sufficient confidence that the arrangement exists.

```yaml
FormationHardStops:
  - ActorOrRepresentativeUnknown
  - PurposeOrScopeUndefined

  - PropertyJurisdictionOrInstitutionalBoundaryErased
  - ContributionUnavailable
  - CommunicationOrAssemblyImpossible
  - ProtectedHouseholdInstitutionalOrDefenseMinimumThreatened
```

## 47. Public-Order and Legitimacy Interface

This portfolio does not own legitimacy as a universal score. It consumes recognition, participation, burden, grievance, resistance, accommodation, remedy, and restoration receipts.

Regional influence weakens when:

- claimed competence exceeds accepted competence;
- burdens are hidden or persistently unequal;
- benefits do not travel with obligations;
- property or institutional limits are ignored;
- petitions, assemblies, judgments, and remedies fail;
- protection or service promises are undelivered;
- information is unreliable;
- coalition exits or handbacks are obstructed.

Regional influence may strengthen through delivered service and accepted reciprocity, but no action guarantees legitimacy or loyalty.

## 48. Military and Security Interface

## 50. Institution, Education, and Knowledge Interface
