## 1. Strengthened Character Verdict

<Person>Diepold II of Vohburg</Person> is Bavaria's first developed regional secular counterweight to the duke, but V1 gave the frontier systems more definition than the person carrying their obligations. V1.1 makes the character's political struggle explicit: recognition must be earned from several audiences, followers must be rewarded without turning service into unlimited entitlement, residents must receive protection and justice rather than only extraction, and every outward commitment risks weakening the frontier communities that make power possible.

**Strategic fantasy:** Become the lord whose protection is worth recognizing. Build a connected frontier order through settlements, justice, assemblies, household service, strongpoints, routes, patronage, and bounded military obligations, while refusing the easier illusion that titles and castles alone create authority.

**Central tension:** <Person>Diepold II of Vohburg</Person> can gain recognition by serving Crown, duke, church, and frontier communities, but satisfying one audience may exhaust resources, offend another, or create obligations the household cannot sustain.

**Core question:** Can <Person>Diepold II of Vohburg</Person> turn uncertain rank and fragmented influence into accepted lordship grounded in protection, justice, service, and recoverable development, or will the pressure to appear powerful produce an isolated and overcommitted frontier?

## 7. Opening Identity and Personal Stakes

### <Person>Diepold II of Vohburg</Person> begins as

- a named regional principal with an important but incompletely bound opening rank;
- a member of a powerful family association without automatic access to every dynastic claim or possession;
- a possible frontier organizer whose authority must become visible through protection, justice, service, and maintained connections;
- a negotiator among Royal government, Bavaria's duke, church institutions, neighboring principals, residents, settlers, and service contributors;
- a conditional commander whose later military career informs capability range but grants no opening army.

### Personal stakes

- Seeking Royal recognition may provoke ducal suspicion or create costly service obligations.
- Seeking ducal acceptance may narrow independent authority or frontier initiative.
- Rewarding followers may secure cohesion while alienating residents, institutions, or competing holders.
- Building strongpoints without relief, supply, justice, and local benefit may create occupation rather than protection.
- Expanding settlement faster than water, households, tools, security, worship, and dispute resolution can support produces fragile claims.
- Refusing outward service may preserve the core while losing recognition and influence.
- A defeat can destroy prestige without ending the character, creating a real recovery arc.
- A successful succession transfers obligations, records, negotiated settlements, and working networks rather than merely titles and claims.

## 24. Conditional Development Paths

- **Accepted Frontier Lord:** earns authority through protection, justice, assemblies, and working connections.
- **Nordgau Consolidator:** builds a reliable core before extending influence.
- **Royal Margravial Servant:** trades service for recognition while risking ducal and local distrust.
- **Bavarian Regional Partner:** supports the duke while preserving bounded independent interests.
- **Independent Frontier Lord:** expands household and strongpoints while accepting recognition risk.
- **Castle and Route Builder:** creates connected protection, supply, relief, and communication.
- **Settlement and Custom Broker:** develops land while negotiating residents, prior uses, labor, and burdens.
- **Ecclesiastical Patron-Broker:** uses patronage and institutions for settlement and mediation.
- **Conditional Frontier Commander:** commands only through valid mission, forces, supply, and recovery.
- **Defeated but Recovering Principal:** rebuilds authority after loss through retrenchment, settlement, justice, and reconciliation.
