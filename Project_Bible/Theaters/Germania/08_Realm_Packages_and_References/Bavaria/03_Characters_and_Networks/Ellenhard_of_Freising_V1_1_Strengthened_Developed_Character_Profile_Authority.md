## 1. Strengthened Character Verdict

<Person>Ellenhard of Freising</Person> is a major institutional character, but V1 placed so much emphasis on foundations, endowments, and manuscripts that the bishop could occasionally read as a portfolio manager rather than the directing person. V1.1 preserves the institutional depth while strengthening the personal arc: <Person>Ellenhard of Freising</Person> must decide what should bear the bishop's name, what should belong to a community, how much present need may be sacrificed for durable memory, and whether Royal alignment protects or compromises the Freising legacy.

**Strategic fantasy:** Shape what Freising will inherit from a long episcopate. Build living communities, secure sustainable endowments, commission books that are actually used, protect learning and records, serve petitioners and travelers, and ensure the bishop's foundations can govern themselves after the founder is gone.

**Central tension:** commemoration and durable institutions can reinforce each other, but they can also compete for property, labor, attention, and legitimacy.

**Core question:** Will <Person>Ellenhard of Freising</Person> leave Freising a self-sustaining religious and intellectual inheritance, or a celebrated but fragile collection of works too dependent on one bishop's wealth, authority, and political relationships?

## 7. Opening Identity and Personal Stakes

### <Person>Ellenhard of Freising</Person> begins as

- a long-serving bishop with enough history to have created institutional expectations and liabilities;
- a founder responsible for whether <Place>St. Andrew's</Place> becomes a living community or remains founder-dependent;
- a patron whose books and liturgical objects require craftspeople, custodians, readers, repair, and purposeful use;
- a political and pastoral principal balancing institutional memory with current need;
- a counselor capable of Royal and Bavarian relationships without a fixed opening alignment;
- a person approaching legacy questions without a scripted death date.

### Personal stakes

- A magnificent foundation that cannot sustain its canons is a personal failure, not merely a low institutional score.
- Books that cannot be used, guarded, copied, or repaired turn patronage into display.
- Excessive memorial expenditure can damage care, hospitality, clergy, and property maintenance.
- Excessive political alignment can preserve grants while compromising institutional autonomy.
- Excessive autonomy can leave foundations without protection or enforcement.
- A successful handoff allows <Person>Ellenhard of Freising</Person>'s influence to continue without allowing the dead founder to govern the living institution.

## 27. Conditional Development Paths

- **Freising Institutional Founder:** converts founder-driven programs into community-owned institutional continuity.
- **Book and Liturgy Patron:** develops purposeful worship, manuscripts, workshops, custody, and memory.
- **Clerical Formation Bishop:** strengthens teachers, learners, records, correspondence, and skilled continuity.
- **Property and Immunity Defender:** secures endowment and rights without treating immunity as sovereignty.
- **Pastoral Provider:** favors care, hospitality, petition access, reconciliation, and present service.
- **Memoria Builder:** prioritizes commemoration while accepting material and legitimacy risks.
- **Bavarian Ecclesiastical Counselor:** works with duke and regional actors without institutional surrender.
- **Royal Church Servant:** adopts strong Royal alignment through campaign decisions.
- **Freising Institutionalist:** protects community, books, property, and governance from political capture.
- **Embattled Founder:** faces unfinished works, weak collection, disputed custody, divided communities, or failed handoff.
