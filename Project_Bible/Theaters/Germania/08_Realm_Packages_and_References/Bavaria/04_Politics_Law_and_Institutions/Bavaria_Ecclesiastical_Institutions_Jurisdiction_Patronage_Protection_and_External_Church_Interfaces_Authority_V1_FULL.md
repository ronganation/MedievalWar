## 5. Evidence and Admission Discipline

Every named institution requires evidence for identity, chronology, continuity, place, offices, community, jurisdiction, property, privilege, external relationships, and January 1062 relevance. Later dioceses, orders, reforms, buildings, jurisdictional configurations, saints' cult portfolios, and famous later arrangements may not be projected backward.

Evidence states are SupportedOrientation, Candidate, ReviewOnly, Contested, Quarantined, Unknown, and IntentionalEmpty. Unknown means unresolved, not vacant, powerless, or free for invention.

The authority keeps separately typed institutions, offices, occupancies, people, communities, buildings, jurisdictions, appointment processes, temporalities, property interfaces, privileges, immunities, patronage, protection, advocacy, sanctuary cases, mediation cases, hospitality and care functions, archives, burial and memoria relationships, communication channels, and external relationships.

## 10. Vacancy Temporary Care and Continuity

Unknown holder is not vacancy. Confirmed vacancy does not dissolve an institution. Temporary care must identify a named actor or institution, authority, narrow functions, prohibited powers, duration, reporting, external receipts, end condition, and handback.

During vacancy or incapacity, worship, archives, property administration, hospitality, relief, and communication may continue at different capacity levels through the institution and community.

## 11. Bavarian Ecclesiastical Geography

Bavaria's ecclesiastical geography is not identical to its ducal geography. Diocesan and metropolitan relationships may cross secular boundaries, while an institution inside Bavaria may own lands, maintain dependencies, or answer appeals outside the ducal package.

The authority therefore treats diocesan, institutional, property, route, settlement, and political maps as overlapping but separate. No map color grants <Person>Otto of Nordheim</Person> ecclesiastical jurisdiction.

## 18. Ducal Household Clergy and Chapel Boundary

Nordheim household clergy and resources remain Nordheim-owned unless a specific transfer or service receipt places them into a Bavarian function.

## 19. Ecclesiastical Jurisdiction

Potential subjects include appointment, confirmation, consecration, clerical discipline, marriage and oaths where competent, Church property, privilege, immunity, sanctuary, reconciliation, sanctions, institutional discipline, liturgy, burial, memoria, and canonical appeal.

Every jurisdiction definition requires competent institution or office, subject scope, personal and geographic scope, procedure, exclusions, appeal, privileges, external locks, evidence, recognition, enforcement owners, and review. Ecclesiastical jurisdiction may overlap secular law without absorbing it.

## 21. Patronage Constitution

Patronage may include lawful endowment, building or repair support, hospitality, education, manuscripts and records, liturgical commemoration, alms, relief, care, protection, or confirmation of a valid right.

Every agreement identifies patron, institution, purpose, resource owner, delivery, duration, obligations, review, breach, recovery, property boundaries, jurisdiction boundaries, and memory. Patronage does not purchase obedience, office, property, or doctrine.

## 22. Protection Advocacy and Vogt-Style Function Gate

Protection or advocacy requires a specific institution, protected interest, authority or agreement, permitted actions, prohibited actions, property, force, route, reporting, duration, review, and termination.

A protector may provide safe conduct, defense, representation, mediation, recovery support, or owner-routed enforcement. Protection does not create lordship or ownership. Any historically specific advocacy or Vogt-style office requires evidence and cannot be inserted as a generic Bavarian mechanic.

## 23. Privilege Immunity Property and Temporalities

Privileges and immunities are scoped. Property, revenue, possession, transfer, stores, labor, and estate administration remain with property and economy owners. Temporalities do not equal spiritual office, and office occupancy does not equal personal ownership.

The player must distinguish protecting an institution's right, confirming a recognized privilege, negotiating an exception, contesting an abuse, and seizing property. These are different actions with different owners and consequences.

## 24. Sanctuary Refuge and Custody

A sanctuary case requires institution, place, person, trigger, claimed basis, knowledge, admission, capacity, secular and ecclesiastical jurisdictions, negotiation, safety, removal, breach, restoration, and trace.

Sanctuary may delay coercion, create negotiation, or protect a vulnerable person. It does not guarantee physical safety, innocence, permanent immunity, or unrestricted institutional capacity.

## 25. Mediation Witnessing Oaths and Peace Support

Institutions may provide mediators, witnesses, records, sacred settings, oath support, communication, or hospitality where competence, capacity, and consent permit.

Sacred setting is not jurisdiction. Witnessing is not automatic truth. An oath is not verified fact. Mediation is not judgment. Failed mediation may still produce information, clarified positions, safer withdrawal, or a record for later settlement.

## 26. Hospitality Relief Care and Almsgiving

Ecclesiastical hospitality may support ducal travel, assemblies, envoys, pilgrims, dependents, injured people, displaced communities, or negotiation. Relief and care require people, food, space, water, records, routes, security, and property access.

Requests create burdens and memory. Repeated ducal use without compensation or respect can reduce cooperation, strain communities, and create property or privilege disputes.

## 27. Education Literacy Clerical Formation and Archives

Institutions may preserve literacy, teach clerks, maintain archives, train clergy, support correspondence, and transmit procedural knowledge. Education does not spawn skilled people instantly. Archive custody does not prove every recorded claim, and seal custody does not authorize every use.

For Bavaria, documentary and communication capacity can make the difference between a nominal ducal request and a request that reaches actors, preserves terms, and survives <Person>Otto of Nordheim</Person>'s absence.

## 28. Burial Commemoration and Memoria

Burial and memoria relationships require person, household, dynasty, institution, site, community, consent, capacity, endowment, access, precedence, contest, records, and continuity.

Ducal or Nordheim patronage may seek remembrance without owning the institution. Later burial or commemoration outcomes cannot be loaded at opening. Office loss does not automatically end a valid memoria relationship.

## 29. Relics Pilgrimage and Sacred Custody

Relics, sacred objects, pilgrimage flows, and custodial rights require separate evidence, identity, custody, site, access, security, routes, ritual function, publicity, and dispute state.

No relic or pilgrimage portfolio is created merely to give Bavaria a religious bonus. Sacred custody is not property ownership, and military capture does not transfer sacred legitimacy automatically.

## 30. External Church Interfaces

They require local and external endpoints, historical basis, knowledge, communication, access, request type, consent, capacity, route, political context, withdrawal, contest, expiry, and trace.

## 31. Papal Relations and Reform Pressure

Papal and reform-facing activity must be issue-specific. Possible subjects include appointments, privileges, appeals, discipline, marriage, sanctuary, property, peace, missions, and political settlement.

There is no single Papal Favor truth. Reform pressure can vary by institution, officeholder, community, issue, and communication. The player may cooperate, delay, negotiate, seek clarification, contest, or accept consequences without one action scheduling a later Investiture Controversy path.

## 32. Royal Salian Church Interface

Royal government may possess separate rights, relationships, requests, protections, confirmations, objections, and political interests concerning ecclesiastical institutions. Bavaria may receive and answer public Royal receipts but cannot invent Royal or institutional private state.

A Royal ecclesiastical position does not automatically bind every Bavarian institution. Ducal cooperation with Royal government does not create episcopal compliance.

## 33. Otto of Nordheim Ecclesiastical Strategy

<Person>Otto of Nordheim</Person>'s strongest opening ecclesiastical tools are credible protection, respectful consultation, purpose-bound patronage, safe conduct, mediation, record support, and acceptance of institutional autonomy.

His vulnerabilities are those of a recent appointee with a separate northern patrimony: overreaching in appointment or property, treating hospitality as entitlement, demanding service without capacity, confusing Royal backing with local consent, or importing Nordheim household assets into Bavaria without receipts.

## 37. Decision Package Appointment Dispute

When an ecclesiastical office becomes contested, the player may support procedure, recommend or object where valid, protect electors or candidates, provide safe conduct, request external clarification, mediate, withhold a specific owner-controlled resource, recognize temporal rights, or abstain.

The player cannot appoint every bishop or abbot. Aggressive interference may create institutional opposition, external review, property conflict, divided recognition, or long-term memory.

## 38. Decision Package Property and Protection Crisis

When an institution faces threat or when ducal government demands access to property, stores, routes, records, or buildings, the player may protect, negotiate limited access, compensate, seek emergency use, refer the dispute, suspend the request, contest a specific claim, or withdraw.

Emergency use does not create ownership. Protection does not create permanent service. Refusal does not automatically constitute disloyalty or impiety.

## 39. Decision Package Sanctuary and High-Political Refuge

A politically important person may seek sanctuary or refuge only through a valid institution, route, claim, knowledge state, and capacity. The player may respect the claim, negotiate custody or safe conduct, request mediation, contest applicability, secure the site without entry, or breach the sanctuary and accept consequences.

This package can later support crisis play involving accusations, office loss, submission, custody, reconciliation, or restoration without scripting a particular historical outcome.

## 40. Institution Lifecycle Crisis and Restoration

Institution states include candidate, operating, constrained, disputed, divided, damaged, occupied, reorganizing, merging, relocating, suppressed, dormant, restoring, restored, dissolved, historical former, quarantined, and unknown.

Building loss does not automatically dissolve a community or institution. Dissolution does not erase archives, property disputes, burial relationships, obligations, or memory. Restoration requires people, property access, records, recognition, security, resources, and owner receipts.

## 41. Failure Modes and Recovery

Major failure modes include appointment conflict, property dispute, patronage failure, exploitative advocacy, route disruption, lost archives, overburdened hospitality, sanctuary breach, damaged buildings, absent specialists, divided recognition, external sanction, and coerced cooperation.

Recovery may require evidence review, mediation, restitution, compensation, repair, record reconstruction, new protection terms, restored access, renewed office process, apology or reconciliation, and persistent memory. No single payment clears every grievance.

## 42. Ecclesiastical AI Doctrine

The AI must identify the exact institution, office, community, jurisdiction, property, and external owners; evaluate knowledge, relationships, capacity, routes, privileges, and risks; compare consultation, patronage, protection, mediation, referral, restraint, contest, delay, or abstention; execute only through owner receipts; preserve institutional memory; and reevaluate.

The AI may not use one Church-loyalty score, invent an officeholder, assume papal omniscience, convert patronage into control, or chase later historical outcomes.
