## 5. Governance Owner Boundary

Governance owns how a recognized Bavarian ducal project operates: issue intake, requests, mandates, delegates, purpose-bound assemblies, counsel, petition reception, referrals, records, messages, reports, backlog, capacity, emergency continuity, review, closure, and handback.

Governance cannot define the ducal title, invent legal jurisdiction, appoint ecclesiastical officeholders, transfer property, create forces, or decide external recognition. It must request those states from their owners.

## 6. Title and Secular Office Owner Boundary

The title owner defines the Bavarian ducal office, title and office states, holders, secular offices, competence, claims, audience-specific recognition, precedence, vacancy, temporary exercise, challenge, office loss, former-holder status, and restoration.

The title owner cannot create government personnel, petitions, judgments, Church offices, property, money, forces, regional compliance, or Royal private state.

## 7. Law Owner Boundary

Law owns legal definitions and procedure: law and custom, privileges, immunities, exceptions, safe conduct, standing, general and mixed jurisdiction procedure, forum, venue, notice, evidence, mediation, settlement, judgment, appeal, enforcement requests, compliance review, restoration, policy, emergency legal measures, and legal trace.

Law cannot create title competence, public mandates, institutional offices, property transfers, treasury entries, physical custody, or military execution without owner receipts. Law owns the legal basis, review, forum, safeguards, remedy, and dispute state for custody; the accepted executing custodian owns operational responsibility within scope.

## 8. Ecclesiastical Owner Boundary

The ecclesiastical owner defines Church institutions, communities, offices, occupancy, appointment stages, canonical competence, institution-specific jurisdiction, patronage, protection, advocacy, privileges, sanctuary, hospitality, relief, education, archives, burial, memoria, sacred custody, and external Church interfaces.

It cannot create secular title effects, ducal mandates, general law, private property transfers, armed forces, military-prisoner custody, or papal, metropolitan, Royal, or foreign private state. Sacred custody, sanctuary responsibility, institutional care, burial, and memoria remain institution-specific and do not overwrite canonical person identity, location, life state, civilian guardianship, household wardship, or an accepted military custodian.

## 11. Petition to Case Pipeline

```text
Petition presented
→ governance validates identity and records receipt
→ governance triages urgency, competence candidate, and referral
→ title or ecclesiastical owner supplies relevant competence
→ law determines standing, jurisdiction, forum, notice, and case admission
→ law mediates or hears
→ law issues settlement or judgment within scope
→ competent owners execute accepted remedies
→ governance tracks administrative follow-up
→ law reviews compliance, appeal, and restoration
→ all owners preserve trace and residual issues
```

A petition may close administratively without becoming a case. A case may conclude without enforceable material remedy.

## 12. Mandate and Competence Pipeline

```text
Governance identifies a function and proposed delegate
→ title owner confirms whether the ducal or secular office has relevant competence
→ law checks procedural and jurisdictional constraints
→ ecclesiastical owner supplies any institution-specific or canonical limits
→ governance prepares the mandate
→ people, household, property, economy, route, and security owners verify capacity
→ mandate activates only through accepted receipts

```

A mandate cannot widen the competence from which it derives.

## 31. Positive Fixtures

- Governance receives a petition, law admits a case, title supplies competence, and a property owner executes restitution.
- A ducal assembly records disagreement without creating universal consent.
- A Church institution accepts patronage while refusing ducal jurisdiction.
- An unknown bishop remains unknown rather than becoming a vacancy.
- A safe-conduct dispute routes through law, institution, person, route, and Royal owners.
- <Person>Otto of Nordheim</Person> loses the Bavarian office while Nordheim household and private-property processes remain separate.
- A judgment is issued but enforcement waits for capacity.
- A delegate acts within a narrow mandate and returns records and resources at handback.
- A Church appointment advances through institutional and external receipts without direct ducal appointment.
- A failed transaction compensates reservations while preserving public memory and costs.
- A military prisoner transfer preserves the outgoing custodian until a receiving owner accepts handover.
- A released captive arrives with continuing care and household reception still pending.
- An ecclesiastical institution provides sanctuary or care without becoming the military custodian or person-state writer.
