## 11. Bavarian Jurisdiction Families

The legal field should support distinct jurisdiction families:

- **ducal and title-specific:** bounded hearing, mediation, protection, service review, and judgment where competence exists;
- **Royal:** grant, office challenge, ban, pardon, service, safe conduct, and political-order matters owned externally;
- **comital and lordship:** local, property, service, security, and dispute functions;
- **household and dynastic:** marriage, dependency, custody, guardianship, inheritance evidence, and internal arrangements;
- **property and tenure:** possession, use, service, damage, revenue, transfer, and restoration;
- **settlement and market:** local order, exchange, tolls, lodging, debt, craft, and market disputes where supported;
- **route, river, and Alpine passage:** access, crossing, toll, escort, hospitality, and damage;
- **military service:** obligation, duration, geography, substitution, supply, release, compensation, and breach;
- **ecclesiastical:** canonical and institution-specific competence owned by Church owners;
- **external and treaty:** passage, truce, hostages, recognition, and reciprocal compacts.

## 12. Jurisdiction Conflicts

Conflict does not permit the player or AI to select a preferred forum silently. Political importance does not create jurisdiction. Concurrent jurisdiction does not mean duplicate authority.

## 21. Mediation Arbitration and Settlement

Bavaria should prefer mediation where jurisdictions overlap and a negotiated result can preserve separate actors.

A mediation record identifies parties, mediator, authority basis, consent, scope, known facts, claims, risks, options, proposed terms, owner dependencies, withdrawal, failure, and closure.

A settlement creates only its accepted terms. It cannot bind absent actors, transfer unrelated property, decide future succession, or create permanent subordination by implication.

## 22. Judgment Order and Remedy

A judgment record must identify the case, jurisdiction, forum, decision authority, parties, findings, uncertainty, applicable law and custom, ordered remedies, costs, appeal, effective date, enforcement owners, and publication.

Potential remedies include declaration, performance, cessation, restitution, compensation, fine request, property or revenue handoff request, service disposition, release, custody review, safe conduct, recognition correction, temporary protection, dismissal, or no relief.

A judgment does not directly transfer property, title, office, custody, treasury, military force, or ecclesiastical state.
