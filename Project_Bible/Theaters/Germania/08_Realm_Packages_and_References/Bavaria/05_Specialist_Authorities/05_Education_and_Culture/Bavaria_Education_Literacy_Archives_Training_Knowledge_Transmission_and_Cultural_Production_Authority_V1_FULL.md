## 11. Practice, feedback and assessment

A practice record identifies task, conditions, supervision, observed result, correction, limitations and relevant owner. It can be partial or unsuccessful. Real work may support learning, but an urgent assignment without feedback does not necessarily teach its performer.

Assessment compares the requested scope with observed evidence. It may use supervised performance, comparison of a work product, repeated task completion, or an accepted practitioner/institutional review. Modern examinations, degrees and universal certificates are not assumed. An assessor must be available and competent for the subject; disputed assessment remains disputed.

A recommendation for routine account work does not authorize legal composition or command. Teaching competence requires its own evidence. Assessment can be revised after errors, changed methods or lost access. The consumer explicitly accepts, narrows or rejects the recommendation.

## 18. Language, interpretation and translation

Language capability is task-, register- and mode-specific. Spoken communication, reading, writing, interpretation and translation remain distinct. Origin, community label, title or religious identity never assigns proficiency.

A translation task records source and target, subject, purpose, provider, source access, recipient, ambiguities, omissions, review and version. High-consequence ambiguity can require independent review or narrower use. One translation does not create general fluency.

## 22. Archive move, loss and reconstruction

Reconstruction marks missing detail and competing versions. It cannot restore authenticity, legal effect or exact holdings automatically. Repairing storage does not recreate its contents. ## 25. Degradation, interruption and recovery
