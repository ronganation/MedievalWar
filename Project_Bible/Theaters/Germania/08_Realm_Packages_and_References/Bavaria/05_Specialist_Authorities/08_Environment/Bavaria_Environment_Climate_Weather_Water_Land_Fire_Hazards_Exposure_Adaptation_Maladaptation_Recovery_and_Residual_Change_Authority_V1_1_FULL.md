## 10. Weather and Ground Condition Families

Weather families include temperature band, precipitation, wind, visibility, cloud/sun exposure, frost/snow/ice candidate, wetness, mud, dust, and transition state. Ground families include dry, wet, muddy, frozen, thawing, rutted, eroded, unstable, obstructed, burned, and recovering.

Conditions are descriptive owner inputs. No universal combat, movement, morale, production, or health modifier is applied by this authority.

## 11. Snow Ice Thaw and Cold

Snow, ice, frost and thaw require place, window, observation and confidence. Potential exposure includes routes, crossings, rivers, Alpine approaches, shelter, fuel, animals, work, buildings, military camps and displaced households. Territory evaluates access and capacity; health evaluates people; economy evaluates goods and animals; military evaluates operations; built environment evaluates assets.

Cold or snow does not automatically close a route, freeze a river, kill people, create attrition, or provide defensive advantage.

## 12. Rain Flood Riverbank and Water State

Rain, runoff, high-water, overflow, flood, ice, low-water, bank damage, erosion, debris and silt remain separate. The Danube exists as a geographical field, but environment writes observed changing water and bank condition rather than navigability, vessels, crossings, property, rights, or transport capacity.

Flood requires a bounded observed scope. One report never creates a regional flood. Flood may bring mixed effects and does not automatically destroy crops, buildings, routes, or bridges.

## 13. Water Availability and Stress

Water condition includes source presence, flow, storage, seasonal reliability, suspected quality, access pressure, sediment, blockage, competing demand and recovery. Economy/property own rights, allocation and delivery; built environment owns wells, channels, drains, storage and physical condition; health owns safety and illness; territory owns location and access.

Water stress exists only when usable, accessible and deliverable water is insufficient or uncertain relative to actual demand. A source creates no right, safety, flow, delivery or unlimited supply.

## 14. Fire Smoke and Combustible Environment

Fire risk depends on fuel, dryness, wind, ignition claim, settlement/camp/workshop layout, water, tools, responders, access, warning, security, and interference. Fire states distinguish risk, report, verified bounded fire, spread candidate, contained, extinguished, owner-confirmed damage, recovery, and residual change.

Heat does not create fire. Smoke does not confirm a source. Extinguished does not mean recovered. Deliberate-fire attribution requires separate evidence and owner review.

## 15. Soil Erosion Slope and Route Environment

Changing condition may include moisture, workability, compaction, rutting, erosion, gullies, damaged banks, slope instability, rockfall, landslide candidate, burned ground and recovery. Stable terrain remains territory-owned. Current ground condition is operation-specific: foot travel, mounted movement, wagons, herds, armies, refugees, and siege transport may receive different consequences from the same condition.

## 16. Vegetation Woodland Pasture and Fuel Pressure

Environment owns observed vegetation, pasture, woodland, fuel-condition and recovery pressure. Economy owns goods, timber, fodder, fuel, crops, stocks and production. Property owners retain rights. Population owns people and labor.

Vegetation creates no inventory. Woodland creates no public property. Pasture creates no animals. Demand does not prove degradation. Recovery requires time, condition, use and protection through actual owners.

## 18. Compound Hazards

Compound hazards preserve each component's evidence and owner. Examples include snow plus damaged shelter, thaw plus route degradation, flood plus blocked crossing, fire plus wind and limited water, rain plus siege damage, pasture pressure plus military demand, or route closure plus household displacement.

Responses may compete for the same people, goods, routes, care, animals, tools, money, buildings and institutions. Combined burden does not automatically create disaster.

Residual change persists until evidence and owner receipts support reclassification.
