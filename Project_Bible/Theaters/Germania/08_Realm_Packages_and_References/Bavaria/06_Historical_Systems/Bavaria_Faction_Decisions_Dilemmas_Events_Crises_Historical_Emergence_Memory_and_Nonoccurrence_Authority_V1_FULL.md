## 10. Decision and Dilemma Contract

A decision is a deliberate player or AI proposal. A dilemma is a bounded choice among materially different responses under pressure. Both require named actors, known issue, valid decision maker, options, prerequisites, costs, owner requests, uncertainty, refusal and abstention, failure, recovery, expiry, and explanation.

No option may be a disguised free reward. Every meaningful option must preserve tradeoffs and owner consequences.

## 11. Incident Opportunity and Crisis Contract

An incident is a bounded occurrence requiring response. An opportunity is an optional favorable possibility with cost and risk. A crisis is a multi-owner condition that threatens material capability, political order, continuity, safety, or office state.

A crisis must expose containment, mediation, accommodation, withdrawal, escalation, failure, and nonoccurrence where applicable. Escalation is not automatic.

## 13. Opening Decision Family: Establish the Ducal Office

Candidate choices include prioritizing personal presence, bounded delegates, records and correspondence, regional consultation, or a minimal office while evidence and capacity are gathered.

The family consumes title, governance, population, economy, route, public-order, and knowledge state. It cannot create recognition, clerks, revenue, offices, or compliance.

## 14. Opening Decision Family: Preserve the Dual Base

Candidate choices include protecting Nordheim reserves, creating bounded resource-transfer procedures, improving communication, maintaining a Bavarian operational reserve, or accepting slower integration.

The dilemma is continuity versus reach, not Bavaria versus Nordheim as a forced cultural fork.

## 15. Opening Decision Family: Regional Consultation

The office may prioritize named Bavarian actors, ecclesiastical institutions, route holders, service contributors, or settlement interfaces. Options include consultation, qualified recognition, mediation, narrower requests, delayed action, or abstention.

One actor's response never becomes Bavaria-wide consent.

## 16. Opening Decision Family: Choose a Development Priority

Potential priorities include office reliability, dual-base coordination, routes and communications, material accounting, finite military readiness, legal-restorative capacity, or institutional partnership.

The knowledge and development portfolio supplies readiness and opportunity costs. Choosing a priority creates no active program or capability.

## 17. Royal Request and Service Suite

A Royal request may concern counsel, court attendance, escort, military contribution, mission, mediation, route support, or another evidence-backed matter.

Player choices may include acceptance, bounded acceptance, substitution, delay, clarification, mediation, refusal, or abstention. Results depend on actual mandate, routes, supply, contributors, household burden, performance, external acceptance, and return.

## 18. Hungarian Expedition Emergence Lane

The later Hungarian expedition is a conditional historical-emergence lane, not an opening mission.

Eligibility may require a valid Royal request, relevant political state, named leaders, diplomatic access, routes, military capacity, supply, command, objectives, settlement terms, and return capacity.

Possible outcomes include no request, refusal, limited support, alternate mission, delay, failed expedition, partial success, negotiated outcome, historical-shape success, or nonoccurrence.

## 19. Papal and Italia Mission Lane

A later mediation or papal-facing mission is a conditional lane requiring request, standing, mandate, safe conduct, routes, funds, hospitality, interpreters, ecclesiastical interfaces, and external acceptance.

Completion does not equal success. Shared faith does not create access. The historical mission may not occur.

## 20. Kaiserswerth Crisis Suite

Kaiserswerth remains a shared Germania political-order crisis after the opening anchor.

Required surfaces may include Royal government state, actor knowledge, communication, transport and route feasibility, custody proposals, guarantees, coalition positions, public-order reception, military safeguards, and external-owner receipts.

Valid Bavaria-facing choices may include nonparticipation, warning, investigation, mediation, qualified participation, negotiated government change, protective escort, withdrawal, or opposition. The event may fail, transform, be prevented, or not occur.

## 21. Office Challenge and Accusation Suite

An office challenge requires an actual claimant or accuser, matter, evidence, forum, competence, notice, representation, safe conduct, timelines, property and office inventories, public-order effects, and external receipts.

Choices may include appearance, delegation, legal contest, mediation, negotiated security, delay, refusal with stated basis, submission, or withdrawal. Accusation never proves guilt or office loss.

## 22. Ducal Office Loss Suite

Office loss can arise only through the title, law, Royal, political, and historical owners. This authority may present resulting dilemmas:

- orderly handback versus contested retention;
- protect household and records versus preserve Bavarian influence;
- seek pardon, mediation, refuge, service elsewhere, or restoration;
- demobilize, transfer, or retain lawful forces;
- separate office resources from Nordheim property;
- accept post-ducal transformation.

Office loss does not delete Otto, Nordheim, relationships, memory, military experience, or campaign agency.

## 23. Restoration, Pardon, and Reconciliation Suite

Restoration requires current claim, support, competence, procedure, recognition, public-order reception, resources, and performance. Pardon, reconciliation, property restitution, office restoration, and reintegration remain distinct.

Possible outcomes include partial reconciliation, service without restoration, restored standing without office, property settlement, negotiated office recovery, failed attempt, abandoned claim, or transformed political role.

## 24. Royal-Saxon and Princely Coalition Lane

Later history does not create opening hostility or guaranteed rebellion.

## 34. Historical Memory Interface

Historical memory records owner-approved remembered incidents, promises, service, refusal, coercion, loss, restoration, and settlements. It preserves actor, audience, confidence, narrative, visibility, contradiction, reinterpretation, and current relevance.

Memory does not replace current state. The same event may be remembered differently by different actors.

## 35. Historical Momentum Interface

Momentum represents accumulated current pressure toward or away from a pathway. It may include repeated cooperation, unresolved burden, failed protection, successful service, office insecurity, coalition growth, or recovery.

Momentum cannot force an event. It modifies eligibility, urgency, AI attention, or option credibility only when current owner state supports the effect.

## 38. AI Decision Doctrine

The AI must:

1. evaluate only visible and owner-published state;
2. verify decision maker and authority;
3. distinguish historical possibility from current eligibility;
4. compare options, abstention, delay, and nonoccurrence;
5. check capacity, routes, contributors, risks, maintenance, and exit;
6. protect Nordheim continuity and Bavarian local minima;
7. avoid deterministic historical outcomes;
8. submit owner requests atomically;
9. record reasons and reevaluate after state change.

The AI may not fabricate consent, hostility, guilt, capacity, or external decisions.

## 39. Player Presentation

Every presented item must show:

- why it is appearing now;
- known and unknown facts;
- actors and decision maker;
- historical orientation without claiming inevitability;
- option prerequisites and blocked consequences;
- likely costs, risks, external locks, and opportunity costs;
- immediate owner requests and longer-term pathways;
- expiration, delay, abstention, and nonoccurrence;
- memory and momentum implications.

The UI must not reveal hidden external state or present one option as the historical correct answer.

## 45. Positive Fixtures

- A Royal request presents limited service, delay, substitution, mediation, refusal, and abstention according to actual capacity.
- Kaiserswerth becomes eligible after relevant actors and state exist but is prevented through mediation.
- Office challenge occurs, Otto appears under safe conduct, and office loss does not follow automatically.
- Office loss closes ducal programs while preserving Nordheim and post-ducal choices.
- A Hungarian expedition never occurs because route and external receipts fail.
- A regional resistance incident remains local and resolves through compensation.
- A failed reform trial preserves knowledge and sunk-cost memory.
- A resolved crisis creates different memories among Bavaria, Nordheim, Royal actors, and institutions.

## 46. Negative Fixtures

- Date automatically triggers Kaiserswerth, Hungarian service, papal mission, office loss, or rebellion.
- One event choice creates troops, money, property, agreement, recognition, or capability.
- Royal appointment guarantees loyalty or service.
- Otto's later opposition creates opening hostility.
- Office loss deletes the faction.
- Historical success is labeled the correct choice.
- Unknown external state is revealed to the player.
- The same casualty, payment, or relationship consequence applies twice.
- Nonoccurrence is treated as missing or failed content.
- An advanced save is overwritten by historical sequence.
