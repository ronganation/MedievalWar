## 9. Bavaria Strategic Thesis

Bavaria's strategic problem is not simply expansion or survival. The Realm must reconcile ducal office, separate patrimony, regional centers, local owners, ecclesiastical institutions, routes, households, markets, services, military burdens, external recognition, public order, and development capacity.

Long-horizon success may mean:

- making ducal action credible without converting every useful resource into ducal property;
- linking regional centers without eliminating their distinct owners and functions;
- turning political support into delivered service and accepted obligation;
- strengthening institutions without subordinating them universally;
- developing settlements without exhausting households, labor, routes, and maintenance;
- protecting Bavaria without militarizing every regional relationship;
- maintaining external relevance without surrendering internal cohesion;
- recovering from disruption through reconfiguration rather than resetting to an idealized state.

## 59. Positive Fixtures

- Bavaria pursues networked cohesion and productive integration simultaneously while limiting overconcentration.
- Ducal governance strengthens through hosted circuits without transferring host property.
- Institutional partnership deepens while ecclesiastical jurisdiction remains separate.
- A military setback redirects Bavaria toward recovery and defensive reciprocity rather than deleting prior development.
- An external-brokerage strategy is suspended when internal coalition backing collapses.
- Administrative consolidation succeeds in records and procedure but produces regional-balance pressure requiring mitigation.
- A path is abandoned while its institutions, debts, benefits, and political memory remain.
- Bavaria transforms through several owner-backed changes rather than one event.
- Nordheim supports a bounded objective while retaining separate household and patrimonial strategy.
- An advanced save preserves mixed paths, setbacks, abandoned objectives, recovery, and reconfiguration.

## 60. Negative Fixtures

- Bavaria automatically selects a campaign path on 1 January 1062.
- One objective tree replaces all political and specialist systems.
- Every path is mutually exclusive.
- Money or research completes objectives automatically.
- A building, office, institution, coalition, army, or market appears because an objective requires it.
- Centralization is always the best or final route.
- Regional autonomy automatically means path failure.
- Military victory completes political settlement.
- Transformation instantly changes faction identity, culture, roster, ownership, or institutions.
- Recovery restores every prior state and erases claims or memory.
- Nordheim is absorbed because Bavaria follows a ducal objective.
- Later historical developments are treated as inevitable.

## 65. Transaction and Collision Protection

A strategic transaction requires expected owner versions, objective or path ref, current and target state, evidence, actors, owners, competence, relationships, inputs, protected minima, compatible and competing commitments, opportunity costs, milestones, setbacks, remedy, exit, memory, idempotency, collision checks, partial failure, rollback or restoration, and trace.

The system blocks:

- objective progress without owner receipts;
- duplicate commitment of money, labor, materials, animals, equipment, services, routes, institutions, or political support;
- incompatible objectives silently progressing together;
- path selection fabricating required state;
- transformation erasing owners, institutions, history, memory, or prior consequences;
- Bavaria objectives consuming Nordheim resources without explicit agreement;
- advanced saves reset to opening assumptions.
