## 1. Authority Purpose

This authority defines the coherent player experience of Holland across campaign identity, political pressure, strategic geography, development, military access, presentation, AI behavior, and alternate outcomes. It converts the established realm foundation into a clear experience standard without creating an Opening State payload, exact territorial binding, character implementation record, unit roster, or runtime system.

Holland must feel like a small but consequential comital realm whose inheritance is legally meaningful before its security is materially assured. The campaign begins with a child count, an active regency, hostile claims, fragmented access, and the need to secure cooperation among households, communities, routes, institutions, and external powers. Survival is not the end of the faction experience. Survival creates the opportunity to consolidate a county, strengthen administration, connect waterways and settlements, cultivate towns and ports, and eventually become a compact maritime and military power through earned capacity.

This authority controls how that journey should feel. Specialist files continue to control the underlying characters, dynasties, history, government, economy, ecclesiastical institutions, campaign objectives, AI details, visual implementation, military sources, units, and roster.

Holland is not a prototype of the later Dutch Republic placed into 1062. Holland is a comital inheritance under pressure. Later commercial, urban, maritime, naval, and gunpowder strengths are possible campaign outcomes only when the supporting people, institutions, routes, finances, workshops, crews, stores, political relationships, and protected bases actually exist.

## 3. Player Promise

The Holland campaign promises the player six connected experiences:

1. **Defend an inheritance before controlling a secure state.** The count's legal position matters, but claims, recognition, local adherence, household survival, and enforceable access determine whether legal authority becomes practical authority.
2. **Govern through regency without replacing the ruler.** Gertrude of Saxony is an active political and household force, while Dirk V remains the hereditary center of the realm. The transition to personal rule must grow from biology, recognition, government stability, and political circumstance.
3. **Contest a prince-bishop through more than battlefield conquest.** Utrecht pressure operates through ecclesiastical authority, administration, imperial recognition, territorial claims, enforcement, local allegiance, and military intervention.
4. **Treat water as an operating environment.** Rivers, estuaries, islands, marshes, ferries, channels, ports, roads, seasons, and weather affect movement and concentration. Water becomes an advantage only when Holland can organize the human and material systems needed to use it.
5. **Choose protection without receiving a free rescue.** Saxon, Flemish, imperial, local, and other relationships may preserve the dynasty or deter enemies, but protection can demand service, access, influence, marriage freedom, territory, revenue, or political dependence.
6. **Build a strong compact realm without requiring territorial blobbing.** Holland may become powerful through connected routes, fiscal reach, defensible positions, towns, ports, fleets, alliances, specialists, and institutions even when the realm remains geographically limited.

### 4.3 Count versus Bishop

The Holland-Utrecht contest must feel like a collision between rival forms of authority, not a standard secular-versus-religious faction trope. Holland relies upon inheritance, comital continuity, household and regional adherence, local defense, and recoverable claims. Utrecht may rely upon episcopal office, ecclesiastical administration, imperial recognition, ministerial support, and outside enforcement.

The player must confront layered pressure:

- disputed rights and territorial claims;
- local communities choosing, resisting, or negotiating allegiance;
- access to river mouths, crossings, roads, settlements, and fortifications;
- ecclesiastical patronage and property;
- imperial judgments and confirmations;
- military occupation or enforcement;
- wider papal-imperial conflict when relevant world conditions emerge.

Victory need not mean annexing Utrecht. It may mean negotiated recognition, limited settlement, defeated enforcement, durable autonomy, recovered access, a favorable imperial accommodation, or effective resistance supported by a wider coalition.

## 5. Campaign Rhythm

### 5.1 Opening Rhythm: Hold, Connect, Negotiate

The opening should combine immediate pressure with several viable responses. The player needs enough agency to assess threats, preserve the dynasty, maintain government, secure routes, and decide where compromise is acceptable.

```yaml
opening_priorities:
  - PreserveDirkVAndDynasticRecognition
  - SustainGertrudesRegency
  - KeepTheComitalHouseholdOperational
  - MaintainCriticalRoutesAndSeparatedHoldings
  - AssessUtrechtPressureAndLocalAdherence
  - SecureMilitaryAndPoliticalSupport
  - AvoidUnmanageableDependency
  - PrepareForPersonalRule
opening_tempo: PressuredButReadable
opening_decision_character: ConsequentialNotBinary
opening_failure_character: RecoverableWhereRealmOrDynasticClaimSurvives
```

### 5.2 Middle Rhythm: Consolidate, Settle, Recover

The middle campaign should shift from emergency survival toward the creation of dependable power. This is where personal rule, shared rule, or an alternate successor government must demonstrate actual capacity.

Core middle-game work includes:

- settling or containing the Utrecht dispute;
- recovering access, rights, or territory where feasible;
- limiting or integrating protector influence;
- improving comital administration;
- strengthening relationships with landholders, communities, towns, ports, monasteries, and route providers;
- turning irregular access into reliable communications and supply;
- improving the predictability of revenue and recruitment pools;
- choosing between compact development and riskier geographic expansion.

### 5.3 Late Rhythm: Specialize, Project, Endure

Late Holland should not simply become a richer version of its opening roster. The player should decide which expensive capabilities the realm can sustain.

Possible late strategic identities include:

- a compact coastal principality with strong defenses and revenue;
- a port-and-market realm able to support allies and concentrated campaigns;
- a North Sea and river-route power with strong transport and escort capacity;
- a fortified county emphasizing artillery and denial of access;
- a wider Frisian or Low Countries coalition built through relationships and resistance management;
- an imperial or reform-aligned regional power;
- a naval and commercial actor with conditional gunpowder capacity.

The realm cannot maximize every path without the necessary political, human, physical, and fiscal foundations.

## 6. Strategic Choice Architecture

Holland should repeatedly present choices in which both options preserve something and endanger something else.

### 6.1 Autonomy versus Protection

Stronger protection may reduce immediate danger while increasing external influence over war, appointments, revenue, marriage, claims, or territory.

### 6.2 Recovery versus Consolidation

A recovery war may restore claims but can expose a fragile route network and interrupt administration. Consolidation may strengthen the realm but allow rivals to entrench.

### 6.3 Compact Strength versus Expansion

Compact development can produce defensible, governable power. Expansion can provide strategic depth and new sources but introduces resistance, route costs, competing institutions, and administrative strain.

### 6.4 Imperial Accommodation versus Reform Alignment

Imperial settlement may improve recognition while strengthening actors hostile to Holland's claims. Reform or papal alignment may weaken an episcopal rival under favorable conditions while creating wider enemies and obligations. Strategic neutrality and transactional realignment remain valid.

### 6.5 Local Obligation versus Central Reach

Reliance on regional providers can preserve cooperation and responsiveness but limit direct comital control. Stronger administration can increase reliability while provoking resistance if imposed without relationships, officers, routes, and institutional capacity.

### 6.6 Maritime Investment versus Immediate Land Security

Boats, crews, ports, stores, shipwrights, and escorts can transform operations but consume resources needed for land defense, household forces, fortifications, and recovery campaigns.

## 8. Realm Differentiation Firewalls

### 8.1 Holland and Frisia

Holland's West-Frisian comital identity does not create ownership of all Frisia. Wider Frisian communities remain politically and militarily meaningful. Access may come through alliance, service, tribute, trade, local leadership, regional recruitment relationships, negotiated influence, or conquest. None is automatic.

### 8.2 Holland and Flanders

Flanders may be protector, dynastic partner, competitor, overlord, ally, or enemy. Shared Low Countries connections do not merge the realms. Holland retains separate ownership, dynasty, objectives, recruitment pools, regional sources, and development logic.

### 8.3 Holland and Utrecht

Utrecht is not a holy reskin of Holland. Utrecht's episcopal office, ecclesiastical administration, imperial relationships, properties, ministerials, and political objectives require separate authority. Holland's opposition does not grant ownership of Utrecht's people or institutions.

### 8.4 Holland and Later Dutch Identity

The player-facing name provides clarity, not permission to import the institutions, wealth, fleets, symbols, cities, or geopolitical objectives of much later periods into the opening.

### 8.5 Holland and the Norse Maritime World

North Sea interaction may create trade, conflict, transport, mercenary, diplomatic, or technological relationships. It does not turn Holland into a Norse roster or grant Norse maritime capabilities without providers and access.

## 11. Government, Economy, and Institutional Experience Boundary

Holland's government and economy must be felt through concrete capacity rather than abstract faction bonuses.

The player should experience:

- household reach and limitations;
- appointments and relationships needed to transmit authority;
- contested collection and service obligations;
- dependence on roads, waterways, ferries, settlements, and safe storage;
- church, monastic, town, port, market, and landholder relationships;
- costs attached to protection, war, rebuilding, fleets, specialists, and fortifications;
- improvement that follows secure routes, cooperation, administration, and investment.

This authority does not assign exact revenues, offices, building effects, settlement ownership, or administrative payloads. Those remain specialist responsibilities.

## 13. Player-Facing Presentation Direction

Holland's visual and textual presentation should make the realm legible before it makes the realm grand.

### 13.1 Opening Presentation

Opening presentation should emphasize:

- a modest but legitimate comital household;
- vulnerable connections across water and difficult ground;
- charters, seals, messengers, household councils, ferries, boats, guarded crossings, toll points, and fortified local centers;
- the visible competition between comital and episcopal authority;
- an Early Era material culture without later national projection;
- a realm that has resources and agency but lacks secure concentration.

### 13.2 Development Presentation

Visual change should follow actual development:

```yaml
Early:
  visual_language: FragmentedVulnerableLocalAndHouseholdCentered
  infrastructure: FerriesLocalCraftLimitedStrongpointsAndUnevenRoutes
High:
  visual_language: ConsolidatedConnectedAdministrativeAndDefensible
  infrastructure: StrongerPortsMarketsCrossingsStoresFortificationsAndComitalPresence
Late:
  visual_language: SpecializedUrbanMaritimeFiscalAndMilitarilyModernizing
  infrastructure: DevelopedPortsWorkshopsShipSupportArtillerySitesAndOrganizedLogisticsWhereEarned
```

### 13.3 Prohibited Visual Shortcuts

- later Dutch Republic flags, civic symbolism, or state identity at opening;
- vast mature cities unsupported by the campaign state;
- a sophisticated standing navy before crews, ships, ports, stores, and command exist;
- artillery or handguns before chronology and material development permit them;
- generic Viking styling used to communicate any North Sea connection;
- invented named courtiers placed into canonical opening scenes;
- a regent represented as a routine battlefield general;
- visual claims of ownership over wider Frisia.

## 16. Historical and Alternate Outcome Space

The historical path should remain recognizable as a strong possibility: minority regency, external protection, Utrecht pressure, adverse imperial support, territorial struggle, wider political realignment, and eventual recovery under favorable conditions. It must not become a guaranteed chain.

Authorized outcome families include:

- a durable compact county that settles with Utrecht;
- a restored county that recovers disputed access and claims;
- a Flemish-protected but constrained Holland;
- an imperially accommodated Holland;
- a reform-aligned Holland using wider conflict to resist Utrecht;
- a reduced county preserving its dynasty and future claim;
- a wider Frisian coalition built through negotiated relationships;
- a commercially and maritimely strong small realm;
- an expanded regional power strained by resistance and administration;
- an Utrecht victory in which Holland survives only as a displaced or reduced claim, where supported;
- dynastic or political failure when no authorized continuation remains.

No outcome receives automatic moral or mechanical superiority simply because it resembles later history.

## 18. Acceptance Tests

### 18.1 Positive Fixtures

- The opening campaign communicates that Dirk V's inheritance is valid while practical control remains contested.
- Gertrude governs actively without erasing Dirk's legal position.
- Utrecht can pressure Holland politically, legally, ecclesiastically, locally, and militarily.
- The player can survive without following the historical marriage path.
- A protector can strengthen Holland while reducing autonomy.
- Water obstructs movement until routes, boats, crews, access, and support make it useful.
- Holland can remain compact and still become strategically powerful.
- Late naval or gunpowder capability follows real development and supply.
- Setbacks produce reduced, dependent, contested, or restoration-focused campaigns where continuation remains credible.
- AI Holland changes strategy according to threat, relationships, routes, capacity, and available providers.

### 18.2 Negative Fixtures

- Robert the Frisian automatically controls the opening regency.
- The 1064 imperial confirmations exist on January 1, 1062.
- Frisia is treated as free Holland territory, population, or recruitment.
- Holland receives blanket movement or combat benefits on water and marsh terrain.
- A calendar date grants mature towns, fleets, artillery, or handguns.
- Utrecht functions only as a religiously themed military enemy.
- Gertrude disappears from politics when Dirk reaches a birthday threshold.
- Dirk instantly becomes an experienced ruler or general.
- The player must conquer large territories to make Holland viable.
- Research creates weapons, crews, officers, ships, or recruitment pools without physical inputs.
- Later Dutch national or republican identity appears as opening faction identity.
