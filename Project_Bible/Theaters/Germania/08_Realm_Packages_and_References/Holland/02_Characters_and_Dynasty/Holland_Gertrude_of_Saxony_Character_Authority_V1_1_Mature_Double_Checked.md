Gertrude's death removes the opening government's principal adult but does not erase Dirk, the dynasty, household records, claims, local relationships, or the Realm. It triggers separate decisions for guardian, regent, household keeper, military protector, and diplomatic representation.

## 29. Development Portfolio
### 29.1 The Secure Regent
**Conditions:** protected household, working routes, recognized delegates, bounded Utrecht pressure.  
**Benefits:** stronger government continuity and a prepared Dirk.  
**Risk:** overreliance on Gertrude may delay independent institutions.

### 29.2 The Billung Diplomatic Regent
**Conditions:** communication, kinship access, and useful Saxon political opportunity.  
**Benefits:** mediation, refuge, recognition, or alternate alliance pathways.  
**Risk:** distant commitments and expectations without dependable material support.

### 29.3 The Flemish Protector Partnership
**Conditions:** valid marriage or agreement and negotiated authority.  
**Benefits:** military protection and political depth.  
**Risk:** Holland loses autonomy or becomes subordinate to Flemish priorities.

### 29.4 The Independent Household Governor
**Conditions:** secure revenue, delegates, routes, and local adherence.  
**Benefits:** reduced protector dependence.  
**Risk:** large domestic burden on a small contested realm.

### 29.5 The Regency in Exile
**Conditions:** territorial loss with surviving dynasty, records, supporters, and refuge.  
**Benefits:** claim continuity and future recovery.  
**Risk:** dependency, information loss, and erosion of local adherence.

### 29.6 The Overextended Regent
**Conditions:** simultaneous war, custody, diplomacy, remarriage, and local crises.  
**Consequences:** delayed decisions, competing orders, household insecurity, and protector opportunity. Recovery requires delegation repair, narrowed commitments, refuge, or settlement.
