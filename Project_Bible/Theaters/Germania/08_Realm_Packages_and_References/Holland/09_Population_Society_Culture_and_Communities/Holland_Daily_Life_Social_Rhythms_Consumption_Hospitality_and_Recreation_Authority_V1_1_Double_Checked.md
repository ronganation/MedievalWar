## 1. Authority Purpose

This authority defines the everyday social life of Holland: household consumption, meals and stores, work and rest rhythms, hospitality, travel support, market-day activity, communal gathering, recreation, seasonal preparation, life-course participation, and the way ordinary routines respond to war, displacement, scarcity, prosperity, settlement growth, and institutional change.

It supplies the missing lived-experience layer between population systems and campaign presentation. It does not turn daily life into decorative flavor detached from economy, households, routes, institutions, or chronology. It also does not invent a universal daily schedule, exact diet, complete festival calendar, named entertainer, standardized leisure practice, or later Dutch civic culture for 1062.

## 31. Acceptance Tests

### 31.1 Positive Fixtures

- A household hosts a messenger by diverting food, space, and labor from other uses.
- A market remains socially active only when routes, goods, providers, security, and recognized practice function.
- Winter changes household and route priorities without imposing one identical schedule across Holland.
- A recovering provider resumes limited household and market participation before the former routine fully returns.
- A prosperous port supports greater hospitality and material choice only because real trade, housing, services, and providers exist.
- A community gathering coordinates shared work without proving unanimous political consent.
- Displaced households preserve different routines and needs rather than becoming one anonymous population category.
- Recreation and sociability appear as lived activity without generating automatic loyalty or morale.
- A household enters a compressed routine because care, service, maintenance, and livelihood compete for the same time.
- A guest stay is extended only after the host burden and available support are reviewed.
- A recurring market gathering remains distinct from an emergency relief assembly in frequency, purpose, participants, and providers.
- Seasonal preparation consumes resources before the season changes rather than appearing as a free modifier.
- Normal routine returns gradually after war because housing, providers, routes, goods, and institutions recover at different rates.
- Material wear reduces everyday capacity until real repair or replacement inputs are available.

### 31.2 Negative Fixtures

- One exact daily schedule applies to every household.
- A feast or festival appears without food, labor, space, providers, authority, or chronology.
- Market or tavern-style scenery creates a functioning community economy.
- Recreation grants automatic public-order or loyalty bonuses.
- Later Dutch national folklore is treated as opening culture.
- Every household consumes the same goods in the same quantity.
- Hosting travelers, troops, or displaced people has no capacity cost.
- A repaired building automatically restores normal daily life.
- Exact recipes, games, festival dates, performers, or customs are invented.
- Elite display is treated as representative of the whole population.
- A household performs work, care, service, maintenance, travel, and recreation at full capacity simultaneously.
- A guest automatically becomes a permanent resident or integrated community member.
- One gathering type is used for markets, petitions, religious activity, relief, and recreation without distinction.
- Seasonal preparation has no labor or material cost.
- Building repair instantly restores ordinary routine.
- Wealth or research automatically replaces worn household goods, tools, boats, or travel equipment.
