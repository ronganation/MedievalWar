## 1. Authority Purpose

This authority defines Holland's population change across life course, household formation, dependency, adulthood, work, care, aging, generational replacement, migration, displacement, mortality, return, and recovery. It connects biological progression to the three-track campaign model without converting population into a single growth rate or treating calendar advancement as automatic social, economic, or military capacity.

The authority distinguishes the passage of biological time from historical development and seasonal campaign operation. It controls population-level doctrine only. Named characters, births, deaths, marriages, succession, offices, and personal relationships remain owned by the Character and Dynasty portfolio.

This file creates no exact opening population, age structure, household formation rate, fertility rate, mortality rate, life expectancy, dependency ratio, generation boundary, population projection, stable identifier, or runtime simulation.

## 31. Acceptance Tests

### 31.1 Positive Fixtures

- An underage cohort advances biologically without instantly becoming trained adult providers.
- Population headcount rises after return, while housing, care, livelihood, and provider recovery remain incomplete.
- Loss of an experienced ferry provider creates a long replacement lag despite available younger population.
- A household splits only when actual people, housing, livelihood, and property conditions support two units.
- Military demand reduces available adult labor and slows household, economic, and provider recovery.
- Older participants retain knowledge, property, community, and institutional functions without a universal incapacity rule.
- Historical availability advances faster than biological aging without changing the seven-month biological track.
- A recovered settlement supports population only when housing, services, livelihood, routes, and security exist.
- An aging cohort becomes eligible for training but does not become a skilled provider until practice, support, tools, and a role exist.
- Population returns to a district while recovery debt keeps provider, housing, and recruitment capacity below the earlier level.
- Internal relocation changes regional population without increasing Holland’s total population.
- A missing group remains unresolved until evidence supports return, relocation, or permanent loss.
- A demographic projection presents several assumption-based ranges rather than one asserted future total.
- A large young cohort fails to replace a scarce provider because mentors, tools, and livelihood are missing.

### 31.2 Negative Fixtures

- Historical progression ages the population by twenty-one months per strategic turn.
- Season changes a character's biological age.
- Birth creates immediate labor, revenue, or recruitment capacity.
- Every person reaching an age threshold becomes a skilled adult provider.
- Buildings or technology create population growth directly.
- Population is spent directly as army manpower.
- Missing people are automatically marked deceased.
- One dependency ratio determines every household and community outcome.
- Exact fertility, mortality, life expectancy, or population-growth rates are invented.
- Named characters are fabricated to fill generational gaps.
- Internal movement is counted as population growth.
- Marriage automatically produces births or a new household.
- Missing or in-transit people are promoted to permanent loss to balance a ledger.
- A population projection is presented as a verified future fact.
- Headcount recovery erases accumulated training, housing, care, and institutional debt.
- A large cohort automatically replaces scarce specialists without mentors or tools.
