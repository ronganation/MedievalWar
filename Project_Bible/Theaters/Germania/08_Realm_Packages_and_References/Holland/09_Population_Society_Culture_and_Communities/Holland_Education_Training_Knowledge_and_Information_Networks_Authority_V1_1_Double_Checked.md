## 1. Authority Purpose

This authority defines how people in Holland acquire, preserve, transmit, lose, adapt, and apply knowledge through households, communities, work, apprenticeship, institutions, written records, oral memory, travel, markets, ports, messengers, diplomacy, military service, and local practice.

It separates education from universal schooling, literacy from general intelligence, research from living expertise, information from truth, and a written record from effective communication or implementation. Holland may develop stronger administrative, maritime, commercial, craft, military, and institutional capacity, but only when real people, teachers, learners, tools, texts, routes, livelihoods, and organizations support that development.

This authority creates no exact opening school, curriculum, literacy percentage, named teacher, training duration, knowledge score, information-network map, stable identifier, or implementation payload.

## 33. Acceptance Tests

### 33.1 Positive Fixtures

- A skilled pilot trains a learner while reducing immediately available transport capacity.
- An imported craft specialist performs work but does not create a local provider network until training, livelihood, tools, and institutional support develop.
- A written agreement requires a qualified reader or interpreter and still depends on recognition and enforcement.
- A captured settlement does not automatically provide the conqueror with channels, customs, providers, and local records.
- A displaced scribe retains knowledge but lacks materials, records, livelihood, and institutional authority to resume the former function.
- A reported route condition remains uncertain until corroborated by a current source.
- Household and community practice preserve knowledge after one building is lost.
- High and Late knowledge capacity develops through people, records, institutions, markets, ports, routes, and repeated practice.
- A written boatbuilding description survives, but practical production remains limited because tacit skill, tools, materials, and supervised practice are missing.
- One experienced mentor trains only a bounded number of learners while sacrificing some current output.
- A copied charter reaches Holland but remains disputed until origin, validity, interpretation, and authority are reviewed.
- An urgent message travels faster only because a real messenger, route, support, and political priority are assigned.
- A limited experiment fails without granting a technological unlock, while preserving an evidence record for later adaptation.
- A correct report reaches the government, but execution still fails because the order, personnel, tools, or route are missing.

### 33.2 Negative Fixtures

- A research unlock creates trained providers, officers, scribes, pilots, or craftspeople.
- A library, school, church, workshop, or administrative building creates knowledge without people and practice.
- Literacy is represented as one exact Realm-wide percentage without evidence.
- A written record is treated as automatically true, understood, accepted, and enforced.
- Capturing territory transfers all local knowledge.
- Information arrives instantly without route, messenger, delay, or intermediary.
- One language label grants equal speaking, reading, writing, and interpreting ability.
- Books or records preserve operational skill without living practice.
- Exact training durations, curricula, institutions, and named teachers are invented.
- Later mass schooling, universities, bureaucracy, or standardized education is projected into 1062.
- One written text fully replaces tacit craft or local knowledge.
- One mentor trains unlimited learners without opportunity cost.
- Completion of training grants universal mastery beyond the assessed tasks.
- A copied record is assumed authentic, current, understood, and binding.
- A secret message is immune to delay, loss, interception, or disclosure.
- Information automatically becomes a decision, an order, and a completed action.
- A failed experiment grants capability because resources were spent.
