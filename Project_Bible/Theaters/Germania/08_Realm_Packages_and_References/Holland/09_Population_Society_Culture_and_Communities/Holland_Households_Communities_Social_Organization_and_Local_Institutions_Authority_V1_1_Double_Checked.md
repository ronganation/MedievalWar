## 1. Authority Purpose

This authority defines how Holland's households, communities, local leaders, provider networks, customary bodies, settlement groups, market and port communities, ecclesiastical communities, and comital dependents organize social life and convert population into workable collective capacity.

It separates a household from a dynasty, a community from a settlement polygon, representation from unanimous consent, and a local institution from a building. It establishes how communities coordinate labor, protection, relief, maintenance, transport, markets, petitions, obligations, local knowledge, and recovery without inventing named officers or reducing society to rigid classes.

This authority does not assign exact opening households, community populations, named representatives, social ranks, local offices, institutional memberships, settlement bindings, stable identifiers, or runtime records.

## 36. Acceptance Tests

### 36.1 Positive Fixtures

- A ferry community coordinates transport and maintenance without becoming a faction unit or permanent political bloc.
- A representative negotiates a labor obligation without proving unanimous household consent.
- A market building remains ineffective until traders, handlers, routes, customs, and security sustain a market community.
- An ecclesiastical institution provides relief while remaining externally institution-owned.
- A household loses an adult provider and reorganizes care, labor, service, and livelihood instead of disappearing.
- Shared route repair succeeds only when labor, materials, burden allocation, access, and maintenance responsibility align.
- A community contains competing factions that cooperate on defense while disputing property or tolls.
- Recovery restores households, providers, institutions, and collective functions at different rates.
- A household meets a transport obligation by reducing other work instead of generating additional free labor.
- A representative has authority over route repair but not property settlement or military service.
- A market institution survives loss of one structure through people, custom, records, and an alternate site.
- A temporary flood-response group dissolves after the emergency instead of becoming a permanent office automatically.
- A patronage relationship becomes strained when promised protection fails, without implying a private emotional state.
- A community splits after displacement while successor groups retain overlapping membership and claims.

### 36.2 Negative Fixtures

- Every settlement automatically has a unified community.
- A building creates an institution or provider body.
- A named local leader is invented because a record requires representation.
- A representative is treated as owning the community or controlling every member.
- Households provide unlimited labor, care, military service, and taxes simultaneously.
- Shared works create free labor or materials.
- Rural communities are treated as culturally and politically identical.
- Patronage creates permanent loyalty or ownership of dependents.
- Community membership proves participation in resistance or crime.
- Later municipal, corporate, or hydraulic institutions are projected into 1062 without evidence.
- A representative receives unlimited authority because the community accepted one earlier agreement.
- Care labor and dependent support are omitted so every adult can be counted as productive or military labor.
- A temporary delegation automatically becomes a permanent institution.
- One building loss is treated as automatic dissolution of the institution.
- Institutional abuse is asserted without evidence or used to infer intent.
- Community merger erases prior property, identity, obligations, and disputes.
