## 1. Authority Purpose

This authority defines Holland's language use, cultural identities, customary practices, social memory, naming and representation principles, cultural contact, selective adoption, accommodation, continuity, and long-term change. It develops the cultural layer of the population and society portfolio without reducing culture to a meter, a static ethnicity label, a binary fork, or an automatic source of loyalty and military bonuses.

The player-facing title County of Holland and the internal design clarification West-Frisian comital realm must remain legible without projecting a uniform later Dutch nation backward into 1062. Holland's society may share practices and connections with wider Frisian, Saxon, Flemish, ecclesiastical, imperial, maritime, and regional worlds while retaining a distinctive comital identity shaped by local communities, dynastic authority, routes, institutions, and campaign history.

This authority defines doctrine and presentation requirements. It does not assign exact opening language percentages, cultural population percentages, dialect maps, customary jurisdictions, localized strings, voice casting, names, stable identifiers, or implementation payloads.

## 29. Acceptance Tests

### 29.1 Positive Fixtures

- A market intermediary enables communication across language contexts without making every participant multilingual.
- Holland preserves a local custom while adapting an external administrative practice.
- Shared Frisian identity supports contact while political adherence remains divided.
- A written agreement exists but requires clerical or institutional providers to interpret and enforce it.
- A Flemish or Saxon practice is selectively adopted without turning Holland into a Flemish or Saxon reskin.
- A community retains distinct practice while participating in Holland's political and economic systems.
- Cultural knowledge declines after provider displacement and recovers through return, training, or institutional restoration.
- High and Late cultural complexity emerges through routes, institutions, literacy, markets, ports, and repeated contact.
- A formulaic administrative phrase enables limited official use without making the speaker broadly fluent.
- A local customary practice survives a new comital order through negotiated exception and continued community support.
- An imported object becomes locally valued without proving local manufacture or universal identity.
- Physical reconstruction finishes before displaced cultural providers and institutional practice fully return.
- A promoted comital identity gains wider recognition without erasing local and institutional identities.

### 29.2 Negative Fixtures

- One culture percentage determines loyalty, revolt risk, recruitment, and military performance.
- Later standardized Dutch is uniformly spoken and written across opening Holland.
- Wider Frisian culture is treated as Holland-owned.
- A research unlock creates translators, scribes, craftspeople, or institutional practice.
- Conquest instantly assimilates a community.
- Language difference automatically creates hostility.
- Shared culture automatically creates alliance or political obedience.
- External influence replaces Holland's canonical identity.
- Every settlement follows the same cultural-development path.
- A costume or concept image is used as proof of exact identity or language distribution.
- One language label grants equal speaking, reading, writing, and interpreting competence.
- A written comital order automatically erases every conflicting custom.
- One elite artifact is treated as evidence of universal material culture.
- Patronage instantly changes the identity of a community.
- Contact spreads beyond the people, routes, institutions, and providers that actually sustain it.
- Physical rebuilding automatically restores cultural practice and memory.
