## 1. Authority Purpose

This authority defines Holland's population, households, communities, social organization, cultural identities, languages, customary practices, local knowledge, movement of people, social providers, and long-term societal change. It establishes how people sustain the Realm's government, production, recruitment, transport, institutions, settlement growth, recovery, and cultural continuity without reducing society to a single population number or a universal culture percentage.

Holland's opening society must be represented as locally rooted, interconnected, politically contested, and dependent upon households, communities, institutions, routes, and customary relationships. The player-facing name County of Holland and the internal clarification West-Frisian comital realm do not authorize automatic ownership of wider Frisia or a fully formed later Dutch national identity.

This authority creates no exact opening population count, household inventory, class distribution, language percentage, migration payload, named community leader, stable identifier, or Unreal representation.

## 35. Acceptance Tests

### 35.1 Positive Fixtures

- The same ferry community supports transport, fishing, local knowledge, and limited military movement but cannot perform all roles at full capacity simultaneously.
- Holland recruits from a provider pool without converting the total population directly into soldiers.
- A displaced household retains property claims, skills, needs, and relationships rather than becoming free labor.
- A market settlement grows only when housing, food, work, routes, institutions, and security can support additional people.
- Local communities share cultural practices while differing in political adherence and institutional alignment.
- A captured wetland community withholds local knowledge, reducing the conqueror's practical capability.
- Women remain visible in household, economic, dynastic, patronage, diplomatic, and institutional life without routine battlefield-general treatment.
- High and Late social complexity emerges through population, routes, institutions, specialization, and campaign development.
- A temporarily present merchant community increases exchange without becoming permanent resident population.
- Replacing lost boat crews restores headcount before full pilotage knowledge and operational skill.
- A community representative negotiates an obligation without proving unanimous individual consent.
- Seasonal construction demand reduces available agricultural, transport, or military labor instead of drawing from a separate pool.
- Relief reaches one isolated community only when resources, routes, providers, and administrative attention exist.

### 35.2 Negative Fixtures

- Holland receives a precise opening population without evidence and calibration.
- Every person in a claimed region is counted as loyal Holland population.
- Shared West-Frisian or Frisian identity grants automatic adherence or recruitment.
- A building creates households, migrants, craftspeople, or sailors.
- Research instantly creates trained providers.
- Population is deducted directly as army manpower using the Rome: Total War model.
- Language percentages are invented to complete a payload.
- Later standardized Dutch language and national identity appear uniformly in 1062.
- Migration occurs without cause, route, destination capacity, livelihood, or security.
- Community membership proves individual participation in resistance or crime.
- Cultural difference automatically produces revolt or military penalties.
- Temporary visitors are counted as permanent residents and recruitment sources.
- One person is simultaneously counted at full capacity in production, construction, transport, and military service.
- A research unlock instantly replaces experienced pilots, builders, scribes, or craftspeople.
- A community representative is treated as proof that every household agrees.
- Literacy is represented as one precise realm-wide percentage without evidence and context.
- Relief or hospitality creates unlimited food, shelter, care, or institutional capacity.
