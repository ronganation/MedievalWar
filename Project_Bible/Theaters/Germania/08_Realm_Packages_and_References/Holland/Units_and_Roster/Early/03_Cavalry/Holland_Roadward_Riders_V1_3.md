## 1. Direct Design Verdict

**Roadward Riders are Holland's low-tier Early light cavalry: dispersed but organized files of locally available combat riders mounted on suitable light horses, trained to observe routes, screen marches, challenge hostile scouts, carry useful battlefield reports, harry isolated light troops, and pursue broken enemies without entering serious line combat.**

The separate card survives only if it remains materially distinct from Graaflijke Ruiters:

- Roadward Riders provide breadth, information, patrol coverage, and low-risk mobility.
- Graaflijke Ruiters provide household loyalty, command security, stronger cohesion, crisis response, and a higher combat ceiling.

## 2. Thirty-Second Player Read

- **What are they?** Holland's ordinary Early light horse.
- **What do they do?** Scout, screen, patrol, report, chase skirmishers, and pursue broken troops.
- **Why recruit them?** They give an army information and mobile coverage without spending scarce household cavalry.
- **What are they bad at?** Any prolonged fight, formed infantry, prepared spears, serious cavalry, dense missile fire, and bad ground.
- **How do they win?** See first, report clearly, deny enemy scouts, choose only safe contacts, and return before the real battle traps them.
- **How do they fail?** They scatter, chase too far, return with stale or contradictory reports, exhaust their horses, or mistake movement for combat power.

> **The first riders see the road. The best ones return with the truth.**

## 12. Complete Battle Cycle

1. **Brief:** Receive the route, observation zone, prohibited contacts, rally point, and report priority.
2. **Ride Ahead:** Establish the outer mounted screen without losing the army.
3. **Observe:** Read routes, ground, dust, movement, numbers only approximately, and cavalry danger.
4. **Classify:** Distinguish routine movement, scouts, raiders, skirmishers, cavalry, infantry columns, and major formations.
5. **Report:** Carry useful information through physical riders, signals, routes, and receiving command.
6. **Screen:** Deny hostile scouts and protect Holland's deployment or march.
7. **Shadow:** Follow stronger forces while staying outside decisive contact.
8. **Intercept:** Catch a messenger, scout, or isolated skirmisher only when the exit remains open.
9. **Refuse:** Yield ground rather than fight formed infantry or stronger cavalry.
10. **Pursue:** Chase a broken target for a bounded distance.
11. **Cover:** Threaten pursuers and protect friendly withdrawal.
12. **Rally:** Reconnect files and account for riders, horses, tack, reports, and wounded.
13. **Return:** Restore local patrol, messenger connections, and provider obligations.

## 18. Contact Rules

Contact is authorized only when:

- the target is weak, isolated, exposed, disordered, or already broken;
- the report or screen mission will not be lost;
- the return route remains open;
- stronger enemy cavalry or infantry cannot close quickly;
- Patrol Order and Horse Endurance remain sufficient.

The company should refuse contact even at the cost of yielding ground when these conditions fail.

## 20. Route and Terrain Doctrine

### Favorable

- firm roads;
- dry causeways;
- open settlement approaches;
- broad field edges;
- dry banks with escape space;
- visible ridges and open ground.

### Conditional

- light woodland tracks;
- firm floodplain;
- secured ferry and bridge approaches;
- broken ground with known exits.

### Unfavorable

- deep mud;
- standing water;
- dense woodland;
- narrow congested crossings;
- ditches, hedges, and enclosed fields;
- spear- or pike-controlled lanes.

Route knowledge improves planning. It does not alter physics or grant immunity.

## 21. Weather and Time

- Fog reduces report quality and file connection.
- Rain damages visibility, tack reliability, and ground.
- Wind affects signals and dust interpretation.
- Darkness narrows observation and increases route error.
- Flooding may close the company's own road or rally route.
- Heat, cold, and prolonged weather exposure affect riders and horses.

No universal night vision or weather immunity exists.

## 38P. Anti-Raider and Baggage Protection

Roadward Riders may defeat small raiding parties only when the raiders are dispersed, lightly protected, and unable to anchor against formed support.

Their proper anti-raider sequence is:

1. Locate the threat.
2. Protect the route or vulnerable asset.
3. Threaten the raiders' escape.
4. Close only where the target remains weak.
5. Drive the raiders away or hold them until proper troops arrive.
6. Stop before entering enclosed or prepared ground.

## 38S. Retreat and Survival Doctrine

Roadward Riders distinguish between:

- **Refusal:** decline contact before impact;
- **Breakaway:** leave a developing fight under command;
- **Screened withdrawal:** protect friendly troops while retiring by bounds;
- **scatter:** preserve individuals when company order collapses;
- **surrender:** possible when riders are enclosed and horses cannot escape;
- **regroup:** reform at the named marker or supporting formation.

A scattered company may preserve riders but loses its current report, screening, pursuit, and coordinated-contact value.

## 42. Encyclopedia Copy

**Roadward Riders** range ahead of Holland's armies along roads, banks, and firm approaches. They are not knights and should not be trusted in a serious cavalry battle. ## 43. Failure Stories

1. **The Stale Report:** A relay arrives after the enemy has changed route, causing Holland to deploy against empty ground.
2. **The Scout Fight:** The company chases enemy scouts too far and misses the main column.
3. **The Closed Road:** Flooding or enemy movement cuts the return route and leaves the riders isolated.
4. **The False Weakness:** Exposed skirmishers draw the intercept files into concealed spears or stronger cavalry.
5. **The Long Pursuit:** Broken enemies lure the company beyond its rally point and exhaust the horses.
6. **The Spent Screen:** Repeated patrol and relay work leaves the riders unable to cover Holland's withdrawal.
7. **The Double Booking:** Messenger or household mounts are silently reused, creating phantom cavalry and broken communication.
8. **The Cheap Medium Horse:** Balance inflation gives the unit enough shock to replace Market Riders.

## 60. Mature Battle Loop Contracts

### Range

Range requires an assigned sector, actual line of sight, an open communication route, sufficient horse condition, and a known friendly reference. Ranging too far improves warning distance but increases capture, stale-report, and return risk.

### Screen

Screening denies easy enemy observation or harassment through physical presence, spacing, threat, and interception potential. It is not an invisible vision aura and does not guarantee detection.

### Turn

Turning shifts the company toward one real threat, exposed target, or open route. It consumes order and may uncover the former sector.

### Pursue

Pursuit attacks a genuinely broken, scattered, or isolated target. Officers preserve a return relationship and stop before the enemy reserve, bad ground, or horse exhaustion closes the road.

### Return

Return requires physical disengagement, signals, surviving leaders, usable horses, and an open route. It is not an instant escape.

## 76. Mature Failure Stories

**The empty road:** riders range too far from the host. The report is accurate but arrives after the enemy has already turned the flank.

**The light-horse duel:** the player mistakes comparable enemy scouts for an easy target. Both forces become entangled while stronger cavalry closes.

**The abandoned missile line:** Roadward Riders pursue skirmishers and leave Reedbank or Tideway missile troops without withdrawal cover.

**The double-booked horse:** messenger, household, and Roadward assignments claim the same riders and mounts, creating paper companies and broken communication.

**The road-made cavalry:** a road building or patrol decision is treated as if it creates riders, horses, tack, officers, weapons, and collective practice.

**The cheap Market Riders:** veterancy and equipment inflation give the company medium charge power and erase the later Market Rider purchase.

**The brave mistake:** morale is interpreted as willingness to remain in melee. The company loses its screen and pursuit function against infantry it should have refused.

**The paper recovery:** rider numbers return, but suitable horses, tack, leaders, route practice, fodder, and provider trust remain damaged.
