## 1. Definitive campaign promise

**Hold the frontier; decide what survives.** The Sword Brethren offer a continuing institutional force where temporary expeditions cannot sustain every obligation. Their strategic problem is to turn military presence into supportable holdings without outrunning personnel, access, political consent and local control. Their successor problem is harder: preserve actual survivors and obligations while negotiating a different institutional future.

The proposed campaign rhythm is **Sponsor, Establish, Hold, Bargain, Overextend or Consolidate, Reorganize, Settle**. Military success can create new burdens; military disaster can open a succession bargain without guaranteeing it. Remaining independent, accepting incorporation, reforming, withdrawing or disappearing are distinct possible outcomes.

The later Livonian branch is not a second free army unlocked by a date. It is an institutional outcome with actual members, rights, assets, debts, commands and unresolved claims. Its relationship to the wider Teutonic institution must be meaningful without making it an ordinary Prussian province or an unrelated recolored faction.

## 4. Emergence prerequisites and alternate outcomes

The proposed emergence chain requires a durable frontier mission, recognizable leadership and membership, competent sponsorship, institutional rule and recognition, actual support, access to a viable operating location, and a credible continuing military purpose. A crusade call or bishop's request alone is insufficient.

Recognition does not create trained members. A donated site does not create its garrison. A recruited group does not automatically form a disciplined institution. Founding commitments need actual sources for people, equipment, animals, maintenance and command.

Possible outcomes include no emergence, delayed emergence, a limited nonterritorial institution, a sustainable frontier establishment, disputed expansion, negotiated reform, incorporation, withdrawal or collapse. These are authored possibilities, not equal-probability outcomes. If the Sword Brethren never exist, a later Teutonic frontier branch can arise only through its own valid development, not by inheriting an imaginary predecessor.

## 7. Frontier bargain and competing authorities

H1 connects the foundation to ecclesiastical sponsorship; H3 describes a region with multiple power holders. The authored campaign should therefore use directed bargains, not a universal Christian alliance.

A grant can convey use, revenue, custody, jurisdiction or a claim without conveying all of them. The grantor must possess the right offered. An institutional claim, papal recognition, local possession and military occupation may disagree. The interface should preserve that disagreement.

Useful negotiations concern garrison service, protection, campaign participation, disputed property, access, revenue and command. A bishop can request service without owning every member. A town can cooperate on supplies while resisting a jurisdiction claim. Shared religion does not guarantee obedience, and conflict does not automatically imply total war.

## 8. Territorialization and population

Military occupation begins a holding problem, not instant sovereignty. Resolve surviving forces, custody, damage, stores, local authorities, property, population obligations, garrison requirements and continuing access. A captured site can be tactically held while politically contested or operationally isolated.

The authored territorial progression is conditional: limited presence, supported custody, actual holdings, workable administration and only then an admitted political expression if the relevant owners approve. It is not a mandatory ladder or a new building chain.

Local populations remain active sources of production, knowledge, negotiation, resistance and military relationships through their own owners. Conquest does not convert everyone, erase existing elites, grant perfect intelligence or create instant loyal recruits. Conversion, legal submission, taxation and military service are separate processes.

H1 describes forced conversion and brutal tactics with reprimands. The design must retain coercion, civilian harm, displacement, damaged support and legitimacy disputes as possible material consequences through existing conduct and governance owners. It must not present conquest as cost-free administrative modernization or award automatic rewards for abuse.

## 9. Institutional government and continuity

H1 supports a master, general assembly and district-castle organization. The authored governance model separates institutional leadership, chapter deliberation, local custody, field command and external negotiation. Exact appointment and voting rules beyond the source remain pending.

Leadership death can interrupt authority without deleting members, property or obligations. Temporary delegation should identify its scope, communication basis and review or expiry. A field commander cannot silently dispose of all institutional estates to secure a battlefield bargain.

Political disagreements can concern expansion, consolidation, sponsorship, local privileges, military risk and incorporation terms. They should produce concrete decisions and resource consequences rather than a universal loyalty meter. Ordinary officers remain aggregate unless a named character has a justified strategic role. No historical character authority is created here.

## 10. Finite membership and military sources

Separate professed armed members, other institutional personnel, employees, local forces, temporary crusaders, allied contingents and hired companies. Service alongside the Order does not confer membership. A subject levy is not an Order brother; a mercenary remains contract-bound to its own source.

Membership requires actual eligible candidates, admission, preparation, equipment and assignment. A donation or recognition cannot refill a formation. The same member cannot remain available in a home garrison and a distant army simultaneously.

Recruitment should use the project's finite pool approach. Population informs and constrains source capacity without turning every recruited soldier into direct Rome-style settlement subtraction. Ordinary safe recruitment is not automatically a major economic penalty; extraordinary mobilization, losses and disrupted work produce pressure through the appropriate owners.

No knight, sergeant, spear, crossbow, garrison or auxiliary card is created. A future card needs a useful battlefield identity and clear source, equipment, service, recovery and AI behavior. Shared-source cards may be valid where the project admits them; rename-only duplication is not.

## 11. Campaign operations and support

The proposed operational loop is assemble, verify, advance, hold, relieve, return and settle. A viable expedition needs actual forces, route access, supplies, transport, information and a return or closure plan. It must expose what remains to protect existing sites.

A castle is not a supply aura. Assess its usable stores, staff, defenders, condition, access and wider connection separately. A stocked isolated site may hold out without supporting an advancing army. A politically accessible site may lack fodder or accommodation.

Terrain and seasonal conditions come from actual map and environment owners. Do not grant the institution generic forest mastery, winter immunity or knowledge of every crossing. Historical, biological and campaign-season clocks remain separate; no extra movement, recovery or harvest cycle is inferred from the historical months per turn.

Naval transport requires real ships, crews, contracts and ports. Germania support does not create a fleet or teleport personnel to Livonia. No exact route graph or site network is instantiated.

## 12. Overextension, defeat and crisis diagnosis

H1's Saule account anchors a severe historical military crisis. The authored crisis model responds to actual losses and obligations rather than a mandatory named battle. Relevant causes include depleted cadre, command loss, exposed sites, unavailable mounts, arrears, disrupted access and sponsor conflict.

A lost battle is not proof that every member died. Resolve fit survivors, wounded, missing, captured and dead personnel; surviving animals and equipment; consumed stores; and outstanding missions. No fixed historical casualty total is imported.

Crisis options include consolidation, actual reinforcement, renegotiated obligations, relief, withdrawal, reform, incorporation talks or dissolution. The AI should not always choose merger because a defeat flag exists. A superficially rich institution may be unable to hold its sites; a battered one may survive through a narrower mission and valid support.

## 15. Transfer ledger and conservation

## 16. Staged transition and failure handling

The proposed transition sequence is assess, negotiate, approve, reserve, transfer, reconcile and close. Each stage preserves the actual campaign state. Negotiations can fail without deleting the predecessor; partial transfer can leave unresolved obligations without duplicating assets.

Pre-commit validation failure releases unspent candidate reservations. After actual movement, spending or transfer, cancellation must preserve consumed goods, casualties, custody and valid completed acts. Rollback of a design candidate is not reversal of an in-world event.

A contested asset stays contested. The receiver may accept a limited portfolio while refusing unsupported burdens. Remaining predecessor functions can continue only through actual surviving authority and capacity. Completion is not declared until each material item has a recipient, retained owner, unresolved claim or other explicit disposition.

No live migration, transaction executor or importer is implemented. The contract is a semantic requirement for existing institutional, property, personnel and persistence owners.

## 17. Livonian branch operations after incorporation

The successor's proposed campaign rhythm is receive, stabilize, reconcile, relieve, consolidate and act within its mandate. Its first burden is not expansion but making inherited holdings and obligations workable.

Regional command must distinguish branch forces, reinforcements actually sent from elsewhere, local allies, subjects and hired forces. The wider institution can request service, but physical movement and existing duties constrain compliance. A force sent north is not simultaneously available in Prussia or another Theater.

Regional disputes with bishops, towns or neighbors survive incorporation unless validly amended. A change of institutional superior does not settle every title or create a unified map. The successor may remain strategically distinct without becoming an unrelated sovereign institution.

Later political independence, secularization or dissolution requires its own world-state and owner decisions. No 1525 or 1561 automatic end-state is implemented; detailed late-era constitutional treatment is outside this first authority.

## 18. Local allies, communities and neighboring powers

The authored frontier must allow negotiation, alliance, refusal, resistance and shifting interests. Local rulers and communities are not interchangeable hostile terrain. Their troops retain actual source, command, service and recovery relationships.

Shared opponents do not create permanent loyalty. A local contingent may have limited terms, a different objective or a valid reason to leave. An Order's demand for further service can create a dispute without automatically generating rebellion.

Neighboring rulers and towns may cooperate commercially while contesting jurisdiction or territory. Danish and other external interests are relationship lanes, not new unit work or predetermined wars. No additional Denmark units are created.

Religion, language or origin alone grants no universal combat bonus, obedience or hostility. Historical religious conflict remains part of the setting, but individual communities' political outcomes follow actual relationships and events.

## 19. Economy, land and institutional support

Separate actual receipts, restricted grants, maintenance, garrison costs, debts, reserves and transferable surplus. A territorial claim is not collected income. A ruined or contested estate cannot support every planned force merely because its nominal title changed.

Holding territory creates administration, protection and access obligations. Overextension can reduce the usable return from a military success. It does not impose a fixed universal penalty or erase ordinary safe recruitment capacity.

Merchants, towns and transport providers retain their own rights and incentives. Institutional recognition is not a universal trade monopoly. Exact taxes, rents, tariffs, land shares and prices remain uncalibrated; no historical fraction is generalized into every conquest settlement.

The player should see the principal tradeoff: maintain the frontier, fund a field force, repair a site, settle a debt or support a transition. Routine transactions remain aggregated.

## 21. AI, uncertainty and counterplay

Institutional AI should compare mission fit, actual cadre, site obligations, support, political relationships, available information and credible closure. It may prefer a smaller sustainable establishment to nominal expansion, or negotiate incorporation before total military collapse.

Receiving AI evaluates both assets and burdens. It must not accept every merger as free power. It can demand narrower terms, actual custody, resolved claims or a different support arrangement. Exact weights and outcomes are not implemented.

Player and AI use the same reservations, eligibility and information classes. Confirmed, reported, contested, unknown and inapplicable facts remain distinct. A favorable aggregate summary cannot override a known missing garrison or receiving failure.

Counterplay targets actual access, support, field forces, claims and political bargains. Defeating an expedition does not automatically erase the institution; disrupting one route does not reveal every hidden asset. No omniscient merger valuation or guaranteed historical enemy behavior is admitted.

## 22. Worked qualitative situations

These are authored examples, not executed simulations or claims of specific historical incidents.

**The sponsor's army that becomes an institution:** a sponsor offers a site and expects permanent military obedience. The proposed institution accepts only bounded service and needs actual members and support. Negotiate the bargain rather than spawning a complete roster.

**The victory that cannot be held:** an expedition captures a site but leaves its existing garrison dangerously reduced. Choose valid relief, limited custody, a negotiated handover or withdrawal. Occupation is not free consolidation.

**The defeat without a receiver:** survivors seek incorporation, but no Teutonic institution exists in this campaign. Preserve their actual state and pursue another valid path. Do not create the receiver or a Livonian army to satisfy chronology.

**The merger with a contested castle:** people and movable assets can transfer, while a castle remains occupied by a third party. Preserve the claim separately. Do not place the castle in both institutional accounts.

**The survivor who is not ready:** a wounded veteran enters the receiving institution. Membership admission does not restore health, replace a horse or create a full formation. Recovery and integration remain real burdens.

**The branch asked to abandon its frontier:** the wider institution requests troops while local obligations are threatened. Negotiate a smaller contribution, actual relief or a valid refusal. Institutional hierarchy matters without erasing geography.

### 25. Frontier readiness and decisive blockers

The player and AI should inspect separate readiness conditions rather than one Order-power rating:

| Condition | Question | Example blocker |
|---|---|---|
| Institutional | Does a recognized body with competent leadership exist? | No admitted institution, vacancy or disputed authority |
| Personnel | Are eligible members and supporting personnel present and uncommitted? | Cadre depleted, wounded, captive or reserved elsewhere |
| Material | Are equipment, horses, stores and money actually usable? | Missing mounts, damaged equipment or restricted funds |
| Site | Can the holding perform the required military or administrative function? | Damaged works, insufficient defenders or unsuitable premises |
| Access | Can people and supplies legally and physically reach the objective? | Closed route, denied passage or absent transport |
| Political | Are sponsor, ecclesiastical, urban and local relationships sufficient? | Contested grant, jurisdiction dispute or withdrawn support |
| Information | Is the decision based on current and credible knowledge? | Stale report, disputed claim or unknown garrison state |
| Closure | Can the operation return, consolidate, transfer or settle? | No receiver, no retained garrison or unresolved custody |

The conditions may be **confirmed, reported, contested, unknown** or **inapplicable**, as supplied by existing owners. They are not percentages and do not combine into an automatic approval. The UI can summarize the strongest blocker, but optional detail must retain the separate cause and responsible owner.

A disrupted condition narrows the feasible action rather than deleting all capability. A politically disputed castle may still be defensible; a recognized institution may lack a field force; a successful army may lack the capacity to hold its conquest.

### 26. Emergence phases and reversible admission

### 27. Holding, occupation and consolidation contract

A newly gained site should pass through a bounded assessment before it becomes reliable frontier infrastructure:

```yaml
frontier_holding_contract:
  acquisition_basis: Battle surrender grant purchase custody or other admitted source
  current_possession: Actual controlling force and access
  title_and_claims: Recognized disputed retained and third_party interests
  population_and_occupants: Existing people obligations and custody
  condition: Works stores damage and usable functions
  garrison: Actual assigned force command and relief plan
  support: Food fodder equipment money transport and communication
  political_relationships: Sponsor ecclesiastical urban local and neighboring positions
  disposition: Hold repair negotiate transfer abandon or otherwise resolve
  record_status: ConceptualNotProductionSchema
```

Occupation creates responsibilities before benefits. The player may hold a site tactically while lacking title, supplies, local cooperation or long-term administrative capacity. Consolidation requires actual resources and decisions, not elapsed time alone.

Withdrawal must resolve people, prisoners, stores, equipment, records, damaged works and promises. Destruction, confiscation or displacement occurs only when relevant owners resolve those acts; it is not a generic exit cost. A negotiated handover may preserve more value but requires a competent receiver and enforceable terms.

### 28. Crisis severity without a universal collapse score

Crisis should be diagnosed across distinct dimensions: leadership, cadre, formations, sites, access, material support, sponsors, local relationships, legitimacy, information and unresolved obligations. Severe failure in one dimension can be decisive even when the others remain strong.

Suggested descriptive categories are **Strained, Degraded, Acute, Existential** and **Recovering**. They communicate planning urgency but do not replace underlying facts or become automatic succession triggers.

A crisis response must answer:

1. What function has failed or is about to fail?
2. What remains usable and must not be discarded?
3. Which obligations cannot currently be met?
4. What real support, reform, withdrawal or receiver exists?
5. What burden survives whichever option is chosen?

The historical Saule defeat remains one possible crisis anchor, not a mandatory scripted event. A nonbattle crisis may justify reform or incorporation. A battlefield defeat may be survivable when leadership, sites and support remain viable.

### 29. Incorporation negotiation, deadlock and interim governance

Negotiation should expose the interests of predecessor leadership, surviving members, the receiving institution, ecclesiastical sponsors, site holders, towns, local elites and other parties with actual rights. Their consent requirements differ by subject. One party's approval cannot transfer another party's property or obligations.

Possible negotiation states are **Exploratory, Terms Proposed, Conditionally Accepted, Contested, Deadlocked, Approved, Partially Executed, Completed, Rejected**. These are conceptual states only.

Deadlock does not freeze the campaign. Interim governance must identify who commands surviving forces, maintains sites, receives income, answers urgent threats and preserves records while talks continue. Interim authority is bounded and reviewable; it cannot dispose permanently of every asset merely because final terms are unresolved.

A receiving institution may accept members but refuse disputed property, accept a site but require its debts to remain with another owner, or delay completion until an actual approving authority acts. Partial agreements must preserve excluded and unresolved subjects explicitly. No hidden all-or-nothing merger is assumed.

### 30. Post-transfer reconciliation and branch stabilization

Completion of legal or institutional transfer begins a stabilization period rather than instantly normalizing the successor. Reconcile:

- surviving members, admission status, assignments and leadership;
- formations, actual strength, equipment, mounts and mission;
- site garrisons, relief plans and operational readiness;
- property title, possession, occupants, maintenance and claims;
- funds, stores, debts, restricted resources and accounts;
- external bargains with bishops, towns, rulers and allies;
- archives, missing records and continuing disputes;
- predecessor symbols, offices and commands that remain, change or cease.

The branch should not field both predecessor and successor versions of the same transferred formation. Existing experience and losses remain. Reinforcements from the wider institution require actual source, movement and assignment.

Stabilization can succeed unevenly. Military command may integrate before property disputes settle; a castle may transfer while its revenues remain contested; members may be admitted while some local auxiliaries keep separate terms. The player sees these as real campaign differences, not as an incomplete cosmetic conversion.

### 31. Conduct, legitimacy and protected population consequences

V1.1 strengthens the implementation boundary: conduct outcomes belong to applicable battle, population, religious, governance and legal owners, but this authority must preserve their strategic effects and never erase them through institutional succession.

Relevant consequences may include loss of local cooperation, displacement, damaged production, broken agreements, resistance, sponsor criticism, ecclesiastical action, claims for remedy and heightened security burdens. These are possible routed outcomes, not automatic penalties or a fixed morality meter.

Conversion, submission, tribute, residence, labor, military service and political loyalty remain distinct. A successor branch does not inherit perfect legitimacy because the receiving institution is better established. Nor does every local party respond identically.

Settlement or remedy can include restored rights, restitution where feasible, amended conduct, negotiated protection, release, return of property, compensation through competent owners or continuing dispute. No favorable relationship score can silently cancel an unresolved harm.

### 32. Rollback, reopening and terminal dispositions

Design rollback removes the unadopted V1.1 candidate and leaves V1 and all sources unchanged. Campaign rollback is different: an in-world emergence, battle, incorporation or transfer cannot be reversed by deleting a record.

A failed or canceled transaction follows the last valid completed stage. Unspent reservations may release; spent resources, moved people, lost equipment, casualties, accepted memberships, completed handovers and accrued obligations remain. Reopening negotiations requires a continuing party, unresolved subject and competent authority.

Terminal dispositions are:

- **Independent continuity:** predecessor persists with actual surviving capacity.
- **Completed branch succession:** transferred subjects settle under defined regional powers.
- **Partial succession:** transferred and retained portfolios remain explicitly separated.
- **Dissolution:** people, assets, claims and obligations receive individual dispositions.
- **Suppression or destruction:** consequences follow actual competent action and campaign events.
- **Never emerged:** no predecessor assets exist to transfer.

A terminal label does not erase unresolved claims or historical provenance. Later revival requires a new valid chain; it cannot restore a dissolved institution from a dormant flag with its former manpower intact.
