## 5. Campaign decision: first contact and patronage

### Player situation

A ruler encounters an emerging or already recognized institution with a compatible mission. The choice is whether to ignore it, support care or property, negotiate military service, or oppose its presence. Historical emergence conditions still govern whether a named Order can exist.

### Proposed decision contract

Offer a readable bargain: the proposed site or support, expected benefit, rights retained, military commitment if any, and strongest blocker. Ordinary members and routine administration remain aggregate. A donation can improve actual resources or relations but cannot buy guaranteed obedience, profession, or a ready cavalry unit.

Acceptance establishes only the agreed relationship. Refusal may leave contact open, redirect the institution to another patron, or close the proposal. It does not force a hostile faction spawn. Player and AI receive the same eligibility and resource rules. [S1: sections 6, 7, 12, 33–35]

## 6. Campaign decision: fortress custody and frontier invitation

The player may offer a fortress, revenue or district rights in exchange for defined defense. The decision should distinguish custody, property, jurisdiction and sovereignty on one panel. A custody agreement can strengthen a frontier without changing the political map.

Proposed outcomes include acceptance with limited forces, a request for better funding or rights, refusal because the Order is committed elsewhere, or a narrower escort/patrol agreement. Local defenders are reserved from their actual sources. A fortress is not filled automatically when the agreement is signed.

If terms are later disputed, display the particular issue: service not delivered, unpaid support, contested reversion, unauthorized expansion, or a threatened local interest. Negotiation, mediation, withdrawal or conflict follow the existing political systems. No one-sided legal victory is inferred from a document title or favorable charter alone.

## 7. Campaign decision: mobilize a contingent

Use the shared mission states for the actual service process. A player selects a mission, sees available strength and local-defense cost, accepts terms, and assembles the contingent through real routes.

```yaml
proposed_mobilization_receipt:
  source: ExistingOrderOrExternalSourceReference
  employer: CurrentMissionRequester
  commitment: ExistingMissionReference
  force: ExistingFormationReferences
  costs: FundingSupplyAndLocalDefenseOpportunityCost
  command: StrategicAndContingentResponsibilities
  restrictions: MissionTargetDurationAndConduct
  return: RecallRecoveryAndDestination
receipt_status: ConceptualNotProductionSchema
```

A fatal assembly failure releases incomplete reservations. Acceptance alone is not field readiness. A mission can be restricted, suspended or recalled without creating a second contingent. A recall received during battle does not remove normal player control mid-fight; campaign resolution follows the battle under the existing Teutonic proposal.

## 8. Campaign decision: territorial autonomy

A grant, conquest or host collapse may give the Order enough territorial responsibility to seek greater autonomy. This is a political decision, not a building upgrade.

Show the actual controlled sites, population, revenue, administrative capacity, armed control, recognition or contestation, and host claims. The host can accept subordinate autonomy, renegotiate, refuse, or resist. The Order can delay if dependence on the host is still essential.

Faction conversion preserves existing people, commitments, treasury rights, debts, claims and units. It must not grant free armies or annex foreign houses. Player switching is optional and scenario-dependent; founding or funding the institution does not automatically give the patron ownership of the resulting polity.

The shared framework routes sovereignty and territorial lordship to Realm/map ownership. This companion therefore does not authorize a new faction ID, map transfer, or production transaction. [S1: sections 2, 18, 38]

## 9. Campaign decision: a viable composite army

The emerged state should be able to fight when its brethren are unavailable, but not with free substitute brethren. It can use existing eligible regional, territorial, allied, visiting or hired sources. No new named infantry or missile card is admitted here.

The recruitment view should answer: what role is missing, which real source can supply it, what terms apply, and what does the commitment weaken? For example, frontage may come from a valid regional infantry family or hired company, while the Order core provides mounted decision power. These are illustrative design choices, not verified inventories of current Germania files.

Garrison remains assignment. Dismounted service remains the same unit and people within its existing profile limits. A separate permanent foot establishment may be researched later, but generic framework categories are not sufficient evidence for one.

## 10. Campaign decision: privileges, debt and consolidation

The state-government proposal's strongest choices should remain ordinary campaign actions, not a separate Teutonic legal simulator. Confirming a privilege may secure cooperation while limiting extraction. Borrowing may fund defense while burdening later budgets. Settlement support may create future production but requires real households and resources.

The integration requirement is that each choice returns consequences to its owning system: property rights to law/property, money and debt to economy, households to population, military availability to pools, and cooperation or disputes to political relationships. Do not create a duplicate Teutonic ledger for each of these.

One consolidated player view may summarize institutional and state finances. Source ownership and restricted funds remain distinguishable behind that view. Routine authorized transfers should not require repeated manual confirmation.

## 11. Campaign decision: defeat, withdrawal and continuation

After defeat, preserve actual survivors, horses, equipment, captives, debt, damage and claims. A lost field army is not automatically the end of the institution. A lost capital is not automatically the end of every territorial district. A suppressed house is not automatically confiscation of all foreign property.

Offer proportionate recovery choices: reduced commitments, repair and remount support, ransom, negotiated service, debt settlement, privilege confirmation, retreat to a defensible core, or vassalage. Their exact effects remain proposals for the relevant shared owners.

The shared framework explicitly separates institutional, military, territorial and relationship failure and preserves disposition on suppression or transformation. The Teutonic package should specialize those outcomes without replacing them. [S1: sections 36–38]

## 12. Battle and campaign return boundary

Before battle, preserve source, owner, actual strength, horses, equipment, command, mission restrictions, condition and readiness. After battle, return losses and survivors to those same sources. The shared battle contract supports those categories; the exact receipt implementation remains unverified. [S1: section 46]

Proposed integration checks must detect duplicate receipts and avoid applying the same casualties twice. If battle resolution is interrupted, do not invent a fresh formation or silently refill a pool. These are future transaction acceptance requirements, not claims that an idempotent engine path already exists.

Autoresolve should use the same actual condition and counters as tactical battle. The Order name grants no automatic victory, unbreakability, anti-faith damage or supply immunity. [S1: section 46]

## 13. AI and information parity

The shared framework calls for equal player/AI eligibility and reservations. The proposed Teutonic specialization should compare military need, local defense, route access, recovery, mission fit and political consequences. [S1: sections 3, 44–45]

The AI must not use knowledge hidden from the player-facing decision model to reserve unavailable foreign contingents or recruit through inaccessible markets. Exact fog-of-war and information contracts need their own owner review.

The state AI may consolidate, negotiate or contract territory instead of attacking continuously. The institution AI may refuse a patron even when relations are favorable if mission or capacity fails. A derived capacity summary explains these choices but must not become a second authoritative resource pool.

## 18. End-to-end player campaign loop

The Teutonic experience should read as one developing political relationship, not a sequence of disconnected authority checks.

```yaml
player_campaign_loop:
  Contact:
    decision: EngageSupportInvestigateIgnoreOrOppose
    output: RelationshipAndInformationOnly
  Presence:
    decision: PermitHospitalHouseEstateOrFortressCustody
    output: ExactGrantAndPhysicalPresence
  Service:
    decision: RequestEscortReliefGarrisonOrFieldContingent
    output: MissionProposalWithRealCapacityCheck
  Commitment:
    decision: AcceptTermsFundSupplyAndReserveSources
    output: AssemblingMissionOrExplainedFailure
  Conduct:
    decision: EmployContingentWithinOrAgainstAgreedRestrictions
    output: MilitaryPoliticalAndInstitutionalConsequences
  Return:
    decision: ReleaseExtendRecallRansomRepairOrRecover
    output: SourceCorrectCasualtyAndReadinessReceipt
  Territorialization:
    decision: GrantRecognizeRestrictContestOrResistAutonomy
    output: PoliticalStatusChangeWithoutResourceDuplication
  Persistence:
    decision: ConsolidateExpandVassalizeContractWithdrawReformOrSuppress
    output: ContinuingInstitutionAndTerritorialDisposition
```

The player should never need to understand every internal record to make these decisions. Each step must show the offered bargain, strongest blocker, opportunity cost, and likely ownership consequences.

## 20. Foreign-house and homeland conflict contract

The same institution may govern a territorial state while retaining houses under foreign rulers. War between the Order-state and a host Realm must not produce impossible dual loyalty or automatic annexation.

```yaml
foreign_house_conflict_states:
  LawfulAndConnected: RoutineRelationshipAndTransfersMayContinue
  Restricted: HostLimitsTravelRecruitmentRemittanceOrMilitaryUse
  InternedOrSupervised: ResidentsAndAssetsRemainButFreedomIsReduced
  Confiscated: HostSeizesSiteThroughExplicitPoliticalAction
  Evacuating: ValidPeopleMovablesAndRecordsAttemptDeparture
  SeparatedBranch: InstitutionalRelationshipChangesThroughExplicitResolution
prohibited:
  - AutomaticAnnexationByOrderState
  - InstantConfiscationWithoutPoliticalAction
  - TeleportingMembersOrTreasuryBeforeHostAction
  - ForeignHouseProvidingUnitsWhileItsPeopleAreInternedOrAssignedElsewhere
```

This contract preserves a genuinely transregional institution while allowing territorial war to matter.
