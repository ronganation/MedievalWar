## 1. Definitive campaign promise

**The Order gains operational reach by maintaining a usable network, not by painting a chain of castles on the map.** A fortress can shelter forces, secure stores, support recovery and protect a route only to the extent its actual people, resources, access and geography permit. The player decides where to concentrate, which site to relieve, which enemy position to contain or bypass, and when to consolidate or withdraw.

This new specialist companion develops those decisions rather than repeating host rights, recruitment or leadership. Its mechanics are authored design proposals for review, not certified historical doctrine or implemented behavior. It admits no new unit family, named fortress, opening garrison, campaign bonus or Teutonic-specific supply executor.

The distinctive proposed campaign rhythm is **Secure, Assemble, Advance, Connect, Relieve, Consolidate, Recover**. V1.1 retains that complete cycle and adds explicit network readiness, operational dependency, reserve, information, occupation and failure-state contracts so the cycle can be implemented without collapsing into a single fortress bonus. The scarce mounted institutional core supplies decisive action and mission support while actual territorial, regional, allied and hired forces provide much of the frontage and site defense. No fixed army composition or magical castle-network aura is introduced.

## 3. Operational network: functions, not new buildings

| Function | What must actually exist | What it does not grant |
|---|---|---|
| Assembly base | Access, space, supply and available assigned forces | Instant recruitment or teleportation |
| Fortress or defended site | Defenders, works, command, stores and maintenance | Automatic control of every surrounding road |
| Forward store | Actual stock, custody and delivery | Infinite supply or duplication of town stores |
| Crossing or route junction | Passable geography and applicable access | Universal passage or guaranteed interception |
| Port or river transfer point | Vessels, crews, landing capacity and valid routes | Free navy or uninterrupted maritime access |
| Recovery site | Care, repair, rest, suitable resources and access | Instant remounts or restored veteran cohesion |
| Production hinterland | Real households, land, workshops and movement | Free extraction or automatic local cooperation |

A single site may perform several functions. Do not create seven separate structures or resource pools merely because the table distinguishes seven functions. A host-owned site may support the Order under an agreement without becoming Order-state territory. A foreign commandery is not a distant province or an instant reinforcement node.

## 4. Connected, accessible and supplied are different questions

A site may be friendly but unreachable, reachable but empty, stocked but politically inaccessible, or supplied for its residents but unable to support a large army. Show the decisive limitation rather than one universal network score.

The proposed network view draws from existing owners: location and passability, political access, actual stocks and consumption, transport capacity, route security, assigned forces and current information. It does not own those facts. A conceptual connection is not a new production graph schema.

An enemy fortress does not automatically seal an entire region. Its operational influence depends on terrain, garrison, patrols, crossings, observation and the ability to act. Equally, bypassing it does not make its defenders harmless. Exact movement, interception and zone-of-control behavior remains with the map and battle owners.

## 5. Secure: establish the defensible base

Before a new operation, choose a base that can support the actual force and preserve critical home duties. Compare a host fortress, a territorial center, a forward position or an allied site using real access and support.

The important decision is what remains behind. Stripping every garrison can expose stores, routes and local administration. Keeping every soldier behind can make relief impossible. No universal garrison floor is assigned here; site support and local threat determine the relevant requirement.

Ordinary safe recruitment alone has no automatic major economic penalty. Extraordinary calls, prolonged service, diverted transport, casualties and damaged production may create pressure through their shared owners. A defended base is not a license to ignore those distinctions.

## 6. Assemble: prepare a mission rather than a stack-shaped wish

Choose an objective and compare the required roles against actual sources. Knight-Brothers retain elite mounted shock; Sergeant-Brothers retain escort, screening, interception and withdrawal support. Existing eligible infantry, missiles, specialists and hired forces supply other needs without being renamed brethren.

Assembly requires people, officers, equipment, suitable animals, stores, transport, funding and a feasible route. Approval of a mission is not arrival. Missing decisive capacity can reduce the operation, change the objective, delay it or stop it.

Reserve actual contributions once. A forward garrison, convoy escort and field army cannot each use the same Sergeant-Brothers. Temporary dismounting retains the same people and accountable mounts. This companion creates neither Foot Sergeants nor generic Garrison Brethren.

## 7. Advance: choose purpose and acceptable exposure

An operation should have a readable purpose: relieve a threatened site, secure a crossing, protect a movement, take a strategic position, contain a threat or restore a route. Do not reward marching farther merely because the faction has an expansion identity.

Compare concentration against route exposure and return feasibility. A heavy cavalry concentration can be tactically strong while exceeding local fodder and transport support. A smaller force may move and recover more reliably but lack siege or holding capacity.

The proposed order of thought is objective, force, access, sustainment, enemy response and return. It is not a mandatory player checklist or a newly implemented turn pipeline. Routine validation remains aggregated; material risks become choices.

## 8. Connect: convert a foothold into usable reach

Capturing a fortress changes military control first. Inspect actual defenders, damage, stores, access and the surrounding political situation before using it as a forward base. No full depot or loyal district appears with the flag change.

Useful consolidation choices include repairing a route, arranging valid supply, retaining a defensible garrison, negotiating local support, repairing works or delaying further advance. Each uses existing economic, political and assignment systems.

A newly held site may support a limited force before it supports a large offensive. It may remain a useful isolated position without providing regular revenue or recruitment. Military control, legal claims, administrative reach and practical support remain separate.

## 9. Contain, bypass or assault

| Approach | Proposed value | Main exposure | Reconsider when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Assault | Rapid removal of a decisive obstacle if feasible | Casualties, equipment and preparation burden | The force lacks means or the objective loses value |
| Invest or contain | Restrict enemy action while preserving a wider plan | Time, detachments, supply and relief risk | The enemy can still operate or a relief force threatens |
| Bypass | Preserve tempo and avoid unnecessary losses | Rear routes and exposed movements | The bypassed force can materially disrupt support |
| Negotiate | Gain passage, surrender or bounded terms | Delay, concessions and uncertain compliance | Competent parties or credible terms are absent |
| Withdraw or redirect | Preserve capability for a better objective | Political cost and abandoned opportunity | A safer feasible operation becomes available |

No approach is always correct. Containment requires real forces or other actual constraints, not an abstract marker. A negotiated surrender must resolve defenders, stores, captives, rights and custody; it does not erase them.

## 10. Relieve: the network creates a real decision

A relief operation must consider the threatened site's actual condition, known endurance constraints, route access, enemy field strength, the relief force and the value of preserving the site. Exact endurance or relief-arrival quantities remain unset.

Proposed choices include direct relief, threatening the besieger's support, escorting supplies through a valid route, arranging evacuation or accepting a bounded surrender. None guarantees success. A diversion matters only if it changes an enemy decision or actual support condition.

The player should see the main risk and known uncertainty. Do not expose exact hidden enemy stores or force strength merely to calculate an optimal relief choice. A garrison can survive without being able to sortie; a relief force can arrive without automatically breaking the siege.

## 11. Stores, transport and competing consumers

Use actual stores and the shared supply system. A fortress, town, hospital, field army and transport train may compete for the same food, fodder, tools, draft animals or repair capacity. The same stock cannot feed two consumers at full strength simultaneously.

Proposed priorities are situation-dependent, not a permanent hierarchy. Feeding a relief army can preserve a threatened site while weakening another district. Preserving hospital and resident support can limit offensive capacity. A wealthy treasury cannot substitute for absent grain, animals or a blocked route.

Do not require manual wagon assignment for routine operations. Present a concise support outlook and decisive bottleneck; retain detailed ownership and movement records for the shared systems and optional inspection. Exact consumption, spoilage, transport loads and allocation formulas are not assigned here.

## 12. Road, river and sea alternatives

Alternative routes create choices only where geography, access and transport actually support them. A river may help movement while a crossing, port or hostile bank constrains it. A sea route needs vessels, crews, loading and landing support; possession of a port alone creates none of these.

Chartered transport, allies and valid hired maritime services can support an operation without creating an Order navy. This companion admits no vessel or naval unit card and assumes no specific regional fleet exists.

A disrupted route can cause delay, reduced delivery, diversion or isolation through the actual owners. It does not automatically destroy all cargo or prove that every alternative is blocked. The UI should distinguish confirmed blockage from uncertain reports.

## 13. Season, weather and operational windows

Use the existing campaign season and world conditions rather than a permanent winter advantage or penalty attached to the faction. Roads, ground, water access, fodder and visibility may matter only through admitted world and supply behavior.

Do not certify frozen crossings, safe ice, universal winter campaigning or fixed weather windows from faction identity. An apparent opportunity can remain uncertain until actual route and information owners resolve it.

Historical time advances +21 months per strategic turn, biological time +7 months, and campaign season one ordered Winter/Spring/Summer/Autumn step. Do not generate extra harvests, recovery cycles, voyages or operational actions by dividing one clock into another. Exact duration and movement calibration remain pending shared-owner review.

## 14. Consolidate or overextend

The Order-state can win a battle and still be unable to govern or sustain the new position. Evaluate route continuity, remaining field strength, garrison demands, production, local rights, administrative capacity and debt before another advance.

Consolidation should be a positive strategic choice, not a passive turn skip. It may restore a crossing, improve support, negotiate service, repair a key site, release extraordinary obligations or recover the mounted core. The benefits require real resources and time, not a generic consolidation aura.

Avoid a universal overextension meter that causes scripted revolt. Show actual pressures: isolated sites, arrears, strained transport, weak garrisons, exhausted cadres or contested local cooperation. Political consequences remain with their owners.

## 15. Withdraw, evacuate and preserve the institution

Withdrawal can preserve the core while losing a position. It must resolve route, rear guard, wounded, captives, stores, mounts, equipment and receiving capacity. No instant evacuation of every asset is guaranteed.

Proposed choices include a limited withdrawal, negotiated safe conduct, phased evacuation, holding a covering position or abandoning material that cannot be moved. Exact destruction or denial actions remain with applicable battle, property and conduct systems; this file does not create a free erase-site button.

Sergeant-Brothers may cover movement within their actual profile and condition. Knight-Brothers are not an inexhaustible rescue force. A lost capital or fortress does not automatically delete foreign houses or the transregional institution. Actual survivors, claims, debts and recovery obligations persist.

## 16. Recover and rotate without instant replenishment

A returning force routes to its actual source and recovery state. Fit survivors, wounded, missing personnel, damaged equipment, surviving mounts and captured property require separate disposition. Source return is not immediate recruitability.

Rotation requires a real relieving force, valid assignment, access and transfer. A site cannot be shown fully defended by both outgoing and incoming forces when only one actually exists. Replacement headcount does not automatically restore veteran cohesion or mounted pairings.

Recovery sites can support rest, care, repair and training only where the resources and personnel exist. The player chooses whether to reinforce, retain a cadre, rotate or reduce commitments. This file does not replace the recruitment/service/recovery authority or assign exact recovery rates.

## 17. Enemy counterplay and fair operational weakness

The enemy should be able to challenge the network rather than fight only the strongest mounted core. Proposed counterplay includes threatening a crossing, forcing competing relief demands, interrupting a real supply route, containing an isolated site, drawing escorts away or contesting local cooperation through valid political action.

None is an automatic success or omniscient AI action. A raid does not destroy every resource in a district, and one lost route does not prove total isolation. Actual forces, information, movement and support govern outcomes.

The Order's corresponding responses include concentration, alternate access, stronger escort, a reduced objective, negotiation, relief or contraction. No religious identity grants immunity to hunger, fatigue, missile losses, debt or defeat.

## 18. AI operational priorities

The proposed AI compares objective value, feasible support, known enemy threats, garrison exposure, source commitments, recovery and a credible return plan. It should choose an operation it can sustain rather than always attack the nearest fortress.

For a threatened site, compare direct relief, indirect pressure, resupply, evacuation and acceptance of loss. For expansion, compare taking a new site against consolidating the existing route. For a depleted elite core, compare valid composite alternatives rather than spawning brethren.

The same capacity, information and no-duplication constraints apply to player and AI. Exact utility weights, movement behavior and tactical results remain unimplemented and untested. A favorable derived network summary cannot override a decisive missing resource.

## 19. Player-facing operation panel and decision portfolio

| Decision | Main information | Useful alternatives |
|---|---|---|
| Prepare the advance | Objective, available force, support and return risk | Proceed, reduce, delay or redirect |
| The forward site is isolated | Known routes, stocks, defenders and uncertainty | Relieve, resupply, evacuate or negotiate |
| A fortress blocks the plan | Actual enemy influence and available means | Assault, contain, bypass or change objective |
| Two sites request relief | Value, threat, route and available reserve | Concentrate, split only if feasible, seek allies or accept loss |
| The army exceeds support | Decisive food, fodder, transport or access blocker | Reduce force, change route, arrange valid support or stop |
| Hold or abandon the foothold | Garrison burden, local position and recovery | Consolidate, negotiate, rotate or withdraw |

The default panel should be concise. Detailed stock, custody and assignment records remain optional. Routine supply is aggregated; only meaningful strategic tradeoffs require decisions. This is proposed UI content, not a screenshot or implemented interface.

## 20. Worked qualitative campaign situations

These are authored situations, not historical incidents or executed simulations.

**The cavalry army that cannot remain:** the institutional core is available and tactically strong, but the chosen base cannot sustain its mounts alongside the garrison. Reduce the concentration, arrange real support or choose another operation. Money alone does not remove the bottleneck.

**The captured crossing:** the army takes a strategic site but finds damaged works and uncertain stores. Keep a defensible force, restore actual access and secure support before treating it as a new offensive base. Capture is useful without being a free operational reset.

**Two relief demands:** one site protects a route and another has political importance. Compare actual threats and reachable forces. Splitting the same reserve on paper is prohibited; accepting one loss may preserve the wider campaign.

**The defensible contraction:** the state withdraws from an isolated position to preserve cadres and a connected core. Resolve people, property and claims; retain the cost of lost investment. Contraction is neither automatic defeat nor free recovery.

## 22. Site readiness and network-state contract

A fortress-network view should report separate conditions rather than compressing them into one strength value. Its minimum readable state is:

| Condition | Question answered | Blocking example | Owner remains |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accessible | Can the force lawfully and physically reach or use the site? | Closed grant, blocked crossing or invalid route | Political access and map owners |
| Defensible | Can the site resist its expected threat with present works and defenders? | Damaged works or inadequate assigned force | Site, garrison and battle owners |
| Supplied | Can current consumers be supported from actual stocks and deliveries? | Food, fodder, tools or transport shortfall | Shared supply and economy owners |
| Connected | Is at least one usable route tied to the wider operational plan? | Isolated position despite local stores | Map, route and information owners |
| Recovering | Can the site support the intended rest, care, repair or remount work? | Missing personnel, workshops, animals or assigned capacity | Recovery and recruitment owners |
| Politically usable | Can support be drawn without exceeding rights, mandates or local tolerance? | Host denial, contested custody or unfulfilled terms | Host-rights, leadership and governance owners |

These conditions may be **confirmed**, **reported**, **contested**, **unknown** or **inapplicable** according to the information owner. They are not percentages and do not combine into an automatic green, amber or red verdict. A concise summary may identify the decisive blocker, but inspection must preserve the distinct cause and responsible owner.

A site can remain valuable while failing one condition. A stocked isolated fortress may still deny ground; an accessible allied castle may still lack support for a field army; a defensible site may be politically unavailable. The operation must respond to the actual failure rather than treating every weakness as the same generic network penalty.

## 23. Operational plan, dependency reservation and release

Each proposed operation should carry a bounded plan record sufficient to prevent wishful or duplicated commitments. The record is conceptual until adopted by the appropriate owners:

```yaml
operation_plan_contract:
  objective: Declared strategic purpose
  assembly_base: Existing accessible site or valid field location
  committed_forces: Existing eligible assignments only
  retained_duties: Garrisons escorts and obligations not stripped silently
  decisive_dependencies: Access stores transport specialists animals and route conditions
  information_basis: Confirmed reported contested and unknown facts
  phase: Proposed Assembling Advancing Connecting Relieving Consolidating Withdrawing Recovering or Closed
  return_or_closure_condition: Credible destination transfer or mission end
  owner_resolution_required: true
```

Reservation prevents one contingent, transport capacity, store, vessel contract or recovery slot from satisfying incompatible simultaneous plans. Reservation does not remove the resource from its owner's ledger and does not guarantee fulfillment. The authoritative owner may reject, reduce, interrupt or release it when conditions change.

A plan closes only after remaining forces, stores, wounded, prisoners, mounts, equipment, debts, contracts and site assignments have a valid disposition. Cancellation before departure should release unspent reservations. Cancellation after commitment must resolve losses, consumed stock, stranded parties and outstanding obligations rather than refunding the plan as if nothing occurred.

## 24. Intelligence, warning and decision fairness

Operational planning must separate knowledge from reality. The player and AI may know a route is blocked, receive an uncertain report, infer risk from recent activity or lack current information. The network panel should expose the basis and freshness category supplied by the information owner without revealing hidden enemy totals.

Warnings should be causal and actionable. Examples include **no confirmed route**, **fodder support unverified**, **garrison commitment conflicts with escort**, **relief arrival uncertain**, or **occupation capacity absent**. Avoid false precision, unexplained numerical danger scores and warnings that reveal an enemy reserve merely because it exists in simulation state.

When conditions materially change after commitment, the operation should be reconsidered rather than silently continuing under obsolete assumptions. Valid responses include continuing with accepted exposure, reducing the objective, rerouting, reinforcing through actual means, negotiating, consolidating or withdrawing. Reconsideration is not a free pause, teleport or automatic escape from an enemy response.

## 25. Capture, occupation and post-operation settlement

Capture begins a new burden. Before a site becomes a reliable network node, resolve surviving defenders, prisoners, civilians, damaged works, fires or spoilage where applicable, remaining stores, property custody, access rights, command responsibility and the force required to hold it. This authority does not predetermine conduct outcomes or create population events; it requires the relevant owners to return an explicit disposition.

A newly occupied site may pass through useful but limited states: tactically held, provisionally secured, connected for restricted movement, capable of local defense, or capable of supporting wider operations. These are descriptive outcomes, not a mandatory ladder or building chain. Legal sovereignty, administrative incorporation, recruitment access and reliable production remain separate decisions.

Post-operation settlement must also account for promises made to allies, hired services and local contributors. A successful assault cannot consume shared support and then ignore payment, custody or return obligations. Equally, a negotiated handover does not require the system to invent destruction merely to impose a cost. Outcomes follow actual terms and surviving assets.

## 26. Degradation, recovery and non-binary failure

A network should degrade through its actual dependencies. Reduced delivery, delayed repair, contested access, insufficient escorts, exhausted cadres, unavailable transport and damaged crossings may narrow what an operation can do without instantly erasing the site. Partial function matters: a fortress may shelter survivors while failing to support cavalry; a port may receive stores while lacking inland transfer; a route may remain passable for messengers but not a large train.

Failure must leave an auditable campaign state. Forces can be delayed, separated, forced to consume reserve, redirected, trapped, withdrawn or defeated according to the responsible systems. Resources are not destroyed unless an owner resolves that result. Uncertainty is not failure, and one failed dependency does not authorize the authority to fabricate every other consequence.

Recovery follows the same rule. Repairing works does not restore stores; reopening access does not create escorts; paying arrears does not resurrect casualties; replenishing headcount does not recreate veteran cohesion. The strongest recovery path is the one supported by real sources, time, access and priority choices.

## 27. Bounded AI and player campaign sequence

The operational sequence is a decision scaffold, not an extra turn system:

1. **Define:** state the objective, acceptable loss and closure condition.
2. **Check:** identify the decisive access, force, supply, transport, information and return dependencies.
3. **Reserve:** commit each available contribution once while preserving visible retained duties.
4. **Execute:** allow movement, battle, siege, economy and political owners to resolve events.
5. **Reassess:** react when a decisive condition changes or knowledge improves.
6. **Settle:** resolve occupation, withdrawal, contracts, casualties, property and remaining assignments.
7. **Release:** return surviving capacity to its source and expose continuing recovery or debt.

The AI uses the same admitted information and reservations as the player. It may choose a less ambitious operation because it preserves a return plan or avoids stripping a vital site. It must not receive hidden certainty, free transport, duplicated garrisons or automatic occupation capacity. The player receives the same causal blockers and alternatives, with optional detail rather than compulsory logistics micromanagement.
