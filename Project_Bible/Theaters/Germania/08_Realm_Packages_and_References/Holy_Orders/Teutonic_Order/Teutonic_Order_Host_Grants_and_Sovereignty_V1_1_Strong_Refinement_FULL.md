## 1. Definitive campaign promise

**A ruler can invite the Teutonic Order to solve a real problem without knowing that the eventual relationship will remain comfortable. The Order can obtain a durable base without automatically becoming independent. The bargain must be valuable enough to accept, limited enough to understand, and consequential enough that either party may later regret its terms.**

The host should have more choices than donate land or refuse. The Order should have more choices than obey or revolt. The playable middle ground includes a supported house, funded mission, bounded fortress custody, frontier jurisdiction, subordinate autonomy, compensated withdrawal, or a negotiated territorial settlement.

This file's new contribution is six concrete bargain packages, an amendment and breach model, continuity on leadership change, claimant and community reactions, and a settlement menu for autonomy or exit. It creates no third brethren card, no generic Church faction, no new claim-token requirement for war, and no automatic Papal punishment.

## 3. One-screen host decision

The player should see one concise offer, not an empty legal form.

| Question | What the offer must communicate |
|---|---|
| What problem is being solved? | Care, route security, relief, fortress defense or frontier government |
| What is being given? | A specified site, use right, revenue stream, custody or jurisdiction |
| What remains mine? | Retained property, courts, taxes, appointments, access and sovereignty |
| What does the Order promise? | Defined functions or a bounded military commitment, subject to actual capacity |
| What does it cost? | Funding, lost direct receipts, land rights, local obligations or strategic exposure |
| How does it end? | Term, review, reversion, compensation, withdrawal and dispute path |
| Who else is affected? | Existing holders, heirs, local communities, overlords and relevant religious authorities |
| What could change later? | Entrenchment, altered threat, rival claims or a request for greater autonomy |

Routine continuation of an unchanged arrangement should be aggregated. A new decision is surfaced when a material right, cost, service or political status changes. The default screen should be understandable without opening a provenance ledger.

## 4. Bargain portfolio

The following are authored presets for designers and future UI, not historical charter templates. Exact terms are populated from the scenario and existing rights.

| Package | Host contribution | Order contribution | Retained host position | Principal risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Supported Hospital | Site use and defined support | Care, lodging and route assistance where capacity exists | Sovereignty and untransferred rights | Unfunded expectations or property dispute |
| Funded Mission | Money, supply and access | One bounded escort, relief or field commitment | No land transfer | Mission scope, funding or recall dispute |
| Estate Endowment | Specified estate or receipts | Institutional support and only explicitly agreed service | Rights reserved by grant | Reduced direct income and claimant objections |
| Fortress Custody | Custody, supply terms and maintenance arrangement | Defense by real assigned forces | Sovereignty unless separately changed | Entrenched custody or unfulfilled defense |
| Frontier Stewardship | Defined district rights and resources | Defense and bounded administration | Overlord and reversion rights as agreed | Local resistance, overreach and growing autonomy |
| Autonomous March Settlement | Negotiated territorial jurisdiction and political status | Defined alliance, tribute or service if agreed | Only expressly retained rights | Durable separation or contested sovereignty |

No package creates people, equipment, horses, administrative staff or a ready garrison. The last package can be offered only when territorial capacity and the existing emergence authority support it. It is not a shortcut around factionization requirements.

## 5. Supported Hospital: low military commitment, real institutional value

Choose this when the host wants care, lodging, pilgrim support or a durable institutional connection rather than a cavalry unlock. The Order can accept a site with a defined support arrangement, request a smaller establishment, or decline if the resources and mission are insufficient.

The host gains only functions that actual staff, stores, routes and funding can sustain. The Order receives no automatic general taxation, military passage or settlement sovereignty. A hospital license must not be presented as an invisible first step in an unavoidable faction-spawn chain.

A later military mission requires a separate agreement or valid amendment. If the host attempts to turn care personnel into soldiers, the recruitment and institutional owners evaluate the demand; no generic hospital-to-army conversion occurs. Loss of the site may impair care and standing without destroying the whole institution.

## 6. Funded Mission: hire service without buying the institution

Choose this when the host needs a particular campaign contribution but does not want a permanent land concession. Specify the task, permitted scope, command relationship, assembly support, service end and return conditions. Use the existing recruitment/service authority to determine whether the force can actually assemble.

The Order may offer a smaller contribution, a later arrival, a narrower mission, or refusal. Favorable relations and common faith are not a guarantee of compliance. A host paying for escort has not bought permission to redirect the contingent indefinitely into an unrelated war.

If the host changes the mission, propose an amendment. If it acts without agreement, record the conduct dispute and actual military consequences. The contingent is not deleted, replaced or seized by a mid-battle event. Consumed supplies, earned pay and losses remain real even if the mission is canceled.

## 7. Estate Endowment: property, receipts and dynastic consequences

Choose this when a patron wants to support the institution through land or revenue. Separate property from receipts: a rent assignment is not necessarily transfer of the estate, and ownership of an estate does not necessarily convey all public jurisdiction around it.

The offer must identify what the donor can actually grant. Existing tenants, co-holders, family claims, local privileges and superior rights remain relevant. An offer involving disputed rights can be proposed, but it cannot be presented as an uncontested completed transfer. No favorable wording in the grant erases an adverse claim by itself.

Institutional support is the default benefit. Military service exists only if agreed and feasible. A donor cannot endow an estate and assume a permanent free Knight-Brother unit. An endowment may remove a particular asset from ordinary dynastic disposal where the governing law permits; it is not a universal disinheritance action.

## 8. Fortress Custody: control the defense without silently changing the border

Choose this when a host wants professional defense of a strategic site. The offer identifies custody, garrison command, supply, maintenance, access, relief, and handback. Ownership and sovereignty remain separate questions.

The garrison is composed from actual assigned personnel. A mixed force may contain existing brethren, local defenders, host troops or hired components, each retaining its source. The Order cannot promise the same contingent to another site or mission simultaneously. The host cannot assume that a custodian's forces are permanently available for unrelated field service.

If the site is attacked, the tactical battle follows the actual force and command state. A custody dispute does not create invulnerability or an automatic surrender. After a term ends, the host can request handback, negotiate extension, accept a compensated exit or contest refusal. Expiry is not teleportation: people, stores, mounts, wounded and access require disposition.

## 9. Frontier Stewardship: the dangerous but useful middle ground

Choose this when the host cannot secure or administer a frontier adequately and offers broader authority. Define the territory and the particular rights: defense, collection of specified receipts, fortification, local justice, settlement or market privileges where supported.

The Order may become materially stronger through this arrangement, but it does not acquire every surrounding community or a universal recruitment pool. Existing local rights and obligations remain visible. New fortifications require resources; new settlement requires households and support; administration requires personnel and records.

The host can retain meaningful leverage through reserved rights, supply, recognition, access or agreed review. Excessively narrow terms may make the mission unattractive or unworkable. Excessively broad terms may establish a durable power that later resists revocation. The campaign interest lies in this tradeoff, not in a hidden inevitability meter.

## 10. Autonomous March Settlement: negotiated political separation

Choose this only when the Order has a sustainable territorial base and political agency. The parties can negotiate subordinate autonomy, a vassal relationship, a protected polity, an alliance or recognition of independence according to the existing political framework and campaign circumstances.

This settlement changes only the identified rights and territory. It does not annex foreign houses, refill armies, cancel debts, remove local opposition or erase the original host's surviving claims. The patron who enabled the polity does not automatically own it. An optional player switch follows scenario rules, not donation size.

Refusal can lead to further negotiation, continued contested control, withdrawal or ordinary war. Neither party is forced to declare war merely because the request was refused. The Order can also decline premature independence when host protection remains essential.

## 12. Offer, acceptance and commencement

A proposal is not an executed grant. A signed agreement is not proof that a garrison has arrived. Commencement occurs through the actual property, treasury, assignment and access owners as applicable.

For a proposed implementation, validate the donor's competence, receiver's acceptance, affected rights, existing reservations and decisive prerequisites before committing a dependent change. If validation fails, retain the prior state and release only uncommitted candidate reservations. Do not reverse actual travel, spending or losses after lawful activity has begun.

A reduced agreement can still be useful: temporary custody rather than a district grant, a smaller mission rather than a full contingent, or a hospital site without military terms. The UI should offer a valid narrower bargain where one exists, not only a red unavailable button.

## 13. Reciprocal service and the limits of obedience

Service should be legible and bounded: defend this site, escort this movement, support this relief, or contribute to this defined campaign. Scope, duration and command terms belong to the agreement and relevant shared owners. Exact historical quantities remain unset.

The Order owes the agreed performance, not unlimited service. The host owes agreed support and rights, not unconditional submission to future demands. A material change in threat, route, supply or mission can justify negotiation without automatically absolving either party of its commitments.

Do not label every failure betrayal. Inability, delay, defeat, obstruction by a third party, changed orders and deliberate refusal are different causes. Their evidence and consequences should differ. A lost battle alone does not prove breach of a defense obligation.

## 14. Breach, remedy and escalation

| Issue | First useful response | Possible later settlement |
|---|---|---|
| Support not delivered | Request payment, supply or revised scope | Reduced service, arrears settlement or withdrawal |
| Service not provided | Establish whether inability or refusal caused it | Substitute service, compensation, revised terms or dispute |
| Custody exceeds agreed scope | Demand clarification or handback | Bounded extension, compensated return or contested possession |
| Unauthorized receipts collected | Challenge the affected right | Restitution, revised allocation or enforcement conflict |
| Host blocks agreed access | Seek passage or an alternative route | Mission suspension, amended terms or claim |
| Local rights harmed | Petition, investigation or negotiation | Confirmation, restitution, revised administration or resistance |
| Sovereignty asserted beyond the grant | Political negotiation | Vassalage, autonomy, recognition, contested rule or war |

This is a proposed campaign menu, not a universal medieval legal procedure. Available remedies depend on actual competence, evidence, leverage and willingness to comply. No universal countdown automatically escalates every dispute to rebellion.

## 15. Recognition, mediation and external patrons

Papal, imperial, royal, episcopal or other support may affect a dispute, but these relationships are not interchangeable. Recognition by one actor need not settle every other claim. A mediator needs communication and acceptance or relevant leverage; a mediation request does not automatically end a war.

Do not invent an automatic excommunication trigger for revoking a grant or fighting the Order. The shared ecclesiastical and Papal owners determine applicable responses from actual circumstances. P3 sections 12–13 explicitly distinguish contextual consequences from universal obedience or automatic sanctions.

A patron can support the Order materially without becoming its overlord. An overlord can hold reserved rights without controlling all internal appointments. Exact office and confirmation procedures remain with their historical and shared owners.

## 16. Succession, vacancy and continuity

The donor's death does not automatically cancel every corporate grant. The Order leader's death does not delete the institution, garrison or estate. Conversely, a successor does not automatically inherit every personal promise or relationship of the predecessor.

Proposed handling: identify whether the obligation belongs to a person, office, institution, estate or polity. Preserve institutional and property continuity; route personal commitments and vacancies to their owners. If renewal or confirmation is actually required, surface that material issue without re-negotiating every routine agreement on every succession.

A disputed host succession may produce competing confirmation offers. The Order may remain neutral, seek clarification, negotiate or align within its actual capacity. No event manufactures a new claimant, new army or legal victory solely to make the dilemma occur.

## 17. Dynastic and membership choices

The user's original CK-style concern is preserved as a design interface: a ruler may support a relative's institutional vocation or endow property, and that may affect family strategy. These are distinct actions.

A proposed relative's admission requires an eligible actual person, the institution's acceptance and the applicable character and religious rules. It is not a compulsory transfer of a child, an automatic profession, or an instant Knight-Brother recruitment action. No age threshold or universal historical admission procedure is assigned here.

Any inheritance consequence must follow the character's actual status and the relevant succession law. Admission alone is not assumed to erase every claim. Endowing land is a property action with its own competence, claimant and reversion questions. This file creates no named relative, character authority or family relationship.

## 18. Communities and existing rights

A district grant affects more than donor and receiver. Towns, landholders, rural households, local religious institutions and subject communities may retain rights or obligations. They are not transferable military inventory.

The player should see the principal local issue: who collects a toll, who administers justice, whether service has changed, whether land is disputed, or whether protection is actually being delivered. Confirmation of local rights can support cooperation; increased extraction or dispossession can create resistance, flight, appeals or rival alliances through existing systems.

Do not assign automatic loyalty or military quality from ethnicity or faith. Conversion does not resolve property disputes, create trained soldiers or make every resident an Order member. Ordinary safe recruitment is not automatically a major economic penalty; pressure follows the actual extraordinary demands and disruption described in the recruitment authority.

## 19. Conquest, occupation and third-party claims

The host cannot grant uncontested ownership of territory it merely claims without surfacing that limitation. The Order can accept a disputed frontier mandate, but the mandate is not possession and does not create an empty map region.

After conquest, resolve military control, surviving inhabitants, stores, existing property and claims separately. A frontier invitation does not pre-authorize every future annexation. Occupation does not automatically abolish institutions or settle all rights.

If a third party captures a granted fortress, custody and service may become impossible while claims and debts survive. The original host and Order may cooperate to recover it, renegotiate, or pursue separate objectives. The loss does not automatically terminate every other agreement between them.

## 20. Threat changes and the end of an emergency

The threat that justified an invitation can recede, convert, fragment, move elsewhere or be defeated. Reassess the mission and any terms explicitly tied to that threat. Do not erase perpetual property automatically because the original enemy is gone, and do not preserve an emergency military call indefinitely without a valid basis.

A host may now want a fortress back; the Order may argue that its investment or ongoing defense role justifies extension. The useful choices are handback, a narrower presence, compensation, renewed service, or a contested relationship. These alternatives make the original bargain matter after the crisis rather than ending it with a generic success reward.

Mongol or other frontier pressure may increase demand under the emergence proposal, but it neither creates the Order automatically nor overrides the grant's actual terms.

## 21. Exit and political settlement portfolio

| Settlement | What changes | What must persist |
|---|---|---|
| Peaceful expiry | Agreed use or service ends | Survivors, property rights, accounts and return needs |
| Compensated handback | Custody or specified rights return | Agreed payment and surviving claims |
| Reduced presence | Mission or jurisdiction narrows | Exact retained site and duties |
| Relocation | Order leaves one site for a valid destination | Real travel, custody and receiving capacity |
| Vassal autonomy | Political subordination is formalized | Institutional membership and bounded reserved rights |
| Independent recognition | Host recognizes specified separation | Debts, foreign houses, adverse claims and other treaties |
| Contested possession | Parties disagree while control persists | Actual military control and unresolved claims |
| Confiscation or expulsion | Host takes explicit coercive action | Consequences, people, assets and wider institutional survival |

No option restores casualties or refunds all consumed resources. No option automatically affects every foreign house. The settlement is not complete merely because a diplomatic label changes; physical and financial disposition still matters.

## 22. Foreign houses during territorial conflict

A foreign house is an institutional property under its own host arrangement, not a disconnected province of the Order-state. War may lead to restriction, supervision, confiscation, evacuation or a negotiated exception. These are possible proposed responses, not automatic global effects.

A house cannot send the same residents or equipment away after they are already interned, captured or assigned elsewhere. Nor can a state use faction birth to teleport foreign receipts or troops. A lawful remittance may continue where routes and permissions remain valid; a war declaration is not proof that every route is physically blocked.

Preserve the distinction between a foreign host's local action and the Order-state's territorial sovereignty. This is central to keeping the institution alive beyond one political map color.

## 23. AI bargaining and anti-exploit rules

The host AI should compare the strategic need, alternatives, affordability, retained leverage, local reaction and long-term autonomy risk. It should not grant a critical district for a trivial threat merely because relations are favorable.

The Order AI should compare mission fit, actual capacity, supply, host reliability, legal and political exposure, competing duties and recovery. It can prefer a narrower sustainable bargain to a grand but indefensible concession. It should not demand independence every time it receives a fortress.

Anti-exploit requirements: no donate-and-revoke loop that duplicates income; no repeated mission acceptance that reserves the same force twice; no automatic free fortification from a grant; no heir-disposal shortcut; no sovereignty obtained through a cosmetic rename; no difficulty-level exemption from the same resource and political constraints. Exact AI weights remain unassigned and untested.

## 25. Worked qualitative campaign situations

All four are authored design situations, not historical incidents, synthetic numeric datasets or executed simulations.

**A ruler needs immediate relief but fears permanent concessions.** Offer a funded mission first. If the Order lacks an available contingent, consider a narrower mission or another valid source. A land grant is not a magic way to bypass missing people and mounts.

**A frontier custodian becomes indispensable.** The Order has invested in defense and administration while the host has become dependent. A request for wider jurisdiction is plausible as a proposed political choice, but not automatic. The host can accept bounded autonomy, offer compensation, reinforce direct control or resist. The actual territory and existing rights constrain the settlement.

**A new ruler contests an old endowment.** Distinguish the corporate property grant from personal patronage. Review applicable rights and claims. Confirmation, restitution, negotiated revision or coercion can follow; neither the new ruler nor the Order receives an automatic legal win.

**The Order-state fights a ruler who hosts a foreign commandery.** Resolve the commandery under its own grant and actual circumstances. It may be restricted, remain protected, evacuate or be seized. The site does not become a state province and its residents do not disappear from their current assignments.

```yaml
  Nonfatal: PermitBoundedAgreementWithVisibleRisk
undefined_term_defaults_to_Order: false
undefined_term_defaults_to_Host: false
```

Silence is not unlimited power. A missing service term cannot generate unlimited service, while a missing revocation term cannot be converted automatically into unrestricted confiscation.

## 30. Rights-conflict and priority resolution

Multiple valid interests may affect one site. The campaign should identify the conflict rather than merging the rights.

| Conflict | Required separation | Possible resolution |
|---|---|---|
| Property versus military custody | Holder and custodian remain distinct | Confirm custody, transfer property, hand back, or contest |
| Toll right versus public route access | Receipt and passage competence remain distinct | Exemption, payment, revised right, or enforcement dispute |
| Order jurisdiction versus town privilege | Exact subject and court competence remain distinct | Confirm privilege, allocate cases, mediate, or contest |
| Host sovereignty versus Order armed control | Political status and physical control remain distinct | Continued custody, vassal settlement, autonomy, or war |
| Local service versus external mission | Service scope and source ownership remain distinct | Local-only service, compensation, substitution, or refusal |
| Perpetual property versus threat-bound duty | Asset duration and military purpose remain distinct | Preserve property while amending service, or negotiate exchange |

No generic priority score silently erases an evidenced right. Where owners disagree, preserve claim, control, evidence status, and the current enforceable outcome separately.

## 31. Fortress investment, improvement, and handback

Fortress custody becomes strategically interesting when the custodian invests resources. Investment must not silently convert custody into sovereignty, but handback must not erase real improvements either.

```yaml
fortress_investment_categories:

    default_disposition: RequiredPerformanceUnderApplicableTerms
  HostFundedImprovement:
    default_disposition: HostAssetUnlessTermsSpecifyOtherwise
  OrderFundedImprovement:

  JointImprovement:
    default_disposition: SharedRecordedInterests
  MovableStoresAndEquipment:
    default_disposition: FollowOwnershipCustodyAndOperationalNeed
  EmergencyWorks:
    default_disposition: PreserveCostAndNecessityForLaterSettlement

  - RepairsDamageAndImprovements

  - RouteAndSafeConductForDeparture
```

Compensation is not always required and is never automatic free money. The applicable terms, ownership, necessity, benefit, breach, and available leverage govern the settlement. Exact valuation belongs to economy and property owners.

## 33. Breach evidence and remedy proportionality

Before assigning deliberate breach, distinguish incapacity, defeat, obstruction, mistake, disputed interpretation, negligent performance, and intentional refusal.

```yaml

  material_harm: Required

  evidence_status: Required
  proposed_remedy: Required
```

A lost defense battle can still expose inadequate preparation or negligence, but defeat alone is insufficient. Likewise, delayed host support may be excused, disputed, or deliberate depending on evidence.

## 35. Host leverage and Order leverage expenditure

Leverage should arise from existing resources and relationships, not a free diplomatic mana pool.

| Party | Practical leverage | What weakens it |
|---|---|---|
| Host | Recognition, supply, roads, surrounding territory, lawful claims, additional grants and allies | Military weakness, dependency, broken promises, contested authority and inability to administer |
| Order | Garrison control, professional capacity, external patrons, property network, investment and withdrawal threat | Depletion, isolation, debt, local opposition, route denial and loss of recognition |
| Local communities | Labor, production, information, compliance, rights and resistance | Displacement, division, coercion, weak institutions and military occupation |
| External arbiters | Recognition, mediation, sanctions or material backing where competent | Distance, contested competence, poor communication and refusal by parties |

Using leverage has opportunity costs. Withholding supply can weaken the disputed Order site while also exposing the frontier. Refusing handback can protect an Order position while damaging other host relationships.

## 38. Stronger player-facing explanation vocabulary

Use specific blockers and risks rather than generic refusal text.

- **The donor cannot grant this right:** the proposed party lacks demonstrated competence.
- **The site is already burdened:** an existing holder, privilege, debt, custody, or claim constrains the offer.
- **The Order cannot staff the commitment:** institutional capacity is committed, depleted, or recovering.
- **The route cannot support commencement:** physical access or supply prevents the agreement from operating.
- **The service terms do not match the mission:** the offered right and requested performance are misaligned.
- **The local settlement is not secured:** political control exists without reliable administration or cooperation.
- **Handback is physically unresolved:** people, stores, equipment, wounded, prisoners, or safe conduct remain unsettled.
- **Autonomy gates are incomplete:** a broad grant exists, but sustainable government or political agency does not.
