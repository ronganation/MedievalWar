The campaign promise is a powerful institution whose leaders must secure cooperation, not a dynasty wearing different heraldry. A new leader inherits commitments, not a clean treasury or obedient map. The player chooses priorities and political settlements while ordinary appointments and correspondence remain aggregated.

This companion develops the selection, vacancy, delegation and internal-dispute interfaces already identified by the government authority. It does not replace that authority, prescribe an unsupported historical electoral constitution, or establish a new government framework. Every detailed procedure below is an authored game-design proposal pending chronology-specific admission, not a statement of medieval practice.

## 3. Functional offices before historical titles

Use a functional office map that can later receive date-appropriate names. Do not install a mature Prussian hierarchy at initial emergence.

| Function | Strategic responsibility | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Institutional head | Direction, representation and highest accountability | Not personal ownership of institutional property |
| Collective governance | Selection and reserved institutional questions where supported | Not a universal parliament or fixed electorate |
| Regional leadership | Connected houses and regional commitments | No automatic sovereign branch |
| House command | Site readiness, resident duties and bounded administration | No unrestricted district ownership |
| Field command | Actual deployed force and mission | No automatic right to select institutional leadership |
| Treasury and records | Receipts, payments, custody and evidence | No second treasury or money creation |
| Religious and hospital responsibility | Rule, care and institutional purpose | Not a combat-unit recruitment button |

One person may hold several functions where feasible. That combines workload and exposure, not people, budgets or rights. A military appointment does not by itself admit someone to institutional membership or confer political voting rights. Ordinary clerks and minor officials remain aggregate capacity unless a strategically meaningful decision requires a character.

## 4. Player role and meaningful control

For a playable territorial Order, the player directs strategic policy through the institution's current constitution rather than personally possessing every member. For an external host, the player negotiates, supports, pressures or contests; the host does not directly select the Order's leader merely by funding a house.

Where supported selection rules permit a player choice, present actual eligible candidates and the terms or support conditions that matter. Where the player lacks that competence, present influence and response options instead of a false appointment button. No universal right to nominate, vote, veto or depose is assigned here.

The strategic screen answers: who currently holds authority, what decision is pending, which commitments are exposed, and what settlement is available? Avoid a vote-management minigame for every minor office. Routine uncontested succession may resolve in a concise summary; disputed or materially consequential succession merits a decision.

## 5. Vacancy, incapacity and disputed authority

Distinguish death, capture, incapacity, absence, disputed removal and rival claims. Capture does not prove death; absence does not automatically vacate an office. Exact grounds and competence follow admitted character and institutional rules.

```yaml
leadership_conditions:
  Occupied: RecognizedHolderCanExerciseApplicableAuthority
  TemporarilyDelegated: HolderPersistsWhileSpecificFunctionsAreDelegated
  Vacant: OfficeLacksAValidHolder
  Contested: RivalClaimsOrRecognitionPreventUnambiguousAuthority
  Isolated: HolderExistsButCommunicationOrAccessFails
  Resolved: ValidSettlementHasBeenRecordedAndCommunicated
condition_labels: ConceptualDesignNotRuntimeEnum
```

Existing garrisons, debts, stores, hospitals, grants and lawful local administration continue. No global paralysis follows a vacancy. A field army retains its actual command arrangements unless a separate command loss occurs. The death of one person holding both offices may create two distinct vacancies with different remedies.

## 6. Caretaker powers and emergency decisions

A caretaker is a proposed bounded delegation, not an automatically historical office. Its authority must be grounded in the admitted constitution, existing delegation or a recorded emergency settlement.

| Decision | Proposed default treatment | Important exception |
|---|---|---|
| Existing defense and supply | Continue within current mandate | Lost commander or broken route requires actual replacement |
| Earned pay and routine maintenance | Continue from valid accounts | Funds may be unavailable or restricted |
| Hospital and resident care | Preserve existing obligations | Physical loss can constrain performance |
| Relief of immediate attack | Permit proportionate action within valid emergency competence | No free forces or unlimited territorial war |
| New permanent alienation of property | Reserve for competent approval | A separately admitted emergency power may apply |
| Major new debt or political separation | Require applicable extraordinary authority | Existing obligations still persist |
| Routine office substitution | Delegate where valid | Cannot silently confer permanent succession rights |

Emergency action records purpose, scope, resources, affected rights and review need. Later review may confirm, amend or dispute it, but cannot undo casualties, consumed stores or completed travel. Caretaker appointment never refills a recruitment pool.

## 8. Candidates, commitments and no fabricated biographies

Eligibility is separate from popularity, administrative suitability and military experience. A rich patron's preferred candidate is not automatically eligible. An experienced field commander is not automatically the institutional heir.

A candidate view should show only supported facts: current office, location, relevant service record available to the campaign, known commitments, eligibility status and the principal political issue. Unknown information remains unknown. This is fictional game-character design, not evaluation of real employees.

No candidate is manufactured from a historical name alone. Shared character generation, if later authorized, must supply an actual person and valid emergence conditions; this file neither runs that process nor creates IDs. Candidate commitments are proposed policies or agreements, not mind-reading or guaranteed future conduct. Winning selection does not instantly fulfill promises.

## 9. Issue coalitions rather than permanent parties

Internal politics should emerge from concrete conflicts. Do not create universal expansionist, reformist or ethnic parties with permanent modifiers.

| Issue | Plausible design interests | Material tradeoff |
|---|---|---|
| Continue the offensive | Field leadership, threatened frontier houses, patrons | Opportunity versus depletion and supply |
| Restore the institution | Cadres, care functions, indebted houses | Recovery versus missed campaign opportunity |
| Support a distant house | Regional leadership and host relationships | Network survival versus territorial defense |
| Confirm town privileges | Local government and commercial interests | Cooperation versus unilateral extraction |
| Tighten accounts | Treasury and responsible offices | Reliable finance versus exposed disputed practices |
| Accept host compromise | Dependent sites and exhausted forces | Survival versus autonomy or prestige |

These are authored situation possibilities, not fixed historical alignments. The same actor may support recovery in one crisis and expansion in another. A community's identity alone determines neither loyalty nor ability. Support on one issue is not transferable currency for every other decision.

## 10. Chapter business and reserved decisions

Collective governance should matter when the decision changes the institution's direction or binds substantial resources. Its exact competence must be admitted by date and rule.

Proposed agenda categories include leadership selection, extraordinary commitments, major property questions, competing regional missions, serious disciplinary disputes and institutional reform. They are functional routing categories, not a certified historical list of chapter powers.

Each material agenda item needs a proposal, competent decision body, affected rights, resource implications and implementable alternatives. A body may approve, narrow, defer, refuse or request a revised proposal where its rules permit. Approval creates authority to act, not the personnel, money or consent needed for performance.

Routine stores, ordinary care and already authorized missions should not require repeated player votes. The purpose is a few consequential strategic decisions, not endless constitutional paperwork.

## 11. Regional autonomy and communication

A regional commander can have lawful discretion without being rebellious. Distance, threatened routes, local grants and prior delegation shape what can be done before central instructions arrive. Do not treat delayed news as disobedience.

Proposed order handling distinguishes issued, transmitted, received, acknowledged, executable and performed. These are conceptual information states, not implemented messaging code. A leader cannot recall a force instantly across a blocked route. A local emergency may justify action within a valid mandate while the center remains uninformed.

A new leader may confirm, narrow or replace a regional mandate through applicable authority. Replacing the officeholder does not repair roads, settle debts, erase host rights or transfer every regional force. Provincial divergence may remain negotiated autonomy rather than automatic branch separation.

## 12. Command, appointment and custody handover

Institutional leadership, army command, fortress custody and treasury access are distinct responsibilities. An election does not teleport the winner to the capital or grant immediate physical access to every seal, archive and store.

A material handover identifies the outgoing and incoming responsibilities, location, existing delegation, records, funds, keys or custody, pending missions and unresolved claims. Routine transfers are aggregated; contested transfers expose the strongest blocker.

During a battle, political selection does not replace the tactical commander through a surprise event or delete units. Immediate battlefield succession follows the battle and command owners. Afterward, institutional review can change future assignments. A political supporter cannot take the same soldiers into a second army while their original assignment remains live.

## 13. Promises, patronage and conflicts of obligation

A candidate or leader may promise a policy, appointment, subsidy, defense priority or review. A promise must identify its beneficiary, scope, competence, resources and any required later approval. Promising something outside one's authority does not make it executable.

Patronage can influence access and willingness without buying guaranteed obedience. A host may offer support conditional on a valid service agreement; it cannot convert that support into universal leadership control. Foreign-house obligations remain local agreements even when central politics change.

If promises conflict, show the collision rather than paying both from the same funds. Renegotiation, reduced performance, compensation or refusal can follow through the relevant owners. There is no favor-token shop that creates offices, membership, troops or sovereignty.

## 14. Discipline, challenge and removal

Disagreement, incapacity, poor outcomes, disputed competence and intentional misconduct are different questions. Defeat alone is not proof of disloyalty. A serious allegation requires an identifiable act, applicable obligation, evidence status and competent forum.

Possible proposed responses include clarification, mediation, temporary delegation, bounded investigation, restitution, reassignment, accepted resignation or contested removal where supported. No universal right of deposition, automatic religious sanction or infallible tribunal is created.

An allegation must not become a free confiscation tool. Office property and institutional accounts do not become personal loot. A removal changes only the responsibilities and rights actually resolved; outstanding debts, soldiers, wounded personnel and foreign grants remain. Appeals and external judgments route to existing ecclesiastical and legal owners.

## 15. Contested selection and graduated crisis

A contested outcome need not produce immediate civil war. Possible consequences include delayed recognition, withheld extraordinary cooperation, competing instructions, disputed custody, negotiated regional discretion or a new competent settlement.

Armed fracture requires actual participants, control of sites or forces, means of support and a political cause. No rebel army spawns from a support percentage. Each existing force retains one live assignment and its real supply and command state.

A settlement can confirm a holder, narrow powers, preserve a regional mandate, arrange resignation, resolve a specific grievance or establish a valid renewed selection. None automatically erases material losses or makes all parties permanently loyal. Branch separation, merger, suppression or secular transformation remains a distinct lifecycle decision under the shared framework.

## 16. Reform and institutional purpose

A reform proposal should solve an evidenced problem: overextension, confused authority, weak accounts, neglected care, unfulfilled service or disputed regional control. It should name the changed practice and its costs, not apply a generic reform bonus.

Examples for authored campaign situations include reserving a recovery cadre, clarifying extraordinary-spending approval, confirming regional emergency mandates or restoring hospital support. Each uses actual people, resources and competent decisions. Reform cannot manufacture administrators or retrospectively legalize every seizure.

A military emergency does not permanently abolish the institution's care and religious commitments. Conversely, preserving those commitments does not guarantee every requested hospital service can be supplied. The tension is a strategic allocation problem, not an automatic morality score.

## 18. AI, information and fair constraints

AI responses should be issue-specific and based on known information. No omniscient knowledge of distant votes, hidden accounts or blocked messages. A patron's money can improve a feasible offer but cannot bypass eligibility or create support automatically.

During vacancy, AI preserves urgent defense and routine obligations within valid mandates, avoids opportunistic permanent transfers without competence, and seeks a sustainable settlement. Both AI and player obey the same resource, communication, command and no-duplication constraints. Exact weights, stochastic selection and balance values remain unset and untested.

## 23. Standing instructions, records, seals, and authentication

Standing instructions allow routine defense, care, finance and reporting to continue without repeated player decisions. They must remain scoped, time-aware and supersedable.

| State | Meaning | Does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| Drafted | Instruction exists as proposed text | Valid authority or delivery |
| Authenticated | Applicable record, seal, witness or process supports issuance | Physical receipt or practical capacity |
| Dispatched | Communication began | Delivery or acceptance |
| Delayed or intercepted | Communication has not reached the intended destination reliably | Refusal |
| Delivered | Destination received the instruction | Capacity, legality or performance |
| Receipt confirmed | Receipt is acknowledged | Agreement with every term |
| Disputed | Authenticity, competence, version or meaning is challenged | Automatic invalidity or supremacy |
| Superseded | A valid later instruction replaces prospective effect | Erasure of historical acts |

Seal or archive custody never creates legal authority by itself. The player handles only material disputes; ordinary authenticated correspondence remains aggregated.

## 26. Recall, revocation, expiry, supersession, and interruption

These outcomes are not synonyms. Recall requests cessation or return. Suspension pauses future exercise. Revocation requires a competent decision plus practical execution. Expiry ends prospective authority at its boundary. Supersession replaces prospective effect without rewriting history. Interruption preserves a potentially resumable mandate that currently cannot operate.

Resumption requires the authority basis, owner versions, scope, time, resources and conflict state to remain valid. A changed basis needs a new mandate or explicit supersession. Partial committed work preserves receipts and consequences; uncommitted work is not reported as success.

No deposed or expired authority should remain quietly active because a flag was not cleared. Conversely, issuing a recall does not teleport a commander, records or treasury custody back to the center.

## 28. Concentration, institutional-capture exposure, and safeguards

Control of command, treasury, appointments, records, seals, correspondence, house access and regional representation creates strategic dependency. Concentration is a risk portfolio, not automatic betrayal or an editable capture score.

Useful safeguards include divided custody, independent reporting, explicit reserved powers, scheduled review, substitute officers, geographically distributed records, accessible appeal routes, and a prepared handback. Each safeguard requires actual institutional capacity.

A leader may centralize during crisis for speed, accepting greater dependency and handback risk. A chapter may distribute functions for resilience, accepting coordination cost. Neither architecture is always optimal. Misconduct, usurpation or rebellion requires owner-specific facts beyond concentration alone.
