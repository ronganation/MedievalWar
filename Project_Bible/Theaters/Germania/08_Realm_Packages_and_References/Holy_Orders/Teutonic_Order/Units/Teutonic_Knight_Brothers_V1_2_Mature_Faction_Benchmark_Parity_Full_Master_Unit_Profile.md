## 1. Definitive Unit Verdict

**Teutonic Knight-Brothers are the Teutonic Order's scarce professed heavy-shock cavalry: a disciplined mounted brotherhood assembled around recognized commanders, standards, trained rider-horse pairings, strong period-appropriate protection, and an institutional recovery network, preserved to deliver one decisive charge or countercharge and then withdraw before sacred prestige becomes irreversible material loss.**

They are not generic German knights in white cloaks. They are not hired cavalry, crusading volunteers, household knights, mounted sergeants, or a free reward for constructing a church. The card survives because its people belong to a transregional institution, service follows an accepted Order mission, losses damage the Order's finite military capacity, recall remains possible, and replacement requires actual professed members, horses, equipment, command, and recovery.

## 2. One-Screen Total War Unit Card

- **Unit:** Teutonic Knight-Brothers
- **Class:** Elite Order heavy shock cavalry
- **Era:** Conditional post-emergence Order military development, never present at the 1062 opening
- **Source:** Professed Teutonic knight-brothers, recognized Order command, suitable trained warhorses, chronology-compatible armor and weapons, attendants, transport, supply, and an accepted military-service relationship
- **Core rhythm:** Assemble, Hold, Align, Commit, Master, Withdraw, Reconstitute
- **Battle promise:** Preserve the brethren in reserve, build one clean heavy-cavalry decision, break or overturn a critical target with a disciplined charge, control the immediate collision, and extract before the Order's rarest mounted capacity is trapped in attritional melee
- **Best use:** Heavy countercharge, decisive flank attack, destruction of committed cavalry, rupture of fixed or disordered infantry, relief of a threatened open-ground sector, and final elite reserve commitment
- **Strong against:** Ordinary cavalry, medium cavalry caught in committed contact, exposed missile troops, disordered infantry, engaged flanks, depleted heavy cavalry, and high-value targets without prepared spear protection
- **Competitive against:** Peer knightly cavalry and elite household cavalry when formation, ground, horse condition, leadership, support, and timing favor the brethren
- **Weak against:** Prepared spears and pikes, deep fresh infantry reserves, concentrated crossbow fire, missile crossfire, mud, woods, ditches, congestion, horse exhaustion, broken alignment, blocked withdrawal, and repeated heavy actions
- **Strengths:** Decisive conditional impact, strong heavy countercharge, disciplined reserve behavior, high morale, strong protection for the admitted chronology, recognizable command cohesion, and credible short post-impact mastery
- **Weaknesses:** Extreme scarcity, very high cost, finite professed membership, difficult horse and equipment replacement, slow recovery, mission limits, recall risk, and severe campaign consequences for wasteful losses
- **Campaign bargain:** Gain access to an institutionally coherent elite cavalry contingent without owning its members, but accept assembly cost, mission terms, Order control, finite replacement, possible recall, and damage to future access if the formation is squandered
- **Player instruction:** **Do not spend the brethren proving that they are elite. Hold them until one controlled commitment can change the battle, then bring them out alive.**
- **Flavor line:** **The brethren are not gathered for every fight. They are gathered for the moment that must be decided.**

## 3. Player-Facing Description

Teutonic Knight-Brothers are professed mounted warriors of the Teutonic Order, fielded only when the institution possesses enough qualified brethren, commanders, suitable horses, equipment, attendants, supply, and mission authority to form a real contingent. Their white-and-black identity makes them immediately recognizable, but their battlefield power comes from organization and material readiness rather than a supernatural aura.

Keep them behind the main line or on a protected wing while infantry and lighter cavalry shape the battlefield. When an enemy cavalry body commits, an infantry flank is fixed, or a high-value formation becomes exposed, align the brethren through clear ground and deliver a deliberate heavy charge. They can dominate the immediate collision and remain dangerous for a short mounted melee, but every extra moment in dense contact risks horses, leaders, weapons, and professed members the Order cannot quickly replace.

## 7. Exact Roster Ownership and Collision Firewall

### 7.1 German Hired Knights

German Hired Knights own rapid contract access, market competition, high wages, mixed personal origins, and employer flexibility. Teutonic Knight-Brothers own professed membership, Order mission, institutional command, recall, and Order-controlled replacement. Similar heavy-cavalry battlefield functions are intentional.

### 7.2 Domestic household or landed knights

Domestic knights own household, lineage, office, estate, obligation, or realm-facing political relationships. They may equal or exceed the brethren in equipment or battlefield quality. Knight-Brothers do not inherit local household recovery or automatic Realm ownership.

### 7.3 Teutonic Sergeant-Brothers and associated mounted personnel

Sergeant-brothers or associated mounted personnel may provide broader, cheaper, or more utilitarian mounted service. They do not become Knight-Brothers through experience alone and cannot refill the professed knight-brother pool automatically.

### 7.4 Other military orders

Templar, Hospitaller, Calatravan, Alcántaran, Santiago, or other order formations belong to their own institutions, rules, networks, missions, and recruitment sources. No universal order-knight hierarchy is created.

### 7.5 Dismounted Order personnel

Emergency or planned dismounted service uses the same people. A separate dismounted card is justified only if a genuinely distinct standing infantry source and role are proven without duplicating the mounted contingent.

## 16. Signature Battle Rhythm: Commit the Brethren

### 16.1 Hold

Preserve the contingent behind the line or on a protected flank. Maintain information, command connection, horse condition, and access to more than one lane. Do not expose the Order's rarest cavalry to low-value missile attrition or skirmishing.

### 16.2 Align

Set frontage, depth, pace, target, standards, and withdrawal reference. Clear friendly troops from the acceleration lane. A late wheel, masked target, or compressed approach produces uneven contact.

### 16.3 Commit

Deliver the charge or countercharge when the target, terrain, formation, weapons, support, and exit justify the risk. Once final acceleration begins, large corrections sharply reduce cohesion and impact.

### 16.4 Master

Exploit the immediate collision long enough to break enemy cavalry, widen a real rupture, displace a fixed formation, or protect friendly intervention. Mastery is short control after successful impact, not permission for endless melee.

### 16.5 Withdraw

Separate before fresh spears, reserves, missile concentration, fatigue, or congestion closes the route. Withdrawal is physical and can fail.

### 16.6 Rally and Reconstitute

Gather around surviving leaders and standards, account for rider-horse pairings and weapons, and decide whether a bounded second action remains possible. Reconstitution after battle restores the institution only through real replacement and recovery.

## 21. Strengths, Weaknesses, and Enemy Counterplay

### Strengths

- Decisive conditional first impact.
- Strong heavy countercharge and reserve timing.
- High formation cohesion while command and cadre survive.
- Strong period-appropriate protection without immunity.
- Credible short post-impact mounted mastery.
- High morale grounded in institution, leadership, preparation, and mutual confidence.
- Strong player-facing identity through finite Order ownership and mission service.
- Severe battlefield presence that forces enemy planning without a fear aura.

### Weaknesses

- Extremely scarce professed manpower.
- Very high assembly, upkeep, horse, equipment, and recovery burden.
- Slow replacement of leaders, brethren, horse pairings, and formation memory.
- Poor value in routine pursuit, screening, and skirmishing.
- Severe vulnerability to prepared anti-cavalry frontage and crossfire.
- Strong dependence on space, alignment, and withdrawal routes.
- Institutional recall and mission limits.
- Losses reduce future Order military capacity, not merely one army's strength.

### Enemy counterplay

- Maintain deep spear or pike frontage.
- Protect missile troops and flanks with reserves.
- Force the brethren to maneuver under fire.
- Block acceleration and rally lanes with terrain, stakes, bodies, or disciplined troops.
- Commit cavalry only with supporting depth.
- Threaten the withdrawal route after contact.
- Draw the unit into low-value pursuit.
- Attack Order logistics, assembly, horse supply, and service relationships on campaign.

## 22. Combined-Arms Contract

### With infantry

Spear and shield infantry fix enemy formations, protect the reserve, deny hostile cavalry, and provide a rally barrier. The brethren strike through an actual lane rather than passing through friendly troops.

### With missiles

Archers and crossbows force shields, injure horses, disrupt formations, or compel movement. Fire must redirect before the brethren enter the lane. No passive combined-arms bonus exists.

### With lighter cavalry

Scouts, mounted sergeants, and lighter horse reveal threats, screen missile skirmishers, shape enemy cavalry, protect reform, and control pursuit. Knight-Brothers should not waste themselves doing these tasks.

### With other heavy cavalry

Domestic or allied knights can pin, support, or exploit beside the brethren. Several heavy bodies need separate lanes and rally space. More cavalry does not make one congested approach wider.

## 23. Whole-Battle Role

### Opening

- Remain in protected reserve.
- Preserve horses, lances, command, and frontage.
- Observe enemy heavy cavalry and anti-cavalry positions.
- Let lighter troops shape the battlefield.

### Main battle

- Countercharge a committed cavalry threat.
- Strike an engaged flank or disordered formation.
- Relieve one critical open-ground sector.
- Preserve enough cohesion to master contact and withdraw.

### Late battle

- Destroy a spent high-value formation.
- Cover a necessary withdrawal.
- Exploit a real rupture without uncontrolled pursuit.
- Preserve surviving brethren and horses once the decision is secured.

## 37. Visual, Animation, Audio, and Card-Art Direction

### Visual silhouette

- Coherent white-and-black Teutonic identity without identical clothing or equipment.
- Strong mounted silhouette with lance, shield, mail-centered or chronology-compatible protection, and practical warhorses.
- Recognizable standards and restrained cross placement.
- Visible campaign wear, repairs, equipment variation, and horse variation.
- No horned helmets, fantasy black plate, universal great helms, identical white horses, or universal late barding.

### Animation

- Reserve files dressing around standards.
- Measured acceleration before full commitment.
- Uneven impact when terrain or alignment fails.
- Sidearm transition after lance loss or entrapment.
- Physical breakaway with vulnerable rear files.
- Rally that visibly depends on leaders, standards, surviving horses, and space.

### Audio

- Concise command, alignment, commitment, withdrawal, and rally calls.
- Distinct standard and command acknowledgement.
- No invented hostile religious slurs.
- No exaggerated supernatural choir or terror effect.
- Localization must preserve the distinction between knight-brothers, sergeant-brothers, associated personnel, and generic knights.

### Card-art composition

The final card art should show a compact body of Teutonic Knight-Brothers beginning a disciplined mounted commitment, with the black cross identity readable but not oversized. The composition should communicate weight, cohesion, and finite elite purpose rather than a solitary fantasy champion.

## 42. Final Design Decision

Teutonic Knight-Brothers now stand as a complete mature unit card rather than an institutional summary. The unit owns one clear battlefield proposition: **preserve a finite Order cavalry contingent, align it for a decisive commitment, master the immediate collision, and withdraw before elite power becomes permanent institutional loss.**

The card is distinctive without inventing a fantasy cavalry species. Its weapon and battlefield overlap with secular knights is deliberate. What makes it different is who owns the people, how service is obtained, how command and mission work, how recall can occur, and how casualties reduce the Teutonic Order's future capacity.
