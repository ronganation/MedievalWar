## 5. What Adalbero is and is not

### Adalbero begins as

- Bishop of Metz;
- a mature ecclesiastical principal;
- a dynastic brother of Frederick under the supported orientation;
- a potential mediator, intercessor, educator, institutional governor, and political correspondent;
- a person with pre-opening exposure to imperial and reform-era church politics;
- one canonical person across Metz, Luxembourg, Lower Lotharingia, Saarbrücken, and papal-history references.

### Adalbero does not begin as

- Frederick's vassal bishop;
- a guaranteed military ally;
- owner of the Lower Lotharingian duchy;
- family chaplain without independent jurisdiction;
- controller of every Metz cleric, abbey, property, levy, or fortress;
- a transferable office-holder whose bishopric can be inherited by the dynasty;
- an automatic supporter of war against Godfrey;
- a substitute for papal, royal, cathedral, chapter, or local institutional authority.

## 16.9 Success, compromise, and failure conditions

### Successful development

Adalbero preserves a functioning episcopal office, maintains documentary and institutional capacity, supports at least one useful educational or mediating function, and assists relatives without converting Metz into family property.

### Productive compromise

Adalbero remains focused on Metz and participates in Lower Lotharingian affairs only through occasional counsel, authentication, intercession, or mediation. Limited political visibility is not failure.

### Failure or overreach

The office can be compromised through dynastic capture, chapter resistance, lost seal custody, depleted resources, coerced military support, failed mediation, unsafe travel, or patronage promises that cannot be funded. Recovery requires specific institutional repair rather than a generic relationship increase.

## 20. Player-facing meaning

Adalbero gives the player access to family politics through a church principal who can help without belonging to the ducal command chain. The player can request mediation, education, authentication, refuge, patronage, or bounded institutional support. Every request may be conditioned, delayed, narrowed, or refused.

The character also demonstrates a future family mechanic: relatives can pursue secular, ecclesiastical, household, scholarly, or later military-order careers, but careers require people, institutions, formation, consent, opportunity, and resources. They are not menu-based disposal options for unwanted heirs.
