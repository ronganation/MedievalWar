## 2. Concise verdict

Ida begins as Frederick's Saxon-born second wife and the principal candidate to make the ducal household more resilient than the duke alone. Her strategic value lies in controlled access, intercession, household continuity, protection of vulnerable dynastic interests, and the ability to preserve communication and political coherence when Frederick is absent or under pressure. Her central tension is usefulness without automatic mandate: the stronger her household role becomes, the more carefully the faction must distinguish confidence, access, marriage, public authority, property, and succession.

## 3. Core character thesis

**The foreign-born duchess who can keep the household connected without becoming an automatic co-ruler.**

Ida enters Lower Lotharingia from a major Saxon ducal family and marries into a contested western imperial duchy. Ida can give the faction continuity, external perspective, household discipline, and a credible intercessory channel. None of those qualities grants independent rule by marriage. The campaign question is whether Ida becomes a trusted household anchor, a bounded political partner, a protected dynastic principal, an external mediator, or an isolated consort whose access exceeds accepted authority.

## 10. Personality and decision tendencies

Ida's personality must be written as bounded interpretation rather than invented inner truth.

### 10.1 Values

- preservation of household dignity;
- protection of dependents and guests;
- credible rank supported by competent conduct;
- discretion in sensitive disputes;
- reliable correspondence and authenticated instruction;
- negotiated solutions that keep multiple futures open;
- personal standing not wholly dependent on one political outcome.

### 10.2 Restraints and aversions

- public humiliation of the ducal household;
- being treated as a passive channel to Saxony;
- ambiguous custody of dependents or records;
- unauthorized use of Ida's name or seal;
- reckless military choices that expose the household;
- irreversible marriage or succession decisions made without evidence;
- being asked to carry public responsibility without resources or mandate.

### 10.3 Decision method

Ida should gather information through valid household channels, identify who owns the decision, seek a private audience or intermediary, test whether compromise protects the household, and escalate only when delay threatens a person, record, property interest, or succession option. Ida may refuse tasks that would make the consort responsible without recognized authority.

## 12. Opening strategic functions

### 12.1 Household continuity

Ida can help preserve access, authenticated communication, dependent care, hospitality, ceremony, and selected routine when Frederick is occupied or absent. Ida cannot replace the full government.

### 12.2 Intercession

Ida may receive petitions, recommend audiences, seek mercy, request delay, mediate family disputes, or convey concerns. Intercession works through access and persuasion. The final decision remains with the valid authority.

### 12.3 Information filtering

Ida may help distinguish urgent household danger from ordinary noise, but concentration of information around the consort risks delay, selective disclosure, resentment, or manipulation by courtiers.

### 12.4 Dynastic connection

Ida's Saxon origin may support introductions, hospitality requests, marriage discussions, correspondence, and external perspective. Saxony remains an external actor and owes no automatic response.

### 12.5 Continuity planning

Ida can contribute to contingency preparation for Frederick's campaign absence, incapacity, capture, or death. Preparation does not appoint Ida as regent.

## 20. Incapacity, capture, widowhood, and succession

### 20.1 Frederick incapacitated

Ida may secure Frederick's person, authenticate access, summon valid officers, protect dependents, and seek an acting-authority settlement. Ida cannot manufacture Frederick's instructions.

### 20.2 Frederick captured

Ida may support ransom, communication, guarantees, dependent protection, or negotiation. Capture does not transfer the duchy or authorize Ida to command all forces.

### 20.3 Frederick dies

Ida becomes a widow and major household-continuity actor if Ida is present and possesses access, resources, and recognition. Widowhood does not automatically create regency, inheritance, custody of Jutta, or control of direct property.

### 20.4 Succession crisis

Ida may support a candidate, argue for household security, mediate with Godfrey, seek royal protection, preserve records, or negotiate a property settlement. Ida's later historical marriage cannot be used to predetermine the choice.

## 21. Conditional development paths

### 21.1 Ducal household anchor

**Conditions:** stable presence, trusted access, competent attendants, secure correspondence, and cooperation from Frederick's officers.

**Benefits:** stronger continuity, clearer access, improved dependent protection, and better recovery after crises.

**Risks:** household centralization and resentment from public officers.

### 21.2 Intercessory duchess

**Conditions:** credible reputation, access to Frederick and institutions, and successful low-level mediation.

**Benefits:** peaceful dispute reduction, mercy, delayed escalation, and wider political acceptance.

**Risks:** petition overload, accusations of favoritism, and commitments beyond authority.

### 21.3 Saxon dynastic connector

**Conditions:** functioning routes, willing correspondents, and goals compatible with Saxon interests.

**Benefits:** introductions, refuge options, marriage discussions, external information, and diplomatic flexibility.

**Risks:** suspicion that Ida serves foreign priorities or overestimation of Saxon support.

### 21.4 Conditional acting partner

**Conditions:** Frederick's absence or incapacity, explicit mandate, recognized officers, and resources.

**Benefits:** reduced disruption and faster crisis response.

**Risks:** jurisdictional dispute, rival instructions, and public rejection.

### 21.5 Independent widow and settlement principal

**Conditions:** Frederick's death, surviving resources, recognized personal standing, and freedom from capture.

**Benefits:** protect dependents, negotiate property, seek refuge, sponsor settlement, or preserve a restoration option.

**Risks:** coercive remarriage, property seizure, isolation, or use as a dynastic token.

### 21.6 Namur future

A later relationship with Namur is one possible branch if Frederick dies and campaign circumstances support it. The path requires Ida's agency, valid communication, marriage negotiation, property and household settlement, and acceptance by relevant actors. It is not a scheduled event.

### 21.7 Marginalized consort

Restricted access, missing resources, court hostility, or separation from the household can reduce Ida's strategic function. Recovery may come through restored access, independent property, outside mediation, successful intercession, or a new household arrangement.

## 23. Operational interfaces and consequences

### 23.1 Correspondence

Ida can correspond only through available writers, messengers, routes, authentication, language support, and access. A message may be delayed, intercepted, ignored, or answered conditionally.

### 23.2 Travel

Travel requires escort, lodging, transport, funds, safe conduct, and seasonal opportunity. A consort's journey may remove continuity capacity from the principal household.

### 23.3 Hospitality

Hosting can build access and reputation but consumes food, lodging, staff, security, gifts, and attention.

### 23.4 Patronage

Patronage requires money, property, labor, materials, institutional consent, and follow-through. The profile creates no automatic foundation or building project.

### 23.5 Workload

Ida cannot simultaneously supervise household recovery, sustain foreign correspondence, mediate succession, travel, host a major delegation, and protect dependents without delegation and resources.

## 27. Player-facing strategic meaning

Ida gives the player a way to make the faction less dependent on Frederick without manufacturing a second ruler. The player decides whether to invest in Ida's household access, correspondence, intercession, independent resources, dependent protection, and crisis mandate.

The fantasy is not “the duchess provides passive bonuses.” The fantasy is **building a capable consort whose influence operates through people, access, records, resources, and accepted responsibility**.

Ida should normally surface zero to one major decision in an ordinary Realm turn, increasing during absence, captivity, succession, household division, or major diplomacy.

## 28. Decision and event hooks

1. **The Duchess's Household:** determine whether Ida receives dedicated attendants, clerical support, transport, and security.
2. **The Unanswered Saxon Letter:** decide whether to resend, change the messenger, seek another intermediary, or abandon the request.
3. **A Petition Through Ida:** accept intercession, refer to an officer, demand evidence, or refuse household bypass.
4. **Custody in Frederick's Absence:** separate protection, residence, education, property administration, and public authority.
5. **The Seal and the Message:** authenticate an instruction allegedly issued during military absence.
6. **Jutta's Protection:** Ida may offer intercession or protection without automatic custody.
7. **A Divided Household:** reconcile Ida's attendants and Frederick's officers over access or records.
8. **The Cost of Hospitality:** receive Saxon, ecclesiastical, or Lotharingian visitors while resources are strained.
9. **After Capture:** use Ida as a ransom and continuity principal without transferring the duchy.
10. **The Unfunded Mandate:** grant Ida authority only if the player can also provide staff, money, routes, and review.
11. **Godfrey's Approach:** Ida may mediate, report, refuse contact, or seek witnesses and guarantees.
12. **The Widow's Settlement:** preserve people, property, and options after Frederick's death.
13. **A Namur Proposal:** consider a later marriage only if campaign conditions create the opportunity.
14. **Duchess or Co-Ruler:** answer a public dispute over whether Ida's influence has exceeded accepted bounds.

## 28.2 Mandate, resources, and acceptance matrix

| Function | Mandate needed | Material support | Acceptance needed | Failure if missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Private intercession | Access or accepted custom | Messenger, audience, evidence | Decision-maker | Request ignored or resented |
| Household supervision | Defined household scope | Staff, food, money, lodging | Household members | Competing instructions |
| Correspondence routing | Custody or instruction | Clerks, seal process, messengers | Recipient and officers | Delay, forgery risk, nonrecognition |
| Council participation | Invitation or office-compatible mandate | Information and time | Council and ruler | Advice excluded or treated as interference |
| Acting responsibility | Explicit delegation | Staff, records, money, communications | Officers and affected audiences | Unfunded or contested government |
| Regency | Separate legal and political settlement | Full administrative support | Royal, local, household, and institutional audiences as applicable | Rival government and succession crisis |

The matrix is person-specific guidance, not a universal system contract. It makes clear that eligibility, readiness, mandate, resources, and acceptance are different conditions.

## 28.7 Success, compromise, and failure conditions

### Successful consort development

- Ida has a clear household scope;
- correspondence and access are reliable;
- intercession resolves selected problems without displacing valid offices;
- protected dynasts and records have contingency arrangements;
- Saxon and institutional contacts remain useful but nonautomatic;
- Frederick's absence does not collapse the household.

### Productive compromise

Ida remains principally ceremonial but has one or two dependable functions such as correspondence, dependent protection, or intercession. This is a valid outcome if the household maintains continuity through other officers.

### Failure or overreach

The household can fail through isolation, resource starvation, capture, access monopoly, competing seals or instructions, disputed custody, public rejection, or use of Ida as a foreign-interest symbol. Recovery requires specific changes rather than an abstract relationship increase.

- Ida is Jutta's biological mother.

- Marriage makes Ida regent or co-ruler.
- Frederick's death is scheduled for 1065.

## 34. Final design summary

Ida of Saxony begins as a strategically important but authority-bounded consort. Ida can make Frederick's rule more sustainable through household continuity, intercession, authenticated communication, dependent protection, and crisis preparation. Ida becomes powerful only when access, people, resources, and accepted mandate support action. The central risk is that the player either leaves the consort politically empty or concentrates so much access around Ida that the household becomes a rival or bottleneck to government.
