## 1. Strong historical verdict

**Frederick is the supported incumbent; Godfrey the Bearded is a displaced political alternative, not the opening duke of Lower Lotharingia. Neither position supplies a uniformly controlled duchy.**

The opened scholarly biographies place Frederick's appointment in 1046 and Godfrey's acquisition of Lower Lotharingia in 1065. This supports the project's distinction between incumbency and a later restoration pathway at year-level scholarly orientation. It does not establish either person's exact location, resources, current intentions, or January 1 relationship graph. [E1, E2; I01]

A particularly important terminology correction follows: Godfrey had held Upper Lotharingia before receiving Lower Lotharingia. The project's word “restoration” describes a displaced dynastic-ducal project and return to ducal standing; it must not imply that Godfrey previously held this same Lower Lotharingian office. A precise claim basis needs its own evidence and title-owner disposition. [E2]

The campaign can develop Frederick's effective government, Godfrey's return, coexistence, settlement, a different successor, or reduced survival. Historical reference outcomes explain the setting; they do not command the simulation. The military authority remains a general capability source. Roster architecture and unit production are deferred by the user's current direction.

## 3. Authority role and claim classes

Historical reconciliation classifies propositions. It does not execute property transfer, recognize a claim, appoint an officeholder, or instantiate a person. A well-supported biography can justify static orientation while leaving exact opening binding unresolved.

```yaml
ClaimClasses:
  SupportedScholarlyOrientation: SupportedAtStatedPrecisionNotAutomaticallyBound
  CandidateForOwnerAdmission: EvidenceAndIdentityReadyForSpecificOwnerReview
  Contested: CredibleOrMaterialSourceConflictPreserved
  Unknown: InsufficientEvidenceForPositiveOrNegativeFinding
  LaterHistory: ExcludedFromOpeningState
  ConditionalCampaignPath: ProposedDesignRequiresActualWorldState
  ExternalLocked: AnotherOwnerRetainsStateAndResponse
  OwnerHeld: ExactSpecialistDispositionNotRecreatedHere
  RejectedBackProjection: UnsupportedTransferOfLaterStateIntoOpening
EvidenceClasses:
  DirectOrNearContemporary: RequiresActualRecordAndContextReview
  ScholarlySynthesis: CriticalStudyOrScholarlyBiographyWithScopeLimits
  ReliableOrientation: BroadContextNotExactBinding
  ResearchLead: DiscoveryOnlyNotPositiveAdmission
  InternalDesign: ProjectDecisionNotIndependentHistoricalProof
```

Classes can combine: a marriage may be scholarly orientation, externally locked, and imprecise in date. Confidence, precision, temporal applicability, ownership, and permitted use are separate dimensions. “LaterHistory” is not “false”; it means inappropriate as opening state. “Unknown” is not “vacant,” “zero,” “absent,” “neutral,” or “unlimited.”

## 7. Frederick: incumbency without fabricated resources

E1's biographical body explicitly attributes the 1046 grant to Henry III and identifies broad Moselgau, Ardennes, and advocacy interests. Those statements support a nonempty research portfolio; they do not prove that every listed right was exercised on January 1, 1062, or held as public ducal property. [E1]

Separate Frederick the person, his house, private property, the ducal dignity, delegated responsibilities, and actual working government. An inherited asset is not necessarily a public account. A recognized title is not evidence of a staffed administration or fixed capital. [I01, I09, I10]

The scholarly source identifies Judith as a daughter and discusses later descendants. Its retrospective statement about no male descendants does not authorize a forced childlessness outcome or a campaign succession rule. Exact opening family membership, marriage dates, and dependency remain character/dynasty questions. [E1; I09, I10]

Frederick's recorded death in 1065 belongs to reference history, not a timer. Opening survival orientation does not guarantee survival to that year, and a divergent campaign may continue his government beyond it.

## 8. Godfrey the Bearded: displacement, reconciliation, and alternatives

E2 supports a politically important figure whose career included conflict, imprisonment, reconciliation, and Italian power. That is stronger and more varied than a permanently hostile rebel archetype. Past alliance with Flanders or conflict with the royal government does not establish a January 1062 alliance or war.

“Restoration principal” remains a useful project identity. It does not create an active legal claim, universally recognized entitlement, fixed restoration objective, or an opening invasion. His earlier Upper ducal tenure and later Lower ducal tenure must remain separate. [E2; I07]

E2 describes Cologne-related fiefs after earlier release. That is a research lead for specific property and institutional relations, not proof that Godfrey owns Cologne, commands its forces, or has unrestricted Rhine access. Likewise, marriage to Beatrice does not merge Tuscany with the Realm.

Valid proposed campaign paths include service, mediation, patience, negotiated return, coexistence, withdrawal, or conflict. Those are design alternatives, not assertions about Godfrey's private opening intent.

## 11. Direct-core reconciliation: Baelen and Limbourg-sur-Vesdre

The direct-core specialist already leaves construction sequence, exact property, and opening physical state unresolved. This authority preserves that caution and adds explicit source tensions. [I02]

E1 attributes castle construction within Judith's possessions to Udo-Walram. E4 instead describes an earlier castle and places the associated possessions with Frederick around 1050, followed by Judith's marriage transfer; it also doubts Walram's identity with Udo. E5's search excerpt attributes construction around 1020 to Frederick on inherited Baelen property. These accounts cannot be flattened into one confident building history. [E1, E4, E5]

**Disposition:** Frederick's broad Limburg-property association has scholarly support; exact acquisition, construction, builder, marriage-transfer date, identity chain, and January 1062 possession remain contested or owner-held. Baelen's precise inheritance and circa-1020 construction narrative remain leads pending stronger direct comparison.

Do not infer that Frederick still directly possessed every property associated with his family, that Judith's transfer occurred only at his death, or that a complete later castle stood at opening. A possible property core is not a chosen capital, garrison, treasury, population, or later duchy.

## 12. Advocacy and institutional history

Its geographic wording for Saint Trudo requires identification review rather than being treated as an exact site allocation. [E1]

Stavelot-Malmedy and St Truiden remain separate institutional portfolios. An advocacy can involve protection or representation without transferring the institution, its land, its population, or its forces to the duke. An institution's own record can support a right while preserving a dispute about its extent. [I01, I02]

The evidence task is proposition-specific: who held which right, when, over what object, under which instrument or custom, with what burden, and with what competing account? No blanket “Church support” or duplicated institutional revenue is admitted.

## 26B. Opening-admissibility decision matrix

A proposition is not opening-admissible merely because it is historically plausible or appears in a scholarly biography. Admission requires a subject match, compatible date, adequate precision, independent source understanding, and a receiving owner capable of using the proposition without inventing adjacent state.

| Decision | Minimum condition | Permitted consequence | Prohibited consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Admit as opening orientation | Identified subject; source explicitly supports a state spanning or applicable to January 1, 1062 at the stated precision | Owner may use the scoped proposition as historical orientation | No automatic location, quantity, relationship, asset, or action |
| Admit as owner-review candidate | Evidence is relevant and sufficiently bounded but exact binding belongs elsewhere | Route proposition and exclusions to the correct owner | No direct mutation by reconciliation authority |
| Hold as contested | Material sources disagree or identity/date/property chain remains unresolved | Preserve attributed alternatives and research burden | No averaging, convenience choice, or combined synthetic account |
| Hold as unknown | Evidence does not establish a positive or negative answer | Preserve uncertainty and identify the missing evidence | No zero, vacancy, neutrality, unlimited capacity, or absence inference |
| Exclude as later history | Proposition belongs after January 1, 1062 | Retain as reference chronology or conditional campaign comparison | No opening state, timer, trait, inventory, or guaranteed outcome |
| Reject as back-projection | Later status, fame, title, territory, relationship, or result is being moved backward without evidence | Record the excluded inference where materially recurring | No hidden use in balance, map allocation, AI goals, or narrative copy |
| Route as external-locked | Exact state belongs to another Realm, Theater, institution, character, or shared owner | Create a directed evidence interface | No copied external private state or presumed response |

```yaml
OpeningAdmissibilityTest:
  required_questions:
    - IsTheHistoricalSubjectUnambiguouslyIdentified
    - DoesTheSourceExplicitlySupportTheClaimRatherThanMerelyMentionTheSubject
    - IsTheClaimApplicableToJanuaryFirst1062AtItsActualPrecision
    - IsTheSourceIndependentOfOtherCitedRetellings
    - AreMaterialContradictionsAndTransmissionLimitsRecorded
    - IsThePermittedUseNarrowerThanOrEqualToTheEvidence
    - IsTheReceivingOwnerIdentified
    - AreAdjacentInferencesExplicitlyWithheld
  failure_default: HoldOrExcludeNotInvent
  passing_test_creates_opening_binding: false
```

An owner may later admit a proposition at lower precision than the evidence appears to permit, but not at higher precision without additional evidence. A year-level tenure can support an opening incumbent orientation when the source establishes continuity across the opening; a year-level action later in 1062 cannot support January 1 presence or intent.
