## 1. Diplomatic thesis and player promise

**Turn contested dignity and fragmented geography into dependable cooperation without promising more than the Realm can deliver.**

The general Realm authority identifies brokered frontier diplomacy: royal recognition, western counterweights, independent institutions, negotiated service, protected routes, and cross-Theater household interests. It explicitly rejects kinship-driven alliances, border-driven hostility, shared political-order wars, and religious omniscience. [S1]

The proposed player experience is practical Total War diplomacy with selective medieval causality. Seek peace, trade, passage, support, recognition, mediation, or war through clear choices. People, offices, institutions, property, routes, resources, and household obligations explain the consequences without turning each diplomatic act into a paperwork minigame.

A strong campaign may make the duke a reliable royal intermediary, a western broker, a selective coalition partner, or an autonomous frontier power. These are compatible development channels, not a forced east-west alignment fork. Success can mean a useful corridor, a narrower service obligation, a survivable settlement, or a dependable partner rather than territorial annexation.

## 4. Diplomatic distinctions with campaign consequences

|---|---|
| Contact / audience / recognition | A meeting channel does not concede a title or policy. |
| Personal relationship / household channel / public mandate | A relative's hospitality cannot bind the Realm. |
| Recognition / alliance / service | Acceptance of dignity does not supply troops. |
| Proposal / agreement / performance | Accepted terms still need actual execution. |
| Gift / subsidy / tribute / compensation / ransom | Purpose and conditions differ; money does not automatically buy loyalty or release. |
| Route / permission / security / supply | A map connection is not a supported expedition. |

## 6. Royal service without automatic alignment

S1 makes royal confirmation, counsel, service, mediation, and resistance to interference legitimate campaign surfaces while rejecting a universal Empire Realm. This authority proposes a negotiable service portfolio: clarify the requested contribution, identify who can commit it, reserve feasible resources, negotiate timing or substitution, and record the actual response.

The player may gain access or recognition through service while weakening home defense. Refusal can concern capacity, scope, or timing rather than universal rebellion. A royal request is not an available force; a favorable audience is not a guaranteed appointment or rescue. No fixed electoral college, mature assembly structure, or opening royal crisis is created.

A useful royal bargain should state what the duke offers and what the counterpart actually accepts. Expected reward remains an expectation until a valid counterpart act exists. The political-order owner retains the royal decision and its legal effect.

## 7. Cologne, Liège, Trier, and Aachen: separate endpoints

S1 explicitly preserves these as independent multi-owner interfaces. Proposed negotiation should identify whether the counterpart is a person, office, institution, temporal government, settlement representative, or royal-site authority. A bishop's statement cannot automatically bind every urban actor, estate, or military source.

Possible choices include limited passage, mediation, protection, safe audience, institutional access, and a bounded contribution. A jurisdiction dispute may coexist with practical cooperation. Shared faith does not provide intelligence, permission, or political alignment. Aachen's ceremonial importance does not make it a free ducal headquarters.

Exact officeholders, charters, routes, and opening reception remain unresolved. These portfolios explain how the actors can matter without making each a full playable faction or absorbing them into the duke's government.

## 8. The two advocacies as diplomatic obligations

Stavelot-Malmedy and St Truiden receive separate negotiations, demands, protection commitments, and performance reviews. St Truiden's Liège interface remains unresolved. [S7]

The player may clarify an obligation, offer a feasible escort, request mediation, negotiate delay, or correct overreach. A single escort cannot be promised as two simultaneous completed missions. Protection can improve cooperation while a jurisdiction dispute remains open.

Diplomacy coordinates the offer, acceptance, and reported performance; institutional, legal, military, and material owners determine rights and effects. No abbey treasury, force, appointment, or spiritual discipline is acquired through a protection agreement.

## 9. Namur, Luxembourg, Hainaut-Mons, and local contributors

The strategic bargain is reach without full administration. The duke can accept a smaller reliable contribution rather than a large nominal obligation, or pay for access instead of seeking permanent control. A contributor may cooperate in one campaign while declining another. The same-Theater label does not establish friendship, contact, or common knowledge.

No count is made a universal vassal and no community response is inferred from an elite agreement. Current Hainaut and Flanders authorities retain their own identities and state.

## 10. Antwerp, Flanders, and western frontier choices

A western partner can be useful against one threat while competing over another objective. The player may seek neutrality, a limited contribution, market access, a safe meeting, or a temporary corridor. Choosing cooperation with Flanders does not automatically mean opposition to royal government.

The key cost is commitment density: a corridor may expose local obligations, require escorts, or constrain operations. Exact places, tolls, garrisons, and agreements remain owner-bound. No frontier war is created by geography.

## 15. Messages, delivery, authentication, and secrecy

Proposed message states include prepared, authorized, entrusted, dispatched, in transit, delayed, lost, delivered, authenticated, interpreted, acknowledged, answered, corrected, superseded, expired, and archived. These are a design vocabulary, not an implemented schema. [S2]

Dispatch creates no knowledge at the destination. Authentication connects a message to an accepted source but does not prove every assertion true. A sealed offer can be stale, incomplete, mistranslated, or beyond its bearer’s mandate. Oral and written channels need not be treated as identical.

Actual messengers, interpreters, transport, lodging, security, and record custody come from their owners. No free courier network is created. Disclosure to one actor does not make information public. Interception, deception, or a leak requires supported means and owner state; this file creates no spy organization or opening forged letter.

## 21. Trade, payments, protection, and material diplomacy

Trade access can create opportunity without creating goods or demand. A subsidy may support a specific contribution without buying command. A gift is not automatically tribute; compensation is not a fine; ransom payment is not release. [S2]

Every material term needs a payer, recipient, actual object/resource, entitlement or consent basis, timing, transport, custody, and performance status. A promisor cannot dispose of another owner's property or an abbey treasury. An uncollectable claim is not spendable cash.

Protection and guarantees require beneficiary, threat, scope, duration, actual capacity, and exit/review terms. Guarantee is not automatic intervention. A witness need not be an enforcer. Failed protection can create a specific dispute without turning every institution hostile.

## 22. Coalitions, alliances, neutrality, and separate peace

A proposed coalition identifies its objective, participants, accepted contributions, decision rules, command, information sharing, access, finance, duration, withdrawal, separate peace, and recovery. Membership does not create war entry; war entry does not create a contribution; contribution does not transfer command. [S2]

The player may prefer limited cooperation, nonaggression, neutrality, mediation, or a single campaign contribution over a broad alliance. Neutrality is not necessarily permanent or universally accepted; it needs a defined issue and known conduct. No party's silence is treated as agreement.

Separate peace can preserve the core while damaging a coalition bargain, depending on actual terms and reception. It must not automatically demobilize other parties or erase outstanding rewards. A coalition victory can leave incompatible promises and a difficult settlement rather than universal friendship.

## 23. War freedom, escalation, and operational consequences

**War is not locked behind a claim, casus belli, Papal permission, or universal legal approval.** This preserves the explicit design boundary in S3. Claims and standing can affect reception, coalition choices, conduct, and settlement, not whether the strategic action is unlocked.

The diplomacy owner processes the war choice; military owners execute operations using actual forces, command, access, supply, and current world state. A declaration does not conjure an army. A claimant's existence, refusal, alleged breach, historical date, or shared political order does not automatically declare war.

Protest, demand, mediation, mobilization, limited hostility, wider war, ceasefire, and negotiation are available design states, not a mandatory ladder. The player can choose conflict without first exhausting every diplomatic step. The interface should explain the known objective, costs, third-party exposure, and exit options without requiring the target's consent to be attacked.

S2's mandate, recognition, notice, and capacity review is consumed as representation, execution, and consequence discipline. It must not be implemented as a hidden legal-permission gate. Exact game action rules remain with the shared diplomacy owner.

Proposed terms include cessation, movement, withdrawal, prisoners, custody, property, compensation, access, recognition, guarantees, demobilization, renewal, breach, and monitoring. The title authority owns political dispositions; law owns legal remedies; diplomacy records accepted terms and performance without duplicating their state.

## 25. Custody, guarantees, ransom, and protected people

Protected person, guest, dependent, hostage, detainee, and prisoner remain distinct states under S3. Diplomacy can negotiate terms; person, household, custody, law, military, and material owners retain the effects.

A proposed release agreement identifies the person, actual custodian, authority, conditions, payment if any, timing, escort, return access, and failure handling. Payment does not itself release a captive; release does not guarantee safe return or restore office/property.

No person is a transferable diplomatic resource. A principal's captivity does not transfer the duchy, external household, or every mandate. Guarantees require actual accepted obligations and capacity rather than automatic punishment or intervention. No opening captive or hostage arrangement is created.

## 26. Office change, succession, and reduced diplomatic survival

Review personal, office-bound, title-bound, institutional, and transaction-specific terms separately after death, removal, incapacity, captivity, or settlement. A title change does not reset all relationships; a household connection does not inherit every public commitment. [S4, S5]

Frederick's household may retain channels after title loss where supported. A restored duke may gain recognition while lacking access to the core. The younger Godfrey may need fresh confirmation rather than inherit the father's coalition. None of these possibilities is an opening assignment.

Pending messages can become stale or require renewed credentials. Already completed performance remains completed. Resources in custody, safe conducts in use, and obligations due require handback/review rather than a clean reset. Historical dates do not install a successor or replay diplomacy.

## 27. Diplomatic development channels and tradeoffs

| Channel | Useful capability | Strategic price |
|---|---|---|
| Royal intermediary | Access, mediation, scoped confirmation | Service burden and exposure at home |
| Western broker | More options than a simple royal/rival binary | Competing commitments and corridor dependence |
| Institutional protector | Credible limited protection and mediation | Restraint, finite escorts, and remedy obligations |
| Core-first negotiator | Smaller dependable agreements and recovery | Less symbolic reach and fewer distant opportunities |
| Coalition concentrator | Temporary force and leverage at a decisive objective | Command friction, rewards, supply, and separate-peace risk |
| Cross-Theater household diplomacy | Communication, refuge, service, settlement alternatives | Distance, agency, divided resources, and external ownership |
| Armed frontier bargaining | Operational leverage and defended access | War costs, damaged cooperation, and difficult peace |

Channels can coexist by issue. Investments improve supported communication, representation, or capacity through their owners; they do not purchase instant friendship or spawn diplomatic staff.

## 28. Developed player decisions

### The King's Request, the Core's Need

**Trigger:** a known royal request competes with a supported home obligation. **Choices:** bounded service, substitution if accepted, delay, negotiation, or refusal. **Cost:** access and standing versus home exposure. No royal reward or retaliation is guaranteed.

### An Audience, Not an Endorsement

**Trigger:** a counterpart offers a meeting without conceding the disputed proposition. **Choices:** attend with limited credentials, seek clarification, send a supported representative, or decline. **Cost:** travel and attention versus an uncertain opening. Attendance does not create recognition.

### Passage Without Provisions

**Trigger:** a corridor is offered without supply or billeting. **Choices:** arrange actual support, narrow the force, negotiate additional terms, choose another route, or cancel. **Cost:** speed versus sustainable operations. Permission does not create food, carts, or animals.

### Two Abbeys, One Escort

**Trigger:** separate accepted protection needs compete for the same available escort. **Choices:** prioritize, negotiate timing, split only if viable, or seek supported alternatives. **Cost:** protection performance versus core security. No duplicate escort or automatic universal hostility.

### A Western Friend in One Matter

**Trigger:** a western actor offers limited cooperation while another dispute remains. **Choices:** accept the bounded term, bundle a wider negotiation, seek neutrality, or refuse. **Cost:** useful cooperation versus unresolved exposure. One agreement does not create a full alliance.

### The Envoy Has Promised Too Much

**Trigger:** a known offer exceeds the representative's mandate. **Choices:** clarify, ratify only through valid owners, renegotiate, replace the envoy, or withdraw. **Cost:** credibility and reliance versus undeliverable terms. Neither automatic validation nor erasure is allowed.

### The Rival's Safe Meeting

**Trigger:** a restoration-facing negotiation has a feasible channel. **Choices:** bounded safe conduct, mediated exchange, supported representation, or no meeting. **Cost:** possible compromise versus security and recognition ambiguity. No restoration or war is scripted.

### A Letter from the South

**Trigger:** an actually received cross-Theater message presents an opportunity. **Choices:** verify, answer through a supported envoy, prioritize the core, or decline. **Cost:** external options versus distance and divided commitments. No Tuscan property or forces transfer.

### Payment Sent, Performance Missing

**Trigger:** a material transfer is confirmed but the reciprocal act is not. **Choices:** seek evidence, negotiate cure, invoke a valid guarantee, pursue remedy, or terminate within terms. **Cost:** recovery effort versus escalation. Nonperformance is not automatically deliberate betrayal.

### The Coalition's Separate Peace

**Trigger:** one participant seeks an exit from a current conflict. **Choices:** negotiate release, revise contributions, accept supported compensation, or contest under actual terms. **Cost:** core survival versus collective leverage. Other participants do not automatically demobilize.

### A Truce Before the Harvest Returns

**Trigger:** fighting can pause while material recovery remains incomplete. **Choices:** limited truce, staged withdrawal, protected access, wider peace talks, or continued war. **Cost:** immediate leverage versus recovery. No harvest amount, date, or automatic resource restoration is invented.

### The Duke Changes, the Promise Remains

**Trigger:** a holder change affects outstanding terms. **Choices:** confirm, narrow, renegotiate, or contest through relevant owners. **Cost:** continuity versus inherited liabilities. The new holder does not inherit every private relationship or erase completed performance.

All twelve are proposed reusable content families, not active opening events. They should produce distinct strategic choices, not routine clerk approvals.

## 29. Failure, nonoccurrence, and recovery

| Failure | Proposed response | Residue preserved |
|---|---|---|
| Lost or stale message | Renew, verify, correct, or abandon | Prior uncertainty and missed opportunity |
| Invalid credentials | Narrow mandate, replace, seek ratification | Counterpart's known reliance and delay |
| Refused audience | Different channel, mediation, patience, withdrawal | No inferred universal hostility |
| Overpromised contribution | Renegotiate, substitute, stage, or release | Resource cost and performance history |
| Access revoked | Review terms, negotiate exit, reroute | Forces/material already in transit |
| Protection fails | Verify harm, remedy, narrower future promise | Institutional grievance and loss |
| Coalition fragments | Reassess objective, separate terms, peace | Outstanding rewards and contributions |
| Peace term blocked | Partial performance, mediation, cure | Completed independent terms |
| Principal removed or captive | Review credentials, continuity, and safe access | Person/title/property separation |
| Opportunity never occurs | Record no agreement or no action | No forced historical replacement event |

Recovery can be partial or fail. Apology, compensation, changed representatives, or renewed access does not automatically restore trust or erase memory. No universal reputation reset or free replacement army is created.

## 32. Reciprocal receipt and counterparty-state boundary

An outgoing Lower Lotharingian act can record only the Realm's own authorization, transmission, and known claim about the act. Foreign receipt, interpretation, acceptance, refusal, reservation, performance, or silence must come from the counterparty owner or a valid observed communication.

## 33. Agreement collision, reservation, and overcommitment control

The authority already prohibits counting one resource as several completed performances. The stronger control below catches overcommitment before execution and keeps incompatible promises visible after they are made.

```yaml
CommitmentCollisionControl:
  collision_checks:
    - SamePersonOfficeOrRepresentativeOverlappingMandates
    - SameMoneyGoodsStoresEscortShipsAnimalsOrContingentReservedTwice
    - ConflictingPassageNeutralityCoalitionOrSeparatePeaceTerms
    - PropertyOfficeInstitutionOrClaimPromisedWithoutAuthority
    - IncompatibleEffectiveWindowsOrGeographicScopes
    - PromiseExceedingKnownSupplyTransportOrRecoveryCapacity
  collision_does_not_automatically_void_all_terms: true
  promised_resource_equals_reserved_resource: false
  reserved_resource_equals_delivered_performance: false
  later_commitment_silently_supersedes_earlier_commitment: false
  overcommitment_may_require:
    - Clarification
    - Prioritization
    - Renegotiation
    - SubstitutionIfAccepted
    - ReleaseOrTerminationUnderTerms
    - BreachAndRecoveryReview
```

A collision can be political rather than material. Neutrality promised toward one actor may conflict with coalition duties toward another. Passage granted to opposing forces may be possible only under carefully contracted scope. The system should identify the affected terms and owners instead of resolving every conflict through an invisible priority rule.
