## 1. Strong thesis: protection that becomes political weight

**Make protection useful, patronage durable, and institutional cooperation worth preserving.**

Lower Lotharingia's proposed church-facing identity is a protector and broker operating among institutions that retain their own interests. The player can turn limited ducal resources into wider political reach by defending a threatened estate, making a protection agreement perform, supporting documentary capacity, negotiating restitution, or bringing competing parties to a settlement. That reach is earned through concrete action, not purchased as universal piety.

The reward must be substantial enough to matter in a Total War-style campaign: a safer route, a dependable communication channel, a functioning place of refuge, accepted mediation, an actual contribution, better-supported clerical education, or a repaired relationship that a rival can no longer exploit easily. These are conditional possibilities, not guaranteed benefits from a religious building.

The central tension is not simply good protector versus bad exploiter. A duke may need supplies while an institution needs the same reserves for dependents. An institution may possess a persuasive record but lack the capacity to enforce a settlement. A reform initiative may strengthen administration while upsetting an established patronage network. Restraint can be strategically expensive; coercion can deliver immediate gains while creating long-lived disputes.

The player remains free to contest rights, exert pressure, wage war, or expand. This authority exposes causes and consequences rather than requiring every actor to approve every action.

Protection can involve an escort, defense of a particular place, support for restitution, temporal representation, or preservation of a threatened route where the relevant basis exists. The player should see what is being protected and what is left exposed elsewhere.

Protection failure has a cause: unavailable troops, delayed communication, route closure, unaffordable support, contradictory instructions, enemy action, or a promise beyond the mandate. The remedy should address that cause. A failed escort is not repaired merely by spending money on an unrelated religious project.

A protector may also overreach after success. Leaving an unwanted garrison, treating hospitality as permanent quartering, or expanding a temporary contribution into a standing demand can convert military usefulness into institutional resistance. Those are proposed conflict pathways, not opening accusations against any named actor.

## 7. Patronage, endowment, and commemoration

Patronage should provide durable choices beyond a one-click favor purchase. Proposed portfolios include repairing damaged institutional property, supporting education, preserving records, sustaining hospitality, sponsoring commemoration, or funding a justified building project through the appropriate owners.

| Investment | Potential useful result | Cost or competing interest |
|---|---|---|
| Repair a damaged institutional function | Restore access, service, or productive continuity | Labor, materials, transport, and time compete with ducal needs |
| Support clerical education | More capable candidates and documentary service over time | Education does not guarantee office or continued family alignment |
| Preserve records | Better continuity and access to evidence | Custody, authenticity, staff, and security still matter |
| Endow a supported activity | Sustained capacity where resources and acceptance exist | Property rights and long-term obligations require explicit treatment |
| Support commemoration | Audience-specific memory and dynastic association | No universal loyalty or legitimacy reward |
| Sponsor hospitality or relief | Bounded support for actual travelers or displaced people | Finite provisions and local burdens remain visible |

A donation must distinguish pledge, acceptance, actual transfer, intended use, and performed result. A promised endowment is not available income. A patron does not gain unrestricted appointment, treasury access, or command because a project was funded.

## 8. Property, income, dues, and contribution

Institutional resources remain institutional until an actual supported transfer or allocation occurs. The same grain, money, labor, transport, or equipment cannot fund an abbey function and a ducal campaign simultaneously.

A contribution may be requested, negotiated, disputed, reduced, or refused. The response can depend on available resources, recognized basis, local obligations, protection performance, and institutional priorities. Refusal alone is not rebellion or proof of hostility.

The player can seek short-term extraction under pressure, including coercive action through the relevant systems. Such action must be presented honestly rather than relabeled voluntary support. Potential consequences include disrupted activity, arrears, restitution claims, damaged cooperation, local resistance, and rival political opportunity. No exact penalty or inevitable reaction is assigned here.

Immunity, exemption, due, estate income, temporal jurisdiction, and settlement control require their own supported scope. Unknown terms do not become unlimited ducal rights or universal institutional immunity.

## 9. Jurisdiction and dispute settlement

The proposed dispute model begins with the specific subject: property, service, access, office competence, custody, payment, or protection performance. Identify who asserts which right, what evidence is available, and which actors have competence to hear or perform a settlement.

Possible responses include negotiated adjustment, documentary review, mediation, temporary standstill, restitution, referral, limited enforcement, or coercive escalation with consequences. This is a gameplay portfolio, not a claim that every institution used the same historical procedure.

A settlement distinguishes agreement from performance. A duke may accept restitution but lack immediate means to deliver it. An institution may accept a mediator without accepting the mediator's proposed terms. A successful armed intervention may remove a threat without deciding title or jurisdiction.

Conflicting records remain conflicting until resolved through evidence and relevant owners. A document's claim of priority is not sufficient by itself to erase another record. Preserve uncertainty, competing claims, and any material consequences already applied.

## 10. Sanctuary, refuge, hospitality, and safe conduct

These are distinct proposed functions requiring local historical support before exact rules are bound. Do not assume every religious site offers the same sanctuary rights or unlimited refuge.

Refuge concerns actual people, access, shelter, provisions, security, and duration. Sanctuary claims concern applicable jurisdiction and recognition. Hospitality concerns supported reception and burden. Safe conduct concerns a particular undertaking and its performance, not invulnerability.

A household seeking refuge can create a strong campaign dilemma: accept a local burden, negotiate escort elsewhere, seek mediation, or decline when capacity is insufficient. A refuge site can be endangered by conflict; religious status does not create a force field or teleport vulnerable people to safety.

The player should not receive unrestricted access to a protected person's private information. Custody, release, onward travel, and return remain distinct. No named refugee, prisoner, or opening sanctuary dispute is created here.

## 13. Clerical education and career development

Education and clerical service are meaningful development routes, not instant methods of removing an inconvenient heir. A candidate needs actual identity, eligibility, formation, support, opportunity, and institutional acceptance. Succession effects remain with the relevant legal and character authorities.

Potential outcomes include education without high office, documentary service, household or institutional clerical work, monastic or cathedral service where supported, senior-office candidacy, rejection, withdrawal, or a career whose institutional obligations diverge from family priorities.

Investment should create capacity rather than guaranteed results. Supporting instructors or records can improve the environment for future service without spawning qualified clerks. A candidate's progress requires actual formation and opportunity. Later military-order pathways remain conditional and do not create an opening recruitment option or unit.

## 14. Reform as competing institutional priorities

Reform is proposed as several interacting channels, not a binary reformist-versus-corrupt faction switch. No later famous controversy is preloaded as an opening crisis.

| Channel | Proposed aim | Political tension |
|---|---|---|
| Documentary reliability | Better records, custody, and continuity | Challenges actors benefiting from ambiguous arrangements |
| Clerical formation | Better-supported service and qualified candidates | Costs resources and may weaken easy family placement |
| Institutional independence | Protect office and property from inappropriate capture | Can constrain patrons and advocates who previously expected access |
| Discipline and administration | Address a supported institutional problem | Competence, acceptance, evidence, and local practice can be disputed |
| Patronage accountability | Make promised support and obligations perform | Exposes unfulfilled promises or conflicting claims |
| Protection restraint | Keep defense from becoming appropriation | Reduces immediate extraction options while supporting longer-term cooperation |

A ruler may support one channel while resisting another. Institutions can disagree among themselves. Reform can fail through poor evidence, lack of staff, overreach, coercion, or inability to sustain the change. A successful reform should have a concrete functional result, not merely a higher piety score.

## 15. Royal, papal, episcopal, and neighboring interfaces

The player can petition, seek recognition, transmit evidence, request mediation, or contest a claim through supported channels. An audience is not a favorable ruling; a ruling is not local performance; a letter is not instant compliance.

S1 specifically retains a Liège-facing question for St Truiden. This file does not resolve that relationship, allocate its rights, or absorb external private state. S2 similarly preserves Metz's independent responsibilities. Royal and wider church audiences can matter without acting as one synchronized bloc.

Ecclesiastical sanctions and recognition disputes, if later admitted, require the relevant authority, subject, grounds, communication, and supported effects. No universal excommunication button, instant deposition, automatic army dissolution, or mandatory permission to wage war is introduced. War does not universally require a claim, papal approval, or target consent; political and institutional consequences remain separate.

## 16. Protection during war and recovery afterward

War can threaten estates, communities, archives, messengers, hospitality, and productive activity. Protection needs actual force, routes, command, supply, and time. This authority creates requests and choices, not troops.

A garrison diverted to institutional relief leaves another place less defended. An escort protecting records cannot simultaneously guard a distant convoy. An institution's contribution can support a campaign while weakening its own relief capacity. These tradeoffs should remain legible without requiring a new administrative screen for every resource movement.

After conflict, distinguish threat removal, return, repair, restitution, restored access, reopened activity, and relationship recovery. A repaired building may lack staff; returned records may be incomplete; an accepted restitution promise may remain unpaid. Recovery can restore a useful reduced function rather than reset every institution to its prior condition.

No actual unit profile, roster card, recruitment pool, opening force, or institutional army is authored here.

## 19. AI behavior and institutional agency

The ducal AI should pursue useful cooperation, not accumulate endless unfunded promises. It can invest, negotiate, contest, coerce, or withdraw according to actual objectives and capacity. It should preserve institutional distinctions and recognize that a smaller performed commitment can be more useful than a grand promise that fails.

Institutional AI should defend supported interests, maintain useful functions, evaluate requests, seek mediation, and preserve continuity. It may cooperate with a powerful advocate without surrendering every right. It may refuse a demand without becoming a military enemy. It may accept a practical compromise without forgetting an unresolved claim.

Adalbero's behavior remains governed by the character authority and actual Metz context. No hidden enemy knowledge, free institutional money, automatic troop contribution, universal papal reaction, or instant replacement officeholder is allowed as an AI shortcut.

Recovery behavior needs a feasible next action: limited restitution, restored communication, reduced service, repaired capacity, or revised terms. Neither duke nor institution should remain permanently inert because a perfect settlement is unavailable.

## 20. Development and player experience

The proposed development loop is: inspect a concrete institutional need, choose a bounded intervention, perform it with actual resources, observe the result, and decide whether to deepen or revise the relationship.

Development channels include more reliable protection, better documentary support, sustained patronage, qualified clerical formation, stronger mediation access, and more resilient post-conflict recovery. These can coexist. No universal cultural fork or compulsory church-control path is introduced.

Suggested original player-facing summary:

> The duchy reaches beyond its own estates through protection, patronage, and negotiated cooperation. Make those relationships perform, and institutions can become enduring partners. Treat every abbey as your treasury, and the same network can carry grievances to your rivals.

Routine actions should aggregate. Escalate contested jurisdiction, major commitments, severe shortages, coercion, vacancy, and irreversible losses. Display the institution, request, supported basis, available response, strongest constraint, affected duty, and performed outcome. Avoid a universal piety or Church-control meter that conceals those causes.

## 22. Persistence, partial failure, and handoff requirements

Future implementation must distinguish institutional definitions, office instances, people, property rights, agreements, requests, resource transfers, disputes, and performed outcomes. A definition is not an active instance.

One accepted resource transfer should have one linked release and receipt. Partial performance leaves a residual obligation. Retrying a failed communication must not duplicate a donation or contribution. Cancellation can release unused reservations but cannot undo consumed provisions, damage, travel, or harm already applied.

Save migration must preserve unknown terms, vacancies, unresolved claims, lost records, partial restitution, and changed relationships. Missing data cannot become favorable cooperation or free capacity. These are design requirements, not implemented schemas or executed persistence tests.

The institution-specific preflight retains its narrower subject. If later evidence changes an exact right, update the appropriate authority with an explicit disposition rather than silently overriding it through this broader companion.

## 24B. Institutional lifecycle and functional condition

An institution should not be represented as simply present or destroyed. Its ability to act depends on people, custody, property access, records, communication, security, and material support. The following states are proposed design conditions, not opening assignments.

| Functional condition | Meaning | Available play |
|---|---|---|
| Functioning | Core routine, records, staff, and access can support normal institutional action | Receive requests, maintain services, negotiate, preserve continuity, and support bounded projects |
| Strained | At least one significant dependency is impaired | Continue reduced activity, prioritize functions, request support, defer investment, or negotiate relief |
| Disrupted | Conflict, vacancy, loss, displacement, or access failure prevents important work | Protect people and records, establish bounded interim routine, recover access, or relocate a function where valid |
| Contested | Authority, custody, office, property, or jurisdiction is actively disputed | Preserve evidence, seek recognition, negotiate standstill, mediate, or escalate through valid owners |
| Recovering | Immediate threat has passed but capacity is incomplete | Sequence return, repair, staffing, debt, restitution, and reopening |
| Extinguished or transformed | Only after supported legal, institutional, and historical resolution | Never inferred from occupation, officeholder death, or one failed relationship |

A smaller functioning institution can be more strategically valuable than a nominally prestigious institution whose staff, records, and resources are unavailable.

## 24C. Recognition, legitimacy, and audience acceptance

Ecclesiastical action should depend on who recognizes an office, act, record, protection claim, or settlement. Recognition is directed and subject-specific. It is not a single Realm-wide legitimacy score.

Potential audiences include the institution itself, chapter or community where applicable, local dependents, the advocate, neighboring lords, the royal government, relevant episcopal authorities, and wider church channels. Each audience evaluates an act from known evidence and interests. Recognition by one audience does not force recognition by all others.

```yaml
RecognitionContext:
  subject_classes:
    - OfficeholderOrInterimAuthority
    - DocumentaryActOrSeal
    - AdvocacyOrProtectionCompetence
    - PropertyOrJurisdictionClaim
    - SettlementRestitutionOrSafeConduct
    - PatronageOrReformAct
  evidence_inputs:
    - KnownRecordAndCustody
    - WitnessAndCommunication
    - RecognizedProcessWhereApplicable
    - ObservedPerformance
    - ContradictoryClaimsAndInterests
  one_audience_recognition_is_universal: false
  military_control_equals_recognition: false
  dynastic_kinship_equals_ecclesiastical_legitimacy: false
  favorable_recognition_is_permanent: false
```

The advantage of recognized action is practical: a wider audience may accept correspondence, attend negotiation, cooperate with a settlement, or treat an officeholder as able to transact. Recognition does not manufacture money, troops, property, or consent. Coercion can achieve material control while leaving recognition contested.

## 24D. Local religious service, dependents, and community interface

The V1 protects institutions from being reduced to treasuries or political modifiers. V1.1 adds the equally important positive side: institutions serve actual communities and dependents. Worship, commemoration, hospitality, relief, education, recordkeeping, burial, pastoral activity, and estate administration may matter where supported by the institution and context.

This authority does not claim identical services for every site. Instead, future institution records should identify which functions exist, whom they serve, what people and resources sustain them, and what happens when those functions are interrupted.

| Pressure | Institutional consequence | Realm-facing consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Requisition or lost stores | Hospitality, relief, or routine support may contract | Local burdens rise and cooperation can weaken |
| Unsafe routes | Messengers, visitors, dependents, and supplies may fail to arrive | Information, mediation, and patronage projects slow or fail |
| Displaced staff | Records and routine may survive only in reduced form | Agreements and claims become harder to perform or verify |
| Property damage | Income or service capacity may fall | Protection and restitution choices gain strategic importance |
| Contested authority | Competing instructions and custody claims emerge | The duke must choose whether to recognize, mediate, delay, or coerce |
| Successful recovery | Specific functions reopen as people and resources return | Cooperation improves through observed performance rather than a generic bonus |

Local communities are not passive extensions of an abbot, bishop, advocate, or duke. Community cooperation, burden, access, and recovery remain separate from institutional office. No new population group or opening disposition is created here.

## 24E. Documents, seals, archives, and witness conflict

The first pass mentions documentary support throughout. V1.1 consolidates it into a strong gameplay system. Documents are evidence objects with provenance, custody, content, authentication, access, and interpretation. They are neither infallible truth nor decorative flavor.

```yaml
DocumentaryConflictReview:
  inspect:
    - ClaimedIssuerAndRecipient
    - DateContentAndScope
    - SealOrOtherAuthenticationWhereApplicable
    - OriginalCopyExtractOrReportStatus
    - ArchiveAndCustodyHistory
    - WitnessesAndCorroboration
    - ContradictoryRecords
    - CurrentLegalAndMaterialPerformance
  possible_dispositions:
    - AcceptedForLimitedPurpose
    - AcceptedPendingPerformance
    - Contested
    - RequiresCorroboration
    - SupersededBySupportedSettlement
    - DamagedIncompleteOrUnavailable
    - RejectedByRelevantAudience
  possession_of_document_proves_claim: false
  seal_makes_false_claim_true: false
  lost_record_erases_applied_right_or_harm: false
```

A document can help the player establish a claim, defend a limitation, obtain an audience, or negotiate terms. A rival can challenge provenance, interpretation, continued applicability, or performance. The system should reward record preservation without making archive ownership an omniscience mechanic.

Seal custody is distinct from office, person, document, and authority. A displaced seal, unauthorized use, contradictory copies, or incomplete archive can create a focused crisis. Resolution may restore documentary capacity without deciding the underlying political dispute.

## 24F. Patronage-project lifecycle and completion gates

Patronage becomes stronger when projects can succeed, stall, be redirected, or fail for visible reasons. A donation is only one input.

```yaml
PatronageProjectLifecycle:
  stages:
    - ProposalAndSupportedPurpose
    - PatronCommitment
    - InstitutionalAcceptance
    - ResourceReservationAndTransfer
    - LaborMaterialStaffAndAccessAssembly
    - WorkOrServicePerformance
    - CompletionInspectionAndCustody
    - OngoingOperationAndMaintenance
  incomplete_states:
    - PledgedNotTransferred
    - TransferredButNotMobilized
    - PartiallyPerformed
    - Interrupted
    - CompletedButNotOperational
    - OperationalAtReducedCapacity
  patronage_creates_appointment_right: false
  construction_creates_staff_or_income: false
  completion_erases_debt_or_dispute: false
```

The player should be able to phase a project, narrow its scope, supply missing resources, negotiate a pause, or abandon it with appropriate consequences. Sunk resources remain sunk. An unfinished project can still preserve some useful work, stored material, or political association where supported.

Projects should serve actual functions: record preservation, education, hospitality, relief, repair, communication, worship, or commemoration. A project added only to generate abstract favor should be rejected or redesigned.

## 24G. Church-facing diplomacy and faction counterplay

Ecclesiastical networks must strengthen Lower Lotharingia without becoming an unstoppable diplomatic shortcut. The player gains opportunities by performing useful acts and maintaining channels. Opponents retain several responses.

| Lower Lotharingian approach | Potential advantage | Opponent or institutional counterplay |
|---|---|---|
| Effective bounded protection | Cooperation, standing, and resilient communication | Offer better protection, expose failures, dispute competence, or attack the support capacity |
| Documentary advocacy | Stronger claim presentation or negotiation position | Challenge provenance, scope, continuity, interpretation, or performance |
| Patronage | Durable institutional function and audience association | Compete with patronage, reveal overreach, or exploit unfunded promises |
| Mediation | Reduce escalation and establish workable terms | Refuse the mediator, demand guarantees, contest neutrality, or delay performance |
| Clerical education | Better long-term documentary and service capacity | Compete for candidates and institutions; benefit is delayed and never guaranteed |
| Coercive extraction | Immediate material relief | Resistance, evasion, rival appeals, damaged services, and long-term recovery costs |

Church-facing diplomacy does not override ordinary diplomacy, title, law, or war systems. A favorable institution cannot compel a neighboring ruler to accept ducal terms. A rival's religious alignment does not determine behavior. No faction receives an automatic immunity from institutional consequences.

## 24H. Institutional capture, corruption, and overreach boundary

Church politics needs failure paths beyond simple relationship loss. Institutional capture occurs when an outside actor or internal network bends an institution's office, property, records, appointments, or services toward unsupported private control. This is a proposed analytical category, not an accusation about any opening institution or person.

Potential warning signs include unauthorized seal use, diversion of patronage, coerced appointments, concealed records, private military occupation, extraction beyond supported terms, or systematic exclusion of required institutional participants. Each allegation requires evidence and a specific actor. Do not convert a disputed policy or unpopular reform into proven corruption automatically.

Possible responses include audit and record review through valid owners, restoration of custody, replacement or discipline through applicable processes, restitution, negotiated guarantees, external recognition, or coercive settlement with consequences. The duke may be the reformer, accused overreacher, mediator, beneficiary, or target depending on the case.

Institutional independence is not immunity from accountability. Ducal authority is not a license to appropriate. Reform is not a magic label that makes coercion legitimate. The player should receive the immediate benefit and long-term risk of each path without moral scripting that predetermines the only playable choice.

## 24I. Recovery ladder and operational closure

Institutional recovery should proceed through concrete closures rather than one generic recovery percentage.

```yaml
InstitutionRecoveryLadder:
  stabilize:
    - ProtectPeopleAndImmediateCustody
    - PreserveOrRecoverRecordsAndSeal
    - EstablishBoundedCommunication
  restore_access:
    - SecureRoutesAndPropertyAccess
    - ReturnOrReplaceEssentialStaffThroughOwners
    - ResolveImmediateSupplyAndShelter
  resume_function:
    - ReopenPrioritizedServices
    - PerformPendingAgreementsWherePossible
    - TrackResidualDebtDamageAndDispute
  rebuild_capacity:
    - RepairPropertyAndEquipment
    - RestoreTrainingRecordsAndRoutine
    - ReconcilePatronageAndRestitution
  close_or_transform:
    - RecordCompletedObligations
    - PreserveUnresolvedClaims
    - ReassessRelationshipAndFutureScope
  peace_or_money_skips_all_steps: false
```

A partial recovery is a valid stable state. An institution may reopen worship and recordkeeping while hospitality remains suspended. A route may reopen while a property dispute continues. The interface should show the next blocked function and the responsible resource or owner rather than hiding the situation behind a single bar.
