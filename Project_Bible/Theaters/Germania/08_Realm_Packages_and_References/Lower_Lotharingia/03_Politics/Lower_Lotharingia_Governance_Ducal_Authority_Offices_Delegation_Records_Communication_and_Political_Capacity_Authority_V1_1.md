## 4. Administrative topology: core, interfaces, and external field

### 4.1 Principal coordination

The ducal principal selects priorities and represents the ducal project. Coordination capacity depends on available people, records, routes, accepted mandates, and resources. No permanent capital, central ministry, or universal chancery establishment is asserted. A principal seat, itinerary, or several administrative nodes remain possible until geography and opening evidence resolve the arrangement. [S1]

### 4.2 Direct-core administration

Baelen–Limbourg provides the candidate material anchor. Government can coordinate verified property-backed support, defense preparation, records, and household-facing continuity only through the direct-core owners. It cannot transform the entire surrounding region into a tax district. [S5]

### 4.3 March-facing coordination

Antwerp-related work concerns bounded frontier responsibilities, warning, routes, fortification support, and negotiation where a valid relationship exists. A march label does not establish a subordinate command chain, regional ownership, or emergency autonomy. The current march authority must be reconciled before exact bindings. [S1, S7]

### 4.4 Institutional coordination

Stavelot-Malmedy and St Truiden receive separate request and obligation tracks. One protection promise cannot be counted as two completed missions. Neither institution becomes an office of the duke. [S6]

### 4.5 External coordination

Royal Salian government, Cologne, Liège, Trier, Aachen, Namur, Luxembourg, Flanders, Boulogne, Hainaut-Mons, Upper Lotharingia, Burgundy, and Italia-facing actors retain their own state. Government can prepare petitions, embassies, service responses, and agreements; it cannot administer them merely because the duke has a regional interest. [S1]

## 8. Records, authentication, and custody

The records portfolio preserves agreements, property evidence, accounts, notices, mandates, correspondence, and unresolved disputes. It does not declare every surviving record true. Conflicting versions, missing witnesses, uncertain seals, and interrupted custody remain meaningful problems.

Separate preparation, authentication, custody, dispatch, and policy decision. One person may perform several functions, but the design must expose the resulting single point of failure. Copies and alternate custodians improve resilience only when people, materials, access, and maintenance are available.

## 9. Communication and the order lifecycle

This is a proposed campaign workflow, not a historical procedural claim or implemented turn pipeline.

1. Receive a report, petition, or known need through a valid information channel.
2. Identify the responsible function and the authority needed for the proposed response.
3. Check the recipient, route, available resources, and competing commitments.
4. Prepare and authenticate a bounded instruction or request.
5. Dispatch through actual communication capacity.
6. Distinguish receipt, acceptance, attempted performance, and verified result.
7. Record partial completion, dispute, failure, or recovery need.

A lost messenger creates uncertainty, not proof of disloyalty. A delivered instruction creates knowledge of the instruction, not capacity or consent. A stale order may require revalidation when its assumptions change. Exact travel durations and delivery probabilities are deferred to geography and communication owners.

## 10. Fiscal machinery without invented revenue

Fiscal coordination distinguishes entitlement, assessment, request, collection, custody, transfer, allocation, and expenditure. These are design stages; no universal medieval tax procedure is asserted.

Direct property income, public resources, institutional contributions, and household funds remain separate until their owners authorize a transfer or allocation. Money promised is not money in hand. Funds in transit cannot also be spent at their destination. A missing account may indicate delay, loss, dispute, or incomplete information; it is not automatic corruption.

A material player choice is whether to reserve verified resources for core defense, meet an accepted protection duty, support royal service, or restore exhausted administration. The interface should show what is being deferred and why. It should not invent a balanced budget to make the faction playable.

## 11. Military coordination and the cost of concentration

Government supports muster notices, assembly communication, supply requests, garrison reporting, demobilization, and recovery. Military owners retain force identity, recruitment pools, command, equipment, horses, supply, and execution. [S2]

The same escort, transport, stores, or defenders cannot be committed simultaneously to core defense, march support, abbey protection, and a distant campaign. A nominal service obligation remains unavailable until its terms, people, command, access, and sustainment permit use.

The player should see practical consequences: a relief force can be organized only by weakening another commitment; a garrison can remain secure while field concentration fails; a successful expedition can leave records, dependent protection, and local recovery under strain. Recruitment remains finite and population-informed under its own authorities, not a governance-generated manpower pool.

## 12. Institutional, legal, and local coordination

Keep separate tracks for Stavelot-Malmedy and St Truiden, including the latter's unresolved Liège interface. Government can prepare a response, inspect a claimed obligation, request mediation, and coordinate an accepted protection action. It cannot resolve institutional law, appoint an abbot, or draw from an abbey treasury on its own. [S6]

Local counts, communities, and ecclesiastical actors may accept one request and reject another. The government records the specific issue rather than assigning a global loyal/rebellious label. A valid judgment still needs communication, enforcement capacity, and owner-confirmed compliance. Military occupation changes neither every right nor every relationship.

## 13. Household, consort, and protected-dynast interfaces

The household supplies access, care, hospitality, records, movement support, and dependent protection where available. Public government consumes those services without absorbing household identity or property. [S4]

Ida's intercession or household coordination does not automatically become public acting authority. Jutta or Judith receives no adult office, inheritance administration, or political mandate from unresolved age and identity. A household officer's access to the duke is not authority over the Realm. [S3, S8]

When public and household needs compete, the player may separate the traveling principal, working records, protected dependents, and administrative support. That creates transport, custody, communication, and political risks. No exact opening residence or evacuation destination is selected here.

## 15. Presence, itinerary, and concentration tradeoffs

The duke's presence can improve an audience, a military concentration, or a disputed negotiation while leaving another function less supported. This is a workload problem, not a requirement that every action wait for the ruler.

Proposed arrangements include a traveling principal with bounded support at a secure node, distributed record custody, or a temporarily concentrated government during crisis. Each requires evidence-backed places, staff, access, and mandates before active use. Moving the court must not teleport the treasury, archives, dependents, or garrison. No modern capital mechanic is used to resolve the opening uncertainty.

## 16. Absence, incapacity, captivity, death, and dismissal

| Trigger | What needs review | What must not happen automatically |
|---|---|---|
| Campaign absence | Routine responsibilities, escalation, records, core security, return handback | All public authority passes to the consort or nearest relative |
| Incapacity | Person availability, accepted acting mandate, protected access, continuing duties | Temporary illness becomes deposition or universal regency |
| Captivity | Authentication, access, ransom coordination, command continuity, dependent protection | Captor acquires title, property, or every government function |
| Death | Title succession, office vacancies, mandates, records, property, household and force continuity | Godfrey installs by date or the whole government resets |
| Delegate dismissal | Pending orders, accounts, resources, records, lawful replacement | Prior actions disappear or resources duplicate |
| Archive or route loss | Surviving evidence, alternate channels, priority narrowing, restoration | Every right vanishes or hidden state becomes known |

The existing Frederick authority requires these transitions to remain separate across household, title, government, property, advocacy, and military command. [S3]

## 18. Succession, restoration, and post-title-loss government

A change of ducal holder must not transfer every private asset or personal relationship. Existing offices, mandates, agreements, institutional obligations, and local acceptance need their own continuity decisions. Some may persist; others may expire, be disputed, or require confirmation. [S1, S2]

The household may preserve a reduced direct-core administration while losing the wider ducal dignity. A new duke may possess recognition while lacking access to records or local resources. A negotiated settlement may divide public responsibility, property, residence, and command. These are conditional design outcomes, not opening succession records.

Godfrey's restoration remains a political possibility, not a scheduled government replacement. Neither a historical date nor a title change reloads the opening template.

## 19. Expansion, occupation, and integration

A captured site creates an administrative problem before it creates reliable capacity. The player may hold a strategic node, negotiate local cooperation, use temporary occupation support, establish a bounded arrangement, or withdraw.

Required design questions are concrete: who can issue notices, which rights remain contested, who keeps records, which supplies are available, what command is valid, and who bears recovery costs? A regional arrangement is justified only when the work and evidence support it. Every conquered region does not receive a generic governor.

Political settlement, lawful rights, public order, and administrative performance remain separate. A peaceful settlement can still exceed the government's operating capacity. A militarily held site can remain legally disputed and economically unproductive.

## 20. Development channels and costs

| Channel | Investment | Campaign value | Material risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dependable core | Records, verified resources, supported local responsibilities, secure communication | More reliable home support and recovery | Less capacity for distant commitments |
| Brokered network | Bounded agreements, envoys, institutional restraint, local negotiation | Wider cooperation without full direct administration | Dependence on continued acceptance and access |
| Delegated reach | Valid mandates, supported delegates, review and handback | Principal attention freed for major decisions | Overlap, delayed review, capture, or scope abuse |
| Concentrated records | Custodians, secure facilities, authentication support | Faster coordination and clearer evidence | Single point of capture or disruption |
| Distributed resilience | Alternate routes, copies, replacement support | Better continuity after local failure | Continuing staffing, transport, and maintenance cost |
| Frontier response | Warning, stores, bounded command support, local arrangements | More credible protection and concentration | Repeated emergencies exhaust the core |
| Retrenched survival | Narrow commitments, preserve household and direct property | A viable reduced political project | Lost wider influence and contested prestige |

These channels can coexist by function. They are not mutually exclusive cultural forks or automatic upgrades.

## 21. Failure, remedy, and recovery

| Diagnosed failure | Immediate response proposal | Durable recovery | Invalid shortcut |
|---|---|---|---|
| Missing mandate | Pause the unsupported act and request authority review | Clarify scope, acceptance, and end conditions | Appointment silently grants all powers |
| Messenger route blocked | Mark delivery uncertain; use a viable alternate if available | Restore access, transport, and reporting | Assume the recipient refused |
| Conflicting accounts | Preserve source records and route discrepancy to owners | Reconcile custody, transfer, and entitlement | Rewrite balances for convenience |
| Unsupported office | Narrow work or seek valid temporary coverage | Restore staff, resources, records, and review | Spawn an official |
| Failed protection | Report the specific unmet duty and known harm | Restitution, revised terms, renewed capacity | Automatic universal hostility |
| Overextended government | Prioritize core and accepted critical duties | Reduce commitments, reorganize, recover | Universal efficiency penalty with no cause |
| Captured archive | Preserve surviving witnesses and records elsewhere | Restore evidence and secure custody | Captor becomes lawful owner |
| Disputed succession | Maintain valid routine functions where possible | Owner-routed recognition and settlement | Historical successor auto-installs |

Recovery can fail or remain partial. Reorganization changes dependencies and costs; it does not instantly repair relationships, replace supplies, or validate claims.

## 22. Player-facing decisions with real consequences

### The Duke Rides Out

**Trigger:** a proposed personal command competes with home administration. **Options:** lead with bounded delegation; remain and assign a valid commander; narrow the campaign. **Tradeoff:** field presence versus core exposure and review burden. No option creates a commander or acting mandate for free.

### One Escort, Two Obligations

**Trigger:** two accepted tasks compete for the same available protection capacity. **Options:** prioritize, split only if viable, negotiate delay, or seek paid/external support. **Tradeoff:** timeliness, defense strength, cost, and institutional reception. The neglected institution is not automatically hostile.

### Who Keeps the Seal?

**Trigger:** movement, incapacity, or custody risk makes authentication fragile. **Options:** retain concentrated custody, create supported redundancy, or restrict high-risk acts temporarily. **Tradeoff:** speed versus capture and contradictory-order risk. Possession does not confer policy authority.

### A Relative Requests Responsibility

**Trigger:** a household-connected candidate seeks a public function. **Options:** bounded appointment proposal, a narrower household role, or another eligible candidate. **Tradeoff:** access and continuity versus availability, conflicts, and review independence. Kinship does not establish competence or loyalty.

### The Abbey's Demand Arrives Late

**Trigger:** a delayed report changes the feasibility of a protection response. **Options:** verify, negotiate a revised action, relieve a viable site, or record inability and seek remedy. **Tradeoff:** information quality, urgency, and competing commitments. Late news is not omniscient proof of what happened.

### A New Duke, Old Accounts

**Trigger:** a holder change leaves records, obligations, and resources divided. **Options:** negotiated handback, owner-confirmed continuity, limited settlement, or contested recovery. **Tradeoff:** immediate reach versus concessions and legitimacy disputes. No title-driven mass transfer occurs.

### The Core Before the Court

**Trigger:** royal service competes with a material home threat. **Options:** limited service, negotiated delay, supported delegation, or home concentration. **Tradeoff:** recognition and access versus actual capacity. No automatic royal rescue or punishment is scripted.

### The Government Must Shrink

**Trigger:** sustained overload blocks critical work. **Options:** suspend secondary commitments, distribute supported functions, restore routes and records, or retrench to the core. **Tradeoff:** lost reach now versus survival and recovery. This is a strategic choice, not an automatic collapse event.

All are proposed reusable content families, not claims of historical incidents or active opening events.

## 25. Public-act decision-rights matrix

Government work must preserve the difference between preparing an act and possessing authority to decide it. The matrix below is a static design contract, not an opening assignment of offices or people.

| Stage | Required function | What the stage may do | What the stage cannot establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intake | Receive a petition, report, claim, command need, or institutional request | Record source, time, access, and known uncertainty | Truth, urgency, jurisdiction, or obligation |
| Classification | Identify the subject, affected owners, and likely responsibility portfolio | Route the matter and identify missing receipts | Authority to decide the substance |
| Preparation | Assemble evidence, options, costs, dependencies, and draft language | Make consequences and uncertainty legible | A binding decision or valid mandate |
| Authorization | Apply an owner-confirmed authority, office, delegation, judgment, or political decision | Approve a bounded public act within scope | People, resources, jurisdiction, or recipient compliance |
| Authentication | Connect the authorized act to valid evidence, witnesses, seal, or other accepted method | Support verification and challenge handling | Policy authorship or independent authority |
| Dispatch | Send through an available messenger, envoy, route, or institution | Create an attempted communication record | Receipt, acceptance, execution, or success |
| Execution | Attempt the instructed or agreed action through the proper owner | Produce observable performance and costs | Legitimacy, permanent control, or automatic repetition |
| Review | Compare authority, instruction, action, evidence, cost, and result | Confirm, contest, remedy, narrow, revoke, or recover through owners | Retroactive creation of missing authority or resources |

No single stage may silently substitute for the others. A seal validates neither the policy decision nor the truth of every statement. A duke's decision does not prove dispatch. Receipt does not prove consent. Performance does not prove the original act lawful.

| Manual case | Expected design treatment |
|---|---|
| Principal leaves with a valid bounded delegate | Continue only the delegated functions; retain other owners' powers and record handback needs. |
| Delegate lacks authority to alienate property | Administrative responsibility cannot validate the sale or grant. |
| One messenger disappears | Delivery becomes uncertain; do not infer recipient refusal. |

These cases were reviewed as design expectations, not executed against a simulator or Unreal project.
