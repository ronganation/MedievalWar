This is substantive Realm-specific campaign design. It explains how Lower Lotharingia can hear disputes, preserve plural rights, secure bounded performance, respond to resistance, and recover after coercion or conflict. It is not a new universal legal system, a copy of the shared public-order framework, or a historical code purportedly enacted in 1062.

Every portfolio, workflow, policy, and decision below is proposed game design unless explicitly attributed to an existing project source. No exact historical court procedure, tariff, penalty, immunity, office roster, appeal hierarchy, or active opening dispute is invented.

## 1. Strong legal and public-order thesis

**Make ducal peace useful without pretending the duke owns every right, court, institution, or armed following.**

The established Realm identity is an Imperial Frontier Ducal Network with a bounded direct core, separate institutional relationships, and a contested political order. This authority translates that design into practical choices: hear or refer a dispute, protect a route, verify a service demand, restrain an overreaching delegate, negotiate an exception, enforce a supported judgment, or accept a narrower settlement. [S4, S5, S6]

The campaign challenge is not to maximize a justice statistic. It is to make specific obligations and remedies perform across uneven authority, access, records, resources, and cooperation. A government may be militarily secure while an abbey's property claim remains unsettled. A community may recognize the duke but dispute a requisition. A rival magnate is not automatically a rebel, and a petition is not a declaration of war.

The intended reward is credible order: safer movement, usable obligations, supported institutions, fewer destructive disputes, and recovery that preserves a viable political project. The costs include time, money, escorts, limited military availability, concessions, precedent, and foregone opportunities elsewhere.

## 4. Legal distinctions that drive play

| Distinction | Practical consequence |
|---|---|
| Law / custom / policy / decree | A temporary priority cannot silently change a durable right. |
| Charter / privilege / judgment / agreement | A document's existence does not establish every claimed effect. |
| Jurisdiction / venue / access | Holding a hearing at a place does not grant authority over every participant or estate. |
| Claim / standing / proof | An asserted interest is not a proven case. |
| Notice / receipt / appearance | A missing response may reflect failed communication or unsafe travel. |
| Judgment / enforcement / compliance | A valid decision can remain partly or wholly unperformed. |
| Compliance / consent / legitimacy | Obedience under pressure does not prove political acceptance. |
| Possession / custody / ownership | Seizing a fortress or archive does not settle every right. |
| Grievance / resistance / rebellion | A material complaint can persist without organization for armed action. |
| Suppression / recovery / reconciliation | Ending violence does not repair livelihood, government, or memory. |

These separations adapt S1–S3. They are not a universal medieval hierarchy or an assertion that every case follows the same sequence.

## 5. Legal-source classes and custom recognition

Proposed source classes include title-specific authority, local usage, property tenure, a validated charter or privilege, an institution-specific right, a witnessed settlement, a competent judgment, an office mandate, an agreement, and a bounded emergency measure. Each needs scope, affected actors, evidence, and an applicable authority basis before executable use. [S1, S3]

Custom may be unwritten and still materially constrain action. Age or repetition alone does not prove a practice binding; a powerful actor's assertion is not a verified custom. Written form is not automatic superiority over every local practice. The player may seek confirmation, investigate competing accounts, negotiate an exception, or propose a supported reform.

No named Lotharingian code, universal ducal ban, fixed peace ordinance, later town charter, or standardized service duration is admitted as opening law here. Where exact law is unknown, the result is uncertainty, not unlimited ducal discretion or a legal vacuum.

## 7. Direct-core property and fortified-seat justice

Baelen–Limbourg supplies the candidate material base, not an exact judicial district. Proposed cases include disputed dues, estate access, fortress custody, record possession, maintenance obligations, and damage caused by defense. The player can seek evidence, protect uncontested activity, negotiate temporary access, or request a scoped decision. [S7]

A castellan's practical custody does not establish ownership or unrestricted jurisdiction. A title settlement does not prove transfer of the core. Interim control must identify what is held, for what purpose, who can use revenue or stores, and when review or handback is due. No opening castellan, estate schedule, court, or legal competence is created.

The distinctive strategic tension is survival versus overreach: extracting heavily from the core may keep the fortress supplied while damaging the very cooperation and recovery needed for household continuity.

A proposed dispute might concern an escort, passage, toll claim, defense service, or a local officer's mandate. Verify the object, competent actor, agreement, and practical access. A valid warning can justify preparation without proving the rival's intent. Frontier readiness does not create a right to requisition every settlement.

## 9. Two advocacies, separate legal burdens

Stavelot-Malmedy and St Truiden retain separate institutional identity, property, jurisdiction, protection terms, and response. St Truiden's Liège interface remains an explicit unresolved dependency. [S8]

Proposed disputes include unsupported collection, failed protection, contested service, estate occupation, an officer exceeding a mandate, and disagreement over a privilege. Options include verification, institution-specific mediation, narrowing a demand, supported restitution, or enforcement through a competent owner. One abbey's agreement cannot settle the other's rights.

Advocacy does not grant spiritual discipline, appointment power, treasury access, or ownership. Institutional resistance to one demand is not proof of hostility to the duke's title. Successful protection can improve cooperation while leaving jurisdiction contested. A legal victory still needs a feasible material remedy.

## 10. Local lordship, communities, and uneven cooperation

A count, local holder, settlement body, or community may accept one obligation and contest another. The design tracks the actual demand, evidence, burden, and response rather than assigning one loyal/rebellious label. [S2]

Proposed issues include service scope, customary collection, local protection, competing demands, and access to a competent forum. A negotiated exemption can preserve cooperation while reducing immediate resources. Enforcing a supported obligation can preserve predictability while consuming escorts and political attention.

No exact community roster, later commune, representative estate, guild constitution, or universal local autonomy is backfilled. Community identity alone does not determine political position. Rural and settlement responses need not match, and elite cooperation does not prove popular acceptance.

## 11. Markets, routes, tolls, and commercial protection

Proposed legal portfolios include passage, merchant protection, disputed toll entitlement, market access, debt or delivery disputes, and damage to transport. These are design categories, not proof of specific opening rights or commercial institutions.

The player may protect a validated route, investigate a collection claim, mediate a delivery dispute, or negotiate access. A route label does not establish ownership, a toll, or the capacity to enforce one. Market activity does not create a treasury receipt. A debt judgment does not create the debtor's ability to pay.

Recovery requires actual repair, transport, security, and market/material owners. Reopening a road is not identical to restoring trade volume or household livelihood. No exact road, bridge, ferry, port, rate, or market privilege is created.

## 12. Service, muster, billeting, and requisition

Law can define a validated obligation; military and recruitment owners determine whether people, equipment, command, access, and finite pools can perform it. The same carts, animals, provisions, or defenders cannot satisfy several simultaneous commitments. [S1, S3]

A proposed service case distinguishes unable, delayed, exempt, substituted, disputed, and refused. These do not collapse into desertion or rebellion. Exact duration, geography, equipment, commutation, and exemptions require evidence before active use.

Emergency requisition must have a valid basis, bounded target, actual resources, review, and an accounting of damage and any supported compensation. Military need does not erase property or create supplies. The campaign choice is often whether to narrow an expedition, pay for alternatives, negotiate delay, or accept the recovery cost of a lawful emergency measure.

## 13. Household, consort, protected dynast, and public mandate

Household access, intercession, custody, guardianship, and public judicial authority remain separate. Ida may be relevant through her existing household authority; no universal judicial mandate or regency is assigned here. Jutta or Judith's unresolved identity, age, placement, and active rights cannot be filled because a case needs an heir or administrator. [S5, S9]

A proposed household-related dispute can concern safe access, records, residence, maintenance, property administration, or the scope of a representative's authority. Representation does not eliminate the represented person's agency. Protection is not ownership, and kinship is not jurisdiction.

Guest, dependent, ward, protected person, hostage, detainee, and prisoner are not interchangeable states. Any constraint needs its own basis, custody owner, scope, review, and release handling. No opening custody arrangement is invented.

## 14. Royal, ecclesiastical, and external interfaces

Royal government, neighboring powers, episcopal authorities, and institutions retain their own law, consent, recognition, property, and decisions. The duke may seek a hearing, confirmation, mediation, safe conduct, or assistance through a valid channel; this file cannot author the external answer.

Temporal and spiritual jurisdiction are not universally interchangeable or universally ranked. A protection request does not grant spiritual authority; an ecclesiastical sanction does not automatically create universal reception or a ducal military action. Exact competences remain owner-controlled. [S1, S2]

Cross-boundary enforcement needs actual access and appropriate agreements or conflict state. Kinship, shared faith, title association, or a favorable judgment does not grant passage or transfer external resources.

## 15. Petition, notice, evidence, and hearing design

The proposed case workflow adapts S1 and S3 without asserting a standardized 1062 procedure:

1. Identify the complaint, parties, affected object, and requested remedy.
2. Review standing, representation, jurisdiction, venue, and access.
3. Establish notice or a specifically justified urgent exception.
4. Preserve available evidence, contradictions, and missing information.
5. Allow the applicable response, counsel, mediation, or hearing route.
6. Route a bounded decision or negotiated settlement to the competent owner.
7. Separate enforcement, compliance, review, and material restoration.

Not every case requires every stage, and no universal modern court procedure is imposed. Urgent protection may precede full review when validly supported, but the exception must remain narrow and reviewable.

Nonappearance is not automatic guilt. A summons may not have arrived; a route may be unsafe; the venue may be contested. A petition can be unproven without being malicious. Referring a matter is not necessarily refusing justice if the proposed forum is competent and reachable.

## 17. Judgment, mediation, review, and appeal

A proposed judgment identifies the competent authority, object, applicable basis, findings and uncertainty, remedy, affected owners, and review route. It does not execute itself. Mediation can preserve plural rights or partial performance without declaring one side universally correct. [S1, S3]

Appeal and review depend on a valid route; there is no invented universal ladder ending at the duke or king. Requesting review does not automatically suspend every act, prove disloyalty, or overturn a judgment. Interim protection and disputed execution require their own owner decisions.

Possible outcomes include a declaration, confirmation, narrowed demand, restitution request, compensation, supported fine, mandate review, negotiated performance, referral, dismissal, or no presently available remedy. The player should see why an outcome is enforceable, blocked, disputed, or incomplete.

## 18. Enforcement, sanctions, and limits

| Proposed response | Required basis and capacity | Failure or review question |
|---|---|---|
| Clarification or renewed notice | Valid channel and intelligible demand | Was the demand known and understood? |
| Mediation or guarantee | Competent participants and accepted terms | Is the promised performance deliverable? |
| Fine or compensation request | Supported decision, payer, entitlement, and transfer path | Assessed is not collected or paid. |
| Office or mandate review | Relevant appointment/delegation authority | What continues while replacement is unresolved? |
| Temporary property restraint | Defined object, valid scope, custodian, and review | Custody is not permanent forfeiture. |
| Escort or limited security action | Actual personnel, command, access, and supply | Is the action protecting or exceeding its purpose? |
| Coercive enforcement or seizure | Appropriate legal/de facto authority, target, force, constraints, and consequences | What harm, opposition, and recovery obligations result? |
| Withdrawal or delayed enforcement | Explicit limitation and preservation of relevant claims | What remains exposed or unresolved? |

This is an option portfolio, not a mandatory escalation ladder. Force can fail, be narrowed, or produce compliance without consent. A harsh response is not universally optimal. Exact penalties, detention rules, forfeiture grounds, and legal consequences require their owners; no punishment schedule is invented.

## 20. Compliance and public-response interpretation

S2 treats public order as a derived, audience- and issue-specific interpretation rather than one universal truth score. This Realm adaptation uses that distinction without duplicating the shared lifecycle or becoming a sole writer.

| Observed response | Interpretation to preserve | Invalid inference |
|---|---|---|
| Routine performance | The specific obligation was performed | Universal loyalty |
| Conditional or partial compliance | Some terms accepted or performed | Complete agreement |
| Request for clarification, substitution, or exemption | The demand is being addressed through a bounded channel | Rebellion |
| Delay or inability | Capacity, notice, access, or timing may be insufficient | Intentional refusal |
| Evasion or noncooperation | A specific act is not being performed as requested | Organized armed opposition |
| Petition or institutional resistance | A grievance or right is being asserted | Hostility to every ducal function |
| Compliance under threat | Performance under coercive conditions | Consent or legitimacy |
| No reliable report | Evaluation is incomplete | Stable, neutral, or grievance-free |

One community can cooperate on route protection while disputing collection. One institution can accept peace while contesting a title claimant. The player summary must preserve those combinations.

## 21. Grievances, redress, and audience scope

Proposed grievance families include unsupported collection, service overload, property loss, protection failure, office abuse, jurisdictional intrusion, military conduct, broken settlement terms, and blocked access to review. They are potential case types, not active opening allegations.

Each case identifies the affected actor or audience, issue, known basis, requested remedy if any, evidence quality, responsible owner or uncertainty, and review conditions. A grievance can be strong while no rebellion capacity exists. A remedy can be offered but refused, accepted but unpaid, or delivered without reconciliation. [S2]

A Province or settlement is not one mind. Do not infer hostility from culture or religion, or use population loss as pacification. Displacement and flight need demographic and material owners, not a public-order score adjustment. Historical memory may preserve the harm after a practical settlement.

## 23. Security, suppression, and durable order

A garrison can improve immediate security while worsening billeting burdens. Successful suppression can end an armed action while leaving property disputes, damaged livelihoods, institutional resistance, and political opposition. Military control is not legitimacy. [S2]

The proposed choice set includes investigation, communication, accommodation, mediation, limited protection, negotiated settlement, proportionate coercion through owners, delay, or withdrawal. The UI should expose known threats, uncertainty, actual capacity, alternatives, likely costs, and review/exit conditions without revealing hidden organization.

Durable order is a reviewable interpretation supported by security, working government, usable remedies, material recovery, institutional function, and managed disagreement. It does not require universal satisfaction, cultural uniformity, or permanent centralization. No garrison size, fixed unrest threshold, or automatic pacification formula is assigned.

## 24. Emergency, occupation, reform, and sunset

A proposed emergency measure needs a known trigger, competent authority, bounded scope, resources, prohibited acts, communication, review, and an end condition. Temporary restrictions or requisition do not silently become permanent custom. Exact emergency powers remain unresolved. [S1]

Occupation can require temporary security and administration without establishing permanent lawful ownership. Existing rights, records, protected persons, institutional functions, and recovery needs remain relevant. A peace agreement does not instantly integrate the occupied area.

Reform is a deliberate project, not a modifier purchase. A useful reform might clarify a recurring obligation, improve notice, regularize review, confirm a scoped privilege, or narrow an emergency practice. It needs consultation/consent where applicable, capacity, transition rules, affected rights, cost, and review. It cannot rewrite all existing contracts or erase local custom by menu selection.

## 25. Law, claims, war, and political consequences

**No claim, casus-belli, or Papal permission gate is introduced for declaring war.** S3 explicitly preserves war as an available strategic action while treating claims and legal standing as influences on reception, conduct, and settlement rather than unlock conditions.

This authority does not declare war or bypass diplomacy, command, access, supply, or technical owner checks. It distinguishes an action being available from that action being accepted as lawful or politically prudent. War without a strong claim can carry different reactions and settlement difficulties; a strong claim does not supply troops or prove victory lawful.

S6's wording that a claim does not itself authorize war is consumed narrowly: a claim neither executes a war action nor creates military support. It must not be interpreted as requiring a claim or legal approval before the player can choose war. The source is not edited or declared superseded here; any contrary implementation interpretation remains an explicit reconciliation issue for the relevant owners.

Rivalry is not automatic hostility, truce is not claim extinction, victory is not title installation, and peace is not complete reconciliation. The title authority retains the political settlement portfolio; this file handles legal/public-response consequences and remedy routing.

## 28. Developed decisions and dilemmas

### The Abbey's Cart Is Taken

**Trigger:** a reported requisition affects institution-associated transport. **Choices:** verify the authority and actual custody; return it if owner-supported; negotiate supported compensation; or pursue a bounded enforcement finding. **Tradeoff:** immediate supply versus protection obligations and recovery. No guilt, ownership, or compensation resource is assumed.

### A Summons That Never Arrived

**Trigger:** nonappearance follows uncertain delivery. **Choices:** renew notice, arrange a safer venue or escort if available, investigate the route, or defer. **Tradeoff:** delay versus a decision based on defective information. Nonappearance cannot automatically prove defiance.

### The Fortress Keeper's Demand

**Trigger:** a custodian claims revenue or authority beyond known terms. **Choices:** inspect mandate and records, negotiate temporary support, seek a competent decision, or replace through valid owners. **Tradeoff:** immediate defense versus uncontrolled precedent. Custody does not become ownership.

### Two Courts, One Estate

**Trigger:** competing jurisdiction claims affect a defined property. **Choices:** split issues, seek mediation, preserve uncontested use, or refer to a competent forum. **Tradeoff:** slower resolution versus unsupported seizure. No preferred court wins by player convenience.

### The Duke's Men Have Already Collected

**Trigger:** a credible complaint challenges an actual collection. **Choices:** review entitlement and accounts, halt disputed further collection where valid, restore through owners, or defend a supported demand. **Tradeoff:** available funds versus correction and cooperation. Collected money is not duplicated for restitution.

### The Levy Cannot Come

**Trigger:** a service response reports inability or exemption. **Choices:** verify, narrow the call, accept a supported substitute, negotiate delay, or contest through the proper owner. **Tradeoff:** campaign strength versus local recovery. No fixed service term or automatic rebellion is invented.

### A Road Kept Open by Fear

**Trigger:** security improves while credible coercion grievances persist. **Choices:** maintain bounded protection, investigate conduct, change supported personnel, or negotiate local cooperation. **Tradeoff:** short-term access versus durable acceptance. Security and consent remain separate.

### St Truiden Requests Another Hearing

**Trigger:** an institution-specific dispute has a known unresolved jurisdiction issue. **Choices:** seek appropriate mediation, clarify the Liège interface, narrow the demand, or preserve a partial settlement. **Tradeoff:** ducal leverage versus credible limits. No external answer is written here.

### Peace Without the Lost Stores

**Trigger:** a political settlement ends fighting but material damage remains. **Choices:** prioritize repair, stage compensation, protect production, or accept slower recovery. **Tradeoff:** renewed campaigning versus viable livelihoods. Peace does not refill supplies.

### The Rival Is Not a Rebel

**Trigger:** a separate political actor contests an outcome without an active rebellion finding. **Choices:** negotiate, monitor through valid information, seek recognition, or choose conflict through diplomacy. **Tradeoff:** political alternatives versus force costs. A claim is neither a rebel label nor a war-permission requirement.

### The Emergency Has Ended

**Trigger:** the valid emergency basis ends or review is due. **Choices:** hand back ordinary authority, seek a supported narrower renewal, restore affected rights, or pursue a separate reform. **Tradeoff:** lost convenience versus credibility and institutional continuity. Emergency practice does not become permanent automatically.

### The Petition That Prevents a War

**Trigger:** a grievance has a credible nonmilitary remedy path. **Choices:** hear or refer, offer deliverable redress, negotiate performance, or explain why no remedy is presently available. **Tradeoff:** limited resources now versus possible escalation later. Successful redress need not create loyalty, and failure need not create revolt.

These are proposed content families, not active opening cases or historical incidents. They should generate distinct campaign consequences rather than twelve versions of the same paperwork prompt.
