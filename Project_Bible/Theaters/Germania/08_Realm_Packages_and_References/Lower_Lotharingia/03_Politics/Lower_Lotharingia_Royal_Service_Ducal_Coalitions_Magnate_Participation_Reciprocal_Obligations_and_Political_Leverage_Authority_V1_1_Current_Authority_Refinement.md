## 1. Core identity: turn a ducal network into decisive action

**Gather enough strength to matter. Deliver enough to be asked again. Keep enough independence to choose the next commitment.**

The proposed player experience is a duke whose directly available resources are only part of the political picture. A well-built network can concentrate support for a campaign, protect a threatened connection, secure a hearing, or sustain a settlement beyond what the direct core could achieve alone. The reward is real strategic reach, not a larger list of acquaintances.

That reach is not permanent ownership. The player must decide which purpose can unite otherwise independent actors, what each participant contributes, what the duke can credibly offer, and when to release the arrangement before its costs outweigh its purpose. A compact dependable coalition can outperform a broad but contradictory one. A broad coalition can accomplish an objective that no small household could attempt.

Royal service is one powerful route to influence, not the faction's only playable strategy. Service can provide opportunities for access, recognition, negotiation, and future cooperation where the counterpart actually responds. Local consolidation, institutional partnership, limited alignment, and independent expansion remain meaningful alternatives. Neither permanent royal obedience nor inevitable rebellion is imposed.

## 4. Participants are not a uniform vassal list

The design admits several possible participant classes: a ducal household, a separately controlled lordship, an ecclesiastical institution, a local community, an external ruler, or a supported intermediary. These are role categories, not an opening membership roster or a claim that all possess the same rights.

A participant may contribute to one purpose while declining another. Supporting route protection does not endorse a claimant. Offering supplies does not grant command of a contingent. Accepting mediation does not accept the mediator's preferred settlement. A family relationship can open communication without supplying another household's consent.

S1 preserves external actors' independent state. S2 preserves separate interests across Lower and Upper Lotharingia, Antwerp, Verdun, and Tuscan contexts. This companion consumes those boundaries and does not merge them into one ducal sphere of ownership. [S1, S2]

## 5. Coalition purpose and contribution architecture

A coalition is a proposed arrangement for a defined purpose, not a permanent mini-faction. Several arrangements may coexist when their commitments are compatible.

```yaml
ProposedCooperationContext:
  identify:
    - PurposeAndIntendedOutcome
    - ConvenorAndParticipatingActors
    - ContributionByActorAndSource
    - ExpectedConsiderationAndAuthorityToOfferIt
    - GeographicIssueAndTimeScope
    - CoordinationAndCommandLimits
    - InformationSharingAndReporting
    - ReviewWithdrawalAndCompletionConditions
  possible_contributions:
    - MilitaryServiceThroughExistingOwners
    - SuppliesMoneyOrTransport
    - PassageOrFortifiedAccess
    - RepresentationRecognitionOrMediation
    - DocumentaryOrCommunicationSupport
    - BoundedProtectionOrRecoveryWork
  coalition_membership_creates_shared_treasury: false
  coalition_membership_creates_unified_command: false
  one_contribution_implies_all_contributions: false
```

The player should choose the contribution mix that solves the problem. A mediator can be more valuable than another contingent if the immediate objective is a hearing. Transport may matter more than nominal troop promises when an army cannot assemble. A coalition can succeed through negotiation without a battle.

## 6. Positive coalition portfolios

| Portfolio | Joint purpose | Player reward | Distinct exposure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home-defense compact | Protect a defined threatened core or connection | Greater local concentration and time to respond | Contributors may not accept pursuit beyond the agreed scope |
| Campaign association | Support a bounded offensive or relief objective | Stronger field operation through actual contributions | Service windows, command, supply, and reward disputes |
| Route-protection partnership | Sustain a specific connection or movement | More dependable communication, procurement, or passage | No universal corridor or permanent access |
| Royal-service association | Assemble a credible response to a known request | Opportunity for useful royal dealings and recognized performance | Home exposure and counterpart priorities |
| Mediation circle | Bring parties to a workable negotiation | Reduced isolation and more credible settlement channels | A mediator cannot compel acceptance |
| Recovery partnership | Restore a damaged function after conflict | Faster useful recovery through shared effort | Unequal benefits and continuing obligations |

These are selectable purpose families, not exclusive cultural paths. Their strength comes from combined contributions and coordination, not a free faction-wide modifier.

## 7. Convening and participation

The proposed interaction loop is deliberately compact: choose the objective, approach reachable actors, compare offers, commit a feasible package, and act. Routine correspondence should aggregate through existing systems.

A request should state what is wanted and why the counterpart might benefit. Useful offers can include a reciprocal service, supported payment, access, protection, mediation, or consideration within the promisor's authority. No offer creates a right to another owner's property or office.

A response can be acceptance, a narrower offer, a counterproposal, delay, refusal, or no confirmed receipt. Unknown response is not rejection. An actor may be willing but unable to deliver; the interface should distinguish a capacity problem from a disputed purpose where the evidence permits.

A coalition need not include everyone relevant to the region. A player can act with fewer participants, choose a narrower objective, or proceed alone. No universal unanimity requirement is introduced.

## 8. Royal service as a two-sided bargain

S2 explicitly separates a royal audience, a promise, completed recognition, and delivered military support. It also requires service cost, consideration, conditions, and review. This specialist develops those distinctions into proposed service choices. [S2]

| Service opportunity | Potential contribution | Possible consideration, if actually offered | Cost to expose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Representation or attendance | Principal or valid representative reaches a known audience | Hearing, negotiation, or clarification | Travel, attention, escort, and absence |
| Military assistance | Actual supported contingent or bounded operation | Received recognition, negotiated support, or future consideration | Casualties, supply, service duration, and home defense |
| Protection or escort | Secure a defined person, route, or movement | Credible performed service and a basis for further dealings | Competing protection duties |
| Mediation or local settlement support | Communication, evidence, and a supported negotiating role | Access or acceptance of a bounded role | Neutrality challenges and failed performance |
| Material assistance | Actual agreed resources or transport | Specific reciprocal terms | Resources unavailable for other uses |

These are proposed opportunities, not attested opening summonses. A request must come through an actual known channel. The royal counterpart retains its own decisions and may offer less than the duke seeks.

## 9. Royal alignment without a compulsory obedience path

The player can offer full feasible service, negotiate a limited contribution, seek substitution where accepted, request delay, decline, or contest the asserted obligation. The applicable owners determine legal and political consequences; this file does not make every refusal treason or every accepted request voluntary.

A service bargain can be strategically worthwhile even without a title reward. It may preserve a channel, resolve a dispute, or secure a concrete reciprocal act. Conversely, repeated expensive service with no useful return can justify changing strategy.

Royal support does not create local cooperation. Local cooperation does not create royal recognition. The faction can build both, favor one temporarily, or seek a compromise. No permanent loyalist-versus-rebel binary or scripted punishment cycle is imposed.

## 11. Concentration, command, and home exposure

A political association can assemble useful strength only through actual military and material systems. Contributor agreement does not automatically produce a field army. Assembly, route access, command, supply, and service duration remain separate.

The duke can preserve a household reserve, accept separate contributor commands, or seek a supported common command for a defined mission. A unified command may improve coordination but is not a prerequisite for every form of cooperation. Contributor independence is not an automatic combat penalty.

Concentration displaces other uses. An escort sent to royal service cannot also protect an abbey. A contributor's supplies cannot be counted as both retained local stores and delivered campaign support. Political success should enable a stronger operation, not bypass finite recruitment or recovery. Actual unit profiles remain deferred.

## 12. Ruler presence, delegation, and representation

S1 owns the government arrangements needed when the duke leaves. This file supplies the strategic choice: whether personal presence is worth the displaced attention and exposure. [S1]

Personal attendance may be useful for a high-stakes audience or joint campaign. A valid representative may be sufficient for a narrower matter. A letter can preserve contact without supplying physical service. The appropriate character, communication, and delegation owners determine what is possible.

No household relative becomes regent by default. No representative can offer every title, property, or force because the duke sent that representative. Returning from service requires handback of relevant duties and review of pending promises, not a reset of the interval.

## 13. Durable cooperation without permanent obedience

Repeated performance can make future coordination easier through concrete continuity: known channels, experienced representatives, familiar assembly arrangements, usable records, or a prior settlement that resolved a recurring dispute. These are proposed causal benefits, not automatic numerical bonuses.

Durability also requires renewal when the purpose changes. A contributor who supported defense need not support conquest. An institution that accepted protection need not finance a claimant. A community that helped restore a route need not accept a new burden indefinitely.

The player can turn one successful arrangement into a more dependable partnership, but must still provide actual inputs and accepted scope. Relationships remain directed and issue-specific. The same actor can cooperate in one lane and contest another without being classified globally loyal or hostile.

## 14. Political leverage and credible alternatives

Leverage is the ability to make a particular offer, refusal, threat, or alternative matter. It is not a spendable universal currency. Proposed sources include performed service, a useful contribution, access to a needed route, a supported negotiating role, an actual reserve, or a credible alternative partner.

The player should see what makes a position stronger: the counterpart needs the offered service, a rival option is available, a promise has been performed, or a material objective has changed. The game should not reveal private motives or hidden reserves to explain the outcome.

Leverage can decline when the need passes, a route changes, an alternative appears, or performance fails. An actor cannot repeatedly collect the same reward from one completed service unless a new valid bargain supports it. Recognition and historical memory can persist without creating an unlimited favor account.

## 15. Rival offers and counterplay

| Ducal approach | Rival or counterpart response | What remains playable |
|---|---|---|
| Gather a broad association | Offer narrower, more deliverable terms to selected participants | Retain a smaller core or revise the objective |
| Rely on royal service | Contest its value, pursue another audience, or exploit home exposure | Seek a concrete return, delegate, or change priorities |
| Depend on one route partner | Withdraw access within applicable terms or offer access elsewhere | Negotiate, reroute, or reduce the operation |
| Promise future rewards | Demand evidence, staged delivery, or a present alternative | Narrow promises rather than create assets |
| Lead a joint campaign | Challenge command or leave when supported terms expire | Reassign the mission, negotiate extension, or demobilize |
| Use institutional mediation | Question scope or decline the mediator | Choose another channel or continue the contest |

A rival offer is not automatic defection. A participant can remain neutral, accept limited cooperation, or maintain compatible arrangements with several actors. Hidden rival terms are not available to the player without a valid information source.

## 16. Withdrawal, expiry, breach, and renegotiation

These are different conditions. Completion can release a contributor honorably. Expiry can end a service window without hostility. Inability can interrupt performance without proving bad faith. A supported breach can create a dispute without dissolving every unrelated agreement.

The proposed response portfolio is to narrow scope, replace a contribution, extend by agreement, sequence obligations, seek mediation, preserve partial operation, or end the association. No contributor can be replaced with free capacity.

Withdrawal must reconcile resources in custody, forces already deployed, return routes, unpaid consideration, and outstanding protection. A coalition cannot dissolve to erase consumed supplies or liabilities. The appropriate owners determine whether a reciprocal term may be withheld or whether coercive action is available.

## 17. Succession, restoration, and rival-principal interfaces

S2 retains Frederick's incumbent context, Godfrey the Bearded's conditional restoration project, and the younger Godfrey's separate agency. This file creates no new opening membership, service record, or allegiance for any of them. [S2]

The same cooperation design can serve retention, restoration, coexistence, or a reduced political role. A successful incumbent can attract support through useful performance. A claimant can assemble issue-specific backing without acquiring every contributor's forces. A younger dynast can build a service record without inheriting the father's network.

Death, captivity, withdrawal, or title change requires review of which duties attach to a person, office, property, or agreement. The coalition is not cloned to a successor. Unrelated valid functions may continue while a disputed political question remains unresolved. Title settlement stays with S2.

## 18. Victory, return, and political consolidation

A completed campaign should produce choices beyond an automatic relationship increase. The player may perform rewards, release contributors, renew a narrower partnership, restore home protection, recover depleted resources, or seek the promised royal response.

Success can expose incompatible expectations. The player must reconcile them rather than receive universal approval. A costly victory can leave the coalition unable to act again immediately. A limited operation that achieves its purpose and preserves capacity can be a strong strategic result.

Failure need not destroy every relationship. Preserve performed contributions, known losses, valid outstanding terms, and opportunities for restitution or revised service. Recovery should rebuild the specific missing capability or relationship rather than spawn a comeback army or reset all participants.

## 20. AI behavior and bounded player attention

The ducal AI should choose a purpose that its known network can support, then seek contributions that solve actual gaps. It should not invite every actor to every arrangement or offer the highest possible reward regardless of deliverability.

Contributor AI should compare the requested purpose, actual burden, available capacity, expected consideration, existing commitments, and known performance. It should be able to counteroffer or cooperate narrowly. No private emotion or hidden intention needs to be inferred to explain observed decisions.

Royal AI remains an external decision-maker. It can accept, narrow, delay, or refuse a proposal according to its own supported state. This companion does not supply hidden royal priorities or guarantee a favorable response.

Routine renewals and compliant performance should aggregate. Escalate material changes: incompatible commitments, major losses, expiry during a critical operation, contested rewards, principal transition, or a new objective. The system should strengthen campaign decisions rather than require a meeting for every contribution.

Readiness is contribution-specific. A contributor can be politically committed but materially delayed. Money may be transferred while troops remain unassembled. Passage may be accepted while a bridge or route is unusable. A mediator may be available while the opposing party refuses the audience.

The player should be able to launch with a reduced arrangement when the remaining contributions are sufficient, delay for stronger concentration, revise the objective, or release actors whose support is no longer needed. No universal readiness score should conceal which contribution is missing.

## 24C. Participation mandate and representation

Participation requires a valid actor or representative able to accept the relevant terms. Presence at negotiation does not prove authority to pledge property, military service, institutional support, or another person's conduct.

| Participation question | Required distinction | Invalid shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Who speaks? | Principal, officeholder, delegate, envoy, intermediary, or institutional representative | Access to an audience means unlimited authority |
| What may be accepted? | Defined contribution, scope, duration, and conditions | General relationship permits every commitment |
| What remains excluded? | Property, command, office, jurisdiction, or private agency outside mandate | Silence transfers unrestricted discretion |
| How is acceptance known? | Delivered and authenticated communication where applicable | Draft language becomes an active agreement |
| When does the mandate end? | Completion, expiry, revocation, incapacity, transition, or review | Representation continues forever |

A representative may negotiate subject to confirmation. A narrow mandate can still produce a useful agreement. A participant should not become unavailable merely because one proposed envoy lacks authority; the convenor can seek confirmation, another valid representative, or a smaller commitment.

```yaml
  royal_silence_equals_approval_or_breach: false
  one_service_creates_permanent_favor: false
```

The receipt supports a strong campaign loop: the player can use performed service as evidence in later dealings without converting it into spendable favor. A counterpart may acknowledge performance but dispute the expected consideration. The player can seek clarification, renegotiate, accept another concrete return, or change future strategy.

Service failure should preserve its cause. Failure because a route closed is not identical to refusal. Failure after accepting an impossible promise can still damage credibility without requiring an inferred private attitude.

## 24G. Observed performance and political memory

Political memory should record observable acts: contributions promised, contributions performed, deadlines met or missed, protection delivered, losses shared, rewards performed, and disputes settled. It should not infer inner loyalty, gratitude, resentment, or sincerity.

Relevant actors can remember different parts of the same undertaking according to actual knowledge. A distant institution may know that a route was protected without knowing a private reward term. A royal audience may know that service arrived without knowing every local burden used to provide it.

Prior performance can reduce uncertainty in a similar future arrangement, support a request, or make a counterparty demand stronger guarantees after failure. It does not predetermine acceptance or create an endlessly stacking relationship bonus.

## 24H. Coalition strain, incompatible goals, and priority conflict

Coalition strain must be causal and issue-specific.

| Strain source | Observable problem | Useful response |
|---|---|---|
| Objective drift | Action expands beyond accepted purpose | Narrow, seek renewed consent, or release contributors |
| Competing command plans | Timing, route, target, or withdrawal differs | Coordinate, divide missions, appoint supported common command, or revise plan |
| Resource exhaustion | Contribution cannot continue safely | Reduce scope, replace through valid source, pause, or withdraw |
| Reward conflict | Promises overlap or become undeliverable | Disclose, renegotiate, compensate where supported, or accept loss of support |
| Unequal losses | One actor bears costs beyond the accepted package | Adjust future tasks, provide supported remedy, or end the arrangement |
| Transition | Principal, officeholder, or mandate changes | Review each commitment rather than copy or cancel all terms |
| Hidden or late information | Participants act on different known facts | Verify, communicate, preserve uncertainty, and avoid automatic blame |

Strain does not create inevitable collapse. A coalition can remain useful with disagreement if the core purpose still holds. Conversely, high nominal standing cannot make incompatible objectives operationally coherent.

## 24I. Confidential terms and knowledge boundaries

Agreements may contain public, restricted, or separately communicated terms where supported by the relevant systems. Confidentiality controls knowledge, not existence or validity.

```yaml
CoalitionKnowledgeBoundary:
  distinguish:
    - PublicPurposeAndObservedParticipation
    - KnownContributionAndDeployment
    - RestrictedNegotiatingPosition
    - ConfidentialRewardOrGuarantee
    - RumorClaimOrPartialReport
    - LaterDisclosureOrCapturedRecord
  player_omniscience_authorized: false
  coalition_members_share_all_private_information: false
  secret_term_creates_undetectable_free_state: false
  discovered_term_rewrites_prior_knowledge: false
```

A hidden term can be discovered through a valid channel, disclosed by a participant, captured in records, or revealed by performance. Discovery changes knowledge from that point; it does not mean every actor always knew it. The interface should explain known sources without exposing hidden truth.

Confidential terms still require deliverable objects and valid authority. Secrecy cannot make an unsupported promise valid.

## 24J. Neutrality, nonparticipation, and limited alignment

Neutrality is a meaningful position, not disguised hostility. An actor may decline military service while allowing passage, offering mediation, maintaining trade, or simply remaining outside the contest. Nonparticipation can result from lack of access, competing commitments, unresolved authority, risk, or an incompatible objective.

The duke can tolerate neutrality, seek a narrower contribution, offer protection, remove a burden, or proceed without the actor. Coercion remains possible through the relevant systems but carries its actual costs and consequences.

A participant can cooperate with more than one side on compatible matters. This should not become risk-free double service: exclusive terms, resource conflicts, secrecy, and command incompatibility remain enforceable where supported.

## 24K. Demobilization, reassembly, and recovery

The end of active performance requires more than removing a coalition icon.

```yaml
CoalitionCloseout:
  reconcile:
    - ForcesPeopleAndCommand
    - StoresMoneyTransportAndEquipmentInCustody
    - SafeReturnAndRouteAccess
    - CasualtiesMissingCaptivesAndDependents
    - RewardsCompensationAndRestitution
    - RecordsReportsAndDisputedPerformance
    - ContinuingProtectionAndRecoveryDuties
    - FutureContactOrRenewalTerms
  demobilization_restores_full_capacity: false
  return_creates_immediate_reassembly: false
  dissolution_erases_relationship_history: false
```

A successful closeout can preserve a dependable future channel. Poor closeout can leave stranded contributors, unpaid claims, disputed custody, or damaged home defenses. Reassembly should be easier only where actual people, records, routes, and resources survive, not because the coalition existed previously.

## 24L. Principal transition and network continuity

When Frederick, a claimant, a royal counterpart, or another principal changes status, review each commitment's basis. Some terms attach to an office, title, defined institution, or property; others are personal, temporary, or conditional.

A successor can inherit an operating channel or performed institutional agreement without inheriting every private promise, command relationship, reward expectation, or confidential term automatically. The successor may confirm, narrow, renegotiate, perform, contest, or end commitments through the relevant owners.

Transition should preserve actual deployed forces and resources until valid orders change them. A principal's death does not teleport contingents home, erase debt, or make a historical successor the new coalition leader by date.

## 24M. Leverage decay and non-stacking

The leverage section is strong but needs explicit anti-stacking rules.

```yaml
LeverageBoundary:
  leverage_is_subject_specific: true
  source_must_remain_relevant: true
  performed_service_can_support_later_request: true
  same_service_collected_repeatedly_without_new_bargain: false
  several_weak_sources_equal_unlimited_leverage: false
  historical_memory_equals_current_capacity: false
  recognition_equals_material_control: false
```

A completed service can retain historical importance while its immediate bargaining value declines. A route matters less after an alternative opens. A reserve matters less after demobilization or loss. A mediator matters only if relevant parties still accept the role.

The game should explain changed leverage through observable conditions rather than silently reducing a hidden score.

## 24N. AI correction and exploit prevention

The AI should preserve coalition identity without becoming passive or exploitable.

- Reject or counteroffer when the requested contribution is unavailable, undeliverable, or outside known authority.
- Prefer a smaller coherent arrangement over a broad package whose objectives or rewards conflict.
- Do not reserve the same force, money, transport, passage, office, or exclusive reward for multiple incompatible commitments.
- Launch reduced action when remaining contributions are sufficient; do not wait forever for an ideal coalition.
- Do not treat neutrality, delay, lost communication, or material inability as proven hostility.
- Do not continue expired service without renewal or supported emergency authority.
- Review home exposure before sending the duke, household reserve, or carriers away.
- Seek concrete consideration rather than repeatedly performing expensive service for an assumed future reward.
- Close out custody, return, reward, and recovery before considering the same contribution fully available again.
- Do not access confidential terms, hidden rival offers, or private capacity without a valid knowledge source.

The player must follow the same rules. Repeatedly inviting actors cannot produce free acceptance checks, duplicate capacity, or perfect intelligence.
