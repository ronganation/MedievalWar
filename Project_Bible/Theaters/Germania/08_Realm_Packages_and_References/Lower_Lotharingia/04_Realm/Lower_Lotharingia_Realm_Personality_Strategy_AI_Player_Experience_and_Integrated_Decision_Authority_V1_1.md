## 1. Strong verdict and identity formula

**Hold the ducal dignity. Build power that can answer the next demand.**

The general authority calls the faction an Imperial Frontier Ducal Network and gives it the identity phrase The Contested Middle Kingdom. Its central problem is converting recognized dignity into dependable capacity without absorbing independent actors or forcing restoration. [S1]

The proposed strategic formula is: bounded material core + performed ducal functions + selective cooperation + contested continuity + recoverable concentration. This is not a centralized Lorraine state, a universal Lower Rhine faction, or a royal administrative subdivision.

The distinctive fear is winning the appearance of power while losing the household, routes, records, contributors, and replacement capacity needed to act again. The distinctive opportunity is to make a smaller performed promise more valuable than a grand unsupported claim.

## 5. Player promise and campaign rhythm

The player should feel the difference between prestige and performance on the campaign map: a protected crossing, a defensible seat, a smaller reliable contingent, a fulfilled service, a negotiated corridor, a corrected abuse, or a settlement that preserves the core. These are design outcomes, not opening facts. [S1]

The proposed rhythm is observe, identify a concrete need, compare distinct alternatives, commit finite capacity, perform through owners, settle, recover, and review. Existing authority and resources can support immediate action; negotiation is useful where counterpart agency matters, not a universal delay.

Routine clerical work stays aggregated. Major decisions explain what is gained, what is exposed, which promise is displaced, and what remains uncertain. Difficulty should come from real scarcity and competing commitments, not hidden AI knowledge, fabricated hostility, mandatory genealogy chores, or historical railroading.

## 8. Posture transitions, commitment, and anti-oscillation

Review posture when owner-backed conditions materially change, an accepted commitment ends or fails, a new threat is known, recovery becomes urgent, or the player redirects strategy. Elapsed date alone does not switch the faction to restoration, rebellion, or western alignment.

A temporary rumor should not repeatedly reverse a costly plan. Before switching, compare the evidence change, sunk costs, exit terms, commitments already in motion, and the cost of waiting. Persistence is not stubbornness: a blocked route or lost principal can justify immediate review.

## 9. Decision-maker, knowledge, and action-specific checks

A strategic recommendation identifies the actor or accepted process able to choose and the owners needed to perform it. The faction label cannot itself sign a treaty, appoint an officer, spend private property, or command an external contingent.

Use actor-visible information with source, scope, freshness, contradiction, and uncertainty. Unknown is neither zero nor a secret available resource. A decision can proceed under uncertainty where the action is valid; complete verification is not mandatory for every move.

Action-specific checks distinguish available, blocked, unknown, stale, contested, external-pending, and not-applicable inputs. A missing escort blocks an escort mission, not an offer to negotiate. Counterpart consent is needed for agreement, not for declaring war against that counterpart. A claim or Papal permission is not required to unlock war.

## 11. Qualitative comparison and risk

Compare strategic contribution, urgency, feasibility, accepted duties, uncertainty, exposure, reversibility, opportunity cost, recovery, and future options. A prestigious gain is not automatically valuable if it destroys the means to hold it. A reversible trial can be preferable when uncertainty is material and delay affordable.

Risk is issue-specific: principal absence, core vulnerability, contributor withdrawal, institutional dispute, route isolation, material loss, recovery debt, succession ambiguity, or external dependence. Unknown risk is not low risk, but uncertainty alone is not proof of danger or an excuse for permanent inactivity.

This authority defines no universal aggression, loyalty, centralization, or legitimacy score. It creates no numeric utility weights, exact thresholds, difficulty rating, or predicted win probabilities. Later calibration must test whether the qualitative doctrine produces recognizable choices rather than merely valid records.

## 12. Portfolio arbitration and finite attention

Proposed presentation default: one primary strategic focus and up to two supporting focuses, alongside necessary maintenance and recovery. This adapts S2 as a design choice, not a hard limit on all campaign actions or a numeric attention currency.

Collision checks cover people in incompatible locations, overlapping mandates, money or stores promised twice, the same escort assigned simultaneously, contradictory neutrality/access terms, and recovery needs displaced by a new expedition. Priority does not reserve a resource. Reservations and transfers belong to their owners.

Resolve collisions by sequencing, narrowing, supported delegation, substitution, delay, renegotiation, withdrawal, or explicit accepted risk. Do not silently cancel an older promise because a newer opportunity has higher apparent value. Material harm cannot be hidden in a routine digest.

## 13. Doctrine: direct core and working government

The Baelen–Limbourg field remains evidence-bounded; no exact capital, estate inventory, treasury, garrison, or route is supplied here. Core-first strategy means protecting actual supported functions, not coloring every nearby region as owned.

The AI should compare the value of a public commitment with the private or institutional support it consumes. Household funds are not automatically public funds; record custody is not decision authority; a fortress is not a field army. A valid delegation can free the principal's attention but still require staff, resources, communication, and handback.

A strong choice may be to make one function dependable before expanding responsibilities. Another may be to accept a narrower settlement to preserve records, dependents, and a recoverable base. Neither is a universal prescription for passivity.

## 14. Doctrine: regional and institutional plurality

Cologne, Liège, Trier, Aachen, counts, communities, and the two advocacies retain distinct roles and owners. A received offer from one actor does not bind every local person or institution. Stavelot-Malmedy and St Truiden cannot become interchangeable support nodes. [S1]

The proposed AI values useful partial cooperation: passage without troops, mediation without title endorsement, protection with disputed dues. Refusal is reviewed by issue, capacity, access, and terms rather than interpreted as global hostility.

Clarification, accommodation, remedy, protection, restitution, and supported enforcement remain options. Force is not universally prohibited or universally optimal. Security can improve while grievance persists, and suppression cannot substitute for material recovery.

## 15. Doctrine: royal, western, and cross-Theater engagement

Royal service is neither permanent subordination nor automatic opposition. Full feasible service, limited contribution, counsel, mediation, substitution, delay, refusal, and abstention remain available according to actual terms.

Flanders, Boulogne, Hainaut-Mons, Upper Lotharingia, Burgundy, and Italia/Tuscany remain external fields, not a ready-made bloc. Kinship creates neither alliance nor private knowledge. A western bargain can coexist with royal cooperation on another issue. [S1, S4]

Cross-Theater opportunities require actual people, agency, travel, access, support, and return. Tuscan property or forces cannot be imported to solve a Lower Lotharingian shortfall. The same canonical person cannot be duplicated to attend two distant decisions.

## 17. Doctrine: military concentration, recruitment, and recovery

S1's military direction is network defense and selective concentration. The proposed AI seeks decisive nodes and feasible objectives rather than matching every rival army or defending every nominal responsibility equally.

Potential recruitment is not ready force. Finite recruitment pools remain population-informed through their owners, not directly created from title or population subtraction here. Equipment, horses, officers, command, supply, routes, home defense, casualties, demobilization, and replacement constrain actual campaigns.

Options include smaller force, local defense, delayed concentration, a valid allied contribution, supported mercenary contract, another route, or withdrawal. Germania regional and mercenary profiles remain Theater-owned. No new unit, roster, recruitment value, opening force, or naval capacity is created.

A victory can justify recovery before pursuit. Recovery is not compulsory inactivity: a limited opportunity can still be worthwhile when its costs and return are feasible. The comparison must expose which losses and obligations remain.

## 18. Doctrine: war freedom and political settlement

War remains available without a claim, casus-belli, Papal approval, or the target's consent. The diplomacy owner processes the action; actual operations need real military capacity. The AI may initiate conflict without first exhausting every negotiation, but it must not invent troops or private enemy intent.

Rivalry, a refusal, a pressure observation, or a historical date does not automatically declare war. Coalition membership is not war entry, contribution, or command. A victory is not title settlement, peace, integration, or reconciliation.

The proposed AI considers the purpose and exit horizon: what can the campaign actually secure, what must be negotiated, and what will be exposed at home? It can choose peace, truce, limited settlement, separate peace where applicable, or continued conflict. Agreement terms remain subject to real counterpart response and owner execution.

## 19. Historical suite, emergence, and objective separation

Memory provides interpreted past; momentum provides current pressure; emergence proposes grounded opportunities; crisis content presents decisions. This authority consumes those outputs without creating facts, people, events, objectives, rewards, or victory conditions. [S3]

Later fame gives no hidden priority bonus. A role need can be met by existing people, aggregate staff, reassignment, delay, or nonoccurrence. The AI cannot request a famous replacement whenever a commander or mediator fails.

Qualitative success signals include performed protection, reliable service, preserved core, closed obligations, restored capacity, and workable settlement. They are interpretation, not activated objectives, progress meters, rewards, or campaign termination rules. A dedicated objectives owner must define any actual victory contract.

## 20. Twelve integrated player decision packages

### Which promise should become dependable first?

**Situation:** several valid functions compete for limited support. **Options:** secure one core function, negotiate a narrower institutional duty, improve supported communication, or hold. **Tradeoff:** dependable reach versus symbolic breadth. **Review:** actual performance, support failure, or a changed threat. No exact opening prompt is assigned.

### How much can the core spare?

**Situation:** a public commitment depends on private or core resources. **Options:** bounded owner-approved support, smaller mission, another contributor, delay, or preserve reserve. **Tradeoff:** immediate influence versus household and defense exposure. **Review:** transfer, return, and recovery receipts. No resource is counted twice.

### The royal request arrives at the wrong moment

**Situation:** an actual received request conflicts with home needs. **Options:** full feasible service, limited contribution, substitution, counsel, delay, or refusal. **Tradeoff:** access and political opportunity versus home performance. **Review:** actual counterpart response, not assumed gratitude or punishment.

### A useful partner refuses the larger bargain

**Situation:** a counterpart accepts passage or mediation but not troops or alliance. **Options:** take the useful limited term, counteroffer, seek another source, or withdraw. **Tradeoff:** partial gain versus wider ambition. **Review:** scoped acceptance and performance, not global loyalty.

### Two protections, one available escort

**Situation:** distinct obligations compete for finite support. **Options:** prioritize, stage, seek accepted alternatives, or renegotiate. **Tradeoff:** timeliness versus strength and core security. **Review:** each institution's actual outcome. One escort cannot be delivered twice.

### The restoration opening is real, but narrow

**Situation:** known conditions make a political alternative feasible. **Options:** service, coexistence, limited settlement, preparation, war, or withdrawal. **Tradeoff:** present security versus future leverage. **Review:** changed recognition, capacity, and terms. No historical winner is required.

### The duke can solve it personally

**Situation:** personal intervention may work but exposes another function. **Options:** attend, delegate within valid scope, narrow the task, use a supported intermediary, or defer. **Tradeoff:** immediate effectiveness versus single-principal dependence. **Review:** performance and handback. No generic substitute official appears.

### Victory opens another road

**Situation:** a new opportunity follows a costly success. **Options:** recover first, take a reduced opportunity, negotiate access, contract supported help, or withdraw. **Tradeoff:** initiative versus losses, stores, and return. **Review:** actual recovery, not victory prestige.

### The western bargain conflicts with an old promise

**Situation:** accepted terms collide with a new proposal. **Options:** sequence, narrow, seek release, renegotiate, or reject the new offer. **Tradeoff:** opportunity versus reliability and breach risk. **Review:** actual agreement receipts. A new priority does not silently void an old commitment.

### The southern opportunity divides the household

**Situation:** a received cross-Theater proposal competes with home continuity. **Options:** supported representation, limited engagement, delay, or decline. **Tradeoff:** external options versus travel and attention. **Review:** agency, access, and actual resources. No foreign household assets transfer.

### A new holder, an unfinished government

**Situation:** an owner-confirmed title or principal change leaves obligations in motion. **Options:** valid interim continuity, handback, confirmation, renegotiation, or reduced survival. **Tradeoff:** speed versus unsupported authority. **Review:** each office, property, force, and agreement separately.

### The strongest move is to stop expanding

**Situation:** continued commitments threaten the ability to act again. **Options:** retrench, repair, reduce service, settle, or accept a bounded risk. **Tradeoff:** lost opportunity now versus future agency. **Review:** concrete capacity restoration or a materially changed threat. Retrenchment is not automatically defeat.

These are proposed reusable packages, not active events or a required order of play. They integrate existing cases instead of duplicating specialist decisions.

## 21. Advice, disagreement, and unavailable inputs

Consult only actual available actors or supported institutional processes with relevant access and knowledge. Store a communicated recommendation as such, not as an inferred private belief. Contradictory advice can remain visible without classifying an advisor as disloyal or inferior.

If advice is unavailable, narrow the question, seek a valid channel, proceed under uncertainty where permitted, or hold. Do not spawn a court to complete the decision packet. A recommendation from one institution does not represent all institutions, and a household recommendation does not confer public authority.

The final explanation should show why the chosen option protects one value while exposing another. Forecasts remain forecasts; later results must not be rewritten to make the recommendation look correct.

## 23. Abstention, initiative, and anti-frustration

No action can be valid when essential authority or capacity is missing, terms conflict, information is too stale for the proposed act, or recovery offers greater durable value. Abstention needs a specific reason and review condition, not permanent inactivity.

The AI can also take initiative: propose a limited offer, prepare a supported defense, seek a new route, request mediation, or choose an available conflict. Unknown external intent does not prevent sending a proposal; it prevents inventing the answer. This distinction avoids turning ownership discipline into universal passivity.

Blocked-option presentation should show the strongest blocker, what is actually missing, a narrower feasible alternative where one exists, and what change would reopen the option. Do not require the player to resolve every background uncertainty to end a turn.

## 24. Player readability, decision budget, and difficulty

## 25. Persistence, determinism, and three independent clocks

Historical +21 months, biological +7 months, and one ordered Winter/Spring/Summer/Autumn step remain separate. AI advances none of them. A season matters through actual routes, supply, labor, availability, and communication; a date creates no strategy or historical outcome.

Later persistence should retain selected focus, pending proposals, owner versions, commitments, deferrals, review triggers, explanations, and unresolved consequences. Advanced saves must preserve different holders, surviving households, changed institutions, expired agreements, dead people, depleted pools, and missed historical pathways.

For future deterministic testing, identical visible inputs, content/tuning versions, stable ordering, and any authorized seed should reproduce the same choice and trace. Wall-clock time, localized labels, file order, or arbitrary iteration should not determine strategy. These are acceptance requirements, not a verified algorithm, save contract, performance result, or Unreal class.

## 27A. Second-pass strategic signature, decision trace, and failure containment

The first pass already establishes sound ownership boundaries and a credible doctrine. This refinement makes the faction more recognizably Lower Lotharingian rather than generically cautious: it converts narrow, successfully performed commitments into wider bargaining options while refusing prestige expansion that cannot be supported, recovered from, or handed back cleanly.

```yaml
LowerLotharingianStrategicSignature:
  primary_player_skill: ConvertBoundedPerformanceIntoNegotiatedLeverage
  preferred_growth_pattern: DeepenOneReliableFunctionBeforeAddingAnother
  characteristic_success: SmallCommitmentPerformedSoReliablyThatItExpandsOptions
  characteristic_failure: PrestigeExpansionOutrunsCoreRecordsRoutesPeopleAndRecovery
  anti_generic_rule: DoNotChooseCautionMerelyBecauseUncertaintyExists
  anti_snowball_rule: NewStandingDoesNotCreateFreeCapacityConsentOrPermanentControl
  anti_turtle_rule: RecoveryMustNameAReopeningConditionOrFeasibleLimitedInitiative
```

Five linked behaviors define the strategic signature:

1. **Performance before breadth.** Prefer one function that can be sustained over several nominal responsibilities that cannot be performed.
2. **Narrow bargains as assets.** Passage, mediation, escort, protection, counsel, and limited service are valuable without being inflated into alliances.
3. **Concentration with a return plan.** A campaign commitment is incomplete unless home exposure, return access, demobilization, replacement, and handback are visible.
4. **Continuity without automatic restoration.** Protect surviving government and household capacity without making a claimant, title dispute, or historical date the universal priority.
5. **Leverage from reliability.** Completed obligations improve future options only through owner-recorded outcomes and actual counterpart response, never through a hidden automatic reputation bonus.

## 27C. Strategy failure modes and corrective responses

| Failure mode | Observable strategic problem | Required correction |
|---|---|---|
| Dignity inflation | New obligations are accepted because the title appears to demand them | Narrow the promise, identify the performing owner, and expose the unsupported remainder |
| Core starvation | Expeditions repeatedly consume records, escorts, stores, attention, or household continuity | Pause expansion, protect the affected function, and define a concrete reopening condition |
| Diplomatic overreading | Limited acceptance becomes alliance, loyalty, or regional consent | Preserve the exact accepted scope and reopen only the unaccepted terms |
| Restoration tunnel vision | Every crisis is interpreted through the ducal contest | Compare coexistence, service, settlement, survival, and unrelated realm needs |
| Recovery paralysis | Repair becomes indefinite inactivity | Require an owner-backed reopening condition or propose a feasible limited initiative |
| Opportunistic breach | A new offer silently displaces an older promise | Seek release, renegotiate, sequence, or reject the new offer |
| Military prestige pursuit | Victory triggers pursuit despite severe depletion or home exposure | Compare reduced exploitation, settlement, withdrawal, and recovery |
| Delegation fiction | Delegation is treated as free capacity or automatic performance | Check mandate, staff, communication, resources, acceptance, and handback separately |
| Historical railroading | Later fame or chronology becomes a required path | Return to current visible conditions and allow divergence or nonoccurrence |
| Administrative overload | Routine uncertainty creates repeated major prompts | Aggregate routine work while escalating material harm, breach, collision, or irreversible choice |

A blocked large option should generate a smaller executable alternative when one is supported. Failure containment must make the AI more decisive, not merely more restrictive.
