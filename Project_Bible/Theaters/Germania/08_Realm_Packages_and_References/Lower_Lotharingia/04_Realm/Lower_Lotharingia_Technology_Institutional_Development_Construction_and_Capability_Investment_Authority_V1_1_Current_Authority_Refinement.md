## 1. Development identity: make a scattered realm work together

**Build a dependable core. Connect useful partners. Turn investment into repeatable capability.**

The proposed Lower Lotharingian development game is neither a generic research race nor permanent austerity. The player begins from the design problem of limited directly usable capacity and develops a stronger realm through selected sites, routes, offices, skilled service, and negotiated institutional cooperation. Successful investment should make the faction more capable of campaigning, defending, administering, trading, and recovering, not merely better at documenting its constraints.

The satisfying progression is visible: a vulnerable base becomes a defensible and supplied center; a costly route becomes a more dependable connection; an unreliable obligation becomes a repeatable service; an isolated project becomes part of a functioning network. Expansion can increase scale, but consolidation makes that scale usable.

The player may pursue several channels at once. A fortified core, commercial access, documentary administration, institutional patronage, and military support are complementary investments with competing inputs. No universal cultural fork, mandatory centralization path, or unique national invention tree is introduced.

All development portfolios below are proposed game design, not independently verified claims about medieval Lower Lotharingian technology or construction.

A purchased text, visiting specialist, diplomatic contact, or captured facility can create an opportunity to learn. It does not instantly convert every settlement. Skilled people, tools, material supply, suitable sites, training, and institutional acceptance determine adoption.

A project can improve an existing capability without introducing a new technology. Better repair, scheduling, storage, instruction, or recordkeeping may be more valuable than a prestigious new structure. Conversely, a new facility can be justified when it creates a genuinely different function.

## 5. Core development portfolio

The following are candidate investment lanes, not opening buildings or certified historical specializations.

| Lane | Proposed investment | Strategic reward | Tradeoff and counterplay |
|---|---|---|---|
| Defensible core | Repair and improve a supported seat, stores, access, and routine | More dependable local defense and campaign staging | Concentrates resources; bypass, siege, or loss can disrupt several functions |
| Connected routes | Repair a supported road segment, crossing approach, landing, or relay arrangement | More reliable movement and communication where access exists | Rights, carriers, security, and alternate routes still matter |
| Storage and distribution | Improve suitable storage, handling, custody, and delivery organization | Better ability to preserve and move actual stocks | Does not create goods; vulnerable to interruption, loss, and upkeep failure |
| Documentary administration | Support clerks, archives, reporting, and delegated routine | More reliable collection, obligations, and continuity | Requires people and cooperation; records do not compel compliance |
| Productive support | Repair or develop an evidenced workshop or productive site | Better supported output, repair, or quality | Inputs, skilled labor, demand, transport, and property rights constrain value |
| Commercial interface | Improve a supported market function or negotiated access | More useful exchange opportunities and procurement | Access is not stock, profit, monopoly, or guaranteed delivery |
| Institutional partnership | Co-fund a useful service or facility under accepted terms | Durable cooperation and wider functional reach | Partner retains property and priorities; benefits are not wholly ducal |
| Military support | Improve supported training, repair, remount care, transport, or coordination | More reliable use and recovery of actual military resources | No free soldiers, mounts, equipment, or instant veteran cadres |

The faction should be able to become prosperous and militarily formidable. Its asymmetry is the route to that strength: reliable connections and performed investments rather than a treasury generated by title or a universal regional resource pool.

## 6. The defensible-core program

A strong core is a bundle of useful functions, not simply the highest wall tier. Candidate components include protected storage, workable access, routine administration, repair capacity, accommodation, and defense through the appropriate owners.

The player chooses whether to strengthen one principal base, distribute functions across several supported sites, or rely partly on partners. Concentration simplifies coordination but increases the cost of losing one location. Distribution improves redundancy but consumes more transport, staffing, and protection.

No exact Baelen or Limbourg-sur-Vesdre building, fortress state, ownership schedule, or opening garrison is inferred. Any named-site project must reconcile with the existing direct-core specialist and geography authority before adoption. This file supplies a program of choices, not a map payload.

## 7. Route and crossing investment

A route project must name the actual function being improved: messenger reliability, civilian trade, movement of a particular cargo class, military passage, or local access. One improvement does not automatically serve every purpose.

Physical repair and political access are separate. A repaired crossing can remain unavailable to an army. A passage agreement can remain unusable because the approach is damaged or carriers are absent. A landing does not create boats or crews. A road improvement does not create a continuous safe corridor.

Redundancy should be valuable. A second supported route can preserve a reduced service after disruption. The AI should compare a prestigious main connection with a cheaper alternative that solves the immediate problem. No exact travel-speed bonus or route throughput is calibrated here.

## 8. Productive sites, skills, and material adoption

Productive development should improve a supported chain: inputs, labor, skill, tools, work, suitable output, storage, and delivery. The player can expand throughput, improve quality, reduce interruption, or restore lost function. These are different objectives and should not all arrive from one generic upgrade.

A workshop requires actual skilled people and materials. Hiring a specialist can support training or production but does not guarantee permanent local expertise. Knowledge can persist through trained successors, records, and repeated practice; losing a single specialist need not erase all progress if those supports exist.

No regional ironworking, textile, horse-breeding, milling, or other specialization is asserted as historical fact. Such candidates require site-specific evidence and comparison with existing economic records before named admission.

## 9. Governance and institutional development

Administrative investment should make existing authority more performable, not create new rights by research. Better reporting can reveal a shortage; it cannot make a disputed due collectible. More clerks can support correspondence; they cannot grant a title or remove an external institution's independence.

Candidate improvements include training existing staff, maintaining records, establishing supported delegation, preserving seal custody, and making reporting more reliable. These can reduce avoidable failure while leaving political negotiation meaningful.

Cooperative institutional projects need explicit purpose, contribution, custody, access, benefit, maintenance, and exit terms. A ducal contribution to an abbey project does not make the building ducal property. A shared archive service does not expose all private records. The ecclesiastical authority retains patronage and church-politics interpretation.

## 10. Military modernization without unit creation

Military development remains an infrastructure and organizational portfolio here. Candidate investments include supported instruction, equipment repair, storage, transport organization, care of actual mounts, and repeated coordination among contributors.

These investments can make recruitment or recovery feasible through existing military owners. They do not add soldiers to finite pools, create horses, generate officers, duplicate garrisons, or turn research into equipment. A training facility without instructors or eligible people remains underused.

No individual unit profile, new roster card, exact tier unlock, opening army, combat statistic, or recruitment-pool quantity is created. The user has deferred that work. Military identity remains the separate authority's subject.

Partial work can retain value. Materials may be recoverable, a reduced function may open, or a later project may reuse completed work. Cancellation does not refund consumed labor, lost material, or damage already applied.

## 12. Investment choices and competing capacity

The player should see familiar construction queues, costs, upkeep, and development choices. Deeper causes should appear when they change a decision, not as mandatory approvals for every shipment.

A compact development budget can distinguish available funds, restricted commitments, expected receipts, and partner contributions. Expected income is not spendable cash. A grant promised by another institution is not a delivered input.

Labor, transport, materials, skilled service, defense, and money compete across projects. A fortress repair can delay a route project. A campaign can draw away carriers. A patronage commitment can consume funds that would otherwise restore a productive site. These tradeoffs should permit deliberate prioritization rather than permanent deadlock.

No exact cost, duration, interest rate, upkeep percentage, or project limit is invented. Qualitative priorities guide later calibration.

## 13. Development channels and progression

| Channel | Initial useful step | Mature capability | Persistent limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core resilience | Restore one important function | A supported base with complementary defense, stores, repair, and routine | Concentration and maintenance costs |
| Network reliability | Repair one actual bottleneck | Redundant supported connections and more dependable service | Access, security, and carriers remain necessary |
| Administrative depth | Improve one reporting or custody problem | Trained staff, continuity, and usable records | Authority and cooperation are not created |
| Productive capacity | Restore an evidenced site | Better-supported output and replacement integration | Inputs, labor, quality, and delivery |
| Institutional partnership | Complete one bounded joint project | Repeated cooperation with clear benefits and duties | Partners retain independent priorities |
| Military support | Restore a specific repair or training function | More reliable sustainment and recovery of actual forces | Finite people, mounts, equipment, and pools |

Early improvements remain useful. A later development need not demolish or replace every earlier facility. Upgrading can change capacity, reliability, or function while preserving the site's identity. New branches should be justified by distinct gameplay, not symmetry with another faction.

## 14. Diffusion, access, and competition

Knowledge can spread through supported travel, service, trade, institutions, correspondence, or captured material. These are proposed opportunity channels, not a universal historical diffusion formula. The exact availability of each method requires evidence and shared-system reconciliation.

Access creates a chance to adopt, not ownership of another realm's expertise. A rival can hire the same scarce specialist, disrupt an input route, offer a partner better terms, or develop a competing market. Knowledge itself should not be arbitrarily forgotten when a treaty ends, but continued operation can depend on access that has ended.

The faction may borrow useful practices without changing cultural identity or replacing its entire military roster. Selective adoption is a strength; unlimited combinations without cost or support are not.

## 15. Maintenance, deterioration, and mothballing

Maintenance is part of the investment decision. A large network that cannot be supported should not remain permanently efficient. Condition can deteriorate through use, damage, absent staff, disrupted inputs, or deferred upkeep, subject to adopted shared systems.

Mothballing can preserve some assets while suspending operation. It should reduce selected ongoing demands without pretending that reactivation is free. Reopening may require inspection, repairs, staff, supplies, and restored access. Exact deterioration rates and thresholds remain uncalibrated.

The player can prioritize a reduced dependable network over an oversized failing one. That is a strategic option, not a universal instruction to avoid expansion. Successful growth should support more ambitious investments.

## 16. War, occupation, succession, and recovery

War can interrupt work, displace specialists, damage sites, divert transport, or consume reserves. Occupation gives physical access where actually established; it does not automatically transfer every right, staff member, institutional agreement, or learned practice.

A successor inherits only the appropriate assets, offices, obligations, and institutional continuity through their owners. Private patronage, contributor relationships, and disputed property need separate treatment. No ruler change resets every project or guarantees every partner's cooperation.

Recovery should prioritize useful functions: secure people and custody, restore access, repair a bottleneck, reopen a reduced service, then rebuild broader capacity. Peace is an opportunity to recover, not automatic restoration. Preserve residual debt, damage, claims, and unfinished work.

| A partner offers co-funding | Accept bounded terms, negotiate benefits, fund independently, or defer | Contribution does not transfer ownership automatically |

## 18. AI investment and counterplay

The AI should identify the current bottleneck and choose a project whose useful output fits its strategy. It should compare repair with new construction, concentrated strength with redundancy, and immediate campaign support with long-term productive capacity.

Avoid idle-building spam, prestige projects without operating inputs, repeated cancellation that refunds consumed work, and military infrastructure that assumes unlimited recruitment. When ideal inputs are unavailable, the AI should choose a reduced feasible project, negotiate, repair, or preserve reserves rather than remain inert.

Opponents can disrupt access, compete for scarce service, threaten concentrated sites, or exploit overcommitment. No hidden knowledge of rival project queues or private reserves is granted. AI and player use the same finite sources and actual project consequences.

## 22B. Portfolio prioritization and strategic postures

Lower Lotharingia should not follow one mandatory build order. The same development portfolio can support several coherent strategic postures, each with meaningful opportunity cost.

| Posture | First priorities | Strategic payoff | Exposure retained |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core consolidator | Defensible seat, storage, repair, records, and dependable access | Strong staging and recovery base | Concentrated loss risk and slower outward reach |
| Network broker | Route redundancy, agreements, relays, commercial and institutional interfaces | Wider access and flexible procurement | Dependence on partners, carriers, security, and performed terms |
| Productive rebuilder | Repair evidenced productive functions, skills, tools, and delivery | Stronger material replacement and economic recovery | Vulnerable to missing inputs, labor, demand, or transport |
| Military supporter | Training support, repair, remount care, transport, and campaign staging | More reliable use of actual forces | Competes directly with civilian and institutional recovery |
| Institutional investor | Joint services, archives, education, hospitality, and protected functions | Durable cooperation and administrative reach | Benefits remain shared and partner priorities remain independent |
| Balanced developer | Smaller investments across several bottlenecks | Flexible resilience and fewer total-failure points | Slower delivery of any single high-capacity function |

These are AI and player strategy directions, not character traits or exclusive cultural branches. Changing posture should redirect future priorities without deleting completed assets or instantly converting staff and projects.

## 22E. Project governance, mandate, audit, and custody

Large projects need a responsible sponsor and an operating owner. These may differ. A duke can fund a route project whose local operation depends on another property holder, community, or institution.

| Project responsibility | Required question | Failure if omitted |
|---|---|---|
| Sponsor | Who commits funds or political backing? | Promise without deliverable resources |
| Mandate | Who may authorize the work and its scope? | Construction becomes unsupported appropriation |
| Site or partner owner | Who controls access and property? | Physical association is mistaken for ownership |
| Project steward | Who coordinates labor, records, and reporting? | Delays and losses become invisible |
| Custodian | Who holds transferred inputs and completed assets? | Duplicate balances and disputed possession |
| Inspector or accepting party | Who determines whether agreed work is complete? | Physical work is confused with accepted performance |
| Operator | Who supplies staff and routine inputs? | Completed asset remains idle |
| Maintainer | Who bears continuing burden? | Benefits persist without upkeep |

Audit means comparing commitment, receipt, work, and custody. It does not create omniscient anti-corruption detection. Reports can be late, mistaken, incomplete, or contested. Investigation and remedy remain with relevant law, governance, property, and relationship owners.

A delegated steward cannot silently expand a project beyond mandate. Applied work remains real even if authorization later fails; remediation may require compensation, restoration, renegotiation, or acceptance.

## 22G. Bottlenecks, capacity, and network interaction

A development network performs only as well as the dependencies relevant to the requested function. Avoid one universal capacity number.

| Requested function | Likely bottlenecks | What improvement can and cannot do |
|---|---|---|
| Preserve campaign supplies | Stock, suitable storage, custody, transport, route security | Better storage reduces avoidable loss but creates no stock |
| Move messages reliably | Clerks, messengers, relays, access, safe routes | More relays do not guarantee truthful or timely reports |
| Repair equipment | Skilled labor, tools, materials, workshop access, custody | A workshop cannot repair without inputs and time |
| Support mounted forces | Actual mounts, handlers, feed, shelter, equipment and recovery | Care capacity cannot create horses or riders |
| Expand productive output | Labor, skill, inputs, tools, demand, storage and delivery | More floor space alone is insufficient |
| Operate a joint institution | Valid agreement, staff, site access, resources and cooperation | Ducal funding does not remove institutional independence |

Redundancy applies to functions, not labels. Two roads that share the same vulnerable crossing may not provide genuine redundancy. Two workshops that depend on one specialist remain a single point of failure. The player should be able to trace the strongest bottleneck without receiving perfect knowledge of hidden external capacity.

## 22H. Military, civilian, and institutional competition

The V1 correctly allows development to strengthen warfare without creating units. V1.1 makes the competition explicit.

- Carriers assigned to campaign supply are unavailable for some construction and market movement.
- Materials used for fortification cannot simultaneously repair housing, routes, workshops, or institutional property.
- Skilled repair personnel may support field recovery or productive output, but not both at full capacity everywhere.
- Mount care for military service competes with wider transport and productive animal use where relevant.
- Garrison and route protection can sustain projects while reducing mobile field capacity.
- Institutional cooperation may provide access, staff, refuge, or records, but those partners retain their own obligations.

These are qualitative design relationships, not opening allocations or exact ratios. The player can favor war, civil recovery, institutional partnership, or a workable mix. A military choice is not inherently wrong; it simply carries visible displaced uses.

## 22I. Failure, salvage, conversion, and recovery

Projects can fail without becoming binary waste. Preserve what actually survives.

```yaml
ProjectFailureDisposition:
  inspect:
    - WorkCompleted
    - MaterialsInstalledStoredLostOrRecoverable

    - SiteAccessPropertyAndSecurity
    - OutstandingPaymentsClaimsAndDamage
    - AlternativeUsefulFunction
  possible_actions:
    - ResumeOriginalProject
    - NarrowToReducedFunction
    - ConvertToSupportedAlternative
    - PreserveOrMothball
    - SalvageMovableInputs
    - TransferOrRenegotiate
    - AbandonAndRecordResidualConsequences
  failure_refunds_consumed_inputs: false
  conversion_is_free: false
  salvage_restores_original_value: false
```

Conversion should require compatibility. A storage structure may be adapted to another bounded use, but not every building can become a barracks, workshop, or market. Reuse preserves some value while creating new adjustment costs and potentially losing original function.

Recovery proceeds from security and access to inspection, repair, staffing, inputs, reduced operation, and mature restoration. Money can fund steps but cannot skip people, rights, transport, or work.

## 22J. AI portfolio correction and anti-spam rules

The AI should maintain a project portfolio, not simply choose the highest nominal upgrade.

- Correct an idle completed asset before building another identical facility when the missing input is obtainable.
- Avoid duplicate functions that share the same unresolved bottleneck.
- Preserve at least one feasible route or operating base when pursuing a prestigious expansion.
- Prefer repair when repair restores the needed function more efficiently than replacement.
- Continue useful partial operation rather than waiting indefinitely for ideal inputs.
- Avoid military-support construction when no supported recruitment, repair, transport, or recovery use exists.
- Avoid partner projects when contribution, access, operation, or exit terms cannot be supported.
- Suspend or narrow unaffordable projects without receiving full refunds.
- Respond to enemy disruption by rerouting, protecting, reducing scope, negotiating, repairing, or changing strategy.
- Do not read hidden rival reserves, project queues, skill pools, or agreement terms.

Different AI personalities can weight speed, resilience, prestige, military readiness, profit, or institutional cooperation, but all remain subject to actual sources and the same failure rules.
