## 1. Strong verdict: field a useful host without hollowing out the Realm

**Hold the core. Concentrate for a purpose. Return with a capacity to act again.**

The general Realm authority describes fragmented ducal capacity, network defense, selective concentration, and negotiated contributions rather than a unified territorial army. The Antwerp specialist separates frontier responsibility from troops, garrisons, revenues, and local obedience. [S1, S5]

The proposed military identity is a **recoverable ducal host assembled from distinct sources**. Its strength is matching a limited force to a useful objective: relief, escort, a crossing, a frontier duty, a bounded expedition, or a negotiated coalition effort. Its weakness is overextension across separated interests, contested command, finite sources, and a return route that may be less secure than the outward march.

This is not a universal small-army restriction. A developed Realm may field a substantial host when actual sources, officers, equipment, routes, and sustainment support it. The design rewards effective concentration, not permanent caution. It also permits defeat, capture, failed relief, broken service, and a reduced but viable recovery base.

## 3. Ownership and entity taxonomy

This authority owns proposed Realm military service interpretation, finite recruitment-pool behavior, command and muster requirements, force duties, operational readiness, battle handoff, military loss accounting, replacement, and demobilization design. It does not appoint a new shared runtime writer.

| Entity | Meaning | Not equivalent to |
|---|---|---|
| Service source | A supported contributor or source of possible military service | A permanent pool or army |
| Recruitment source | Place, community, household, relationship, or contract supporting recruitment | All residents of a historic region |
| Pool definition | Finite recruitment eligibility and recovery rules | Current available recruits |
| Unit profile | Content identity, equipment, role, strengths, and weaknesses | A fielded formation |
| Formation | Actual organized military body with people and assignments | A reusable card or free garrison |
| Host | Temporary coordinated grouping of contributing forces | One permanently unified national army |
| Duty | Scoped task such as watch, escort, garrison, or reserve | A second copy of its assigned force |
| Command | Accepted authority over a defined military scope | Title, fame, or universal obedience |

Population retains people, household availability, skills, and demographic consequences. Character owners retain canonical identity, location, and life state. Economy retains material and fiscal inputs; the verified shared Supply contract determines operational custody and consumption. Geography retains places and routes. Governance and law retain competence and service disputes. Diplomacy retains war, peace, access, coalition, ransom, and capitulation terms. Battle retains tactical resolution. Health retains diagnosis, treatment, fitness, and mortality cause.

## 8. Reservation, collisions, and emergency overdraw

The same person, officer, mount, weapon, transport asset, or source capacity cannot perform incompatible assignments simultaneously. Check household work, care, institutional duties, core defense, existing service, training, transport, and recovery before committing.

A reservation identifies consumer, purpose, window, source, related assets, release condition, and partial-failure handling. Canceling recruitment releases only unused recoverable reservations; it does not reverse travel, consumed supplies, wages already earned, or public consequences.

Emergency overdraw is a deliberate choice with competent authority, visible displaced functions, bounded purpose, duration, return priority, and recovery plan. It can damage the source permanently. It creates neither additional people nor a free exception to equipment and supply requirements.

## 13. Readiness, training, experience, and cohesion

Readiness is mission-specific: personnel, command, equipment, mounts, training, cohesion, rest, supply, finance, access, and service scope. A formation may be locally ready but not expedition-ready. Surplus money cannot replace a missing commander or inaccessible route.

Training requires supported people, instructors or cadres, place, equipment, supplies, time, and practice. Experience persists where supported but does not grant command authority. Replacement can change cohesion; veteran identity is not infinite replenishment.

Battle and military owners resolve morale, fatigue, cohesion, and discipline in their proper contexts. No cultural, language, or institutional label predetermines quality. Health findings remain external. A readable summary may show the decisive limitation without replacing these causes with one universal score.

## 14. Garrisons, watch, escorts, patrols, and reserve

A fortification creates no defenders. Distinguish actual movable garrison formations from local or assignment-only defense represented by the adopted military system. Neither representation creates free additional field troops.

When a garrison departs, update the defense and burden left behind. A reserve is an actual assignment with opportunity cost, not uncommitted capacity available everywhere. Patrols and escorts protect bounded routes or tasks; they do not secure every crossing or grant property rights.

An escort diverted to relief can leave a convoy exposed. A watch strengthened at the core can reduce frontier coverage. Rotation requires an actual replacement, travel, handover, supply, and a continuing local defense plan. Return is not automatic reassignment.

## 15. Movement, concentration, and quartering

Review purpose-specific routes, physical condition, access or hostile operational circumstances, transport, supplies, communication, rest, concentration, and return. River presence creates no boats, crews, landing capacity, or naval control. [S4]

Quartering needs actual support and applicable terms or authority; it is not free settlement capacity. Distinguish camp presence from property ownership. Record host burdens, damage, compensation where applicable, and departure. Coercive use can occur through the relevant systems but must not be disguised as accepted hospitality.

A concentration plan should identify what remains at the core, which obligations lose coverage, and what happens if the return corridor closes. Alternatives include staging, reduced scope, another route, supported relief, negotiation, withdrawal, or explicit risk. None guarantees success.

## 16. Equipment, mounts, transport, and support

A formation needs suitable issued equipment and trained use. Stocks, ownership, condition, issue, custody, loss, repair, and return remain distinct. Captured equipment requires actual recovery and suitability; a battlefield report does not fill the armory automatically.

A riding animal is not necessarily a trained military mount. Mounted service needs rider, suitable mount, tack, handlers, feed, water, rest, and route compatibility. Pack and draft animals can compete with cultivation and civilian transport. A stable or pasture creates no remounts.

Guides, messengers, engineers, carriers, handlers, and care liaisons need actual availability. They can be represented at an appropriate aggregate level without named paperwork for every participant. Their presence does not create equipment, medical treatment, siege progress, or route knowledge beyond their supported role.

## 20. Siege, relief, capitulation, and occupation

Proposed siege stages are feasibility, preparation, approach, partial or sustained investment, engineering where supported, interruption or relief, negotiation, battle or entry, withdrawal, and aftermath. These are campaign design stages, not real-world technical siege instructions.

Investment does not prove complete blockade. Engineering proposals need actual skills, tools, materials, labor, protection, and completed work. A timer alone creates no breach, surrender, or relief failure. Service expiry can reduce coverage mid-operation.

Capitulation terms belong to diplomacy and relevant legal owners; acceptance is not performance. Entry requires actual execution and accounting. Occupation needs defenders, support, command, and local interfaces, not a free garrison. Siege closure leaves damage, care, custody, return, compensation, and recovery unresolved where appropriate.

## 21. Casualties, missing, capture, and accountable custody

Record the incident, affected formation or bounded group, observed status, confidence, source, material consequences, and referrals. Casualty is not automatically death; missing is not automatically captured or deserted. Canonical life state and health conclusions remain with their owners.

Military handles operational guard, escort, movement, and handover for admitted military prisoners. Law and governance retain custody basis and review; diplomacy retains ransom, exchange, safe conduct, and release terms. Custody is not ownership of a person and creates no free labor, recruit, or automatic ransom value.

A transfer distinguishes preparation, transit, receiving acceptance, release, departure, arrival, and reintegration. Failed handover preserves the last accepted responsibility while the actual physical situation and safety needs are resolved. Payment alone does not release a captive; release does not return a commander to field readiness. Foreign-held subjects remain externally controlled reports, not locally writable state.

## 22. Replacement, reconstitution, and veteran continuity

## 30B. Force-generation feasibility envelope

The player needs an honest answer to what can be assembled for a specific mission without treating every service source, pool, equipment store, officer, or transport asset as simultaneously available. The same nominal force can be feasible for core defense and infeasible for a prolonged expedition. The important output is the causal blocker and the smaller executable alternative.
