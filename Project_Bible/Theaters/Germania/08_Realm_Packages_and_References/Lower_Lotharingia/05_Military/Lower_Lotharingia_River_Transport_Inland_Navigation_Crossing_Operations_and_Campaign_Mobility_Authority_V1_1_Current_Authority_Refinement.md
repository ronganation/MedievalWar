This copy-first V1.1 refinement proposes river operations as a usable campaign capability. It creates no opening boat, fleet, ferry, bridge, landing, navigable segment, crew, cargo, troop allocation, crossing entitlement, or transport agreement. All new procedures, portfolios, cases, and consequences below are proposed game design, not verified historical practices or implemented systems.

## 1. Identity: make the water a working route

**Gather the craft. Secure the banks. Move the weight. Keep a way home.**

The proposed Lower Lotharingian river game rewards preparation with operational reach. A supported water movement can bring actual supplies to a forward position, move a bounded force, connect a partner, or recover people and equipment after a reversal. The river is not merely a border line or an income icon. It becomes a contested means of action.

The player should gain meaningful alternatives to marching every resource along the same road. Water transport can complement a land host, create a different concentration opportunity, or preserve a reduced position when another route fails. Its usefulness depends on the actual segment, craft, crew, cargo, access, and threat. No universal river-speed or capacity advantage is assumed.

This is not a compulsory naval-faction transformation. A modest hired transport arrangement can be valuable. A more developed campaign network can support repeated operations when actual investment and counterpart relationships make that feasible. Direct ownership, hired service, partner contribution, and captured assets remain distinct sources with different burdens.

## 6. Craft acquisition, readiness, and competing use

Possible sources are existing owned assets, accepted hire, partner contribution, supported construction or repair, and captured craft. Each requires its own actual source and disposition. A market, settlement, river icon, or technology unlock creates none automatically.

Readiness depends on condition, suitability, equipment, crew, access, and maintenance. Capturing a hull does not provide its former crew's service, knowledge, or consent. Construction requires the relevant development and economic owners to provide actual inputs and work.

Civilian movement, procurement, local crossing service, and military transport may compete for the same craft. Hiring an asset removes its availability for incompatible tasks during the actual commitment. A return journey is not free capacity for another mission until the asset is genuinely available.

## 7. Crews, operators, and local knowledge

A crew is not interchangeable with passengers or soldiers. The mission needs actual operating skill and sufficient people through the relevant owners. Local knowledge can support navigation where evidenced, but it is not perfect intelligence or universal passage authority.

The player can retain a dependable service relationship, train successors through supported systems, hire another available source, reduce the mission, or accept a different route. No named boatman, pilot, guild, or hereditary office is invented.

Loss, capture, departure, fatigue, or conflicting service can reduce capacity. Surviving trained people and actual records can preserve some continuity; neither one specialist's departure nor one new building should reset or create all skill.

## 8. Loading, mixed cargo, and receiving capacity

The relevant transport owner determines whether a craft is suitable for the requested cargo. People, animals, stores, bulky equipment, and fragile records need not be equivalent loads. Exact capacities and handling rules remain uncalibrated.

Loading and unloading require usable access, labor, handling equipment where applicable, custody, security, and a receiver. A defended destination can still lack a usable unloading point. A landing can accept passengers while being unsuitable for another cargo class.

Mixed loads create priorities. More supplies may mean fewer passengers; saving records may displace equipment. The player should see the decisive tradeoff without managing individual sacks. No cargo is duplicated between a departing stockpile, craft manifest, and receiving store.

## 9. Navigation and world conditions

Segment suitability must come from actual geography and world state. Direction, current, water conditions, obstructions, craft suitability, and supported operating method are potential design inputs, not certified conditions on any named river.

Winter does not prove frozen water. A high-water report does not guarantee safe navigation. A historical date does not open a route. No modern map is treated as evidence of exact 1062 usability.

The interface should show known blockers and uncertainty. A player can choose a feasible smaller mission or take an explicit risk. No hidden omniscient pathfinding benefit is granted to AI or player.

## 10. Crossings as operations

A bridge, ferry, ford, or other supported crossing method must come from the appropriate geography, infrastructure, and transport owners. This file does not place or engineer one.

The operation includes approaches, assembly, crossing order, security on both sides, cargo suitability, communication, and continuation or withdrawal. A host can become divided during movement. Moving the first contingent does not move the remainder or its supplies.

The player can secure a bank, seek a less exposed point, stage movement, coordinate a diversion through military systems, negotiate access, or change the objective. Hostile action does not require target consent. Peaceful passage and the physical ability to cross remain separate.

## 11. Protection, interdiction, and tactical boundary

River missions can face danger at assembly, transit, crossings, and unloading. Protection requires actual forces, command, observation, and a feasible response. An escort on one bank is not automatically protection for every segment or destination.

Opponents can threaten a landing, disrupt an approach, attack through existing battle systems, seize accessible assets, or force a detour where actual state permits. No automatic blockade radius, naval battle mode, boarding unit, or new combat statistic is introduced.

If tactical representation cannot support a proposed interaction, preserve it as a design dependency rather than claim it works. Later implementation must choose an adopted battle or campaign resolution path before activation. The present file supplies requirements, not a fabricated engine capability.

## 12. Agreements, tolls, and external owners

Hire, cargo passage, troop transit, landing use, storage, protection, and toll collection are distinct terms. Paying one fee does not buy all services. A bank-side fortification does not establish ownership of the whole river.

A partner can offer limited capacity, require return, exclude military cargo, or decline an extension. A ducal title or family relationship cannot pledge another owner's vessels. Conversely, war can change actual control without resolving every property claim.

Use existing diplomacy and urban-partnership records. The same agreement must not charge twice because geography, river operations, and urban cooperation all consume it.

## 14. Return, evacuation, and recovery

Return planning includes actual craft, crew, route, access or military situation, destination, and competing duties. A vessel arriving downstream does not become available at its origin immediately. No universal return-time formula is assigned.

Evacuation priorities are a player choice within actual capacity. Protecting people, records, command continuity, supplies, or equipment can preserve different future capabilities. No option guarantees that everyone and everything escapes.

Closeout reconciles assets, people, cargo, payment, damage, prisoners or missing persons where relevant, continuing claims, and release. Repair restores only the completed function. Peace does not refill cargo or crews; reloading does not repeat relief rewards.

## 15. Development and faction asymmetry

The player can develop a dependable core-to-partner connection, diversify several limited transport relationships, strengthen one crossing, or maintain a modest reserve for emergency movement. These are independent investment directions, not cultural forks.

Useful investment can support repair, storage, handling, communication, crew continuity, landing access, and protected approaches where supported. A completed landing without craft or a workshop without skilled labor remains limited.

Lower Lotharingia's proposed advantage is coordinating a network that concentrates finite resources at a useful point. Its exposure is dependence on separated assets and counterpart terms. Success should allow bolder campaigns, not merely reduce penalties. Rival interdiction and alternate routes keep that strength contestable.

## 16. Developed decision cases

All cases are proposed reusable design. Link to existing operational cases when the underlying incident is already represented.

### 16.1 The grain reaches the bank

Actual stores are ready, but available craft cannot carry the full order. Deliver a smaller priority load, obtain another supported source, or combine water and land movement. Preserve the cargo left behind and the competing use of each transport asset. No accepted order becomes full supply automatically.

### 16.2 The host crosses in parts

A feasible crossing exposes the first contingent before the rest arrives. Secure the far bank with actual capacity, stage a narrower operation, or choose another approach. The opponent can contest the landing through supported systems. No shared command or instant reinforcement is assumed.

### 16.3 The landing cannot receive the cargo

The route remains usable but the destination's handling function fails. Repair a supported bottleneck, unload at another valid site and arrange onward movement, or return. Arrival alone grants no delivered-supply benefit.

### 16.4 The hired craft are due back

The accepted service window ends before the operation does. Negotiate extension, release and replace through another actual source, reduce the mission, or contest the terms through the proper owner. No indefinite free service or automatic hostile defection follows.

### 16.5 The bank is threatened

A known threat endangers loading or unloading. Divert an actual protection force, seek shared security, use another supported point, or accept explicit risk. Protection displaces another duty. A safe embarkation does not guarantee a safe entire route.

### 16.6 The boat is captured, the crew is not

An actual conflict leaves craft in custody without confirmed operators. Secure suitable crew and equipment, hold for later use, transfer under supported terms, or abandon the intended mission. Capture creates no skilled workforce or free military transport.

### 16.7 The relief arrives too late

A destination changes control or condition before delivery. Verify what is known, redirect to a supported receiver, negotiate, or withdraw. Preserve actual cargo and costs. A stale objective cannot award a successful relief merely because the convoy arrived nearby.

### 16.8 The return route closes

The outward movement succeeded, but return becomes uncertain or blocked. Seek another actual route, negotiate, protect a feasible movement, or preserve a reduced position. No teleportation, opening-state reset, or guaranteed rescue is supplied.

## 17. AI and player presentation

AI should compare the mission's useful output with craft suitability, loading, crews, known route conditions, destination, threat, return, and displaced uses. It should choose partial delivery or mixed-mode movement when that solves the problem, and ambitious concentration when actual support permits it.

Do not reserve one craft or crew for incompatible missions, assume every river is navigable, treat hired service as ownership, or count cargo at both ends. Do not reveal hidden enemy deployments or private partner inventories. A failed ideal plan should lead to a smaller feasible action, repair, another source, or a changed objective rather than permanent inactivity.

The player sees purpose, known route, actual committed transport, cargo priority, strongest blocker, destination readiness, and return exposure. Routine movement aggregates. Material interruption, losses, expiring service, and irreversible evacuation priorities deserve explicit decisions.

Suggested original summary:

> A river becomes an advantage when your craft, crews, banks, and destination work together. Move what the next operation needs, protect the transfer, and leave yourself a way back.

## 19E. Command, convoy, and communication coordination

A mission needs a responsible coordinator, but river transport command does not automatically transfer political authority or battlefield command over every passenger and escort.

| Responsibility | Required question |
|---|---|
| Mission coordinator | Who sequences assembly, departure, diversion, and closeout? |
| Craft operator | Who controls the movement and safety of each craft? |
| Cargo custodian | Who holds and releases the transported object? |
| Military commander | Who controls actual troops and protection forces? |
| Landing receiver | Who accepts people, animals, equipment, or stores? |
| Report channel | How are delay, loss, diversion, and arrival communicated? |

Several craft can move together without becoming a permanent naval formation. Separation, delay, or lost communication should preserve each craft's last known state rather than applying one universal convoy result.

## 19F. Animal, mounted-force, and equipment transport

Transporting animals or mounted forces requires suitable craft, handlers, loading, unloading, space, support, and a usable destination. A cavalry formation cannot be treated as ordinary passengers while its mounts, equipment, and handlers appear automatically at the destination.

The player may move riders and mounts together where feasible, separate movement components, use land movement for part of the force, or reduce the mission. Separation carries actual readiness and reunion consequences through military owners.

Bulky equipment and specialized materials can displace people or supplies. Exact loads and vessel capacities remain uncalibrated. This section creates no transport unit or naval roster entry.

## 19G. Ferry, bridge, ford, and crossing continuity

Crossing methods are distinct capabilities. A bridge can support repeated movement without craft but requires condition, approaches, capacity, and security. A ferry requires craft, operators, landing access, and repeated cycles. A ford or other crossing depends on actual world and movement conditions.

One method's failure does not prove every crossing method is unavailable. Conversely, a nearby alternative may be slower, unsuitable for the load, controlled by another actor, or exposed to another threat.

Control of one bank does not equal control of the crossing. Capturing a bridge does not repair it. A ferry agreement does not authorize bridge tolls or unrestricted military passage.

## 19I. Denial, damage, obstruction, and recovery

Opponents and world events can disrupt approaches, crossings, craft, landings, handling capacity, or route knowledge through supported systems. No generic blockade field is assumed.

Damage persists at the affected object. Destroying a bridge can impede several actors and the destroyer's future return. Obstructing a landing can leave the water segment usable but the mission incomplete. Seizing craft does not erase cargo already unloaded elsewhere.

Recovery must address the actual failure: repair infrastructure, clear a supported obstruction, replace lost equipment, obtain another craft or crew, secure the site, reroute, or reduce the mission. Money alone cannot skip work, access, people, and materials.

## 19K. Crew fatigue, replacement, and skill continuity

Crews need rest, support, and recovery through the relevant population and service owners. Repeated urgent missions should compete with civilian work, local ferry service, repair, and future availability.

A replacement crew must have suitable skill for the craft and mission. Passengers, soldiers, or laborers do not become qualified operators solely because a crew is absent. Experienced people may teach successors through supported systems, but training consumes time and capacity.

Fatigue and recovery need not become a heavily micromanaged meter. They should matter when repeated service, losses, or emergency tempo materially affect readiness.

## 19L. Emergency crossing alternatives

When the preferred crossing fails, the player may seek a temporary ferry arrangement, repair a supported bridge, use another verified crossing, stage smaller transfers, combine land and water movement, protect an emergency site, or abandon the objective.

An emergency solution should trade speed, scale, exposure, cost, or durability. It cannot produce a free permanent bridge, instant engineering corps, unlimited craft, or safe retreat. Temporary capability ends or requires review when its actual inputs and purpose end.

The strongest emergency choice is the one that solves the immediate operational problem without pretending the longer-term network is restored.

## 19N. AI portfolio correction and anti-exploit rules

- Assemble missions from actual craft, crews, cargo, receiver, access, and return capacity.
- Prefer partial or staged delivery when it meaningfully solves the objective.
- Do not count one craft, crew, escort, cargo, or landing capacity in incompatible missions.
- Use mixed-mode movement only when every handoff has a supported source.
- Distinguish bridges, ferries, fords, temporary landings, and transport craft rather than treating all river access as one capability.
- Reassess after destination change, landing failure, service expiry, capture, route damage, or lost communication.
- Preserve civilian and local-service displacement when military demand reserves capacity.
- Do not infer hidden navigation conditions, enemy positions, partner inventories, or crew skill.
- Close out cancellation, diversion, delivery, return, custody, and repair before restoring availability.
- Prevent repeated cargo, payment, relief, crossing, salvage, reconnaissance, and objective rewards from one receipt.

The player follows the same constraints. Cycling a route, mission purpose, landing, or service agreement cannot duplicate assets or bypass recovery.
