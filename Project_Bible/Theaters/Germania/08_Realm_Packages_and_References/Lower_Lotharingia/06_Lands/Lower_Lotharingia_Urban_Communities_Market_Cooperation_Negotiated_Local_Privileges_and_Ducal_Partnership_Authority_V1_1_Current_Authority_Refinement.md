## 1. Identity: make the towns useful partners

**Protect the exchange. Earn the contribution. Keep the road open.**

Lower Lotharingia should gain more from towns than a tax number or a public-order problem. A useful urban partnership can connect procurement, transport, skilled repair, reporting, storage, and defense. It can give the duke a dependable staging relationship without requiring annexation. It can also become a powerful directly governed center when actual campaign development supports that outcome.

The player chooses what the relationship is for. A campaign supplier needs security and deliverable terms; a recovering town needs access and productive capacity; a strategic crossing needs protection and workable passage. A single generic favor payment should not solve every need.

The intended reward is repeatable capability. A town that repeatedly delivers a bounded service can free the household for other work. A duke who protects an exchange route can gain a stronger procurement opportunity. Neither side becomes the other's property merely because cooperation succeeds.

## 4. A town is not one actor with one interest

Distinguish settlement, community, property holders, institutional precincts, officeholders, traders, workshops, and households where those distinctions affect play. Do not instantiate every category at every site.

A representative can negotiate for a defined group without speaking for every inhabitant. A bishop can possess an office without representing the entire urban community. A lord's control of a gate does not automatically establish every market or jurisdictional right. A trader's offer does not pledge the town's treasury.

Representation should be as simple as the actual case permits. Use an existing actor and mandate when sufficient; create a new local entity only when persistent independent agency genuinely requires it. No universal elected council, mature guild federation, commune, or later civic constitution is assumed at the opening.

## 5. Partnership portfolio

| Partnership | Concrete payoff | Ducal commitment | Counterpart leverage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Protected exchange | More dependable access to actual sellers and goods | Route security or bounded remedy | Sellers can withhold, relocate, or seek another route |
| Campaign provisioning | A supported source of supplies or transport | Payment, security, custody, and accepted delivery terms | Capacity, local needs, and competing buyers |
| Repair relationship | Access to evidenced skilled service and tools | Inputs, compensation, and safe working conditions | Skills remain finite and cannot work everywhere |
| Storage arrangement | A usable place to hold actual stocks | Custody, space, handling, and maintenance | Local property and priority-of-use terms |
| Crossing partnership | More reliable passage at a defined scope | Protection, repair, payment, or reciprocal service | Access can exclude traffic or expire |
| Recovery partnership | Reopening a useful damaged function | Actual relief, reduced burdens, or shared work | Participation and material feasibility |
| Local defense arrangement | Protection of a specific place or function | Command terms, support, relief, and release | Local capacity and limits on external deployment |

These are proposed functions, not seven mandatory buildings or universal agreement cards. A small settlement can support one useful relationship; a larger center may support several distinct functions if actual capacity exists.

## 6. Procurement without free wealth

Access to a market creates an opportunity to exchange, not stock, income, profit, or military supply. Distinguish seller availability, offered goods, accepted price, payment, custody, delivery, and consumption. Buying locally can still require carriers and protection.

A favorable relationship can improve reliability through known contacts, accepted routine, or preserved access. It cannot guarantee unlimited cheap goods. Competing demand, lost inputs, damage, insecurity, and local consumption can reduce availability.

The player can buy early, diversify sources, provide protection, fund supported repair, accept a smaller order, or seek another source. Exact prices, quantities, seasonal effects, and rates remain uncalibrated.

## 7. Negotiated privileges as scoped choices

A proposed privilege must identify its object, beneficiaries, issuer, geographic reach, duration or review basis, excluded matters, and continuing duties. A concession can concern a specific burden, access, procedure, or function where the issuing actor has authority.

```yaml
    - RecordCommunicationAndRecognition

  ducal_title_grants_universal_issuing_power: false
  charter_creates_people_goods_or_force: false

  recorded_promise_equals_performed_benefit: false
```

The strategic tradeoff is concrete: accepting a narrower recurring demand may support a more dependable contribution; preserving local procedure may make cooperation feasible while limiting direct discretion. The counterpart can refuse. A concession without useful return can still be a deliberate political choice, but no hidden bonus should manufacture the missing benefit.

## 8. Contributions and competing burdens

Money, stores, labor, transport, accommodation, and military service are different contributions. One accepted duty does not imply all the others. Repeated demands can consume the productive capacity that makes the relationship valuable.

Show the principal collision. Carriers assigned to a host cannot also deliver the same market cargo. Skilled repair personnel cannot work simultaneously at every site. A store reserved for local survival is not automatically campaign stock. A payment promised by one group cannot be charged silently to another.

The player can phase duties, negotiate substitution, pay for additional actual capacity, reduce the operation, or impose a demand through the appropriate systems and accept its consequences. Substitution requires actual feasibility and acceptance when agreement is needed.

## 9. Protection and coercion

Protection is a performed function: escort, defense, relief, safe access, or a bounded remedy. A promise alone does not keep a route safe. A successful escort does not grant ownership or permanent taxation rights.

Coercion remains a playable option through military, law, and public-order systems. It can obtain actual resources or compliance while exposing resistance, lost production, rival intervention, or continuing dispute. It must not be relabeled voluntary cooperation.

Neither generous terms nor severe demands guarantee the desired response. Local actors retain independent agency and actual capacity. The design should reward effective strategy without making one moralized option universally optimal.

## 10. Local defense and military interface

A town's walls do not create defenders. A defense agreement does not create a militia card. Any local military contribution requires existing eligible people, equipment, command, finite recruitment support, supply, and accepted or enforceable scope through the military owners.

Local protection, garrison duty, escort, and external campaigning are distinct missions. Service at home does not authorize unlimited deployment abroad. Taking local defenders into the field exposes their original function unless a supported replacement exists.

Regional units and mercenaries retain canonical Theater ownership. No faction-specific urban roster, recruitment quantity, unit profile, or opening garrison is created here.

## 11. Dispute, mediation, and divided jurisdiction

A partnership can function while one issue remains contested. A community may accept protection and dispute a levy. An institution may preserve its own precinct while supporting a route project. A market agreement may continue while a property claim is unresolved.

Route disputes to the existing law and diplomacy owners. Identify the disputed object, known evidence, relevant parties, immediate practical need, and available remedy. A mediator can facilitate terms but cannot guarantee acceptance.

Preserve actual partial performance. A paid contribution and a failed escort are separate results. Remedy does not erase the original loss. Do not duplicate the same dispute as an urban event, ecclesiastical crisis, and occupation penalty.

## 12. Rival offers and nonexclusive cooperation

A rival can offer a safer route, better purchase terms, supported protection, mediation, or an alternative buyer. Such offers matter only when communicated and deliverable. No hidden rival terms become player knowledge automatically.

A town can cooperate with several actors on compatible matters. Trade with a rival does not itself prove hostile alignment. Exclusive terms, resource conflicts, and wartime restrictions remain relevant where actually established.

The duke can improve performance, narrow demands, secure a bottleneck, seek another partner, or act militarily through existing systems. A rival offer should create a strategic problem, not an automatic settlement flip.

## 13. War, occupation, and continuity

The territorial-integration specialist retains occupation and local-government settlement. This companion asks which urban relationships survive, fail, or require review. Military control alone does not transfer every workshop, archive, market right, or representative mandate.

Preserve damage, displaced people, consumed stocks, unpaid terms, and surviving local routine. Reopening exchange can precede complete political settlement when actual access and security permit it. Peace does not restore goods, labor, or trust by declaration.

Recovery priorities can be a safe approach, a working store, skilled repair, or a reliable messenger channel. The strongest first investment is the one that restores a useful function, not necessarily the largest building.

## 15. Developed decision portfolio

All cases are proposed game design. Attach them to an existing relevant case where one exists.

### 15.1 The market needs an escort

A known supply opportunity is exposed on a usable but threatened route. Send an actual escort and expose another duty; negotiate shared protection with a valid counterpart; or buy elsewhere and accept the changed cost and availability. Success means actual movement, not permanent ownership of the route. Rivals can raid, bypass, counteroffer, or remain inactive according to actual state.

### 15.2 A contribution for a concession

A valid representative offers a defined contribution for a scoped concession. Accept if deliverable; counteroffer a narrower benefit and contribution; or refuse and pursue another supported source. A recorded bargain reserves nothing until the appropriate owners act. Preserve partial delivery and conflicting rights.

### 15.3 The workshop or the host

Actual skilled service is needed for local recovery and military repair. Prioritize the host, prioritize productive recovery, or sequence a reduced service. No option creates another specialist. The consequence is which function operates now and which remains delayed.

### 15.4 The gate and the grain

A counterpart offers cargo access but not troop passage. Accept the limited delivery opportunity, negotiate military scope, or change the operation. Paying for one traffic class does not unlock every class. Failure can leave paid money, undelivered cargo, or an unperformed obligation separately.

### 15.5 A rival buyer arrives

Known competing demand threatens a procurement plan. Offer better actual terms, reduce the order and diversify, or preserve funds and change the campaign. The counterpart is not automatically disloyal. No option reveals private inventories or guarantees acceptance.

### 15.6 The protection promise was not performed

A confirmed failure leaves loss and a dispute. Deliver a supported remedy, negotiate a revised arrangement, or contest responsibility through the relevant owner while addressing immediate exposure separately. Compensation is not a refund of every loss or an automatic relationship reset.

### 15.7 Exchange after the siege

An actual conflict has damaged access and production. Restore the principal bottleneck, seek shared recovery, or reduce demands and accept slower restoration. The existing territorial and recovery cases retain damage and costs. No duplicate relief reward is added.

### 15.8 The successor asks for renewal

A principal transition raises a real question about continuing terms. Confirm valid obligations, negotiate changes, or end what can validly end. Existing goods, arrears, and performed work remain. No historical successor or civic officeholder is spawned for the case.

## 16. AI and presentation

AI should select partners for an actual function, compare deliverable offers, preserve local and campaign capacity, and diversify when one bottleneck threatens several operations. It should be capable of firm bargaining and aggressive action without treating every town as an enemy or an unlimited resource pool.

Do not buy unavailable stock, pledge another owner's rights, count the same carriers twice, or build a military plan on an unaccepted offer. When ideal terms fail, seek a smaller arrangement, another partner, a changed objective, or a supported coercive alternative.

The player panel should show the function, valid counterpart, accepted terms, actual availability, strongest blocker, displaced duty, and outstanding dispute. Routine exchange should aggregate. Escalate meaningful choices rather than require approval of every shipment.

Suggested original player-facing summary:

> A dependable town is more than a purse. Keep its useful connections working, bargain for what it can actually provide, and turn local cooperation into campaign reach.

## 17. Time, persistence, and anti-exploit boundaries

Historical +21 months, biological +7 months, and one ordered Winter/Spring/Summer/Autumn step remain independent per strategic turn. None is a literal delivery, privilege, training, or recovery duration. A date alone does not create a council, charter, market, revolt, or civic transformation.

Future persistence must retain accepted terms, mandate, custody, reserved capacity, performed contributions, damage, arrears, knowledge, and review conditions. Retrying a receipt or renewing an agreement cannot duplicate goods or rewards. Retiring a prompt does not cancel the underlying obligation.

A single settlement may host several actors and functions. Several nearby settlements may share one exchange relationship if the proper owners support it. Interface aggregation must not collapse their property, obligations, or representation into one fictional treasury.

## 18C. Representation, mandate, and continuity

A valid representative needs a known constituency, subject scope, temporal or review boundary, communication route, and acceptance method. A person may negotiate provision without authority over property, military service, or jurisdiction.

| Representation issue | Required distinction | Invalid shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Constituency | Household, property holders, institution, traders, community, or office | Visible speaker represents everyone |
| Subject | Purchase, passage, protection, repair, storage, or privilege | One mandate covers every urban matter |
| Acceptance | Immediate authority, conditional acceptance, or confirmation required | Draft or attendance activates terms |
| Continuity | Office-bound, institutional, personal, temporary, or revocable basis | Successor automatically inherits every private term |
| Dissent | Outside groups, conflicting owners, and unresolved claims | Majority support erases another owner's rights |

The game should not require a separate representative for every routine delivery. Reuse valid mandates until the subject, actor, office, or terms materially change.

## 18E. Toll, passage, market, and custody distinctions

A toll is a specific demand or term associated with a supported movement or access context. It is not automatic ownership of a road, bridge, gate, market, or cargo.

Cargo passage, personal travel, messenger access, military transit, market entry, storage, and sale are distinct permissions. An actor can accept one and refuse another. Paying toll does not guarantee safety, transport, delivery, legal recognition, or priority over competing traffic.

Collection requires actual authority or coercive capacity, accessible traffic, administration, and receipt. Nominal toll rights are not collected income. Evasion, alternate routes, rival claims, damaged infrastructure, and interrupted traffic provide counterplay through existing owners.

## 18G. Credit, advances, arrears, and guarantees

Delayed payment, advances, staged performance, surety, and arrears can support meaningful exchange where adopted by the relevant owners. This is not an invented universal banking system.

| Arrangement | Opportunity | Risk retained |
|---|---|---|
| Advance payment | Secures preparation or scarce capacity | Counterparty failure, loss, and tied-up funds |
| Payment after delivery | Preserves immediate cash | Seller exposure and more demanding guarantees |
| Staged payment | Aligns payment with partial performance | Dispute over stage acceptance |
| Guarantee or surety | Provides another supported remedy source | Guarantor authority and finite capacity |
| Arrears settlement | Preserves a relationship after nonpayment | Debt, reduced willingness, and competing claims persist |

Credit creates a claim and exposure, not money or stock. One guarantee cannot secure incompatible obligations beyond actual capacity. Interest, penalties, exact enforcement, and historical financial institutions require owner and evidence closure.

## 18H. Storage, custody, spoilage, and release

A storage arrangement must distinguish the owner of goods, the property containing them, the custodian, authorized access, priority of release, handling obligations, loss, and inspection.

Stored goods can spoil, be damaged, consumed under valid terms, seized through supported action, or become inaccessible. A warehouse does not preserve everything perfectly. Occupation of the site does not automatically transfer every stored object.

Release can be full, partial, disputed, blocked, or redirected only under supported authority. Retrying release cannot duplicate stock. Storage fees and exact deterioration rates remain uncalibrated.

## 18I. Skilled service, apprenticeship, and continuity

Urban repair and productive partnerships depend on skilled people, tools, inputs, workspace, time, and a viable livelihood. A workshop building alone provides none of these automatically.

A specialist can produce, repair, inspect, or teach, but not perform all functions everywhere. Apprenticeship and repeated practice may improve continuity through shared character, population, and development owners. Training consumes time and may reduce immediate output.

Departure or loss of one specialist can reduce a function without erasing knowledge held by others. Conversely, written records alone do not create practical competence. No named craft specialization or urban guild is admitted by this refinement.

## 18J. Emergency provisioning and scarcity competition

War, siege, displacement, route loss, crop failure, or competing armies can make urban stocks strategically decisive where actual state supports it. Emergency procurement must still use real goods and custodians.

The player may reserve goods for defense, prioritize local survival, purchase at accepted terms, requisition through supported authority, ration military demand, import through another route, or reduce the operation. Each choice displaces another use.

Emergency authority does not create stock or erase compensation and dispute. A relief shipment cannot be counted simultaneously as local reserve and campaign supply. Scarcity should increase the value of protection and diversification without guaranteeing universal price behavior.

## 18L. Urban growth, decline, and functional recovery

Urban development should emerge from supported population, access, security, exchange, production, institutions, property, and investment. One successful bargain does not instantly promote a settlement to a new universal tier.

Growth can increase useful capacity while raising demands for storage, access, protection, adjudication, and administration. Decline can reduce one function while others survive. A damaged market relationship may recover before population, fortification, or production fully recovers.

No predetermined civic-development ladder is introduced. A duke can cultivate a connected market center, a protected repair relationship, a military staging partnership, or a diversified network of smaller places. These are campaign outcomes, not cultures created by research.

## 18N. AI partnership portfolio and anti-exploit rules

- Build partnerships around procurement, passage, repair, storage, recovery, or defense needs that actually exist.
- Diversify when several critical functions depend on one route, custodian, specialist, or representative.
- Do not buy goods that are unavailable or count accepted offers as delivered supply.
- Do not use one representative's mandate for unrelated property, privilege, or military commitments.
- Do not reserve the same carriers, stores, skilled service, or payment for incompatible duties.
- Distinguish firm bargaining, lawful demand, coercion, protection, and voluntary exchange.
- Preserve partial performance, arrears, custody, and disputes during renewal or transition.
- Reopen the principal functional bottleneck after war rather than applying a generic recovery bonus.
- Do not access private inventories, rival terms, hidden capacity, or motives without a valid source.
- Prevent repeated purchase, escort, relief, privilege, and relationship rewards from one receipt.

The player follows the same rules. Cycling an agreement, representative, storage site, or decision prompt cannot duplicate goods, refunds, capacity, or standing.
