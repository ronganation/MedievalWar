## 1. Campaign promise: make the middle country matter

**Hold the ducal dignity. Build a power that can survive its next victory.**

The proposed campaign identity joins ducal dignity, fragmented capacity, contested continuity, and strategically valuable connections. This is a design direction pending full reconciliation with the general Realm and personality authorities.

The proposed objective system turns that identity into concrete achievements: a defensible material base, a functioning corridor, a reliable field force, a useful institutional bargain, a settled ducal contest, and a government that survives a change of holder. The player can expand aggressively, broker a powerful network, pursue royal influence, or rebuild after disaster. Caution is an option, not the faction's defining accomplishment.

The distinctive achievement is not collecting every place associated with the historical duchy. It is making a fragmented position strategically decisive. Conquest matters when the gain can be held; diplomacy matters when terms are performed; succession matters when the government continues; recovery matters when it restores the ability to choose.

## 1A. Strategic identity and objective design spine

Lower Lotharingia should play as a **ducal frontier power assembled through functions rather than inherited as a finished block**. Its campaign strength comes from turning scattered assets, obligations, routes, armed followings, and negotiated relationships into a position capable of concentrated action. Its weakness is that each source of strength can remain legally, geographically, or institutionally separate.

The objective portfolio therefore follows five connected design rules:

1. **Build a usable core:** the player needs somewhere from which government, logistics, household continuity, and field operations can actually function.
2. **Connect what is separated:** roads, crossings, escorts, agreements, and fortified positions matter because the Realm is strategically fragmented.
3. **Convert dignity into performance:** ducal standing becomes valuable when it produces protection, settlement, mobilization, or accepted leadership.
4. **Concentrate force selectively:** the faction should be able to create dangerous local superiority, but not sustain every front, obligation, and expedition simultaneously.
5. **Survive political change:** succession, incapacity, defeat, concession, or a new ducal settlement can alter the form of the faction without automatically ending its political project.

These rules create a stronger Total War-style player fantasy than passive survival. Lower Lotharingia can become a corridor state, a militarized frontier duchy, a royal power broker, an expansionist territorial power, or a resilient successor polity. Each direction requires tangible performance and exposes a different weakness.

### 1B. Core campaign tension: reach, concentration, and continuity

The campaign repeatedly asks the player to balance three capabilities:

- **Reach:** where the faction can move, supply, influence, negotiate, protect, or intervene.
- **Concentration:** whether enough troops, money, escorts, officers, stores, and political attention can be assembled at one point to achieve a result.
- **Continuity:** whether the government, household, obligations, and strategic purpose remain functional after that concentration has been spent or disrupted.

Reach without concentration produces promises the faction cannot enforce. Concentration without continuity wins battles that hollow out the Realm. Continuity without reach protects a court while surrendering strategic relevance. Strong progression expands all three, but no objective family should treat them as a single abstract score.

## 4. Opening pressure interpretation

These are potential campaign pressures, not initialized meters or January event assignments.

| Pressure | Player-facing question | Meaningful response | What is not assumed |

## 6. Portfolio and competition

Present a small set of consequential goals, not a compulsory administrative checklist. A primary direction can coexist with supporting goals and urgent recovery. No exact hard portfolio cap is calibrated here.

Before commitment, expose competition for the same treasury, escort, principal, route, labor, equipment, garrison, or recruitment pool. Compatible ambitions can still collide in timing. A new priority does not cancel an accepted obligation or release its resources.

Resolve conflicts through sequencing, reduced scale, supported delegation, substitution, renegotiation, deliberate withdrawal, or explicit accepted exposure. The player may choose a risky offensive. The interface should explain the exposed home function rather than prohibit initiative merely because risk exists.

## 6A. Strong campaign portfolio patterns

The objective families are modular rather than mutually exclusive, but the following portfolio patterns make the faction's alternative playstyles legible.

| Portfolio pattern | Primary objective families | Battlefield and campaign behavior | Principal vulnerability |
|---|---|---|---|
| Corridor Duchy | LLCO-01, LLCO-03, LLCO-08, LLCO-10 | Fortify a viable base, secure movement, fight for decisive nodes, and fund repeated passage or defense | A single broken connection can divide the system |
| Ducal Consolidator | LLCO-02, LLCO-07, LLCO-10, LLCO-12 | Turn title and administration into durable government, revenue, and succession capacity | Formal recognition can outrun material control |
| Royal Sword | LLCO-06, LLCO-09, LLCO-11, LLCO-12 | Build an expeditionary force, serve selectively, return intact, and convert service into leverage | External campaigns can drain the home core |
| Middle-Country Broker | LLCO-04, LLCO-05, LLCO-11, LLCO-13 | Use bargains, protection, mediation, and repair to assemble influence without mass annexation | Agreements remain counterpart-dependent and can collide |
| Frontier Ascendant | LLCO-01, LLCO-08, LLCO-09, LLCO-10 | Seize supportable gains, recover the host, and turn military success into a broader territorial base | Occupation, supply, and recovery costs compound rapidly |
| Realm Reforged | LLCO-12, LLCO-13, LLCO-14 with one renewed expansion family | Preserve agency through collapse, rebuild a smaller core, then reopen strategic choice | Retrenchment can become permanent irrelevance without renewed reach |

These patterns do not create classes, locked paths, hidden cultural forks, or mandatory sequences. They are player-readable strategic identities and AI planning references. A campaign can shift from one pattern to another when actual state changes.

### 6B. Objective-chain discipline

A major ambition should normally combine **foundation**, **decisive action**, and **consolidation** rather than ending at the first visible success.

| Ambition | Foundation | Decisive action | Consolidation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Secure a strategic corridor | LLCO-01 or LLCO-10 | LLCO-03 and, if contested, LLCO-08 | LLCO-09 or a maintained-access review |
| Establish practical ducal power | LLCO-02 | LLCO-07 | LLCO-10 and LLCO-12 |
| Profit from royal service | LLCO-09 | LLCO-06 | LLCO-11 and return/recovery review |
| Expand the territorial base | LLCO-01 and LLCO-09 | LLCO-08 | LLCO-10, LLCO-13, and owner-backed integration work |
| Recover from strategic defeat | LLCO-14 | LLCO-13 or LLCO-12 | Reopen LLCO-01, LLCO-03, or LLCO-09 as supported |

The chain is interpretive, not a hard prerequisite graph. It prevents a single battle, treaty, title grant, or construction from masquerading as complete faction development.

## 8. Progression: from vulnerable dignity to decisive power

Progression is multidimensional, not a centralization score or a mandatory linear technology tree.

| Proposed milestone | Concrete change | What it does not grant |
|---|---|---|
| A viable foothold | A supported base and a functioning decision process | A free capital or invulnerable household |
| A working ducal function | A public responsibility reliably performed | Universal jurisdiction |
| A connected position | A useful corridor actually usable for its intended purpose | Ownership of every intervening settlement |
| A dependable network | Distinct accepted bargains repeatedly performed | Permanent obedience |
| A recoverable host | Readiness supported by finite recruitment, supply and return | Unlimited military expansion |
| A settled political future | A title/continuity settlement with practical government | Guaranteed dynastic permanence |
| A frontier power | Durable leverage through military, economic or diplomatic results | Automatic supremacy over Germania |

A player may achieve a strong army before settling the ducal contest, or secure diplomacy before expanding direct holdings. Milestones can overlap, regress or remain partial. Loss of a current capability does not erase its past achievement. A large empire can qualify through sustainable power; a smaller broker can qualify through dependable reach. Neither receives credit merely for choosing its label.

## 8A. Military expression inside the objective system

The objectives should reinforce a battlefield identity based on **selective concentration, protected movement, fortified leverage, and costly recovery**.

- LLCO-03 makes movement and supply strategically meaningful without turning every route into a settlement-ownership requirement.
- LLCO-08 gives the faction a reason to choose crossings, relief operations, fortified nodes, and enemy withdrawal as decisive military results rather than counting battles generically.
- LLCO-09 prevents victory from erasing losses. Horses, equipment, command capacity, finite recruitment pools, demobilization, and return remain separate recovery needs.
- LLCO-06 creates a true expeditionary dilemma: external service can bring leverage, but only if the field force returns in a condition that leaves the home position governable and defensible.
- LLCO-13 makes devastation and institutional harm strategically relevant after the army has moved on.

This design supports aggressive play. It does not require pacifism or bureaucratic permission before war. It makes the strongest offensive campaigns those that choose a decisive purpose, assemble a viable force, create the needed access, win the result, and retain enough capacity to hold or exploit it.

### 8B. Escalation and counterplay

Opposition should be able to attack the same capabilities the player is building. Counterplay can obstruct a route, contest a crossing, refuse terms, draw the field army away, damage recovery capacity, challenge recognition, or exploit an unsupported succession. None of these responses is automatic; each requires its actual owner and campaign cause.

A strong objective should expose at least one counterplay surface before commitment:

| Player ambition | Counterplay surface | Recovery choice |
|---|---|---|
| Corridor expansion | Obstruction, rival access, fortified resistance, supply interruption | Alternate route, negotiated passage, relief action, phased withdrawal |
| Ducal consolidation | Competing recognition, weak administration, local nonperformance | Narrow jurisdiction, demonstrate function, renegotiate support |
| External service | Home exposure, delayed return, attrition, conflicting obligations | Limit contingent, substitute contribution, settle service, rebuild host |
| Territorial conquest | Garrisons, integration cost, retaliation, damaged production | Consolidate, release, bargain, transfer, or abandon the gain |
| Institutional protection | Competing duties, grievance, incapacity, disputed remedy | Prioritize, compensate, mediate, or revise terms |
| Succession continuity | Missing authority, disputed property, divided household support | Interim government, owner-backed settlement, smaller surviving polity |

## 9. Standing and reception

Standing is a sourced interpretation of observable reception, not an inference about private feelings. Distinguish royal recognition, institutional acceptance, community compliance, military participation and household continuity. A successful escort can improve the terms of a later negotiation only when the counterpart actually responds; no automatic universal reputation bonus is created.

Potential audiences include the royal government, individual institutions, counts and local powers, communities, military contributors, household actors and external partners. Each retains its own knowledge and response. The interface can report a received commendation, renewed agreement, refusal or complaint. It must not invent gratitude, resentment, loyalty or hidden intent.

| Master of the Corridors | Durable access or control at strategically useful connections, performed movement and supportable defense | Connections can depend on independent partners | Owning one crossing treated as owning a river |
| Broker of the Middle Country | Several distinct useful arrangements that deliver real cooperation and preserve agency | Partners can disagree and withdraw | A pile of unperformed treaties |
| The Frontier Ascendant | Supported territorial or military expansion that can be held, supplied and politically managed | Conquest competes with recovery and local resistance | Battle count or occupation alone |
| The Crown's Indispensable Partner | Repeated performed service and actual reciprocal political opportunity without hollowing out home capacity | External demand can exceed what the core can spare | Service automatically converted into royal favor |
| A Contested Dignity Secured | An owner-confirmed ducal settlement with practical continuity, whether through retention or a valid alternative | Settlement may require concessions and a changed holder | The historical restoration year reached |
| The Realm Reforged | Recovery or transformation after major loss, producing a durable functioning political project | Earlier ambitions may remain abandoned | A surviving character automatically counted as faction victory |

These directions can combine. They are not mutually exclusive cultural forks. A broad campaign achievement should require a coherent durable result across the chosen direction, core agency, material sustainability and unresolved critical threats. The exact combination is a later scenario contract, not an invented numeric formula here.

Conquest remains a legitimate route to greatness. The design rejects compulsory annexation of every independent actor as the only route, not territorial ambition itself. Peaceful play must still face meaningful commitments and scarcity; military play must still confront holding costs and consequences.

## 11. Defeat, setbacks, and campaign continuation

A lost battle, expired bargain, dead ruler, changed dynasty or lost title is not automatically total defeat. Nor does this file guarantee continuation through total destruction. Campaign termination requires explicit scenario rules and current owner-backed evidence of the relevant terminal condition.

Distinguish a failed objective from a failed campaign. Abandoning an offensive can preserve the army and open another path. Losing the ducal dignity can preserve private property while ending a title-retention objective. A negotiated change of holder can satisfy a continuity goal without satisfying the previous holder's ambition.

Recovery changes the current position; it does not undo casualties, consumed stores, broken terms or public history. Reconstituted troops use actual sources. A replacement officer is not the same person's knowledge or relationships. A repaired settlement can retain grievances.

## 12. Completion review, maintenance and rewards

A completion review identifies the objective definition version, scoped target, relevant owner observations, actual result, remaining obligations and strongest unresolved blocker. Unknown is not zero and is not success. Contested evidence remains contested. Partial completion is reported at its actual scope.

One receipt can support several interpretations without being paid several times. The same protected journey might advance corridor reliability and institutional protection, but its expense and any reward occur once through their owners. No currency, unit, building, person, title, standing modifier or research unlock is awarded by this static file.

If a later objective includes a reward, its writer, source, capacity, timing and duplicate protection must be explicit. A research or decision unlock permits capability; it does not create people, equipment, horses, officers or recruitment pools. A promised political benefit requires actual counterpart action.

Maintenance is scoped. A completed delivery remains complete even if a route closes later. A maintained-corridor goal can reopen when access fails. Do not retroactively revoke a one-time achievement merely because a different ongoing condition deteriorates.

## 12A. Objective evidence and anti-shortcut matrix

| Result offered to the player | Minimum evidence pattern | Shortcut rejected |
|---|---|---|
| Base secured | Supported possession or use, operating functions, viable access, appropriate defense | Flag over a fortress with no stores, staff, route, or legal standing |
| Corridor opened | Usable scoped passage plus an actual movement, delivery, or sustained access result | Signed terms alone |
| Bargain completed | Counterpart acceptance plus performed exchange at the promised scope | One-sided declaration or unreceived payment |
| Military objective achieved | Specified battle/operational result plus separate confirmation of any claimed territorial or political consequence | Battle win automatically becoming peace, annexation, or integration |
| Host recovered | Verified command, equipment, supply, horses where relevant, and finite recruitment support | Unit roster presence or automatic refill |
| Ducal settlement secured | Owner-confirmed title/recognition result plus functioning government appropriate to the claim | Calendar year, claimant existence, or title text alone |
| Continuity preserved | Valid acting authority plus separately accounted household, property, title, obligations, and military state | New ruler inheriting every prior relationship and asset |
| Community recovery achieved | Restored function and delivered remedy with remaining harm visible | Public-order suppression or promised compensation |

This matrix applies across objectives and victory directions. It is intentionally stricter than a checklist of map ownership because the faction's identity depends on making fragmented power actually work.

## 13. Historical suite and crisis integration

Memory explains an interpreted past. Momentum represents current pressures. Emergence presents conditional opportunities. Crisis content offers consequential choices. Objectives express selected goals and review achievements. The existing combined suite remains the consumer for historical content; this file does not duplicate its event catalogue.

One underlying dispute should have one principal decision surface with linked goals, not several simultaneous prompts. An objective may point to a crisis case without creating that crisis. A chronological reference may contextualize a choice without triggering it. A historical person is not spawned to complete an objective.

Historical nonoccurrence is valid. If a restoration opportunity never develops, its conditional family remains dormant or nonoccurring rather than punishing the player for failing to reproduce history. If a valid different ruler or political arrangement emerges, current state controls subsequent evaluation.

## 14. AI selection and player readability

The strategic personality proposes priorities; this file supplies the achievement definitions. AI selection should compare strategic contribution, feasibility, existing commitments, opportunity cost, recovery and future options. It must consider an offensive, a narrower initiative and a supported diplomatic alternative where relevant, rather than always selecting the safest action.

Show the player the goal, why it matters, available methods, current progress, the strongest blocker, the cost of continuing and what would count as completion. Routine work stays aggregated. Serious damage, breached obligations and critical recovery needs remain visible. Do not require every historical uncertainty to be resolved before ordinary campaign action.

Illustrative proposed card text:

**The Road Between Our Holdings**

Your position is only as strong as the connection that supplies it. Secure usable passage, open another corridor, or take and hold the contested route. A signature alone will not move the stores.

**Progress:** agreement received; delivery not yet confirmed.

**Tradeoff:** the available escort is already committed elsewhere.

This is an illustrative interface state, not an assertion about the opening campaign or a simulated result.

## 15. Three clocks, deadlines and persistence

Historical progression is +21 months per strategic turn; biological progression is +7 months; campaign season advances one ordered Winter/Spring/Summer/Autumn step. Objectives advance none of these clocks and cannot substitute one for another.

Any later deadline must identify its clock, start anchor, inclusive/exclusive boundary and treatment of suspension. No exact deadlines are assigned here. A historical year is not an objective timer. Seasonal route conditions depend on actual geography and supply state, not on assuming historical and biological time are identical.

Future persistence should retain definition version, selected goals, scoped targets, owner observations, partial results, completion reviews, maintenance state, abandonment reasons, unresolved consequences and reward receipts. Loading an advanced save must not restore opening holders, revive dead people, refill pools or reactivate missed historical opportunities.

Definition updates require an explicit migration disposition: retain, remap, suspend for review, or retire with explanation. A new definition must not silently award success, erase failure or duplicate rewards. Deterministic acceptance should use stable inputs and ordering; no implemented evaluator, save schema or replay result is claimed.
