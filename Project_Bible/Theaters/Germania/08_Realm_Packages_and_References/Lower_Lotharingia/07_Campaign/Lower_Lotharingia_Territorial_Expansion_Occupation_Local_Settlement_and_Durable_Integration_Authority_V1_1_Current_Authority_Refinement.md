## 1. Campaign promise: win ground that strengthens the next campaign

**Take the position. Make it work. Keep the freedom to strike again.**

Lower Lotharingia should be capable of forceful expansion, not trapped in permanent defensive brokerage. The distinctive challenge is turning a selective gain into a stronger connected position. A fortress can protect a route; a negotiated local arrangement can release the field host; restored production can sustain future operations. Conquest should produce real rewards when the player supplies the means to use them.

The best gain is not always the largest province. A useful connection, dependable base, or accepted local arrangement can transform the campaign more than a distant possession that consumes the army. Conversely, a broad conquest can be worthwhile when the player has prepared its defense, administration, and support. Neither smallness nor caution is a victory condition by itself.

Integration is not the erasure of local identity. A place can become strategically dependable while retaining local institutions, property holders, customs, and negotiated responsibilities. Direct administration, delegated government, limited military holding, and cooperative local arrangements are tools, not compulsory stages of one centralization ladder.

## 5. Expansion portfolio and strategic value

## 6. Post-gain operating sequence

The following is a proposed gameplay sequence, not a universal historical procedure or mandatory turn-by-turn checklist.

First identify the achieved result and the immediate purpose. Then secure enough actual capacity to prevent avoidable collapse. Establish who can govern, what functions can operate, and which rights or agreements remain contested. Restore the most valuable blocked function before attempting universal reform. Review whether the position can release part of the field host, support another operation, or should instead be reduced or relinquished.

```yaml
ProposedConsolidationConditions:
  conditions:
    - MilitaryResultConfirmed
    - ImmediateSecurityAndCustodyEstablished
    - BoundedGovernmentOrHoldingArrangement
    - PriorityFunctionOperating
    - LocalBurdenAndDisputesVisible
    - SupportAndRecoverySustainable
    - ReviewForExpansionRetentionHandoverOrExit
  conditions_are_fixed_turn_stages: false
  all_rights_must_be_settled_before_any_use: false
  elapsed_turns_alone_complete_integration: false
```

Several conditions may develop together or regress. A place can operate at reduced capacity while a dispute continues. An enemy raid can disrupt a previously dependable route without erasing every political arrangement.

## 7. Immediate security and custody

The holding requirement follows the purpose, threat, terrain, available forces, and supply. There is no identical mandatory garrison for every settlement. A fortified node, open route, productive estate, and institutional precinct can require different arrangements.

Use actual formations and command. Leaving troops reduces the field army unless another supported source replaces them. Occupation does not spawn a local garrison. Local forces, if later available, require their own eligibility, acceptance, equipment, command, and finite pools.

Secure stores, records, equipment, and access through their owners. Captured stores are not necessarily intact, known, usable, or delivered. Record losses and consumed resources. A treasury, archive, and private household property are not one undifferentiated loot balance.

## 8. Local settlement and government choices

| Arrangement | Useful when | Positive capability | Continuing limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Temporary military holding | Immediate operational need exceeds political closure | Keeps a position usable while decisions remain open | Costly, exposed, and not permanent civil government |
| Direct administration | Supported rights, staff, access, and resources permit it | More dependable control of specific functions | Staff, enforcement, records, and local acceptance remain finite |
| Delegated local government | A valid local operator can perform defined duties | Releases ducal attention and preserves routine | Mandate, reporting, conflicting interests, and handback |
| Negotiated association | Cooperation supplies the desired function without annexation | Access, service, or security with less direct burden | Partner independence and actual performance |
| Accepted handover | Another supported actor can hold the result on agreed terms | Releases force and may preserve a useful relationship | No guaranteed future access or automatic reward |

These are arrangements for functions, not new universal government types. Different functions at one place can have different operators where the relevant owners support that distinction. Do not require an extra government wrapper solely because military control changed.

## 9. Property, institutions, and local continuity

A military gain does not erase every estate, office, institution, or claim. Identify which objects actually changed and which continue. Disputes may be negotiated, enforced, referred, or left unresolved with visible consequences.

An abbey's protection arrangement does not become ducal ownership after relief. A bishop's office does not become the settlement government automatically. A local community is not identical to a chapter or a lord's household. Institutional continuity can provide useful records, services, and mediation while retaining independent interests.

The player can contest existing arrangements or impose changes through supported systems. Such action must preserve its actual costs and consequences rather than be relabeled consensual integration. No specific historical immunity, appointment right, or legal outcome is certified here.

## 10. Community burdens and productive recovery

The useful postwar loop is security, access, actual return where feasible, repair, inputs, labor, and resumed activity. These can proceed unevenly. A road can reopen while households remain displaced. A workshop can survive while its skilled people or tools are absent.

Requisition, quartering, repair labor, taxation, and protection duties compete with recovery. The player can prioritize immediate campaign needs, lower demands to restore capacity, or negotiate shared support. None is universally correct. A harsh short-term policy can provide resources while damaging the base needed for future collection.

No demographic totals, protected-characteristic classifications, population transfers, or forced cultural conversion are created. Population remains with its owner; it informs recruitment capacity without becoming a direct army-manpower currency. No profitable devastation-and-relief reward loop is allowed.

## 11. Revenue and military availability

A held place becomes economically useful through actual production, entitlement or imposed collection, available stock, administration, transport, and security. Nominal income is not collected money. An accepted contribution is not delivered supply.

Recruitment access must be reviewed separately from control. Capturing a region does not reveal or refill every pool. Regional profiles remain Theater-owned, and the player receives only supported recruitment relationships and actual finite availability through the military owners.

This authority creates no unit profiles, exact roster, recruitment rates, starting forces, or numerical occupation penalties. The reward of successful consolidation is access to real supported capacity, not a free package of soldiers and income.

## 12. Resistance, compliance, and rival opportunity

Resistance should have a specific object: a disputed demand, coercive act, absent protection, damaged livelihood, contested authority, or rival claim. Compliance with one task does not prove loyalty, and opposition to one demand does not mean universal rebellion.

The player can change the burden, perform protection, restore a function, negotiate, mediate, enforce, or withdraw. Suppression can change immediate security without repairing property or settling a grievance. Negotiation can settle a duty without ending every rival claim.

Rivals can threaten routes, offer support to local actors, contest recognition, raid, besiege, or exploit a depleted holding force through their own systems. No automatic rebellion army, hidden destiny meter, or compulsory historical restoration is introduced.

## 13. Durable integration and release of the host

Durability means the specified useful function can continue with its actual support arrangement. It does not require uniform identity, universal loyalty, or permanent direct rule.

```yaml
DurabilityReview:
  inspect:
    - ActualSecurityAndReliefOptions
    - FunctioningGovernmentOrAcceptedLocalArrangement
    - SupportedRevenueOrMaterialSupport
    - UsableRoutesAndCommunication
    - StaffRecordsAndCustody
    - ManageableBurdenAndKnownDisputes
    - MaintenanceAndRecoveryCapacity
    - FieldForceReleaseAndReturnFeasibility
  one_integrated_meter_controls_all_functions: false
  stable_today_equals_permanent_immunity: false
```

A reduced garrison can be sensible when a dependable local arrangement and relief plan exist. Keeping a large host can be sensible while a decisive threat remains. The player should see which function becomes exposed if troops leave, rather than receive an unexplained occupation penalty.

## 14. Retrenchment, negotiated exit, and loss

Relinquishing an unsupportable gain can preserve a stronger campaign position. Options include accepted handover, phased withdrawal, negotiated access after exit, removal of movable assets where permitted, or abandonment with actual losses. No exit creates consent or safe passage automatically.

Reconcile troops, people, stores, equipment, records, property claims, compensation, and continuing agreements. Withdrawal does not teleport forces or erase consumed resources. Losing military control does not automatically delete every prior right or relationship. A later return encounters the changed campaign state, not a reset settlement.

A reduced position can recover into renewed expansion if actual capacity supports it. Defeat and terminal campaign conditions remain possible; this file creates no guaranteed comeback.

## 15. Succession and cross-owner continuity

A change of duke, claimant, local operator, or institutional officeholder reviews the basis of each arrangement. Some duties attach to an office, some to property, and some to a personal agreement. None should be copied or cancelled universally.

The existing title authority retains recognition and succession settlement. The royal-service specialist retains coalition promises and consideration. This file asks what happens to the held position and its functions after those changes. It creates no new successor or opening allegiance.

## 17. AI strategy and player readability

AI should compare the next gain's usable function with the cost of holding it, including known threats, routes, home exposure, staff, recovery, and counterpart terms. It should be capable of aggressive expansion when supported, not universally favor caution.

Prefer a smaller useful consolidation over an idle occupation when that solves the objective. Do not leave every captured place with the same force. Do not assume local income or recruitment exists before the relevant owner confirms it. Seek relief, negotiated arrangements, repair, reduced scope, or exit when a position becomes unsupportable.

The player-facing summary should show: what was gained, what currently works, what is contested, what consumes the holding force, and what would permit a different next move. Routine updates aggregate. Major scope changes, severe shortages, contested handover, and irreversible losses deserve explicit decisions.

Suggested original text:

> The banner is yours. The position is not yet dependable. Decide what this gain must do, give that function the support it needs, and free the host for the next campaign.

## 19C. Occupation posture and transition

Occupation is a temporary condition of military presence and control, not the final government form. The player should choose a posture that fits the situation.

| Posture | Intended use | Strength | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Secure and assess | Stabilize immediate security and discover known conditions | Avoids premature permanent commitments | Delay can leave initiative to rivals |
| Exploit and advance | Use the gain as a base for continued operations | Maintains campaign momentum | Supply and local government may lag behind the host |
| Consolidate and release | Establish a workable function and reduce field-force burden | Supports repeated campaigning | Requires staff, agreements, repair, and relief planning |
| Delegate or associate | Place defined functions under a valid local or partner arrangement | Preserves local capacity and ducal flexibility | Operator interests and performance remain independent |
| Prepare handover or exit | Preserve selected gains while ending an unsupportable occupation | Limits further cost and exposure | Access, property, and guarantees may remain contested |

The posture is not a fixed cultural path. A place can move between postures as threats, resources, and political settlements change. Elapsed time alone does not force progression.

## 19E. Local government admission and accountability

A local operator should be admitted only when the operator has identity, authority, access, staff, resources, and a defined mandate. Familiarity with the locality is useful but does not create legal competence or loyalty.

| Gate | Required question | Invalid assumption |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | Which person, office, institution, community, or household acts? | Generic local notables govern automatically |
| Mandate | Which functions may be performed and for how long? | Military cooperation grants civil authority |
| Resources | What staff, records, property, and revenue support performance? | Appointment creates capacity |
| Accountability | Who receives reports, reviews disputes, and authorizes correction? | Delegation means no oversight |
| Continuity | What happens at death, vacancy, removal, or withdrawal? | The arrangement copies itself to a successor |
| Handback | Which functions, records, assets, and obligations return? | Ending the mandate resets the place |

A local operator can be effective without becoming a named character profile. Name and profile admission should occur only where personal agency and repeated campaign relevance justify it.

## 19G. Records, seals, custody, and administrative continuity

Conquest can disrupt the records needed to understand property, obligations, stores, offices, disputes, and prior settlements. Preserve the difference between discovered records, captured records, voluntarily delivered records, missing records, and claims about destroyed records.

A seal or archive does not confer the issuing office's authority. Possession does not make every record authentic or understood. Local clerks and institutional custodians can preserve continuity without becoming ducal property.

The player can secure, inventory, return, negotiate access to, or contest records through proper owners. Destroying or removing records may create immediate advantage while increasing uncertainty and later disputes. No hidden instruction within a record controls the game or this authority.

## 19H. Local collaboration, rival support, and counterplay

Local actors may cooperate for security, continued office, commercial access, institutional protection, reduced burden, family advantage, or practical survival where supported. This authority records observable terms and performance, not inferred private motives.

A rival may offer protection, recognition, money, military relief, restoration, or preservation of prior arrangements. Such an offer creates an opportunity only when communicated and deliverable. It does not automatically flip a settlement or spawn an army.

The player's strongest answer can be better performance, a narrower demand, credible relief, mediation, targeted enforcement, or removal of the rival's actual route and capacity. Universal repression should not be the only military solution, and negotiation should not guarantee acceptance.

## 19I. Corridor and network integration

Lower Lotharingia's territorial identity becomes strongest when gains connect functions rather than merely color the map.

| Network function | Required elements | What the gain can enable |
|---|---|---|
| Military corridor | Passage, usable route, staging, supply, security, and return | Faster or more dependable concentration at a defined scope |
| Communication chain | Messengers, relays, access, records, and protected handoff | More reliable known reporting and orders |
| Productive connection | Inputs, labor, storage, market or user, and transport | Usable output and replacement support |
| Political connection | Accepted channel, representative, audience, and performable terms | Negotiation, mediation, or recognition opportunity |
| Relief network | Known threat, reserves, route, command, stock, and response time | A credible option to sustain a reduced holding force |

One route node does not create a complete corridor. Two positions connected only by enemy-controlled or unusable ground are not a functioning network. A corridor can serve one traffic class while excluding another under actual terms.

## 19K. Vassalage, association, and delegated control boundaries

Do not collapse negotiated association, service, tribute, protection, vassalage, delegated government, and direct possession into one integration tier. Each requires its own supported terms and owner.

A local lord can provide service while controlling property independently. A community can accept protection without accepting direct administration. An institution can cooperate materially while retaining jurisdictional objections. A delegated operator can govern defined functions without acquiring hereditary ownership.

This authority does not create new vassal contracts or impose modern territorial hierarchy. Its purpose is to prevent a successful settlement from being misrepresented as every possible political relationship at once.

## 19M. AI expansion planning and anti-exploit rules

- Select gains for a stated strategic function rather than map-painting value alone.
- Permit ambitious multi-position expansion when supply, command, governance, and recovery can support it.
