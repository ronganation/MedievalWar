A claimant may gain the ducal title while failing to obtain the core. A household may preserve the core while losing the title. A fortress may be occupied while property rights and local cooperation remain disputed.

## 17. Development pathways

### 17.1 Dependable direct core

Property records, local administration, supply, defenses, routes, local cooperation, and household capacity reinforce one another.

### 17.2 Ducal administrative seat

The core supports government records, audiences, messengers, fiscal preparation, and bounded delegation without becoming the whole Realm government.

### 17.3 Defensive refuge

The fortified seat protects people and records during regional crisis but may sacrifice wider influence if the faction retreats too readily.

### 17.4 Successor-house nucleus

A recognized property and household settlement preserves the Ardenne-Luxembourg line or another valid successor house after loss of the ducal title.

### 17.5 Negotiated shared settlement

Property, residence, command, revenue, and title outcomes are divided among Frederick's household, a royal appointee, or an Ardenne-Verdun settlement.

### 17.6 Militarized and exhausted core

Heavy defense preserves immediate possession while depleting population, stores, finance, buildings, and local cooperation.

### 17.7 Hollow fortified seat

The place retains symbolic value but lacks supply, records, accepted officers, reliable routes, or defenders.

### 17.8 Captured seat, surviving claim

An opponent controls the fortification while displaced household members preserve records, recognition, relationships, or property claims elsewhere.
