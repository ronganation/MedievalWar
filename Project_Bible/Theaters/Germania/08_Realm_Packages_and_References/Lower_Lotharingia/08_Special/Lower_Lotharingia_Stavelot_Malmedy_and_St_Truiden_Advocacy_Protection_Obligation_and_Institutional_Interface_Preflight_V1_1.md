This preflight develops the two principal monastic advocacy fields connected to Frederick's Lower Lotharingian campaign. It preserves Stavelot-Malmedy and St Truiden as distinct institutions with separate property, jurisdiction, settlement, protection, and relationship questions. It creates no abbey-owned Realm, no generic Church faction, no arbitrary income, and no free institutional troops.

## 22. Development pathways

### 22.1 Responsible protector

Frederick performs bounded obligations, respects institutional distinction, and gains dependable cooperation without absorbing property.

### 22.2 Negotiated contributor network

The institutions provide evidenced and reviewable contributions in exchange for protection, access, confirmation, or settlement.

### 22.3 Institutional broker

Frederick mediates among abbeys, local actors, Liège, royal agents, and regional magnates while accepting that mediation does not guarantee compliance.

### 22.4 Overmighty advocate

Military capacity and access are used to pressure institutions beyond supported rights. Short-term gains produce legal, political, and reputational risks.

### 22.5 Failed protector

Routes, estates, dependents, or rights suffer because promised protection cannot be performed. Recovery requires restitution, renewed capacity, mediation, or revised terms.

### 22.6 Divided obligations

Simultaneous crises force prioritization. One institution receives timely support while the other negotiates alternatives or records a grievance.

### 22.7 Rival protector pathway

An institution seeks another viable protector or mediator when Frederick cannot perform or repeatedly overreaches. This is conditional and requires historical-political feasibility.

### 22.8 Institutional autonomy under pressure

The institution preserves identity and rights through records, local cooperation, external recognition, and negotiated defense without becoming a sovereign Realm by default.

## 27. Prohibited assumptions

- Advocacy makes either abbey Frederick's property.
- The institutions are interchangeable because Frederick is connected to both.
- The advocate controls spiritual jurisdiction or abbatial appointment.
- An abbey treasury is available ducal revenue.
- Protection creates a free garrison or field contingent.
- Institutional estates equal settlement or regional control.
- Religious identity creates alliance, intelligence, passage, or obedience.
- Failure to answer a demand proves hostility or rebellion.
- One officeholder's relationship transfers unchanged to a successor.
- Advocacy is hereditary without institution-specific evidence.
- St Truiden's Liège relationship can be omitted or absorbed into Lower Lotharingia.
- Military occupation extinguishes every institutional right.
- A later dispute or reform conflict is active at opening without evidence.
- Unknown terms may be filled with convenient taxes, troops, or jurisdiction.
