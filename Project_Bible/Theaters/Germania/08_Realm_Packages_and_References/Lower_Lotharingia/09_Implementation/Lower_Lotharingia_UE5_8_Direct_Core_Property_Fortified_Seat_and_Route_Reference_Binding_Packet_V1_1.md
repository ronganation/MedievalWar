## 6. Property reference requirements

S1 section 8 supplies the property-object model. A later property binding should distinguish object identity, location and extent, holder or possessor, legal/customary basis, revenue or service rights, encumbrances, administrators or custodians, disputants, documentary evidence, and Opening State status.

This packet does not fill those fields. It proposes the following review outcomes:

- **Descriptive association only:** retain the strategic link, but expose no owned asset or revenue.
- **Existing endpoint identified, rights unresolved:** reference the object without assigning a holder or suppressing the dispute.
- **Conflicting candidates:** retain the conflict for owner disposition; do not merge by name similarity.
- **Evidence sufficient for later state work:** hand the evidence to the property and Opening State owners under separate authorization. This document still performs no assignment.

A place name, genealogy, ducal title, charter custody, or military occupation is not a universal proof of every property right. Records can support a proposition without being self-proving or exhaustive. Record loss does not erase all rights. These boundaries follow S1 sections 8, 13, and 15 and S2 sections 4 and 16.

## 7. Fortified-seat function without free capacity

The proposed inspection should distinguish supported functions rather than display one uncaused core-control score.

| Function | Necessary separate evidence/state | Prohibited inference |
|---|---|---|
| Refuge | Available space, access, protection, and support | Everyone in the household is already present |
| Defense | Fortification condition, defenders, equipment, command, stores | A fortress icon creates a garrison |
| Records continuity | Actual records, custody, access, authentication | Archive possession proves every claim |
| Administrative support | Staff, mandates, communication, resources | Seat equals capital or entire government |
| Storage and sustainment | Real stores, custody, capacity, consumption | Storage function creates food or money |
| Relief and evacuation | Feasible routes, transport, people, competing commitments | Selecting evacuation moves people instantly |

S1 sections 7 and 10–14 and S2 sections 5–6 support these distinctions. A place may be defended through valid occupation or another supported arrangement without uncontested legal ownership. S1's narrower defense wording is not converted into a universal legal-ownership prerequisite: S2 sections 4 and 16 explicitly preserve military control alongside disputed rights. This is a disclosed interpretive boundary, not a silent amendment to S1.

Envoy passage is not army passage; transit is not billeting, provisioning, recruitment, or toll entitlement. A repaired bridge does not renew an expired agreement. A river on a map does not create boats, crews, landing capacity, or safe passage. A second route may share the first route's bottleneck or transport assets and therefore provide no independent capacity.

Peaceful access terms are not a universal prerequisite for hostile movement. S2 sections 13 and 20 explicitly reject a claim, target-consent, or Papal-approval gate for war within the project design. This packet grants no war state and creates no additional gate. Actual movement remains subject to the relevant game systems and operational capacity.

## 9. Material transition and recovery matrix

This adapts S1 sections 15–16 and S2 sections 16 and 21B–21F. The matrix describes future review, not a scheduled transition.

| Changed layer | Preserve separately | Review outcome required |
|---|---|---|
| Ducal title changes | Private property, possession, residence, records, command | Identify affected objects; no whole-core transfer |
| Fortress occupied | Property claims, local cooperation, damage, stores | Distinguish occupation from lawful transfer and usable capacity |
| Household relocates | Property rights, office mandates, garrison, treasury | Record actual people and custody moved; do not move everything |
| Archive lost or recovered | Surviving rights, competing evidence, knowledge | Preserve uncertainty and evidence limits |
| Route repaired | Access terms, transport, safety, competing commitments | Restore only the physical function addressed |
| Garrison depleted | Fortification, command, recruitment and recovery limits | No automatic refill after repair or recapture |
| Settlement damaged | Population, production, obligations, local response | Persist material loss; no opening-state reset |
| Property settlement agreed | Delivery, possession, encumbrances, counterpart response | Agreement is not completed transfer or execution |

The core may survive title loss, be divided, be occupied, or remain contested. No named relative or claimant automatically receives it. Later Limburg development remains a possible long-horizon outcome, not an opening polity or guaranteed event.

## 10. Proposed read-only presentation contract

This is a design recommendation derived from S2 sections 14 and 21G, not an implemented API or invented project function.

A compact inspection should lead with the place's supported role, the known action-specific constraint, and the uncertainty that matters. Detailed provenance may expose the selected authority and locator. It must not reveal private foreign stores, garrisons, or access terms that the actor does not know.

Distinguish at least:

- Descriptive source association versus current possession.
- Existing endpoint versus unresolved or conflicting endpoint.
- Candidate fortified-seat function versus actual capital or residence.
- Nominal right versus collected revenue or performed service.
- Known route conditions versus unknown or stale reports.
- Proposed function versus available resources and completed execution.

These are conceptual distinctions, not required enum names. Unknown is not zero, absent, neutral, hostile, automatically permitted, or automatically blocked. Read-only inspection must not allocate IDs, create placeholders, reserve stores, assign people, or mutate campaign state.
