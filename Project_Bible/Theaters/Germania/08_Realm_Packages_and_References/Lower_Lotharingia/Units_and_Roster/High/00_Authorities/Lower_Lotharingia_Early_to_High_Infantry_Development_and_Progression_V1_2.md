### 2. Direct Progression Verdict

Lower Lotharingian infantry develops through **institutional deepening, equipment specialization, stronger campaign continuity, and more deliberate combined-arms differentiation**.

The accepted Early roster now establishes six durable infantry foundations, including the completed Lotharingse Boeren V1.3 lowest-tier rural emergency family:

1. broad spear-and-shield frontage;
2. ducal command-connected reserve infantry;
3. castellany strongpoint and relief infantry;
4. civic watch and urban-security infantry;
5. ordinary regional shielded close-combat infantry;
6. a lowest-tier general peasant, labor, and emergency-defense infantry family.

The High roster does not erase these foundations. It adds five more specialized expressions:

1. professional foot for sustained campaigning;
2. axe assault infantry for bounded line breaking;
3. long-spear infantry for prepared reach and cavalry denial;
4. dismounted knightly infantry for elite anchoring and counterassault;
5. later mixed-polearm infantry for armored-target control, checked-cavalry exploitation, and passage combat.

### 4. Portfolio-Level Development Channels

#### 4.1 Muster and command channel

Early development begins with local, lordly, ducal, castellany, and civic authority relationships. High development improves inspection, command continuity, service records, officer depth, standards, messengers, relief practice, and campaign administration.

#### 4.2 Equipment and craft channel

Early infantry relies on variable but serviceable spears, shields, practical sidearms, helmets, and limited protection. High infantry requires stronger weapon inspection, more reliable armor and shield maintenance, long-weapon or specialist-weapon manufacture, replacement parts, transport, and craft labor.

#### 4.3 Service and contract channel

Early service remains heavily connected to bounded obligation, local defense, household, castellany, and town duties. High professional infantry increasingly depends on paid, maintained, contracted, repeated-service, or elite political relationships without becoming a universal standing army.

#### 4.4 Formation and doctrine channel

Early formations emphasize frontage, connection, local relief, prepared positions, and bounded close combat. High formations add guarded advances, planned assault lanes, prepared reach fronts, formal mission budgets, reorganization, specialist reset, and tighter combined-arms timing.

#### 4.5 Campaign sustainment channel

High capability requires better food, transport, repair, treatment, camp security, compensation, replacement, and return systems. Battlefield access without sustainment cannot maintain High Era availability.

### 8. Development Portfolios

#### 8.0 Rural emergency and labor continuity portfolio

Preserves Lotharingse Boeren as the lowest-tier rural emergency, labor, camp, rear, evacuation, and local-defense family. Better records, assembly, food, tools, compensation, and return administration may improve reliability, but no development step automatically creates formed spear, shielded, professional, ducal, knightly, or specialist infantry.

#### 8.1 Broad infantry continuity portfolio

Improves Early Speerlieden and Schildknechten through better inspection, equipment maintenance, leadership continuity, route administration, relief practice, and provider trust. These improvements strengthen the same card but do not grant High specialist identities.

#### 8.2 Professional field-service portfolio

Creates the conditions for Voetserjanten through contracts, repeated service, captain and veteran continuity, more consistent equipment, camp and baggage support, treatment, pay, compensation, and real reorganization capacity.

#### 8.3 Assault specialization portfolio

Creates Bijlknechten only when qualified people, maintained axes, protective equipment, veteran assault cadence, prepared approach relationships, and recovery support exist. The portfolio does not convert every axe owner or ducal retainer.

#### 8.4 Reach and cavalry-control portfolio

Creates Langspiesknechten through long-weapon production and repair, formation practice, threat-sector doctrine, depth, flank support, and prepared reception. It strengthens but does not replace ordinary Speerlieden.

#### 8.5 Knightly foot portfolio

Creates Ridders te Voet by deliberately reallocating real knightly and upper-retainer capacity from mounted service to a high-value foot mission. Horse custody, banners, armor, attendants, ransom risk, and remounting remain physical.

#### 8.6 Later mixed-polearm portfolio

Creates Hellebaardiers only after later High chronology, regional weapon forms, craft support, specialist files, formation compatibility, and battlefield purpose pass. This is not a generic technology upgrade from long spears or axes.

### 14. Recommended Player-Facing Development Sequence

1. **Preserve the complete Early foundation.** Maintain Boeren only for justified emergency, labor, rear, or local-defense needs while preserving enough Speerlieden, Schildknechten, ducal reserve, castellany capacity, and town security to keep the realm functional.
2. **Improve reliability before specialization.** Develop inspections, officers, repair, pay, transport, treatment, relief, and return systems.
3. **Add Voetserjanten as the first broad High professional expression.** This gives sustained field service without erasing ordinary infantry.
4. **Add Langspiesknechten or Bijlknechten according to strategic pressure.** Choose prepared reach and cavalry control, or bounded assault and shield disruption.
5. **Add Ridders te Voet only for elite high-value missions.** The player must accept real mounted opportunity cost.
6. **Add Hellebaardiers later.** Mixed-polearm capability follows validated later High weapon development, craft support, and specialist practice.
