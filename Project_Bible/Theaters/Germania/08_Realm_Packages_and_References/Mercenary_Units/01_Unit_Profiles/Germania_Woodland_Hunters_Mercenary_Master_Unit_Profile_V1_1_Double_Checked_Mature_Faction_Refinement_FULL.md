## 1. Strong Unit Verdict: Work the Edge

**Woodland Hunters are affordable light mercenary bowmen who make exposed troops pay for crossing a screened approach, then move before the enemy fixes them.** Their signature is **Work the Edge**: use a woodland margin, hedge, shallow ridge, or friendly spear shoulder to obtain a useful firing window, spend arrows on an appropriate target, and leave while a return lane remains.

They are real archers, not strategic agents or unarmed observers. Against exposed light troops their arrows must cause useful casualties and pressure. They can screen a deployment, support a flank, help contest ordinary skirmishers, and cover a withdrawal. Their moderate bows, light protection, ordinary military discipline, and limited melee mean that better missile troops and closing cavalry remain serious threats.

The name does not grant elite accuracy, invisibility, superior range, or free food. Hunting familiarity can inform the visual and source concept, but an available company must already possess enough military organization to take orders, identify targets, conserve arrows, and rally. A poor-quality offer can be uneven without making the entire family a joke.

The role is deliberately simpler than a regional relationship portfolio. An eligible army sees a finite mercenary offer, pays to hire it, and uses the unit. Source and supply constrain the pool behind that interface; they do not turn every hire into a miniature diplomatic mission.

## 2. One-Screen Total War Card

- **Unit:** Woodland Hunters.
- **Class:** Light mercenary bow infantry.
- **Quality:** Basic to practiced; ordinary established company as the useful baseline.
- **Battle promise:** Affordable arrow pressure against exposed light troops, with flexible screening and timely relocation.
- **Best use:** Woodland edges, hedges, broken approaches with clear lanes, flank screens, camp approaches, and withdrawal cover.
- **Strong against:** Unshielded light infantry, exposed missile crews, poorly protected reserves, and disordered targets within a safe firing window.
- **Weak against:** Shielded fronts, substantial armor, superior archers or crossbows, open-ground cavalry, sustained melee, blocked exits, and exhausted ammunition.
- **Equipment:** Moderate self-bow candidate, finite arrows, knife or modest sidearm, clothing and limited light protection.
- **Campaign bargain:** Lower signing cost than specialist hired missiles, but wages above comparable domestic low-tier bowmen and a finite replacement pool.
- **Player instruction:** Find a clear shot and a way back before taking the first shot.
- **Flavor line:** Loose from the edge. Leave before the charge.

## 3. Encyclopedia and Recruitment Text

Woodland Hunters provide a modest but useful service: bowmen ready to screen an army and shoot at enemies who expose themselves. Their equipment is light, their bows are moderate, and their best work comes from selecting a sensible position rather than standing in the open against superior missiles.

A company at a woodland edge can trouble an advancing light formation, force skirmishers to seek cover, or support a spear line from an open shoulder. When the enemy turns toward them, the Hunters must judge whether another release is worth the time it costs. Light equipment helps them relocate, but it does not let them outrun cavalry across an open field or pass through friendly ranks without space.

They remain hired troops rather than a permanent domestic institution. A compatible market may provide a ready company at a relatively low signing price, while wages and replacement needs make prolonged service more expensive than developing comparable local bowmen. Veterans improve fire discipline, warning, and recovery; they do not automatically become elite forest archers.

**Recruitment text:** Affordable hired bowmen for screening and harassment. Effective against exposed light troops, but lightly protected and poor in sustained melee.

## 8. Bow Handling, Shot Cycle, and Sidearm Transition

The proposed cycle is halt, establish a clear lane, nock, draw, aim briefly, release, recover, and reassess. Movement interrupts the shot. The unit does not fire accurately while running or instantly complete a full release after a sharp turn.

The design target is a more continuous ordinary bow rhythm than the hired crossbow company's slower bolt cycle, with less threat against protected targets. This is a comparative tuning proposal, not an imported historical rate of fire. Sustained drawing tires the company and reduces useful output. A high initial rate cannot continue indefinitely.

When contact becomes unavoidable, the bow is secured or dropped and the sidearm used. That transition preserves some self-defense but does not create a second melee unit. Broken bows, lost arrows, and abandoned equipment remain part of the post-battle state.

## 9. Ammunition, Effective Range, and Armor Ceiling

Hunters need a usable missile envelope. At a sensible fighting distance, exposed low-protection troops should suffer enough casualties to make ignoring the company a mistake. At long range, moving targets, scattered targets, poor visibility, and obstructions reduce useful hits. Maximum flight distance is not the same as reliable fighting distance.

Shielded fronts and substantial armor sharply reduce return on arrows. The Hunters may pressure an exposed flank, unprotected crew, or disordered target rather than repeatedly firing into the best-protected front. No generic armor-piercing arrow package is granted.

```yaml
missile_envelope:
  exact_ranges: Unbound
  useful_target: ExposedLowProtectionTroops
  ordinary_range: UsefulButNotElite
  maximum_range: LowerHitReliability
  moving_target: Harder

  ammunition: Finite
  victory_refill: false

```

Ammunition resupply belongs to the shared army supply system. A quiver does not refill because the company hides, changes formation, wins a skirmish, or enters another patch of woodland.

## 23. Screening Is a Fighting Task, Not an Agent Role

A screen occupies useful ground, shoots at exposed threats, and gives the main army time and space to deploy. Hunters can warn of enemies they actually observe through the shared battle visibility system. They do not reveal the campaign map, discover hidden armies automatically, or become a strategic intelligence network.

A successful screen may inflict casualties, make an enemy light unit change route, or force a cavalry detour toward friendly spears. It may also fail when counterfire pins the Hunters or a second threat closes the exit. The player should see these outcomes through movement and combat, not through an invisible screening aura.

The company can fight in ordinary open terrain when supported. Woodland flavor must not make it useless whenever no forest is present. Conversely, a forest icon must not guarantee safety when the actual trees block fire and command.

## 24. Ambush and Concealment Without Elite Inflation

Ambush is an opportunity created by concealment, approach, timing, and the opponent's information. Hunters can hold fire near a suitable edge and release at an exposed target that enters their lane. The first useful shots do not automatically rout the target or prevent return fire.

Firing, movement, proximity, observation angle, and enemy search can reveal the company. Once revealed, moving behind cover may break sight but does not erase the opponent's knowledge of the last position. Dense woods can conceal and obstruct at the same time. A company hidden too deeply may have no useful shot at all.

```yaml
ambush_contract:
  concealment: ActualTerrainAndSharedDetection

  first_release: OrdinaryWeaponEffectsAgainstActualExposure

  relocation: TimeSpaceAndCommandRequired
  automatic_ambush_success: false
  forest_invisibility: false
  instant_reconcealment: false
  guaranteed_surprise_morale_damage: false
```

The positive payoff is a favorable firing window, not a new assassin mechanic. The unit remains a bow company after the ambush begins.

## 25. Relocation, Escape, and Skirmish Control

The player should be able to order ordinary skirmish behavior without repeatedly issuing a dozen specialist commands. Hunters halt to fire, move when a threat closes, and reform where another lane is available. The automated behavior must consider the route and friendly support, not merely run directly away from the nearest enemy.

Light equipment supports useful foot relocation. It does not guarantee speed superiority over every infantry unit, endless stamina, or escape from mounted pursuit. A late withdrawal can lose the company. Repeated retreat without shooting can also surrender ground without delivering value.

## 27. Melee, Morale, and Contact Survival

Hunters can fend off scattered survivors or briefly resist another poor missile unit, but a knife does not make them capable line infantry. A depleted enemy may still be dangerous. Casualties, morale, fatigue, and support determine whether a short contact is survivable.

The company should not receive automatic mercenary cowardice or automatic hunter courage. A clear mission, captain, nearby spears, and an open route support confidence. Cavalry closing, heavy counterfire, isolation, and a blocked retreat undermine it. Captain loss can reduce control without forcing instant rout.

Ammunition exhaustion is not a morale collapse by itself. The player may retain the company as a reserve, withdraw it, or accept a limited emergency task. The AI must not send it into heavy melee simply because the arrows are gone.

## 28. Germania Whole-Battle Plan

**Deployment:** put the Hunters on a usable flank or forward screen with a known route toward friendly infantry. A woodland edge or hedge is helpful only if it offers an actual firing lane and enough space to leave. Keep a separate lane for cavalry.

**Approach:** use hold fire when concealment matters; otherwise begin useful pressure on exposed light troops. Do not waste the opening quiver on the best armored shield front merely because it is the nearest target.

**First contact:** when the enemy turns toward the screen, choose another short release or withdraw. The decision must account for enemy closing speed, not only distance. Hired Spear Companies can receive a threat after the Hunters clear the lane.

**Main battle:** work a shoulder or exposed flank while German Crossbowmen address more protected targets. The Hunters should not mask the crossbows or stand where a knightly charge needs to pass.

**Crisis:** relocate before counterfire or cavalry fixes the company. Hired Mounted Sergeants can contest a pursuer, but no rescue is guaranteed. Friendly bodies can block escape as effectively as terrain.

**End battle:** conserve survivors and remaining arrows. Shoot suitable exposed routers only when the lane is safe; let cavalry conduct long pursuit. An intact low-cost company may be worth retaining for the next march.

## 36. Art, Animation, and Audio Identity

## 37. Player UI and Accessibility

The unit does not require a unique multi-button minigame. Standard move, attack, hold fire, skirmish, formation, and retreat controls cover the core loop. Contextual feedback can explain terrain and concealment without exposing hidden enemy information.

## 43. Acceptance Criteria and First-Draft Decision

The card passes design review only if it delivers useful exposed-target arrow pressure at an affordable mercenary price while remaining clearly below better missile specialists. Screening and relocation must add practical value without invisibility, guaranteed escape, or a unique command minigame.

The player must be able to use Hunters in an ordinary supported battle as well as at a woodland edge. Terrain changes lanes, cover, command, and escape. It does not replace the weapon or create a flat cultural advantage. Finite ammunition and poor melee remain meaningful.

The campaign transaction stays simple: a ready finite offer, signing price, wages, and compatible recovery. Domestic development remains valuable. Regional source ownership and cross-Theater persistence prevent duplication behind the interface without burying the player in administration.

**First-draft verdict: retain Woodland Hunters as Germania's affordable hired light-bow option.** The core loop is screen, choose a useful target, halt and shoot, relocate, rally, and shoot again if arrows, space, and condition permit.

## 46. Refined One-Screen Total War Card

- **Unit:** Woodland Hunters.
- **Class:** Light mercenary bow infantry.
- **Battle promise:** Screen a useful approach, punish exposed light troops, and relocate before heavier opposition fixes the company.
- **Strong against:** Unshielded light infantry, exposed skirmishers, lightly protected missile crews, disordered reserves, and troops crossing an open lane without cover.
- **Best use:** A woodland edge, hedge, shallow ridge, open flank with spear support, camp approach, or withdrawal screen with a known return lane.
- **Weak against:** Shields, substantial armor, superior archers, protected crossbows, concentrated missile fire, cavalry closure, blocked exits, and sustained melee.
- **Battle rhythm:** Observe, select a worthwhile target, halt and shoot, reassess threat and ammunition, move deliberately, rally, and resume if another lane exists.
- **Campaign bargain:** Lower hiring cost than specialist missile mercenaries, with finite arrows, finite company availability, and higher wages than comparable domestic low-tier bowmen.
- **Player instruction:** Spend arrows where exposure makes them count, and leave before the return lane disappears.
- **Flavor line retained:** Loose from the edge. Leave before the charge.

## 65. V1.1 Final Refinement Decision

Woodland Hunters remains a strong affordable mercenary missile card. The double-check confirms that the unit earns its place through useful arrow pressure, broad deployment flexibility, target judgment, and controlled relocation rather than fantasy concealment or elite bow performance.

V1.1 keeps the company modest and makes that modest tier more playable. Exposed light troops must respect its fire. Shielded and armored troops remain poor targets. Open supported ground works. Woodland terrain can help concealment while also obstructing fire and retreat. The company can move, but it cannot kite forever or outrun cavalry after a late decision.

The refined profile is settled at the double-checked design stage and can advance cleanly within the Germania mercenary sequence.
