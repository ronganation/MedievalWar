## 1. Strong Unit Verdict: Lock the Ground

**Prussian Spearmen are veteran shield-bearing contract infantry hired to seize a defensible piece of ground, absorb the enemy's first committed answer, and keep that ground locked long enough for the army to win around them.**

Their battlefield identity remains **Lock the Ground**. They are not another generic spear company with slightly better defense. The player buys a harder, more deliberate shield-and-spear body whose broad protection, disciplined frontage, and practiced local recovery let it dominate one bounded defensive problem: a cavalry-threatened shoulder, a crossing exit, a gate court, a missile battery's vulnerable approach, a captured rise, or the hinge between two formations. The company earns its premium by preserving that sector through the first committed attack and still possessing enough order to answer rather than merely survive.

The company is also capable of positive infantry work. Once the enemy's first attack loses order, Prussian Spearmen lower the shields enough to drive short spear actions, restore their front, and take a measured step forward. They can defeat poor infantry, outlast many ordinary medium troops in a fair frontal contest, and punish cavalry or assault troops that spend themselves against the locked frontage. They are not passive scenery waiting to die.

Their limitation is commitment. A ground-locking formation turns slowly, advances cautiously, and becomes vulnerable when attacked from several directions, drawn into pursuit, broken by concentrated missiles, or forced to fight after shield order collapses. The player buys a local fortress made of people, shields, and discipline, not a mobile answer to every threat.

**Purchase promise:** choose the ground, close the shields, survive the answer, and make the enemy pay a second time for trying to take it.

## 3. Encyclopedia Text

Some mercenary companies are hired to fill space. Prussian Spearmen are hired when one part of the battle must remain under control. Their shields form a dense, visible frontage; their spears deny an easy mounted approach; and their veteran files know how to close a gap before a local success becomes a rout.

On suitable ground they receive the first serious attack with more confidence than common contract spearmen. An enemy that fails to break the front immediately must then contend with short spear thrusts, advancing shields, and fresh files replacing the exhausted men in front. The company can take a crossing exit, hold the shoulder of a missile line, or secure a captured gate court while the rest of the army changes direction.

That steadiness has a cost. The formation does not wheel like light infantry, pursue like cavalry, or attack with the destructive freedom of heavy axemen. Concentrated bolts can open the shield line, repeated forced turns can separate the files, and an assault on both shoulders can turn disciplined depth into congestion. If committed carelessly, the company becomes an expensive body trapped behind its own defensive posture.

Prussian Spearmen enter service through finite eastern and Baltic-facing mercenary contacts. Their people, leaders, shields, spears, and experience are not created by local wealth alone. A surviving company may earn repeated contracts far from its source, but heavy losses become increasingly difficult to replace.

## 8. Lock the Ground: Full Combat Loop

**Choose:** identify a bounded sector whose loss would expose missiles, cavalry, a crossing, the army's hinge, or a captured objective. The company performs best when the commander knows what ground matters.

**Occupy:** arrive before the crisis if possible. Face the likely threat, preserve depth, leave a usable rear route, and avoid stretching the company across more frontage than its shields can truly cover.

**Close:** tighten the working frontage as danger approaches. This raises local shield coverage and mutual support while reducing turn speed, missile spacing, and freedom of movement.

**Receive:** accept the first committed contact through spear reach, shield facing, depth, and veteran replacement of disrupted front files. A formed reception is strong, not invulnerable.

**Pin:** keep the enemy engaged on the locked ground. The company's success may be measured by denying movement and preserving the army's geometry rather than immediate kills.

**Answer:** when the enemy loses momentum, deliver short connected spear actions and a one- or two-step shield press. Recover the original line or gain a small useful space.

**Reset:** halt before the files lose connection. Replace damaged front files, restore facing, and prepare for the next pressure.

**Release:** withdraw or accept relief before fatigue, missiles, flanking, or fresh heavy assault turns the locked position into a trap.

Lock the Ground is the card's ordinary combat identity, not an activated invulnerability stance.

## 10. The Shield Front Is the Distinction

Prussian Spearmen are not defined solely by having spears. Their defining battlefield system is the active shield frontage.

- Shield facing reduces frontal missile and melee exposure according to the shared protection model.
- Connected files support local recovery when one fighter is pushed back or lost.
- Deeper ranks replace pressure at the front but do not attack through occupied bodies.
- Close order improves the local wall while increasing density and reducing turning freedom.
- Spear actions must remain compatible with the shield posture; the unit cannot receive full protection while performing impossible wide attacks.
- A flank or rear attack bypasses much of the purchased advantage.

The desired result is not a literal single rigid shield wall. Small gaps, individual movement, uneven ground, casualty replacement, and fatigue remain visible. The formation should look worked by people, not rendered as an unbroken collision box.

## 11A. Frontage, Depth, and Size Contract

Prussian Spearmen should remain a full company-scale formation rather than a tiny elite guard with inflated survivability. Their defensive quality comes from equipment, veteran file behavior, and useful depth.

- **Wide frontage:** protects more ground but weakens replacement depth and makes a shoulder easier to isolate.
- **Balanced frontage:** the normal battle expression, maintaining enough depth for local recovery.
- **Deep frontage:** best for a crossing exit, gate court, or relief reserve, but covers less width and presents a denser missile target.
- **Understrength company:** must shorten frontage or lose the very depth that supports Lock the Ground.
- **Casualty replacement:** rear files can restore the front where space exists; they cannot attack through occupied models or refill gaps instantly.

Exact model count remains unbound. Calibration must compare equal-cost armies as well as equal-headcount formations so that premium quality does not become free army-wide frontage.

## 13. Cavalry Reception and Counterattack

Prepared cavalry reception depends on facing, depth, spear continuity, footing, confidence, and warning. The unit should make a frontal charge costly enough that a rational mounted commander searches for another answer.

A charge caught while the company is moving, turning, climbing, fragmented, exhausted, or opening a passage is fundamentally different. The shield-and-spear identity does not provide universal anti-cavalry safety.

After a failed mounted contact, front files may continue spear actions while rear files preserve depth. A limited counterpush can clear stalled riders or restore the original frontage. The company should not pursue mounted troops into open ground.

No abstract anti-large multiplier should replace actual weapon, formation, and target conditions. If the shared model uses category modifiers, they must preserve the visible logic above.

## 14. Infantry Fighting and the Spear Scrum

The company needs a real infantry game. Spear points create the first problem; shields and veteran file replacement sustain the contest; sidearms become more relevant only when the formation compresses beyond useful spear handling.

Against ordinary infantry, the Prussians should not simply stand still with low damage. Connected thrusts, controlled advances, and a local push after enemy momentum fails create a positive player experience.

When a deep or aggressive enemy reaches full compression, the fight becomes a **Spear Scrum**. Front files lose some reach, shields collide, sidearms appear, and rear depth matters more than clean spear geometry. Prussian veterans remain steadier than ordinary hired spears, but the unit's premium advantage is fading.

Fresh heavy axes, maces, and elite swords are appropriate ways to exploit that transition. Relief or a friendly counterattack must remain valuable.

## 15. Missiles, Density, and Counterplay

A locked shield front should visibly reduce frontal exposure to ordinary arrows without becoming projectile immunity. Gaps, angles, tired arms, raised spear actions, and casualties create opportunities.

Crossbows, javelins at a flank, high-angle fire, sustained concentration, and shooting during a turn remain serious. Dense order makes the company easier to target even as individual shields become more useful.

The player can respond through terrain, suppression, an open approach order, Hired Mounted Sergeants, Woodland Hunters, German Crossbowmen, or faster commitment to the objective. None of those partners grants a passive defense bonus.

Autoresolve and AI must distinguish a short frontal arrow exposure from a prolonged crossbow concentration. A high shield statistic alone cannot flatten all missile situations into the same result.

## 17. Whole-Battle Employment

**Deployment:** assign one serious problem. Place the company where the army gains something concrete if the ground remains secure. Preserve a rear route and connect both shoulders to terrain or friendly formations.

**Opening:** move in supported line or open movement order. Do not begin locked unless immediate pressure justifies the fatigue, turning, and missile costs.

**Commitment:** close the front as the enemy's intent becomes clear. Receive cavalry, ordinary infantry, or a local assault with full facing. Crossbowmen and Hunters can work beside or behind the frontage through actual firing lanes.

**Decision:** if the attacker loses momentum, counterpress by a few paces. If fresh heavy assault enters, accept relief or bring Saxon Axe Veterans, German Foot Knights, cavalry, or missiles into the fight.

**Crisis:** form the defensive ring only when a multi-direction threat is real and escape is temporarily impossible. The ring buys time; it does not win an encircled battle by itself.

**End state:** secure the sector, restore line order, or withdraw before the company becomes spent. The correct success may be a held crossing, preserved missile battery, protected retreat, or enemy charge denied, not merely a high kill count.

## 19. Leadership, Morale, and Veteran Character

Captains and file leaders are central to the card. They choose frontage, close the shields, control passage lanes, replace the front files, halt a counterpush, and recognize when a locked position has become a trap.

Morale is steady, not unbreakable. The company expects a hard first contact and should not rout merely because casualties begin. Encirclement, loss of both shoulders, captain loss, concentrated missiles, exhaustion, and army-wide collapse remain meaningful.

Veteran character appears through order and judgment, not inflated individual hit points. Better companies close more cleanly, recover gaps sooner, preserve spear actions longer during compression, and withdraw with less fragmentation.

Replacement can restore headcount before it restores veteran trust and timing. A heavily rebuilt company may retain the card name while performing closer to the lower edge of its quality band.

## 20. Fatigue and the Defensive Burden

Locking ground is tiring. Shields remain raised, rear ranks brace the front, files replace casualties, and the company repeatedly absorbs local impact.

Fatigue should reduce spear rhythm, shield recovery, turn speed, gap closure, morale recovery, and the strength of the short counterpush. The formation does not simply retain maximum defense because it has stopped moving.

A pause on secured ground can recover part of the burden. It cannot repair shields, replace missing spears, heal wounds, or restore dead leaders.

The unit must have a readable transition from fresh lock to bloodied lock to spent company. Without that transition, the premium defense becomes endless; if the transition occurs immediately, the card loses its purpose.

## 22. Hiring, Cost, and Campaign Bargain

Recruit through the ordinary mercenary panel. The offer should show company strength, experience, shield and spear readiness, hire cost, upkeep, replacement access, and current source condition.

The signing price is proposed above general Hired Spearmen because the player purchases veteran formation quality and a better defensive equipment envelope. It remains below exceptional household or fully armored mercenary heavy foot unless later calibration shows otherwise.

The bargain consists of four parts:

1. Immediate access to a premium defensive company.
2. A real chance to solve one dangerous frontage problem.
3. Higher upkeep and more difficult replacement than ordinary hires.
4. Significant loss of strategic value if the company is spent in a careless assault.

Scarcity should make the offer interesting, not unusable. A broad source network can support more than one company, but severe losses cannot be repaired instantly by money.

## 23. Casualties, Equipment Damage, and Reconstitution

A company needs fit personnel, leaders, coherent spears, serviceable shields, sidearms, protection, pay, supply, and time. These elements may recover at different rates.

Shield and spear damage matter because they directly weaken the defining frontage. Headcount alone does not restore the unit if replacements lack equipment or formation practice.

Local Germania workshops may repair or replace compatible equipment where supported. Generic Germania population does not automatically become Prussian company personnel. Compatible recruits may join through source and mercenary networks without requiring an individual paperwork authority for every soldier.

Dismissal preserves actual losses and equipment condition. Rehire cannot refill the company. Captured, wounded, missing, dispersed, and veteran personnel return only through shared campaign outcome rules.

## 24. Era Development and Persistence

The candidate is High Era development. The Stainless Steel “after 1200” presentation remains a mod lead, not an adopted unlock date.

Emergence requires a supported historical and campaign context plus an actual available company. The calendar alone creates neither people nor equipment.

Later development may improve the consistency of helmets, textile protection, mail, shields, and leadership without converting the card into plate infantry or pikes. Existing companies may persist after recruitment conditions change.

No automatic progression into Teutonic troops, German Foot Knights, Swiss pikes, or later state infantry is permitted. Those identities require separate sources and designs.

## 25. Campaign AI

Recruit when a valuable objective, missile line, crossing, army hinge, or cavalry-threatened shoulder lacks reliable protection and ordinary Hired Spearmen do not solve the problem cheaply enough.

Do not recruit merely because the company has high defense. Armies expecting rapid pursuit, dispersed raiding, heavy siege assault, or missile-dominated warfare may need another card.

Retain experienced companies when future campaigns still require locked frontage. Release when the burden exceeds expected use, but preserve the one-company state, actual losses, and source recovery.

AI comparison should consider Hired Spearmen, Brabançon access, Foot Knights, domestic spears, and the army's current missile and cavalry mix without treating any one card as a hard exclusion.

## 26. Battle AI

The AI should select one valuable bounded sector, connect the shoulders, preserve a rear route, and close order only when actual pressure approaches. A strong Lock the Ground candidate should combine at least three of the following: meaningful objective value, a likely frontal threat, secure or terrain-supported shoulders, usable depth, nearby relief, a rear route, and limited concentrated missile exposure.

It should receive rather than chase, counterpress only after enemy momentum falls, and request or accept relief against fresh heavy assault. It should not form the defensive ring simply because cavalry exists somewhere on the field.

Withdrawal should begin before loss of both shoulders or the rear route. Under missile concentration, the AI should seek terrain, open spacing, support, attack the threat through another unit, or leave the position if the objective no longer justifies the attrition.

These are behavioral targets, not claims about existing implementation.

## 27. Siege and Settlement Portfolio

Prussian Spearmen are strongest after a gate, breach exit, courtyard, or wide street offers enough frontage to use shields and spears. They can secure access won by another formation and make a counterattack pay heavily.

They are not ideal ladder assault infantry. Narrow stairs erase depth, expose files, and prevent relief. Pushing them through every breach would waste the company.

In defense, the card excels at gate courts, inner approaches, wide streets, camp entrances, and missile protection. High-angle fire, fire, multiple alleys, roof access, and blocked withdrawal remain real counters.

No engineering, wall demolition, gate-breaking, or free garrison function is added.

## 28. Player UI and Compact Card Language

**Prussian Spearmen**  
*Veteran Shielded Spear Infantry*

Veteran mercenary spearmen with substantial shields, hired to lock a threatened sector and survive the enemy's first committed attack. Strong against frontal cavalry and ordinary infantry while formed, but expensive, slow to reface, and vulnerable to missiles, heavy axes, and flanking.

**Strong against:** frontal cavalry approaches, poor and ordinary infantry, bounded crossings, exposed missile approaches, attackers already losing momentum.

**Weak against:** concentrated crossbows, two-sided pressure, heavy axes and maces in compressed melee, repeated forced turns, deep pursuit, exhaustion.

**Use:** Occupy ground that matters, close the shield frontage, receive the attack, and counterpress only while both shoulders remain secure.

**Traits:** Substantial Shields; Veteran Spears; Lock the Ground; Prepared Reception; Finite Company; Difficult Replacements.

**Extended tooltip:** Prussian Spearmen are premium defensive mercenaries built around substantial shields, connected spear frontage, and veteran gap recovery. Lock a valuable sector before contact, punish a failed frontal attack with a short counterpress, and withdraw before missiles, fatigue, or a broken shoulder turn the dense formation into a trap.

**Flavor line:** The shield line closes. The ground stays bought.

## 29. Visual, Animation, and Audio Direction

Visual priorities are substantial but individually varied shields, coherent spears, helmets visible through most files, a mixed textile-and-mail protection band, practical clothing, disciplined spacing, and a company marker that does not imply Realm ownership.

Avoid Teutonic iconography by default, modern Prussian colors or symbols, uniform later kite or pavise shapes without research, plate armor, exaggerated spear lengths, and identical equipment across every model.

Animation priorities include supported movement, close ordering, shield adjustment, spear reception, front-file replacement, short connected thrusts, a controlled counterpress, emergency refacing, formation-ring transition, equipment loss, fatigue, relief, withdrawal, surrender, and rout.

Audio should emphasize ground and formation: close the shields, hold the shoulder, spears ready, keep the line, one step, halt, riders, turn left, open the passage, back in order. These are English design prompts, not historical-language claims.

## 33. Final Double-Checked Verdict
