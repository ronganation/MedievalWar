## 4. External research orientation

The present external review supports only broad orientation. General references describe ministeriales as a class associated with knightly, administrative, and official service in the High Medieval Empire; one surveyed account also places the emergence of a distinct servile ministerial class in the early eleventh century. These references do not establish a specific Royal Salian opening unit, pool, number, equipment package, commander, or service obligation for January 1, 1062. citeturn26search84turn26search86turn26search88

```yaml
external_orientation_disposition:
  MinisterialServiceAsResearchTheme: AdmittedForFurtherResearch
  MinisterialesAsExactOpeningRoyalUnit: RejectedWithoutSpecificEvidence
  PermanentRoyalStandingArmy: NotEstablished
  UnifiedImperialHost: Rejected
  Exact1062EquipmentAndNumbers: Unknown
  ExactRoyalVersusPrincelyPoolOwnership: Unknown
```

The Salian monarchy operated within a wider hierarchy of kingdoms, princes, clergy, nobles, and emerging ministerial service structures; this reinforces the need to model composite and conditional force sources rather than one uniform faction army. citeturn26search86

The following statuses describe design admission readiness, not exact opening availability.

| Capability family | Current status | Reason | Next evidence need |
|---|---|---|---|
| Royal command and protected core | Required, profile not yet admitted | The faction requires government and household protection | Source-specific household, escort, guard, command, and equipment research |
| Common spear-capable infantry | Candidate family | Basic line and defense capability is structurally necessary | Regional and source-specific evidence, pool ownership, equipment, and naming |
| Shielded general-purpose infantry | Candidate family | Composite hosts require sustainable line capability | Distinguish from levy, retained, regional, and service sources |
| Missile support | Candidate family | Roster requires screening and defensive support | Bow, crossbow, javelin, regional, date, and pool evidence |
| Mounted noble or service cavalry | Candidate family | Mounted service is a major potential force source | Free noble, ministerial, household, princely, and regional distinctions |

| Household protection force | Required, exact unit rejected for now | Function is established, exact profile is not | Avoid invented elite bodyguard; establish source, pool, equipment, and battle use |

| Local garrison and emergency defense | Candidate system role | Direct-base protection requires place-bound forces | Settlement, local polity, population, equipment, and command evidence |

## 8.2 Opening-capability anti-inference rules

- A required battlefield role does not prove a dedicated opening unit.
- A candidate family does not prove a recruitment pool.
- A historical social category does not prove uniform equipment or tactical use.
- A service obligation does not prove mobilization or arrival.
- A regional military tradition does not prove Royal Salian eligibility.
- A mercenary market does not prove local availability in Winter 1062.
- A later-era capability cannot be backfilled into the opening roster.
