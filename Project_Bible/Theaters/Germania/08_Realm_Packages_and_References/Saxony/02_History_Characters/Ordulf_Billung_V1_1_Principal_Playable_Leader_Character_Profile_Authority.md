## 1. Developed Profile Verdict

<Person>Ordulf Billung</Person> is the opening human face of Saxon Billung power: a duke whose title carries recognition but not automatic control, a dynast whose family network carries continuity but not obedience, and a northern regional ruler whose success depends on turning household reach, inherited standing, negotiated service, ecclesiastical pressure, comital rivalry, Royal politics, and frontier experience into a political order that can survive without pretending to own all Saxony.

**Player fantasy:** Hold a plural Saxon power network together through ducal presence, negotiated coalition, household continuity, selective coercion, frontier judgment, and political bargaining, while converting inherited Billung weight into durable capacity before conflict, absence, incapacity, succession, or overreach exposes how little is directly controlled.

**Central campaign question:** Can <Person>Ordulf Billung</Person> transform recognized ducal standing and Billung regional influence into a resilient political network without overclaiming Saxony, exhausting finite service sources, alienating church and comital powers, or leaving every function dependent on one ruler?

The profile must remain valid if <Person>Ordulf Billung</Person> reconciles with Hamburg-Bremen, escalates against it, builds cooperation with Stade, fails against a named Slavic opponent, avoids a war, survives beyond 1072 through divergent play, dies earlier through valid gameplay, strengthens <Person>Magnus Billung</Person>, chooses another continuity arrangement, or leaves a fragmented succession.

## 7. Core Character Thesis and Central Tension

<Person>Ordulf Billung</Person>'s defining strength is **recognized position across multiple political worlds**. He can speak as duke, Billung dynast, household principal, military candidate, Royal participant, ecclesiastical negotiator, northern dynastic connection, and frontier actor. His defining weakness is that every role depends on a different owner, obligation, audience, route, resource, and form of consent.

His central tension is **ducal standing versus executable regional power**. The more he treats title, dynasty, household, property, and regional influence as interchangeable, the more resistance, confusion, and overextension he creates. The more carefully he negotiates each layer, the slower and less spectacular consolidation may feel, but the more likely the political network is to survive absence, defeat, death, or succession.

## 13. Opening Strategic Function

<Person>Ordulf Billung</Person>'s opening function is to make a fragmented political field legible and actionable without erasing its owners.

He must:

1. determine what the ducal title can actually request, mediate, confirm, or contest;
2. distinguish direct Billung capacity from wider regional influence;
3. establish which counts, institutions, communities, and external actors will negotiate;
4. preserve household and dynastic continuity while meeting public burdens;
5. choose whether Royal, ecclesiastical, comital, northern, and frontier pressures are answered through presence, delegation, bargaining, force, delay, or refusal;
6. ensure that any success becomes transferable capacity rather than another personal dependency.

## 14. Opening Decision Portfolio

- **Clarify ducal competence.** Determine which requests, mediation, service, and recognition functions are supported instead of treating the title as universal authority.
- **Audit the Billung core.** Separate household, dynasty, title, direct property, patronage, service, and military capacity.
- **Choose an ecclesiastical posture.** Seek accommodation, evidence review, mediation, patronage, bounded pressure, or confrontation with specific institutions.
- **Choose a Stade posture.** Observe, negotiate, divide issues, coordinate regionally, compete for influence, or prepare for escalation.
- **Review northern connections.** Determine what information, hospitality, diplomacy, or dynastic access exists without presuming alliance or troops.
- **Review frontier exposure.** Identify named actors, routes, settlements, obligations, forces, supply, and political purpose before action.
- **Prepare government during absence.** Define household continuity, reporting, authority, military bounds, and emergency response.
- **Prepare <Person>Magnus Billung</Person> without appointing an heir.** Review age, education, household role, recognition, and differentiated experience.
- **Clarify <Person>Hermann Billung</Person>'s place.** Seek evidence, bounded cooperation, or separation without assuming loyalty or rivalry.
- **Build failure recovery.** Preserve reserve, routes, messengers, officers, service goodwill, negotiation options, and household continuity.

## 19. Command Alternatives and Absence Tradeoff

Personal command may improve authority visibility, coalition coherence, responsibility, and negotiation at the military center. It also exposes the package to capture, injury, household disruption, succession risk, and neglected regional relationships.

Divided command spreads risk but may weaken concentration. Refusal, delay, or demobilization may preserve the package while costing prestige or opportunity.

- **Ducal coalition builder:** turns recognition into repeatable consultation, mediation, and service without erasing owners.
- **Billung core consolidator:** clarifies direct property, household resources, offices, records, and continuity.
- **Northern political broker:** uses Wulfhild's connection and existing channels for diplomacy without false alliance.
- **Ecclesiastical accommodator:** resolves specific disputes while preserving institutional independence.
- **Frontier protector:** creates sustainable defense and named-actor diplomacy rather than permanent war.
- **Royal-Saxon mediator:** balances Royal service and regional autonomy.
- **Dynastic steward:** prepares Magnus and household continuity without scripting succession.

### Compromise and alternate paths

- accepts a narrower but executable ducal role;
- shares regional influence with counts or institutions;
- prioritizes direct Billung capacity over wider Saxon ambition;
- delegates field command while remaining strategic coordinator;
- trades prestige for peace, recovery, or institutional settlement.

### Failure and overreach paths

- title without execution;
- household exhaustion under public burden;
- ecclesiastical conflict that isolates the Billungs;
- comital coalition fracture;
- Royal opposition or punitive intervention;
- frontier campaign that consumes finite service without durable gain;
- premature Magnus elevation and succession conflict;
- personal-rule dependency that collapses on absence or death.

## 31. Failure, Recovery, Captivity, Incapacity, and Removal

### Defeat

After defeat, <Person>Ordulf Billung</Person> may preserve surviving forces, negotiate withdrawal, review command and service failures, secure routes, restore household and regional confidence, narrow the objective, or pursue reconciliation. Defeat need not produce reckless revenge or permanent disgrace.

### Captivity or isolation

Captivity triggers review of household authority, title execution, military command, negotiation, ransom, Royal involvement, <Person>Wulfhild of Norway</Person>'s role, <Person>Magnus Billung</Person>'s protection, and temporary regional coordination. No one person automatically inherits every function.

### Incapacity or prolonged absence

Owners must reassess access, correspondence, household direction, ducal representation, service requests, military command, property administration, and succession preparation.

### Death

A valid death ends personal agency but does not automatically transfer title, property, household leadership, forces, relationships, or the package. Historical 1072 does not override campaign state.

## 35. Player-Facing Presentation Contract

Present <Person>Ordulf Billung</Person> as an established but constrained duke whose importance lies in connecting political layers that do not automatically obey one another.

The player should understand:

- why his presence matters;
- which owner or audience is implicated;
- what authority is valid and what must be requested;
- what the action costs in household capacity, service, force, routes, supply, political goodwill, or continuity;
- what alternatives exist;
- what remains unknown;
- how refusal, failure, accommodation, or restraint changes future options;
- what institutions and relationships may survive him.

Do not present him as a generic Saxon warlord, a weak paperwork duke, an omnipotent feudal ruler, or a destined rebel against the Salian monarchy.
