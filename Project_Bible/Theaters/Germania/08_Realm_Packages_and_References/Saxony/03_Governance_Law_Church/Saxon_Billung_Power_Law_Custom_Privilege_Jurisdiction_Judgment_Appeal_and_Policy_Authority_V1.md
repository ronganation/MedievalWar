Saxon law is a plural, negotiated, and uneven legal order. Ducal standing, regional and local custom, household arrangements, comital jurisdictions, ecclesiastical competence, privileges, agreements, witnessed acts, political-order procedures, and practical enforcement may overlap without becoming one centralized code or universal ducal court.

## 12. Saxon Jurisdiction Families

The Saxon legal field contains overlapping but non-identical jurisdictions:

### 12.77 Ducal and title-specific

Bounded hearing, mediation, representation, service review, and judgment where validated competence exists.

### 12.222 Comital and lordship

Separate local, property, service, security, and dispute functions under their own owners.

### 12.347 Household and dynastic

Marriage, support, access, custody, education, dower, inheritance evidence, and internal arrangements through the proper owners.

### 12.512 Property and tenure

Possession, use, rent, rights, service, damage, transfer, and restitution questions without law owning the property.

### 12.662 Settlement and market

Local peace, exchange, tolls, weights, debt, lodging, transport, craft, and market disputes where evidence supports them.

### 12.819 Route, river, crossing, and frontier

Passage, protection, toll, escort, tribute, truce, and damage questions without asserting universal control.

### 12.978 Military service and command

Obligation basis, duration, geography, substitution, supply, release, compensation, and breach without law owning forces.

### 12.1142 Ecclesiastical

Canon-law and institution-specific competence retained by ecclesiastical owners.

### 12.1252 Royal and political order

Royal acts, recognition, service, consultation, and shared procedures retained by external owners.

### 12.1391 External and treaty

Safe conduct, passage, truce, foreign judgment, hostage exchange, and compact through reciprocal owner consent.

A single issue may involve several jurisdictions. Overlap does not automatically establish priority, appeal, or ducal control.

## 28. Ducal Peace, Protection, and Public Order

A candidate ducal-peace concept may support protection of routes, assemblies, households, markets, institutions, and settlements where competence, recognition, local cooperation, records, and enforcement exist.

It does not create universal criminal jurisdiction, a modern police power, or automatic pacification.

Possible approaches include direct hearing, mediation, sureties with safeguards, safe conduct, charter confirmation, compensation, bounded restraint, negotiated settlement, amnesty with guarantees, or deliberate abstention when authority or evidence is insufficient.

## 31. Ecclesiastical Jurisdiction Boundary

Ecclesiastical owners retain competence over institutions, church offices, internal discipline, marriage process, sanctuary, spiritual sanctions, burial, patronage, and institution-specific appeals where applicable.

Royal government, political-order participants, Denmark, Norway, named Slavic actors, counts, bishoprics, abbeys, and local entities retain their own law, consent, recognition, and reverse jurisdiction.

Saxon judgments, safe conducts, or settlements bind external actors only through accepted owner receipts.

## 33. War, Occupation, Submission, and Post-Conflict Legal Form

War remains available through the appropriate military and political owners. Law does not impose a claim gate. Claims and recognized standing may affect reactions, settlement terms, restoration, and historical memory without unlocking the attack command.

Possible post-conflict forms include temporary occupation, retained local administration, negotiated submission, client arrangement, property settlement, tribute or service agreement, truce without integration, restoration, or continuing resistance.

Every form requires explicit owners, scope, duration, jurisdiction, property access, garrison responsibilities, supply, justice, restitution, transition, and withdrawal.
