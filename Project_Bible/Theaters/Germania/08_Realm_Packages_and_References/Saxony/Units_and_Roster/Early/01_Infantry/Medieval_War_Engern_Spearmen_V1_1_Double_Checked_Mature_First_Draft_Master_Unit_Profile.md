### 1. Direct Unit Verdict

**Engern Spearmen are Saxony's dependable early territorial spear line: regionally gathered spear-and-shield infantry whose neighboring files, recognized local leaders, practical equipment, and understood obligation allow the formation to establish a real front, withstand ordinary pressure, and answer one damaged or overextended approach without pretending to be a household guard or permanent professional regiment.**

Their signature is **Muster, Set, Withstand, Answer**.

Their battlefield purchase is not merely “cheap anti-cavalry.” Their purchase is a body of ordinary but credible Saxon infantry that can become a stable point in a politically divided host. Once settled, Engern Spearmen give archers, axemen, mounted retainers, and the ducal household a line around which to act. Their value declines sharply when they are hurried into contact, stretched beyond local control, subjected to prolonged missiles, turned repeatedly, or ordered to pursue beyond their standard and neighboring files.

- **Class:** Territorial Saxon spear-and-shield infantry
- **Signature:** Muster, Set, Withstand, Answer
- **Battle promise:** Establish useful Saxon frontage, receive ordinary infantry or a readable mounted approach, preserve the center long enough for better troops to act, and deliver one short answer when the enemy loses order.
- **Best use:** Main early line, village and settlement approaches, roads, fords, field boundaries, archer protection, mixed-host junctions, and defensive sectors requiring more reliability than an emergency levy.

### 3. Player-Facing Description

Engern Spearmen are drawn from the territorial depth of Saxony's Angrian heartland. They are neither a crowd dragged from the fields nor a permanently maintained corps. The formation represents communities and lesser landholding networks capable of supplying enough fighting men, spears, shields, leaders, food, and accepted service to make a credible field line.

The unit becomes dangerous through order rather than individual superiority. When the files have time to gather around known neighbors, settle behind shields, identify an approach, and retain a clear standard or leader, the formation can withstand pressure that would break a hurried rural levy. That order is conditional. Missiles tear holes in the front, casualties remove the men who know how the local files fit together, flanking attacks turn shields and spears away from their purpose, and a proud pursuit can dissolve the unit's entire advantage.

The player's satisfaction comes from making the ordinary host matter. Engern Spearmen hold the center while forest bowmen work a shoulder, receive an enemy horse probe that expected an easy line, stabilize a junction between two Saxon contingents, or step forward together after attacking infantry lose order. They do not need elite kill counts to justify the card. They need to preserve the army relationship entrusted to them.

### 4. Why the Card Exists

Saxony needs a credible common infantry foundation before Billung household troops, noble cavalry, frontier specialists, and later ministerial formations can become meaningful. If the baseline is worthless, every stronger Saxon unit becomes mandatory. If the baseline is too strong, the faction's political and military development loses purpose.

### 9. Core Battle Loop: Muster, Set, Withstand, Answer

#### 9.1 Muster

The formation becomes available only when a real regional source provides eligible fighters, recognized local leaders, serviceable equipment, food, assembly, and an accepted purpose. A treasury balance or barracks cannot skip this step.

#### 9.2 Set

The files establish facing, depth, standards, neighboring relationships, and protected shoulders. Setting the line takes time and physical space. A moving or hurried unit possesses spears but not the complete card promise.

#### 9.3 Withstand

The formation receives ordinary infantry pressure or a readable mounted approach. It preserves enough order for Saxon missiles, axemen, cavalry, or retainers to operate. Withstanding is successful when the army relationship survives, not only when the enemy is destroyed.

#### 9.4 Answer

When a mounted probe recoils, ordinary infantry overextends, or Saxon missile pressure creates visible disorder, the spearmen can take one short forward step, restore ground, close a gap, or relieve a neighbor. The Answer must remain bounded and haltable.

#### 9.5 Halt and Redress

After the Answer, the formation stops, reconnects files, restores facing, and either resumes the line or accepts relief. Continued pursuit loses the unit's purchase.

#### Recruit when

- the army needs a dependable early center;
- bowmen, skirmishers, or regional axemen require a protected frontage;
- a mounted threat must be denied an easy approach;
- local providers can bear the loss of defense and labor;
- stronger retainers should be preserved for decisive contact;
- the campaign can support assembly, service, relief, and return.

#### Do not recruit when

- the army needs fast pursuit or aggressive flanking;
- selected armored infantry must be defeated head-on;
- the Engern source is already depleted or politically strained;
- heavy missile superiority makes a slow receiving line untenable;
- an emergency local levy would solve the mission at lower opportunity cost;
- a professional retainer or ministerial body is required for sustained high-pressure service.

### 43. Failure Stories

**The hurried muster:** the enemy arrives before neighboring files and leaders finish setting the front. The men possess spears and shields, but the card's actual promise never forms.

**The proud Answer:** the spearmen repel a mounted probe and pursue beyond standards, bow support, and local depth. Fresh infantry catches the elongated formation and turns success into regional loss.

**The empty home district:** the player repeatedly raises cheap Engern bodies because the treasury permits it. Local defense, leaders, equipment, labor, and future pool recovery collapse even though the recruitment button remains politically available.

**The arrow-worked line:** the player leaves Set Spears active under concentrated missiles. Shields reduce some exposure, but fatigue, casualties, damaged equipment, and leader loss destroy Muster Cohesion before contact.

**The false household unit:** inflated morale and armor allow Engern Spearmen to perform the same crisis work as retainers. The territorial roster foundation and the later Saxon military progression both fail.

### 59. Final Strong First-Draft Verdict

**Engern Spearmen pass as Saxony's first mature unit candidate.**

They give the faction an actual military foundation rather than a placeholder levy. The unit is broad enough to anchor an early army, reliable enough to make Saxony's infantry identity felt immediately, and limited enough that household retainers, Billung ducal troops, regional axemen, missile units, mounted service, ministerials, and later developed spear formations all retain meaningful space.

Their identity is neither “peasants with spears” nor “continental Saxon housecarls.” It is the territorial line produced when real Angrian communities, neighboring fighting groups, local leaders, practical equipment, recognized service, and ducal or comital coordination successfully converge. On the battlefield they **Muster, Set, Withstand, and Answer**. In campaign they consume and risk the same local capacity that keeps settlements, roads, households, and regional authority functioning.

The unit's key strength is **dependable ordinary usefulness**. The player can trust Engern Spearmen to solve a normal early battlefield problem when they receive time, facing, support, and a clear mission. The player cannot trust them to survive elite shock, concentrated missiles, open flanks, endless campaigning, or prideful pursuit.
