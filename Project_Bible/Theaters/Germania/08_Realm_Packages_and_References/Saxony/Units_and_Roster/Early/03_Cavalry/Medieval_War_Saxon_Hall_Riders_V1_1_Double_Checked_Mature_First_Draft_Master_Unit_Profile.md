### 1. Direct Unit Verdict

**Saxon Hall Riders are maintained mounted retainers furnished by Saxon halls, lordly households, estates, and comital followings: better mounted, better equipped, and more consistently led than Angrian Levy Horsemen, yet still intended to cover threatened ground, strike exposed opponents, and recover in order rather than imitate the decisive shock of noble knights.**

Their signature is **Attend, Cover, Strike, Recover**.

Hall Riders are the mounted counterpart to Saxon Hall Spearmen, but not a duplicate on horseback. Hall Spearmen stabilize and restore the infantry battle. Hall Riders attend the open flank, cover a withdrawal or movement, strike an exposed missile body, light cavalry formation, or compromised infantry shoulder, and recover before a harder counterattack. Their value is reliability under movement. Their cost is social and material concentration: selected mounted followers, suitable horses, remounts, tack, mail, shields, weapons, officers, maintenance, loyalty, and service.

### 2. One-Screen Total War Card

- **Unit:** Saxon Hall Riders
- **Class:** Maintained lordly mounted retainers
- **Signature:** Attend, Cover, Strike, Recover
- **Battle promise:** Reach the open sector that matters, cover friendly movement, defeat or drive off a suitable exposed target, and recover before elite cavalry or formed infantry traps the company.
- **Best use:** Flank reserve, bowmen protection, countering light cavalry, exploiting an infantry shoulder already fixed by Saxon foot, command escort, road and camp security, and controlled pursuit.
- **Strong against:** Light cavalry, scattered skirmishers, exposed archers and crews, routers, tired territorial horse, and ordinary infantry attacked from a compromised angle.
- **Conditional against:** Peer mounted retainers, medium cavalry, and unsupported ordinary infantry when horse quality, approach, officers, depth, and escape favor the Hall Riders.
- **Weak against:** Prepared spears, noble cavalry, knights, dense armored infantry, concentrated crossbows, rough enclosed terrain, and prolonged melee without relief.
- **Campaign bargain:** Finite mounted retainers, selected service horses, remounts, mail, helmets, shields, spears, swords, tack, officers, fodder, maintenance, loyalty, and hall support are concentrated into a reliable mounted company.
- **Player instruction:** Use Hall Riders where Angrian horsemen are too light and knights would be wasteful. Strike one suitable target, then recover the company for the next crisis.
- **Flavor:** **The territorial riders show Saxony where danger is moving. The Hall Riders are sent when danger must be met.**

### 3. Player-Facing Description

Saxon Hall Riders are drawn from lordly followings capable of maintaining both a fighting retainer and a serviceable mounted packet. Compared with Angrian Levy Horsemen, they possess stronger equipment, more consistent command, better horses on average, and greater willingness to accept controlled melee. Compared with noble cavalry and later knights, their social status, horseflesh, armor, lance practice, and shock responsibility remain lower and more varied.

Their battlefield identity is mobile reliability. They cover the open flank while lighter horsemen range farther out. They protect bowmen or a relieving infantry line. They strike enemy light horse, missiles, and exposed shoulders. Most importantly, they recover. A good Hall Rider company survives several useful interventions because officers control the limit of each action and preserve enough horses, riders, and order to rally again.

### 4. Why the Card Exists

Angrian Levy Horsemen already give Saxony reach, screening, messages, and pursuit. The roster still lacks a mounted body that can accept real but bounded contact. Jumping directly from levy horsemen to noble or knightly cavalry would flatten Saxony's social and military development.

#### 8.3 Engern Spearmen, Bowmen, and Angrian Axemen

Hall Riders protect open flanks, threaten enemy missiles, cover withdrawal, and exploit exposed shoulders. They do not replace territorial frontage, missile pressure, or close infantry assault.

#### 8.4 Billung Household Cavalry

A future Billung Household Cavalry family must own direct ducal attachment, command escort, exceptional loyalty, stronger equipment concentration, and much smaller availability. Hall Riders remain plural-provider cavalry.

Noble cavalry must own the mounted warrior status and personal horseflesh of free aristocratic service, with stronger shock and a narrower social pool. Hall Riders include maintained lordly followers without pretending every rider is noble.

#### 8.6 Saxon Ritter

Saxon Ritter must own mature knightly equipment, lance practice, castles and ministerial or noble providers, stronger shock, and later campaign development. Hall Riders do not become Ritter through veterancy alone.

### 9. Core Battle Loop: Attend, Cover, Strike, Recover

#### 9.1 Attend

Remain close enough to reach a threatened flank, open route, missile shoulder, command movement, or exposed enemy target.

#### 9.2 Cover

Protect friendly movement and deny enemy light cavalry, skirmishers, or exposed troops an uncontested opportunity.

#### 9.3 Strike

Commit against one suitable target: light cavalry, exposed missiles, crews, a compromised infantry shoulder, or a router group worth pursuing.

#### 9.4 Recover

Disengage, rally, restore sections, inspect horses and equipment, and preserve the company for another intervention.

#### 9.5 Hold or Reassign

Maintain the flank if the threat remains or move to a new sector only when horse condition, officers, routes, and support permit.

### 13. Formation Portfolio

#### 13.1 Hall Riding Line

Default flexible mounted formation for reserve movement, flank duty, and controlled contact.

#### 13.2 Covering Screen

Wider formation for protecting movement and denying light threats. More reliable than levy screening but less suited to immediate compact melee.

#### 13.3 Compact Intervention

Sections close for one bounded strike or countercharge. This is ordinary formation and attack behavior, not a shock ability.

#### 13.4 Controlled Pursuit

Part of the company follows a broken enemy while officers preserve a rally body and return relationship.

#### 13.5 Rally and Recover

The company breaks contact, reforms, checks horse and rider condition, and returns to reserve.

#### Recruit when

- the army needs a reliable mounted reserve above levy horse quality;
- enemy light cavalry or exposed missile troops must be defeated rather than merely observed;
- a valid hall can sustain riders, horses, officers, equipment, fodder, and remounts;
- the army needs mobile cover during relief, withdrawal, or command movement;
- noble and knightly cavalry would be unavailable, excessive, or wasteful.

#### Do not recruit when

- Angrian Levy Horsemen can solve the screening mission at lower cost;
- the army needs knightly shock against formed infantry;
- horses, remounts, fodder, officers, mail, or loyalty are thin;
- rough terrain removes recovery;
- personal guard, transport, breeding, or estate security must be preserved;
- the only plan is prolonged cavalry melee.

### 36. Failure Stories

**The cheap knights:** the player sees mail and better horses and charges formed spears. The company loses mounts, officers, and the difficult-to-replace provider packet.

**The empty hall:** riders and equipment still exist, but suitable horses and remounts have been exhausted by prior campaigns and estate needs.

**The divided mounted host:** several halls possess partial companies, yet competing standards, service terms, and rivalries prevent timely concentration.

**The stalled intervention:** Hall Riders defeat enemy light horse but remain in melee as fresh noble cavalry arrives. A successful cover action becomes a destroyed company.

**The stolen provider:** Hall Spearmen and Hall Riders are both raised from the same retainers, officers, and mail without single reservation. The roster duplicates people and equipment that exist only once.

#### Saxon Hall Riders

_Maintained lordly mounted retainers_

Saxon Hall Riders are better-mounted followers furnished by the halls and households of Saxon lords. More reliable in close action than Angrian Levy Horsemen, they cover threatened flanks, protect withdrawals, defeat exposed light cavalry or missile troops, and recover before heavier mounted forces can answer. ### 51. Final Double-Checked First-Draft Verdict

**Saxon Hall Riders pass as Saxony's sixth double-checked mature unit candidate and first maintained mounted-retainer formation.**

They are the correct next step after Saxon Hall Spearmen. The roster now develops vertically in both infantry and cavalry. Territorial formations remain the broad base. Hall formations add reliability, better equipment, stronger leadership, political cost, and campaign continuity. Future Billung, noble, ministerial, and knightly units still retain higher status and harder battlefield responsibilities.

The name is flavorful and structurally useful. **Saxon Hall Riders** gives the player an immediate sense of lordly mounted service without calling every rider a knight or assigning every provider to the Billung household. **Saxon Mounted Retainers** remains the controlled fallback if “Hall Riders” fails later review.
