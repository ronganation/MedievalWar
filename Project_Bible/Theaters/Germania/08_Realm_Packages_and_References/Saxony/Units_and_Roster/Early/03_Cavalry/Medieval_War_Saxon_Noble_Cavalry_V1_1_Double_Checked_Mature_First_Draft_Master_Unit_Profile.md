### 1. Direct Unit Verdict

**Saxon Noble Cavalry are the aristocratic mounted warriors of Saxony and the armed followers directly sustained by their high-status households: better mounted, more heavily equipped, and more willing to accept decisive shock than Hall or Billung Riders, capable of gathering behind the host, fixing one target and approach, delivering a powerful bounded charge, and reforming before pride turns victory into ruin.**

Their signature is **Gather, Fix, Charge, Reform**.

They are not yet the mature Saxon Ritter. Noble status, personal horseflesh, armor, weapons, household support, and mounted confidence give the formation the roster's first genuine high-impact cavalry charge, but not a universal answer to prepared spears, difficult ground, concentrated crossbows, disciplined knightly opposition, or repeated unsupported impact. The unit creates a decisive mounted option while preserving later institutional development.

### 2. One-Screen Total War Card

- **Unit:** Saxon Noble Cavalry
- **Class:** Aristocratic Saxon shock cavalry
- **Signature:** Gather, Fix, Charge, Reform
- **Battle promise:** Concentrate high-status mounted warriors against one suitable target, deliver the strongest current Saxon charge, survive the first contact, and reform before prepared resistance or fresh counterattack traps the company.
- **Best use:** Open flank, enemy cavalry already committed, compromised infantry shoulder, exposed household troops, decisive reserve commitment, high-value pursuit, and battlefield positions where one charge changes the local battle.
- **Strong against:** Light and medium cavalry, exposed missiles and crews, ordinary infantry attacked from a compromised angle, worked retainers, and routers worth destroying.
- **Conditional against:** Peer noble cavalry, selected household cavalry, armored infantry flanks, and medium-to-heavy cavalry when approach, horse quality, formation, support, fatigue, and reform space favor Saxony.
- **Weak against:** Prepared spears, deep braced infantry, mature knights, concentrated crossbows, marsh, dense woods, steep or broken approaches, blocked reform space, and prolonged melee.
- **Campaign bargain:** A narrow pool of aristocratic warriors, qualified mounted followers, superior horses, remounts, mail, helmets, shields, spears or lances, swords, officers, pages, grooms, farriers, fodder, income, ransom exposure, and political cooperation is concentrated into one expensive cavalry company.
- **Player instruction:** Do not use Noble Cavalry as scouts or routine escort. Create the angle, fix the target, charge once with purpose, and reform before a second problem reaches the company.
- **Flavor:** **The Hall Riders answer danger. The Billung Riders protect ducal movement. Saxon nobles ride when one charge must force the battle to change.**

### 3. Player-Facing Description

Saxon Noble Cavalry represent aristocratic mounted-warrior service rather than a standardized regiment or a universal class of fully developed knights. The card gathers nobles and qualified followers whose status, household wealth, horses, equipment, and military expectations permit harder mounted action than the broader Hall layer. Some riders may approach emerging knightly practice, but the company as a whole does not presume the later Ritter institution, uniform heavy lance doctrine, or castle-based professional continuity.

The formation wins through concentration and timing. Noble Cavalry gather behind an infantry or mounted screen, identify one flank, cavalry body, or worked formation whose defeat matters, and charge across a prepared approach. The first impact is powerful. The danger begins immediately afterward. Horses lose momentum, files mix, weapons break, proud riders pursue, and fresh spears or cavalry move against the disordered body. Reform is therefore part of the identity, not an afterthought.

### 9. Core Battle Loop: Gather, Fix, Charge, Reform

#### 9.1 Gather
Concentrate the noble riders, followers, officers, standards, and selected horses behind a protected approach or reserve position.

#### 9.2 Fix
Choose one target and one physical line of action. Friendly infantry, missiles, lighter horse, terrain, or enemy commitment must limit the target's ability to turn, evade, or countercharge cleanly.

#### 9.3 Charge
Deliver one decisive mounted impact against a suitable flank, cavalry body, exposed missile force, or compromised formation.

#### 9.4 Reform
Break off or pass through where possible, rally around standards and officers, restore rider-horse sections, and decide whether the company can charge again or must accept a reduced mission.

### 35. Failure Stories

**The first-target charge:** the player charges the first available infantry because the company is powerful. The target was not fixed, turns its spears, and destroys expensive horses and aristocratic leaders.

**The glorious pursuit:** the enemy line breaks, and noble riders chase beyond banners and support. Fresh cavalry catches the scattered company before reform.

**The false Ritter:** art and balance give the unit universal heavy lances, completed couching practice, uniform armor, and later warhorses. The Saxon Ritter lane disappears before it is created.

**The title-only company:** several nobles exist in the realm, but their suitable horses, followers, standards, officers, service agreements, and collective practice do not. Titles cannot fill a unit card.

**The divided nobility:** physical capacity exists across several households, but rivalry, prestige disputes, or conflicting obligations prevent a coherent charge body.

**The cheap countercharge:** the company meets mature knights frontally because both are cavalry. Better knightly alignment, equipment, and lance practice defeats the Saxon nobles.

**The hollow recovery:** rider numbers return, but selected horses, standards, officers, armor, collective reform practice, ransoms, and provider confidence remain damaged.
