### 1. Executive Unit Identity

Saxon Armored Axemen are High-Era professional non-knightly assault infantry maintained by developed Saxon castles, comital establishments, ministerial service networks, and selected mature regional providers. They carry one-handed axes and strong shields, wear armor adequate for sustained close contact, and operate under officers capable of controlling approach, commitment, local pressure, security, relief, and recovery.

Their battlefield identity is **sustained objective fighting**. Angrian Axemen exploit a short supported opening through regional aggression and local weapon tradition. Saxon Armored Axemen are organized to remain at the hard point after the first collision. They can contest a gate, street, bridgehead, breach shoulder, defended road, shielded infantry sector, or local objective long enough for the wider army to exploit the result.

They are not berserkers, household champions, dismounted knights, or a generic armor-piercing solution. Their strength comes from disciplined axe-and-shield work, practical armor, professional officers, developed supply, and a formation trained to accept relief rather than destroy itself in uncontrolled pursuit. Their offensive ceiling sits above ordinary regional infantry but below elite knightly assault troops.

### 3. Historical-Design Interpretation

The profile does not claim that medieval Saxony maintained one universally standardized regiment officially titled “Armored Axemen.” The display name is a clear gameplay classification for developed professional infantry whose primary battlefield identity combines armor, shield, axe, repeated service, and controlled assault responsibility.

The formation may draw from several historically plausible service relationships: castle-service infantry, ministerial military establishments, comital and lordly retainers, selected regional axemen retained for longer service, and paid or institutionally sustained soldiers. The shared battlefield standard matters more than identical legal status.

This approach follows Medieval War's middle-ground design. Social and provider differences remain meaningful, but the unit stays readable as a Total War formation rather than becoming a character-level simulation of every soldier's legal category.

### 16. Weapons and Combat Method

The protected identity is one-handed axe and strong shield. Axes deliver concentrated pressure against shields, limbs, helmets, and armor weak points without granting magical universal armor penetration.

```yaml
primary_weapon: One-handed battle axe or service axe
shield: Strong round, kite-derived, transitional, or heater form according to chronology and provider
secondary_weapon_mix:
  - Long knife or seax
  - Arming sword in limited numbers
  - Mace among selected mature providers
  - Hand axe variation within approved silhouette
excluded_core_identity:
  - Universal two-handed axe
  - Poleaxe formation
  - Dane-axe guard imitation
  - Berserker weapon style
  - Throwing axe as primary doctrine
```

Combat emphasizes shield protection, short controlled axe work, local pressure, file support, and officer-directed advance. Wide reckless swings should be rare. The formation needs enough space to work axes but enough density to preserve shields and mutual support.

### 16A. Axe Interaction Boundary

The axe is valuable because its mass and edge can pressure shields, exposed limbs, helmets, mail-covered targets, and imperfect armor at close range. That does not justify a universal armor-piercing or shield-destruction rule. Outcomes depend on weapon quality, angle, target equipment, fatigue, formation spacing, and repeated contact.

```yaml
axe_advantages:
  - Strong close strikes against ordinary shields and protected infantry
  - Useful hooking and control opportunities where animation and collision support them
  - Credible pressure against mail and helmets through concentrated impact
  - Distinct offensive identity from the spear line
axe_limits:
  - Shorter reach than spears and many polearms
  - No automatic shield destruction
  - No automatic armor penetration
  - Difficult weapon spacing in excessive density
  - Substantial fatigue during prolonged assault
  - Vulnerable approach without shields and support
forbidden_balance_shortcuts:
  - Flat bonus against every armored target
  - Guaranteed shield removal
  - Fantasy cleaving through multiple opponents
  - Two-handed behavior applied to one-handed axes
```

### 23. Strengths

- Sustained offensive performance above regional axemen.
- Strong protected approach through shields and armor.
- Good pressure against ordinary shielded and armored infantry.
- Useful in gates, streets, bridgeheads, breach shoulders, and defended roads.
- More available than Dismounted Ritter.
- Better objective security than short-commitment regional assault troops.
- Strong partnership with Armored Sergeants and Castle Crossbowmen.
- Credible professional morale and recovery.
- Distinct High-Era offensive identity without knightly status.
- Can absorb ordinary hard assault work that would waste elite household or knightly troops.

### 24. Weaknesses and Counterplay

- Expensive armor, shields, axes, officers, and maintenance.
- Slower and less regionally available than Angrian Axemen.
- Poor dedicated response to heavy cavalry.
- Vulnerable to deep spears, polearms, elite swordsmen, and knightly infantry.
- Concentrated crossbows and heavy archery can disrupt the approach.
- Fatigue accumulates during sustained assault.
- Limited pursuit and operational speed.
- Flank and rear attacks degrade shield protection.
- Provider rebellion or armory loss can sharply reduce availability.
- Not efficient as routine garrison frontage.
- Every hard assault consumes equipment and experienced personnel.
- Cannot break every shielded target through axe identity alone.

### 26. Combined-Arms Relationships

#### Saxon Armored Sergeants

Sergeants hold frontage and receive cavalry. Armored Axemen attack the chosen hard sector. Sergeants then relieve and secure the gained line.

#### Saxon Castle Crossbowmen

Crossbowmen weaken armor, shields, and morale before the assault. Armored Axemen protect the missile investment by exploiting the disruption rather than forcing crossbow crews into close combat.

#### Westphalian Javelinmen

Javelinmen deliver short-range disruption and withdraw. Armored Axemen enter the resulting disorder if the target and terrain justify it.

#### Engern Woodland Bowmen

Woodland Bowmen harass, shape, and screen approaches. Armored Axemen remain dependent on formed support outside favorable terrain.

#### Saxon Ministerial Lancers and Ritter

Cavalry fixes flanks, countercharges enemy horse, or exploits the objective after infantry pressure succeeds. Armored Axemen should not chase beyond support.

#### Dismounted Ritter

Armored Axemen perform the ordinary hard assault. Dismounted Ritter remain available for the decisive elite point.

### 27. Open-Field Use

In open battle, Armored Axemen should rarely form the whole center. They deploy behind or beside spear frontage, near a target selected for offensive pressure. Missile infantry, terrain, and cavalry must help establish the approach.

A successful pattern is: Armored Sergeants fix the enemy, Castle Crossbowmen or regional missiles weaken the sector, Armored Axemen advance under shield, work the local fight, and friendly line infantry secure the ground. A failed pattern is an unsupported frontal march across open ground into concentrated missiles and fresh spears.

### Appendix G. Assault Doctrine Detail

#### Protected Advance

The formation closes with shields presented, axes held ready but controlled, and files maintaining enough room for weapon use. Speed is deliberately reduced. Sudden turns, steep slopes, obstacles, and missile impacts can disrupt the order. Protected Advance is therefore a behavioral posture, not a universal missile-resistance bonus.

#### Sustained Assault

On contact, the unit attempts to maintain local pressure through shield engagement, short axe work, file support, and officer direction. The doctrine is strongest against ordinary infantry already fixed or fatigued. It becomes costly against deep fresh formations, elite troops, or unsupported flanks. Fatigue and equipment damage grow throughout the assault.

#### Secure Objective

After gaining ground, the formation stops uncontrolled pursuit, reforms shields, protects officers and standards, and creates space for relief. Secure Objective reduces offensive momentum in exchange for local stability. The order should not instantly restore formation or morale.

#### Accept Relief

A friendly unit approaches through a usable route while the axemen preserve enough order to withdraw. Relief can fail if the enemy presses too closely, lanes are blocked, officers are lost, or both formations become disordered. Successful relief preserves veterans and permits later recommitment.

```yaml
assault_doctrine_safeguards:
  - No posture grants immunity
  - Every action consumes time and stamina
  - Relief requires a real friendly formation and lane
  - Objective security competes with pursuit
  - Recommitment requires recovery
  - Officer control materially affects every phase
```

### Appendix H. Future Naming and Regionalization Boundary

The current title remains broad enough for a realm-level professional family, but later implementation may reveal provider-specific visual sets or regional variants. Those differences should normally remain inside one unit family unless a genuine recruitment, battlefield, and campaign distinction develops.

A provider from Angria may preserve regional shield devices and axe forms. A ducal castle may show stronger equipment consistency. A comital provider may use different cloth colors or standards. An ecclesiastical provider, if authorized, may carry restrained institutional symbols. These variations do not automatically justify Angrian Armored Axemen, Billung Armored Axemen, Episcopal Axemen, and Castle Axemen as separate cards.

```yaml
variant_remains_within_base_family_when:
  - Battlefield role is unchanged
  - Pool and provider requirements substantially overlap
  - Difference is mainly color, shield device, helmet mix, or equipment quality
separate_variant_requires:
  - Distinct provider and pool
  - Distinct battlefield loop
  - Distinct campaign opportunity cost
  - Distinct progression or emergence logic
  - Clear player-facing reason to occupy another roster slot
```
