### 1. Executive Unit Identity

Saxon Retinue Archers are High-Era maintained bow infantry drawn from developed halls, castles, comital establishments, towns, ministerial service networks, and separately validated household or ducal archer packets. They are selected for longer service, supplied with more compatible bows and arrows, organized under enduring officers, and expected to remain useful throughout a field campaign rather than assemble only for shorter regional obligations.

Their signature is **Establish, Sustain, Shift, Preserve**. The formation establishes a protected shooting lane, sustains disciplined bow pressure against worthwhile targets, shifts by sections as the army frontage changes, and preserves ammunition, officers, bows, and trained shooters for later phases and future battles.

They are not English longbowmen, elite woodland rangers, bow-armed household guards, or crossbowmen using a faster weapon. Their strength comes from service continuity, field discipline, compatible equipment, officers, ammunition management, and professional integration with Saxony's developed armies.

### 17. Core Battle Loop

#### Establish Lane

Choose a shooting sector with visibility, safe arcs, accessible arrows, and a transfer or withdrawal path. Officers arrange sections and target priorities.

#### Sustain Fire

Maintain controlled shooting against targets worth the ammunition. Rate, accuracy, and target concentration decline with fatigue, weather, officer loss, and poor lanes.

#### Shift

Move by sections to a better lane, counter enemy missiles, support a changing frontage, or avoid cavalry and close assault. Shifting interrupts fire and can fragment the formation.

#### Preserve

Conserve arrows, bows, strings, officers, and trained shooters. Withdraw or reduce the mission before the unit becomes trapped or wastes its campaign value.

```yaml
success: Sustained useful pressure while preserving enough order and ammunition for later phases
failure: Wasteful shooting, missile defeat, cavalry interception, blocked shift, melee contact, or ammunition collapse
```

### 17A. Fire-Mission and Shift-State Control

```yaml
fire_mission_states:

    purpose: Sustain normal professional pressure
  concentrated_work:

    purpose: Contest enemy ranged troops when the exchange is favorable

  - New position lacks safe arcs or withdrawal
  - Missile pressure fragments moving sections
```

An aborted shift costs time, cohesion, and fire pressure. It does not teleport the unit back into a perfect formation. The player chooses the new lane; professional training governs execution.

### 25. Strengths

- Sustained disciplined bow fire above the regional ceiling.
- Better officer and section continuity than Engern Bowmen.
- Better missile-duel capability against ordinary ranged troops.
- More reliable campaign service away from home providers.
- Better ammunition handling and lane transfer.
- More mobile and higher-rate than Castle Crossbowmen.
- Useful support for Armored Sergeants, Armored Axemen, and cavalry.
- Professional but not elite, allowing meaningful field presence.
- Clear High-Era bow identity without longbow imitation.
- Persistent value in sieges, settlements, and field armies.

### 26. Weaknesses and Counterplay

- Higher cost and narrower access than Engern Bowmen.
- Weak in melee.
- Vulnerable to fast cavalry and flank attack.
- Limited armor pressure compared with crossbows.
- Ammunition-intensive over prolonged battles.
- Officer and arrow losses slow recovery.
- Movement interrupts sustained fire.
- Cannot match specialist longbow or elite archer cultures at their own strengths.
- Weather, smoke, darkness, and obstruction degrade output.
- Maintained service competes with regional defense and other professional troops.
- No automatic fire arrows or armor-piercing arrows.
- Poor use in dense woodland without real lanes.

### 34. AI Battlefield Use

The AI should establish a protected lane, choose targets by value, sustain fire while support remains, shift before the lane collapses, and preserve ammunition. It should avoid wasteful heavy-armor shooting, exposed cavalry flanks, friendly obstruction, and voluntary melee.

AI should distinguish Retinue Archers from Engern Bowmen by holding the maintained lane longer and transferring more deliberately, not by refusing withdrawal.

### 34A. AI Target-Economy and Withdrawal Gates

```yaml
ai_fire_priority:

  - Friendly melee blocks the lane
  - Heavy shields and armor make the exchange wasteful

```

The AI should not remain in a losing exchange merely because the unit is professional. Preservation is part of the unit's doctrine.

### Appendix F. Section-Shift Doctrine Detail

Section Shift is the unit's strongest behavioral distinction and its greatest implementation risk. It represents controlled transfer of part or all of the formation between shooting lanes, not fire while moving and not an instant reposition ability.

```yaml
shift_phases:
  assess:
    purpose: Identify the better lane, threat, and route
  cease_or_reduce:
    purpose: Prevent disorder and friendly risk during movement
  move_sections:
    purpose: Transfer selected sections through physical space
  establish:
    purpose: Rebuild spacing, arrows, target calls, and flank awareness
  resume:
    purpose: Return to useful shooting after real preparation
shift_costs:
  - Reduced fire volume
  - Temporary loss of target pressure
  - Formation separation risk
  - Ammunition and equipment handling burden
  - Exposure to cavalry and enemy missiles
shift_failure_conditions:
  - Route blocked
  - Officer or signal loss
  - Enemy closes during transfer
  - Friendly congestion
  - Terrain fragments sections
  - New lane lacks visibility or withdrawal
shift_does_not_grant:
  - Fire while sprinting
  - Instant formation restoration
  - Friendly phasing
  - Concealment
  - Free escape from cavalry
```

The player should decide when a lane is no longer worth holding. The unit's training determines how effectively that decision is executed. Ordinary movement and formation controls should carry most of the behavior.
