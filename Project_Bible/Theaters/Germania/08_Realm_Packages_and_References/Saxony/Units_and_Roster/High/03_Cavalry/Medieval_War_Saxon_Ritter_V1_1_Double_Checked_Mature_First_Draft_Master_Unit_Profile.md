### 1. Direct Unit Verdict

**Saxon Ritter are Saxony's mature knightly cavalry family: mounted warriors and qualified household or ministerial followers whose social service, warhorse threshold, armor, lance practice, officers, castles or maintained providers, and campaign support have converged into a more consistent shock formation than the earlier Noble Cavalry.**

Their signature is **Equip, Align, Couch, Recover**.

The Ritter do not invalidate Saxon Noble Cavalry. Noble Cavalry remain the broader coalition of aristocratic mounted warriors, with more varied equipment and lower institutional thresholds. Ritter represent the narrower body that has crossed into mature knightly service and repeated lance-shock practice. Their charge is stronger and more consistent, but their providers, horses, armor, officers, and recovery are even more demanding.

### 2. One-Screen Total War Card

- **Unit:** Saxon Ritter
- **Class:** Mature Saxon knightly shock cavalry
- **Signature:** Equip, Align, Couch, Recover
- **Battle promise:** Concentrate a qualified knightly packet, align a disciplined lance approach against a fixed target, deliver Saxony's mature cavalry shock, and recover the expensive rider-horse formation before prepared resistance or prolonged melee destroys it.
- **Best use:** Decisive flank charge, countercharge against noble cavalry, compromised elite infantry shoulder, command reserve, and a second charge only after real reform.
- **Strong against:** Light and medium cavalry, Noble Cavalry caught at disadvantage, exposed missiles, ordinary or selected infantry attacked from a compromised angle, and targets already fixed by Saxon combined arms.
- **Conditional against:** Peer knights, heavy household cavalry, and deep armored infantry flanks when approach, warhorses, lance alignment, support, fatigue, and recovery space favor Saxony.
- **Weak against:** Prepared spears, deep braced formations, concentrated crossbows, superior knightly countercharge, ditches, stakes, mud, dense woods, blocked breakoff, and prolonged melee.
- **Campaign bargain:** A narrow pool of knightly-status riders and qualified followers, warworthy horses and remounts, heavier armor, lances, swords, officers, castle or household support, grooms, farriers, fodder, income, ransom capacity, and political access is concentrated into one exceptionally costly company.
- **Player instruction:** Ritter are not a permanent melee solution. Align the lane, charge the fixed target, then recover the formation for another decision.
- **Flavor:** **Noble Cavalry make aristocratic shock available. The Ritter make it repeatable, disciplined, and ruinously expensive to lose.**

### 3. Player-Facing Description

Saxon Ritter represent the mature knightly layer that develops from, alongside, and sometimes outside Saxony's broader Noble Cavalry. The card requires more than noble birth, battlefield experience, or ownership of a horse. Knightly service demands a validated provider relationship, a rider and horse meeting the higher shock threshold, suitable armor and lance equipment, officers capable of alignment and breakoff, repeated collective practice, and sustained household, castle, ministerial, or lordly support where historically appropriate.

On the battlefield, Ritter win through alignment and disciplined impact. The company equips and checks the complete packet, aligns sections behind a clear lane, couches or otherwise delivers a mature lance shock where the historical and equipment review supports it, then recovers before momentum becomes entanglement. The company can deliver a second meaningful charge only if officers, riders, horses, weapons, route, and time permit genuine reform.

### 4. Why the Card Exists

Saxon Noble Cavalry already provides broad aristocratic shock. Ritter survive as a separate card only if they purchase something more specific than higher charge statistics: mature knightly provider qualification, stronger lance alignment, a higher warhorse and equipment threshold, and more reliable campaign continuity through validated household, castle, ministerial, ducal, comital, ecclesiastical, or lordly institutions.

### 9. Core Battle Loop: Equip, Align, Couch, Recover

#### 9.1 Equip
Verify the rider, warhorse, remount relationship, armor, lance, shield, sidearm, tack, officers, standards, grooms, and campaign support required for the knightly role.

#### 9.2 Align
Concentrate sections behind a physical charge lane, synchronize pace and direction, and ensure the target is fixed or compromised enough that the formation can maintain order into impact.

#### 9.3 Couch
Deliver the mature knightly lance shock, or the closest historically supported equivalent, through ordinary Total War movement, formation, and attack systems.

#### 9.4 Recover
Pass through or break off, rally around officers and standards, replace broken lances where stocks permit, check horses and riders, and restore enough order for a reduced mission or another charge.

### 35. Failure Stories

**The castle-made knights:** a castle or research unlock is treated as if it manufactures qualified riders, warhorses, armor, lances, officers, and collective practice. The company exists on paper but fails every physical recruitment gate.

**The first-lane charge:** the player sees the strongest Saxon cavalry and charges immediately. Friendly troops congest the approach, the target turns its spears, and the Ritter lose alignment before impact.

**The permanent shock bonus:** the charge lands, but the game allows first-impact power to continue throughout melee. The Ritter erase infantry and cavalry distinctions instead of needing breakoff and recovery.

**The false uniformity:** every provider receives identical legal status, armor, warhorses, couching style, and castle support. Saxony's plural noble, ministerial, ecclesiastical, ducal, and lordly development paths disappear.

**The obsolete Noble Cavalry:** Ritter become a direct statistical upgrade available from the same providers. The broader aristocratic unit loses its earlier access, flexible equipment, coalition role, and lower threshold.

**The empty lance store:** riders and horses survive, but broken lances, damaged tack, exhausted remounts, and missing grooms prevent another true knightly charge.

**The second charge illusion:** the player orders another immediate impact without physical reform. Scattered sections and blown horses arrive piecemeal and are defeated.

**The institutional collapse:** casualties are replaced numerically, but officers, standards, castle or household support, provider trust, and collective knightly practice remain damaged.
