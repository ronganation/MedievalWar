## 1. Authority Purpose

This authority defines the faction-level proposition for the Swabian Rheinfelden package. It converts the accepted candidate, source inventory, physical package root, initial foundation, and mature Rudolf profile into a coherent playable Realm design without replacing the future specialist owners for title, governance, dynasty, household, territory, property, population, economy, military, diplomacy, public order, institutions, historical memory, presentation, or Opening State.

It owns the faction-level answers to these questions:

- What is the playable Swabian project?
- Why is the package distinct from Bavaria, Saxony, Bohemia, Royal Salian government, Burgundy, Italia, and the Lower Rhine?
- What does the player protect, build, negotiate, risk, and transform?
- How do ducal office, Rheinfelden patrimony, regional owners, Burgundy administration, Alpine connections, and Royal service interact?
- What counts as success, failure, recovery, fragmentation, and continuation?
- Which content and specialist authorities are substantively required next?

## 4. Realm Thesis

Build a durable southwestern ducal power by converting Rudolf's appointed Swabian office, the Rheinfelden patrimonial base, negotiated relationships with regional owners, Royal and political-order standing, and a Burgundian administrative mandate into executable capacity without pretending that Swabia is a centralized state or that Burgundy belongs to the package.

The faction is strongest when office, patrimony, household, regional relationships, routes, records, service, military contribution, ecclesiastical settlement, and external mandates reinforce one another. The faction is weakest when title outruns recognition, patrimony is exhausted to simulate public authority, Burgundy drains attention without delivering legitimate capacity, or Rudolf's personal reach substitutes for transferable institutions.

## 5. Player Fantasy

The player acts as the strategic steward of an appointed ducal network anchored by Rheinfelden power rather than the administrator of a uniform territorial duchy.

The fantasy is to:

- turn a prestigious appointment into issue-specific, recognized, executable ducal authority;
- protect and strengthen the Rheinfelden household, dynasty, property, routes, and reserves;
- negotiate cooperation from counts, bishops, abbeys, communities, crossings, and other owners without erasing them;
- perform, renegotiate, narrow, or relinquish a Burgundian mandate without copying Burgundian private state;
- balance Royal service, political-order participation, regional autonomy, and dynastic continuity;
- use High Rhine, Upper Rhine, Black Forest, Lake Constance, Alpine, Burgundian, and Italia-facing connections as strategic opportunities and liabilities;
- assemble finite military capacity from actual owners, communities, equipment, horses, officers, and supply;
- survive defeat, office challenge, mandate loss, household weakness, personal absence, succession stress, and political reversal;
- pursue loyal service, mediation, regional consolidation, qualified autonomy, or conditional rival kingship without guaranteed history.

## 6. Central Campaign Question

Can an appointed duke with a real but incomplete patrimonial base turn Swabian standing and cross-regional reach into a durable political order before overextension, appointment dependence, regional refusal, household weakness, or extraordinary ambition expose the difference between title and executable power?

## 11. Opening Strategic Situation

The static foundation supports a living, experienced duke and a distinct playable coordination project, but not a centralized Swabian state.

The opening situation is shaped by:

- several years of Rudolf's ducal formation since 1057;
- a patrimonial Rheinfelden base whose exact property and capacity remain unresolved;
- regional counts, dynasties, bishops, abbeys, settlements, routes, and communities with independent rights and agency;
- a Burgundian mandate that may strengthen standing or produce dangerous overextension;
- Royal Salian appointment and service relationships without predetermined loyalty or opposition;
- Matilda's death and unresolved Adelaide marriage timing;
- unknown household, dynasty, succession, officer, resource, military, and administrative state;
- proximity to Bavaria, Carinthia, Burgundy, Italia, Alsace, and political-order corridors.

## 13. Success Is Not Territorial Completion

Valid success can include:

- a smaller but reliable Rheinfelden-centered power base;
- recognized issue-specific ducal competence;
- a durable coalition of independent regional owners;
- stable ecclesiastical settlements and record-backed jurisdiction;
- reliable routes, communication, crossings, hospitality, and Alpine access;
- sustainable Royal service that does not exhaust local capacity;
- successful Burgundy mandate performance without annexation;
- finite military readiness with recovery and replacement;
- a household and dynasty capable of surviving Rudolf's absence or death;
- qualified regional autonomy without permanent conflict;
- refusal or nonoccurrence of rival kingship when conditions do not justify it.

## 14. Failure Is Not Automatic Campaign Loss

The faction can continue after:

- refused recognition;
- failed Royal service;
- Burgundy mandate loss or return;
- regional-owner opposition;
- military defeat or withdrawal;
- patrimonial depletion;
- ecclesiastical conflict;
- household displacement;
- Rudolf's capture, incapacity, office loss, or death;
- disputed succession;
- failed or withdrawn rival-kingship candidacy;
- regional fragmentation and strategic retrenchment.

Failure must preserve losses, surviving capacity, grievances, relationships, memory, and recovery choices.

## 16. Faction-Level Decision Portfolio

- **Define the Swabian project.** Favor a reliable Rheinfelden core, a broad coalition, issue-specific ducal competence, Royal service, mediation, external mandate performance, or selective autonomy.
- **Audit actual capacity.** Determine household, patrimonial, property, revenue, route, institutional, population, supply, and service realities before promising action.
- **Build a regional consultation order.** Choose which counts, bishops, abbeys, communities, crossings, and route owners receive attention first.
- **Clarify the ducal office.** Separate title, competence, recognition, property, revenue, service, military request, and jurisdiction.
- **Manage the Burgundy mandate.** Personal supervision, bounded delegation, owner coordination, clarification, renegotiation, narrowing, withdrawal, or return.
- **Choose a Royal posture.** Loyal service, qualified service, burden negotiation, mediation, delay, refusal, reconciliation, or conditional opposition.
- **Develop the Rheinfelden base.** Consolidate, reserve, document, fortify, patronize, delegate, or diversify without turning patrimony into all Swabia.
- **Choose an ecclesiastical strategy.** Protect, patronize, negotiate, mediate, litigate, pressure, accommodate, or withdraw issue by issue.
- **Prepare military alternatives.** Personal command, delegated command, coalition force, home reserve, deterrence, withdrawal, or nonparticipation.
- **Protect continuity.** Build household, dynasty, office, mandate, record, delegate, and succession capacity beyond Rudolf.
