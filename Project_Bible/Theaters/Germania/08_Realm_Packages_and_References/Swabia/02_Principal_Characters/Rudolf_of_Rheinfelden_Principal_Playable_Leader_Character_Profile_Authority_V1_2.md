## 1. Developed Profile Verdict

Rudolf of Rheinfelden is the opening human face of the Swabian campaign: an appointed duke whose title gives recognized standing but not uniform command, a Rheinfelden lord whose patrimonial base can sustain action but not the whole duchy, and a Burgundian administrator whose external mandate widens opportunity while multiplying dependence. Rudolf begins with an unusually broad political reach across Swabian, Rheinfelden, Royal Salian, Burgundian, ecclesiastical, comital, and Alpine fields, but every field answers to different owners, rights, routes, obligations, and audiences.

```yaml
PlayerFantasy: ConvertAnAppointedDucalOfficeAndRheinfeldenPowerBaseIntoDurableSwabianLeadershipThroughRecognitionNegotiatedServiceRegionalSettlementAndSelectiveCommandWithoutPretendingToOwnTheDuchyOrBurgundy
CentralCampaignQuestion: CanRudolfTurnDucalAppointmentPatrimonialWeightAndCrossRegionalMandatesIntoAResilientPoliticalOrderBeforeOverreachRoyalConflictHouseholdWeaknessOrSuccessionExposeTheDifferenceBetweenTitleAndExecutablePower
DefiningStrength: AbilityToOperateAcrossSeveralPoliticalWorldsAndTranslateBetweenOfficePatrimonyRegionalOwnersAndRoyalExpectations
DefiningWeakness: NoSingleRoleSuppliesTheConsentResourcesPeopleInformationOrContinuityNeededToExecuteTheOthers
```

The profile remains valid if Rudolf remains loyal to the Royal Salian government, negotiates qualified autonomy, loses or relinquishes Burgundy responsibilities, reconciles with competing regional powers, never becomes rival king, becomes a failed candidate, suffers defeat or captivity, loses ducal recognition, dies before 1077, survives beyond 1080 through divergent play, develops a different succession, or leaves the Swabian field fragmented.

Rudolf can act as a person, dynast, household principal, patrimonial lord, appointed duke, Burgundian administrator, Royal participant, ecclesiastical negotiator, and conditional commander. ## 13. Opening Strategic Function

Rudolf's opening function is to determine whether the Swabian ducal office can become executable without erasing the region's owners or exhausting the Rheinfelden base.

Rudolf must:

- identify what the ducal office can actually request, confirm, mediate, protect, or contest;
- separate Rheinfelden household, patrimony, ducal office, and Burgundy mandate;
- determine which counts, bishops, abbeys, settlements, crossings, and external owners will negotiate;
- allocate personal presence between Swabian, Rheinfelden, Royal, and Burgundian responsibilities;
- decide when Royal service strengthens recognition and when it creates dangerous dependence;
- preserve continuity if Rudolf travels, commands, loses office, becomes captive, is injured, or dies;
- ensure that success becomes transferable capacity instead of personal overextension.

## 14. Opening Decision Portfolio

- **Clarify ducal competence.** Establish issue-specific authority rather than treating the title as unlimited jurisdiction.
- **Audit the Rheinfelden core.** Separate household resources, property, tolls, routes, fortifications, service, and reserves.
- **Choose a regional consultation sequence.** Prioritize specific comital, ecclesiastical, monastic, settlement, or route owners without claiming universal consent.
- **Define Burgundy performance.** Clarify mandate, information, travel, delegates, obligations, costs, and return conditions.
- **Choose a Royal posture.** Cooperate, condition service, seek clarification, negotiate burden, mediate, delay, refuse, or abstain where valid.
- **Resolve household continuity.** Determine how Rudolf's absence, widowhood, uncertain remarriage, dependents, officers, and succession exposure are managed.
- **Choose an ecclesiastical posture.** Verify property and jurisdiction, seek mediation, patronize, protect, litigate, pressure, accommodate, or reconcile through specific owners.
- **Prepare command alternatives.** Decide personal command, delegated command, divided responsibility, defensive reserve, or nonparticipation.
- **Build information routes.** Develop correspondence, messengers, records, authentication, reporting, and redundancy without creating omniscience.
- **Protect failure recovery.** Preserve reserves, negotiated exits, household security, routes, officers, service goodwill, and demobilization capacity.

## 15. Player Perspective and Attachment

The player should experience Rudolf through visible judgment and consequence, not through a wall of Unknown fields.

Attachment should grow from:

- an appointed office converted into real but bounded performance;
- a Rheinfelden base protected, developed, mortgaged, or exhausted;
- a count, bishop, abbey, or community persuaded rather than erased;
- a Burgundian mandate performed, narrowed, lost, or transformed;
- a Royal request accepted, negotiated, delayed, or refused with consequences;
- a military action prepared, survived, withdrawn from, or recovered after;
- a household made resilient or left dependent on Rudolf alone;
- an extraordinary rival-kingship opportunity embraced, mediated, rejected, or never produced;
- a Swabian political order made more durable than its duke.

## 21. Ecclesiastical Strategy

Rudolf's church-facing gameplay must be institution-specific rather than generic piety or church control.

Valid approaches include:

- verify property, immunity, jurisdiction, protection, patronage, and service claims;
- distinguish bishops, offices, cathedral institutions, abbeys, settlements, and temporal property;
- seek records, mediation, Royal review, papal channels, or negotiated coexistence;
- provide protection or patronage where resources and authority exist;
- contest a specific claim without treating the whole Church as an enemy;
- reconcile after bounded conflict.

Potential consequences include improved records and mediation, stronger or weaker recognition, reduced access, institutional opposition, Royal involvement, local resistance, fiscal cost, grievance memory, or durable cooperation.

## 25. Command Alternatives and Absence Tradeoff

Personal command can improve visibility, coalition confidence, responsibility, and negotiation at the military center. It also exposes Rudolf to capture, injury, death, household disruption, mandate failure, office challenge, and neglected relationships.

Delegated command preserves Rudolf's presence elsewhere but requires a valid commander, mandate, reporting chain, service compliance, resources, and handback. Divided command spreads risk but can weaken concentration. Delay, refusal, demobilization, or withdrawal may preserve the package while costing standing or opportunity.
