## 6. Swabian Presentation Pillars

- **Appointed office, incomplete reach.** Prestigious ducal standing must not look like settled territorial command.
- **Rheinfelden continuity.** House, household, patrimony, dependents, records, and reserves remain distinct from public office.
- **Negotiated plurality.** Counts, dynasts, institutions, settlements, communities, and contributors retain separate agency.
- **Performed authority.** Protection, mediation, consultation, service, records, handback, and recovery make authority legible.
- **Cross-regional pragmatism.** Upper Rhine, Burgundy-facing, Alpine, Danube, and Italia-facing lanes remain conditional and owner-bound.
- **Differentiated three-house development.** Rheinfelden, Zahringen, and Hohenstaufen remain separate lanes without scripted succession.
- **Finite military and material capability.** Pools, officers, equipment, animals, supply, casualties, return, and recovery remain visible.
- **Transformation rather than deletion.** Office loss, house change, retrenchment, mediation, and restoration preserve alternate futures.

## 7. Anti-Flattening Rules

- Do not display one uniformly owned Swabia.
- Do not merge Rheinfelden household resources with ducal resources.
- Do not portray all regional or ecclesiastical actors as one Swabian voice.
- Do not reduce the faction to generic German knights, forests, castles, or later Swabian stereotypes.
- Do not use later Hohenstaufen imperial imagery, later heraldry, or modern regional symbols as secure 1062 identity.
- Do not make Rudolf a heroic super-unit, predestined rival king, or inevitable rebel.
- Do not give Berthold adult presentation or independent agency.
- Do not show Frederick or Hohenstaufen as inevitable ducal successors.
- Do not use one loyalty, relationship, public-order, or map-control meter as complete truth.
- Do not hide routes, records, households, burdens, recovery, and owner locks behind court grandeur.
