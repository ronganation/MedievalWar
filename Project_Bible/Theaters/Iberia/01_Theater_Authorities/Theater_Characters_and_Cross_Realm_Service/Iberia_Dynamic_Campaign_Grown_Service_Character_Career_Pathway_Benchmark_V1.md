## 1. Executive Verdict

This benchmark proves Band 3 without inventing a historical celebrity. It defines how an existing lightweight person or an owner-approved generated person may become strategically important through lived campaign development and current opportunity.

The benchmark uses a Zaragoza-facing service need because the developed Realm authorities already support court learning, patronage, institutional and knowledge development, records, petition review, reporting, regional service, and alternative candidates. The benchmark does not assert that a specific unnamed person exists at the opening and does not populate the Zaragoza court roster.

## 6. Campaign-Growth Stages

### 6.1 Lightweight existence

The person exists through a valid owner but has no major strategic function. Only identity, lifecycle, location or context, broad source, limited development seeds, and activation triggers are required.

### 6.2 Exposure

A household, office, court, institution, regional authority, community, or crisis creates a bounded opportunity to observe or assist. Exposure creates no competence.

### 6.3 Supervised task

The candidate receives a real task with scope, supervisor, information, resources, reporting, review, and failure limits. The task may be administrative, institutional, diplomatic-support, regional, documentary, or logistical.

### 6.4 Dated evidence

Performance produces evidence tied to the task. Evidence may support reliability, readiness, limitations, error, recovery, or mismatch. Evidence is not a universal merit score.

### 6.5 Expanded responsibility

A larger task is available only when the preceding evidence, current need, access, resources, and owner judgment justify expansion. Repeated trivial work gives no automatic advancement.

### 6.6 Strategic activation

The character becomes active only when the role materially affects government, institution, diplomacy, region, crisis continuity, or another strategic owner. Promotion records the reason, source, turn, visibility, and specialist references.

### 6.7 Established function, transformation, or retirement

The person may establish a current function, redirect into another domain, lose access, fail, recover, retire, or return to lightweight simulation. No career state is permanent by default.

  - HiddenFutureHistory
  - FreeGeneratedCompetence

### 8.1 Dynamic candidate surpasses a named candidate

The dynamic person may receive a function when current evidence, access, trust, expertise, availability, or local acceptance is stronger than the named candidate's.

### 8.2 Named candidate remains better supported

Rodrigo, Yusuf, or another named person may receive a function when current owners establish better fit. The dynamic person remains available for another path and is not deleted.

### 8.3 Several dynamic candidates qualify

The relevant owner compares current evidence and requirements deterministically. No candidate receives automatic priority because that candidate was generated first or appears first in a file.

### 8.4 No candidate qualifies

The valid outcome is delay, reduced scope, institutional distribution, another solution, or nonoccurrence.

## 11. Player and AI Experience

The player should see:

- why the person became visible;
- the source context;
- which task produced evidence;
- current readiness and limitations;
- who recognizes the person and why;
- available bounded responsibilities;
- costs, resources, risks, supervision, and alternatives;
- why a named or another dynamic candidate was selected instead;
- valid delay, refusal, reduction, retirement, and nonoccurrence.

The player should not allocate generic stat points, choose a permanent class, or receive repetitive notifications for routine minor work.

AI may evaluate current visible state and propose bounded action. AI may not generate identity, competence, office, institution, resource, relationship, or success.

## 12. Static Acceptance Fixtures

### Positive fixtures

1. An existing lightweight person receives a supervised records task.
2. One successful task produces bounded evidence but not competence mastery.
3. Repeated relevant work supports function-specific readiness.
4. Strategic activation occurs only after the function matters to an owner.
5. A dynamic person receives an administrative function instead of Yusuf because current evidence is stronger.
6. A named character receives another role without deleting the dynamic candidate.
7. Failure leads to narrower responsibility or domain redirection.
8. Loss of relevance returns the person to lightweight simulation without erasing obligations or evidence.
9. The person becomes important without receiving a famous name, dynasty, title, or generalship.
10. No dynamic candidate emerges and the campaign remains valid.

### Negative fixtures

1. Generate a talented courtier because an office is vacant.
2. Create identity without a population, household, institution, community, or service source.
3. Grant competence from age, time, one event, research, or role need.
4. Convert access into office or office into permanent authority.
5. Create an institution, manuscript, work, account, payment, route, property, relationship, or reputation.
6. Copy Rodrigo's or Yusuf's identity, biography, relationships, or destiny.
7. Give a generated candidate priority based on creation or file order.
8. Require the dynamic character to become a battlefield general.
9. Keep full simulation after strategic relevance ends without another valid reason.
10. Replay the generation or pathway over an advanced save.
