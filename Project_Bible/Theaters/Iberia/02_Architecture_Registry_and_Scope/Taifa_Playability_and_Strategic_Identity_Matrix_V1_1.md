## 1. Concise Verdict

Retain the default first-wave trio, but change its internal production order:

1. **Seville: First-Wave Playable and recommended first complete taifa package.** It best exposes taifa-specific urban-fiscal power, aggressive absorption of neighboring polities, household succession, juristic legitimacy, maritime-river access, and both Christian and Almoravid diplomacy without depending on one narrow frontier script.
2. **Zaragoza: First-Wave Playable.** It is the best reciprocal stress test for Aragon and Barcelona and the strongest proof that cross-confessional military service, tribute, access, and frontier bargaining cannot collapse into religious blocs.
3. **Toledo: First-Wave Playable.** It offers the clearest high-pressure survival campaign and strongest interface with León-Castile, but requires unusually careful paria chronology, border control, communal governance, and anti-railroading design.
4. **Granada: Later Playable.** Distinct Zirid-Berber household politics, southern mountain geography, and a powerful Jewish vizierial-administrative interface are valuable, but January 1062 authority and succession framing require focused reconciliation.
5. **Badajoz: Later Playable.** It offers a unique western river-and-depth campaign but carries ruler-date conflict, territorial-coherence uncertainty, and high map-data cost.

Initial supporting classifications:

- **Valencia:** Later Playable, not Simulated-Only. Its January 1062 ruler is date-correct and its exposed dynastic/diplomatic position is too strategically valuable to lock away, but it should follow the three first-wave packages.
- **Almería:** Later Playable. Strong port, mint, court, and maritime profile justify eventual playability.
- **Denia:** Simulated-Only initially. Preserve as an independent maritime actor and external owner; do not flatten it into Valencia or Zaragoza.
- **Málaga:** Simulated-Only initially, with an External Lock on any Granada-facing control claim until date-specific authority is reconciled.
- **Murcia:** External Lock at opening. In January 1062 it is not a free-standing taifa runtime candidate; model the date-correct superior authority and local governorship only after source reconciliation.
- **Córdoba, Silves, Morón, Arcos, Ronda, Niebla, Carmona, Albarracín, Alpuente, Tortosa, Lérida and Ceuta:** ReviewOnly unless required as reciprocal actors, routes, settlements, or external locks.

The matrix creates **no Registry entries, Opening State records, runtime instances, armies, treasuries, agreements, relationships, property, or territorial-control facts**.

### 2.4 Temporal boundary

- Historical progression: +21 months per strategic turn;
- Biological progression: +7 months per strategic turn;

- Historical progression does not age characters;

## 3. Comparative Scoring Criteria

Scores are comparative planning aids, not historical facts or runtime variables.

- **5:** exceptional first-wave value or confidence;
- **4:** strong;
- **3:** viable with bounded reconciliation;
- **2:** meaningful but material uncertainty/cost;
- **1:** weak initial candidate or primarily external;
- **U:** unresolved; never convert to zero.

The twenty required criteria are grouped for readability:

- **A. Authority confidence:** exact January 1062 ruler and political form.
- **B. Household complexity:** dynasty, succession, household, delegated authority.
- **C. Map identity:** capital, major settlements, regional distinction.
- **D. Strategic geography:** routes, rivers, ports, crossings, frontier access.
- **E. Economic identity:** productive base, urban economy, market/mint evidence.
- **F. Treasury evidence:** quality of evidence for liquid fiscal capacity, explicitly not urban wealth.
- **G. Paria exposure:** evidence and strategic relevance of tribute/payment pressure.
- **H. Military pressure:** capacity, frontier threats, campaign reach.
- **I. Christian interfaces:** number, quality, and reciprocity of Christian-realm interactions.
- **J. Taifa rivalries:** meaningful Muslim-polity competition and alliance options.
- **K. Juristic/urban legitimacy:** ruler-city-jurist-institution tension.
- **L. Internal governance:** elite, household, communal, military and administrative pluralism.
- **M. Distinct campaign loop:** differentiated from other candidates.
- **N. AI explainability:** can priorities and tradeoffs be summarized clearly.
- **O. Non-scripted historical pressure:** supports divergence without outcome injection.
- **P. Reciprocal value:** strengthens existing Aragon, Barcelona, León-Castile and Almoravid anchors.
- **Q. Implementation cost:** 5 means comparatively low cost; 1 means very high cost.
- **R. Source quality:** availability and agreement of primary, academic, archaeological and numismatic evidence.
- **S. Shared-owner/Registry safety:** 5 means bounded ownership risk; 1 means many contested locks.
- **T. Overall recommendation:** synthesized production value, not arithmetic destiny.

### 4.1 Interpretation

- The summary preserves **Seville, Toledo and Zaragoza** as the first-wave set.

## 5. Candidate-by-Candidate Strategic Profiles

### 5.1 Seville

**Authority:** Abbad II al-Mu'tadid is the January 1062 ruler. Al-Mu'tamid is an important son and future successor, not the opening sovereign.  
**Form:** Abbadid urban-dynastic emirate with an expansionist court and recently absorbed western/southern polities.  
**Capital and settlements:** Seville is the administrative, riverine, fiscal, cultural and strategic anchor. Lower Guadalquivir access, western approaches, Córdoba-facing ambition, and recently absorbed centers matter more than a simple contiguous province color.  
**Economic identity:** river commerce, agricultural hinterland, urban taxation, court expenditure, mint/coin evidence, and access toward Atlantic routes. Wealth does not establish opening treasury cash.  
**Pressure:** rivalry with Badajoz and Granada; coercive relations with smaller taifas; impending but not opening-assumed León-Castile paria pressure; household succession; juristic judgment of rule; military consolidation costs.  
**Distinct loop:** convert urban and tributary reach into durable territorial authority without exhausting legitimacy, overextending garrisons, or making every neighbor a permanent enemy.  
**AI explanation:** “Secure the Guadalquivir core; prevent a two-front taifa war; price tribute against campaign readiness; preserve the succession.”  
**Reciprocal value:** creates southern and western economic pressure for León-Castile, a future invitation/intervention interface for the Almoravids, and a major rival for Badajoz, Granada and Almería.  
**Risk:** later fame of Al-Mu'tamid and the 1086 invitation can leak backward. These remain possible later pathways only.  
**Recommendation:** First-Wave Playable; first full taifa package.

### 5.2 Zaragoza

**Authority:** Ahmad al-Muqtadir is secure for January 1062; sources vary between 1046 and 1049 for accession and 1081/1082 for death.  
**Form:** Banu Hud Upper March emirate combining court culture, frontier fortification, tributary diplomacy and multi-directional military contracting.  
**Capital and settlements:** Zaragoza and the Ebro corridor dominate; Huesca, Tudela, Calatayud, Daroca, the Cinca approaches, Tortosa/Lérida questions and eastern connections require exact date-specific ownership locks.  
**Economic identity:** irrigated Ebro agriculture, city and mint, corridor trade, palace patronage, and payments that move across Christian-Muslim boundaries.  
**Pressure:** Aragon, Barcelona and Navarre interfaces; León-Castile paria pressure; internal Banu Hud territorial division; frontier fortresses; taifa competition.  
**Distinct loop:** maintain a profitable frontier balance by combining fortress defense, paid military service, selective tribute, partner-switching and targeted expansion without becoming a passive Christian client.  
**AI explanation:** “Hold the Ebro corridor; keep one northern interface neutral or paid; deny rival encirclement; use partners only for bounded objectives.”  
**Reciprocal value:** highest direct value for Aragon and Barcelona, and a strong test of Christian soldiers serving a Muslim ruler without vassalage or religious-bloc logic.  
**Risk:** later El Cid associations and the 1065 Barbastro recapture can be projected backward. Do not pre-create them.  
**Recommendation:** First-Wave Playable; second production package after Seville.

### 5.3 Toledo

**Authority:** Yahya I al-Ma'mun is the January 1062 ruler.  
**Form:** Dhunnunid Middle March urban emirate with deep frontier traditions and a broad, difficult-to-integrate territorial reach.  
**Capital and settlements:** Toledo anchors the Tagus; Madrid, Guadalajara, Talavera, Cuenca, Uclés, Huete and Calatrava-facing country require date-specific control and route proof.  
**Economic identity:** major urban craft and fiscal base, agricultural river valleys, transit between central plateau and eastern/southern routes, mint evidence and culturally plural urban institutions.  
**Pressure:** León-Castile campaigning and possible tribute, Zaragoza rivalry, Valencia dynastic connection, local frontier defense and communal governance.  
**Distinct loop:** survive as a wealthy central hinge by deciding when to pay, fight, hire, exchange access, reinforce frontier nodes, or redirect pressure toward a rival.  
**AI explanation:** “Protect the Tagus capital; prevent tribute from consuming military options; avoid simultaneous Zaragoza and León-Castile war.”  
**Reciprocal value:** strongest interface with León-Castile and a critical future bridge to Valencia.  
**Risk:** the 1085 fall is so famous that it can dominate the design. No fall timer, Reconquista meter or compulsory surrender path is permitted.  
**Recommendation:** First-Wave Playable; third production package.

### 5.4 Granada

**Authority:** Badis ibn Habus is the opening ruler; his grandson Abd Allah is later and must not be substituted backward.  
**Form:** Zirid Sanhaja Berber dynastic emirate with military-household origins, urban administration, mountain depth and powerful court officials.  
**Capital and settlements:** Granada is the anchor; Elvira legacy, mountain corridors, southern coast access and Málaga authority require exact reconciliation.  
**Economic identity:** fertile irrigated basin, mountain and coastal connections, urban crafts, court patronage, tax administration and access to maritime trade through controlled or dependent ports.  
**Pressure:** Seville rivalry; household succession; relations among Zirid military elites, Andalusi urban groups, jurists, and Jewish administrative elites; uncertain Málaga integration.  
**Distinct loop:** preserve a coalition between dynasty, army and city while using terrain to deter conquest and preventing delegated administration from becoming either a caricature or a single “minority influence” meter.  
**AI explanation:** “Keep the dynasty and capital coalition intact; hold mountain approaches; prevent Seville from isolating the coast.”  
**Reciprocal value:** gives Seville a genuine southern rival and creates a distinct future Almoravid interface.  
**Risk:** narratives about Samuel ibn Naghrila, Joseph ibn Naghrela and the 1066 violence require precise chronology, restrained representation, and no communal essentialism.  
**Recommendation:** Later Playable; first package after the first-wave trio if southern depth is prioritized.

### 5.5 Badajoz

**Authority:** sources disagree on whether Muhammad al-Muzaffar died in 1060, 1065 or 1068 and on the timing/form of the succession struggle. January 1062 ruler authority therefore remains unresolved pending primary and numismatic reconciliation.  
**Form:** Aftasid western territorial emirate with a large, river-linked domain and stressed frontier cohesion.  
**Capital and settlements:** Badajoz, Mérida, Évora, Lisbon, Santarém and western Tagus/Guadiana corridors are strategically potent, but exact January 1062 control must be proved node by node. Coimbra is still a western Badajoz frontier holding at the January 1062 opening on the standard chronology; its 1064 conquest by León-Castile is a later pressure pathway and must not be projected backward. Exact local control and garrison status remain uncreated.  
**Economic identity:** river valleys, western cities, Atlantic-facing routes, agriculture, tax collection and cultural patronage. Distance and route interruption matter more than nominal area.  
**Pressure:** León-Castile tribute and frontier advances; Seville expansion; long communications; succession risk; western local elites.  
**Distinct loop:** preserve territorial coherence across long river corridors while pricing tribute, defending frontier nodes and preventing Seville from cutting the realm into disconnected fiscal regions.  
**AI explanation:** “Keep the Tagus-Guadiana network connected; do not pay more than the west can move; avoid succession fragmentation.”  
**Reciprocal value:** major western opponent for León-Castile and Seville; valuable future Portugal-facing groundwork without projecting later Portugal backward.  
**Risk:** ruler chronology and command structure are first-order blockers.  
**Recommendation:** Later Playable; no full package before ruler-date reconciliation.

### 5.6 Valencia

**Authority:** Abd al-Malik al-Muzaffar is a strong January 1062 candidate, ruling from 1061 to 1065; he has a dynastic relationship with Toledo but does not share its government.  
**Form:** exposed urban emirate in an Amirid succession context, with high dependence on diplomacy and military protection.  
**Capital and settlements:** Valencia and its huerta; Sagunto/Murviedro, Xàtiva, Alzira and southern/eastern routes require date-specific locks. Murcia is subordinate at opening rather than an automatically independent taifa.  
**Economic identity:** irrigated agriculture, Mediterranean port access, urban production, mint circulation and route wealth.  
**Distinct loop:** keep a rich coastal capital independent by balancing Toledo, Zaragoza, Denia and Christian powers, converting marriage or aid into bounded agreements rather than shared rule.  
**Risk:** later Toledo takeover, El Cid and 1090s politics are not opening facts.  
**Recommendation:** Later Playable.

### 5.7 Almería

**Authority:** al-Mu'tasim ibn Sumadih is the opening ruler.  
**Form:** Sumadihid port-centered emirate with strong court culture and maritime-commercial identity.  
**Capital and settlements:** Almería, its port, hinterland routes, eastern mountain corridors and Granada/Murcia-facing approaches.  
**Economic identity:** maritime trade, textiles and crafts, mint/coin evidence, port customs, agricultural supply and court patronage.  
**Distinct loop:** protect a high-value port city whose wealth depends on sea access, hinterland supply and diplomatic insulation from stronger land powers.  
**Risk:** port wealth must not become a fabricated treasury; fleet and trade-route instances require separate evidence.  
**Recommendation:** Later Playable.

### 5.8 Denia

**Authority:** Ali ibn Mujahid rules in January 1062.  
**Form:** maritime Amirid emirate with mainland and island-facing strategic interests.  
**Identity:** port power, Balearic and Mediterranean connections, naval access, trade, coastal fortification and pressure from Valencia and Zaragoza.  
**Distinct loop:** survive through sea mobility, commercial diplomacy and selective mainland commitments.  
**Risk:** the exact configuration of island authority, fleet capacity, Tortosa and later conquest must not be inferred into 1062.  
**Recommendation:** Simulated-Only initially, with enough AI and diplomacy to remain an independent actor.

### 5.9 Málaga

**Authority:** the independent Hammudid taifa ended around 1057/58; January 1062 control lies within a Granada/Zirid authority problem, not a clean independent Málaga realm.  
**Form:** major port city under superior or delegated authority requiring exact owner separation.  
**Identity:** Strait/Mediterranean access, port customs, coastal defense, Granada supply and competing elite interests.  
**Distinct loop if later promoted:** manage a port whose local capacity may diverge from inland dynastic authority.  
**Recommendation:** Simulated-Only plus External Lock. No independent Opening State without new evidence.

### 5.10 Murcia

**Authority:** January 1062 precedes the renewed independent taifa beginning in 1065. The city is under Valencia-facing superior authority with local Banu Tahir/governor questions requiring specialist reconciliation.  
**Form:** governed city and irrigated regional center, not an opening independent realm by default.  
**Identity:** Segura irrigation, urban agriculture, inland-coastal routes, strategic connection between Valencia, Almería and the southeast.  
**Recommendation:** External Lock. Preserve the city, local authority and future emergence conditions without pre-creating independence.

### 5.11 Ceuta and other candidates

Ceuta matters to the Strait and Almoravid interface, but its exact January 1062 separation, ruler and relationship to Málaga/Granada require specialist review. Smaller polities such as Silves, Morón, Arcos, Ronda, Niebla, Carmona, Albarracín, Alpuente, Tortosa and Lérida should be represented only where they create reciprocal routes, fortress pressures, diplomatic partners or external ownership locks. They are not automatically factions merely because a historical list names them.

## 8. Shared Architecture Requirements

Implement shared architecture once. Realm packages consume it but do not own it.

1. **Typed diplomatic instruments:** payment, paria, one-time indemnity, military service, subsidy, access, truce, hostage, prisoner release, marriage compact, commercial privilege, protection, guarantee, invitation, expedition mandate, intervention scope, withdrawal term, non-annexation assurance, claim recognition.
2. **Agreement bundles:** one negotiation may contain several typed clauses without collapsing them into vassalage.
3. **Directed obligations:** payer-to-recipient and recipient-to-payer duties are distinct; knowledge and enforcement are explicit.
4. **Settlement/route/control separation:** legal claim, military controller, administration, market operation, mint operation, port operation, road access and local workforce remain separately owned.
5. **Urban governance:** ruler household, vizierate, military command, qadi/juristic authority, market supervision, communal institutions, fiscal administration and regional governors use typed offices/relationships.
6. **Liquid-fiscal state:** urban wealth, assessed revenue, collectible revenue, treasury cash, earmarked funds, arrears, debasement/mint output and emergency exactions are separate.
7. **Service markets:** commanders and contingents may serve across confessional lines through scoped contracts.
8. **Intervention lifecycle:** request, evaluation, mandate, access, deployment, objective, renewal, withdrawal, breach, dependency pressure, annexation claim and resistance are separate states.
9. **External locks:** reciprocal actors, settlements, ports, routes, institutions and persons have one canonical owner.
10. **AI explanation:** each command identifies objective, typed instrument, expected cost, risk, duration and exit condition.
11. **Save/migration protection:** new-campaign initialization only, advanced-save rejection, deterministic ordering, versioned mappings, no alias chains, quarantine ambiguity.
12. **Temporal bindings:** Historical Date, Historical progression, Biological progression and Campaign Season persist and hash separately.

## 9. Paria and Tribute Requirements

### 9.1 Definition

A **paria** is a typed negotiated or coerced payment obligation with a payer, recipient, amount/formula or bounded unknown, cadence, duration/review, consideration, enforcement context, arrears state, evidence confidence and termination conditions.

It is not:

- loyalty;
- vassalage;
- ownership;
- shared government;
- permanent protection;
- automatic military access;
- automatic knowledge;
- a universal “submission” meter.

### 9.2 Required separation

Store and present separately:

- claimed obligation;
- acknowledged obligation;
- scheduled installment;
- paid installment;
- arrears;
- disputed amount;
- protection or non-attack consideration;
- military-support clause;
- access clause;
- hostage/security clause;
- breach response;
- renegotiation and expiration.

### 9.3 Evidence rule

Do not create opening payment amounts from general statements that a taifa was wealthy or tributary. Exact amounts, schedule, recipient and start date require source support. Unknown is not zero. A later documented paria must not be projected into 1062.

### 9.4 Strategic consequences

Paria pressure may affect:

- liquid treasury and arrears;
- recruitment timing;
- fortification and supply;
- diplomatic leverage;
- elite acceptance;
- tax pressure and public explanation;
- rival calculations;
- future renegotiation.

Payment must remain one strategic option among war, coalition, service, access, hostage exchange, territorial concession, deferral and refusal.

## 10. Assistance, Protection, Invitation and Intervention Requirements

### 10.1 Assistance

Military assistance is a bounded service or coalition commitment. It records objective, theater, command relationship, supply responsibility, duration, payment/reward, access and withdrawal. It grants no annexation permission.

### 10.2 Protection

Protection is a promise or service against named threats under named conditions. It is not ownership, government, occupation or permanent intervention authority.

### 10.3 Invitation

An invitation is a request by authorized actors for defined help. It is not vassalage, permanent access or automatic Almoravid control. Separate or competing invitations must be possible.

### 10.4 Intervention

Intervention requires:

1. a valid request, claim, emergency doctrine or hostile decision;
2. route and access feasibility;
3. force and supply availability;
4. scope and objective;
5. owner validation;
6. explicit occupation and withdrawal rules;
7. later political consequences evaluated from conduct, dependence, legitimacy and force, not from the word “invitation.”

### 10.5 Permitted paths

The architecture must permit no intervention, limited assistance, temporary access, post-victory withdrawal, negotiated dependency, resistance, coerced annexation, failed intervention and rival invitations. None is pre-created.

## 11. Christian-Realm Interfaces

Cross-confessional diplomacy uses the same typed architecture as Muslim-Muslim diplomacy. Religious identity can influence legitimacy, trust, juristic response and AI preference, but does not force alliance or hostility.

- **León-Castile:** direct first-wave interfaces with Toledo; western pressure and routes with Badajoz; paria and campaign interfaces with Zaragoza and Seville; no automatic inheritance of Ferdinand I's later partition outcomes.
- **Aragon:** direct fortress, route, tribute, service and campaign interface with Zaragoza; eastern commercial and military consequences for Valencia and Denia.
- **Barcelona:** reciprocal frontier and service-market interface with Zaragoza; maritime/commercial interface with Denia and Valencia; no automatic Catalan bloc.
- **Navarre:** strategically relevant to Zaragoza and Upper March balancing, but remains separately owned and is not inferred into Aragon.
- **Aquitaine and trans-Pyrenean actors:** external pressure and service participants only where evidence and strategic routes justify them.

Required instruments include truces, parias, military service, guides, access, safe-conduct, hostages, ransoms, market privileges, convoy protection, fortress guarantees and bounded coalitions.
