## 4. Source Findings

A probable <Person>Ian Heath</Person> extract on Spanish knights describes twelfth- and thirteenth-century Iberian knightly equipment, including mail, lances, swords, and shield variation. The extract reports round shields and overarm lance use in earlier Iberian material, while a thirteenth-century example from the Cantigas uses a straighter-sided shield with a rounded base, a short-sleeved surcoat, and a round-topped helmet. These are broad Spanish knight directions rather than Calatrava-specific uniform proof. 

A probable <Person>Ian Heath</Person> extract on Spanish military-order brethren states that historians disagreed about early Calatravan dress and that the red floriated cross belonged to a much later change. A separate <Person>Helyot</Person> extract likewise reports disagreement over clothing and associates the red floriated cross with a 1397 change. These sources strongly prohibit projecting the familiar later cross onto foundation-era battle dress.
