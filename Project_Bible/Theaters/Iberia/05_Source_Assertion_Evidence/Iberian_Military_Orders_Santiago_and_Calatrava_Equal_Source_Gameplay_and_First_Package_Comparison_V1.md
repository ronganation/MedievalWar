# PART III. SANTIAGO SOURCE PACKET

## 6. Origin and Early Development

A reference account identifies an August 1170 confraternity of knights at Cáceres under <Person>Ferdinand II of León</Person>, led by <Person>Pedro Fernández</Person>, initially associated with frontier defense. The account reports the Brethren of Cáceres name, a 1171 agreement with the archbishop of Santiago de Compostela, papal protection in 1173, and papal approval of a rule in 1175. citeturn105search102

```yaml
Santiago_origin_working_direction:
  initial_form: LeoneseFrontierKnightlyConfraternity
  working_foundation_window: 1170
  early_name: BrethrenOfCaceresResearchLead
  papal_protection_lead: 1173
  rule_approval_lead: 1175
  certainty: RequiresScholarlyAndPrimaryCrossCheck
```

## 7. Mission and Political Scope

The same account connects the early institution to protection of León’s southern frontier, while also describing the master’s effort to build an institution not restricted to León. # PART IV. CALATRAVA SOURCE PACKET

## 11. Origin and Early Development

<External>Encyclopaedia Britannica</External> states that the order originated in 1158 when <Person>Sancho III of Castile</Person> ceded the fortress of Calatrava to <Person>Raymond</Person>, abbot of the Cistercian monastery of Fitero, with instructions to defend it. The source states that the knights and monks defending the fort received formal papal recognition in 1164 and that the order later became closely affiliated with Morimond. citeturn105search38

```yaml
Calatrava_origin_working_direction:
  initial_form: FortressDefenseInstitutionFromCistercianContext
  working_foundation_window: 1158
  papal_confirmation: 1164
  early_site: FortressOfCalatrava
  Cistercian_relationship: CentralResearchRequirement
  certainty: StrongOrientationPendingFullScholarlyPacket
```

The Britannica account explicitly connects Calatrava’s origin to defense of a specified fortress. It also records later land grants in Castile and Aragon and participation in Andalusian conquest, but those later developments must not be assumed at foundation. citeturn105search38

```yaml

recommended_first_broad_followup: Santiago
reasoning:
  Calatrava:
    - CleanerFortressEntrustmentOrigin
    - StrongHouseGarrisonPropertyAndMobilizationTest
    - MoreBoundedInitialPoliticalSurface
  Santiago:
    - BroaderInstitutionalAndCrossRealmCoverage
    - BetterSecondPackageForExpansionAfterCoreLoopWorks
binding: false
```

This recommendation reverses the earlier default leading-candidate assumption only at the prototype level. Santiago remains the richer broad package. Calatrava is currently the cleaner first implementation because it can test the core framework through a specific fortress, patron, religious relationship, finite garrison, property base, and field-mobilization decision without requiring the entire cross-realm Santiago network at once.
