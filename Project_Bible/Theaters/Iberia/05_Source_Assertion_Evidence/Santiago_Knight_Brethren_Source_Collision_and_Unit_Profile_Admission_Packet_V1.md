# PART I. HISTORICAL FOUNDATION

## 1. Origin and Confraternity

A located reference account describes the <External>Order of Santiago</External> as originating from a knightly confraternity established at Cáceres in August 1170 under <Person>Ferdinand II of León</Person>. The account names <Person>Pedro Fernández</Person> as master and says that the brethren were known as the Brethren of Cáceres until January 1171. citeturn112search51

```yaml

opening_1062: NotExisting
```

## 2. Compostela and Papal Relationships

The same reference reports a 1171 agreement with the archbishop of Santiago de Compostela, under which the archbishop became an honorary brother and the master and knights entered the cathedral chapter. It states that the brethren adopted Saint James as patron, that papal protection was obtained in 1173, and that the rule received papal approval in 1175. citeturn112search51

```yaml
institutional_relationships:

```

## 3. Membership and Governance

The located account states that the rule provided for knight brethren dedicated to warfare and clerics following the Rule of Saint Augustine. It describes a master elected from among the knights and a general chapter whose assent formed part of governance. citeturn112search51

The reference account says that <Person>Ferdinand II of León</Person> intended the militia to defend León’s southern frontier, while <Person>Pedro Fernández</Person> pursued a broader scope. It also reports that <Person>Alfonso VIII of Castile</Person> granted the castles of Mora and Oreja in 1171. citeturn112search51
