## 1. Strong Aragon Design Verdict

Aragon requires a dedicated title, claim, lordship, and succession owner because the rulership of Aragon, the unequal recognition of Aragon, Sobrarbe, and Ribagorza, Ramiro I's bounded titulature, the legitimate Sancho's expected succession, the elder Sancho's property and command interfaces, Pamplonese seniority questions, castle custody, tenencias, later regional dignities, and conquest-facing title questions cannot remain implicit across Registry, Historical Reconciliation, Dynasty, Character, Governance, Ecclesiastical, Opening State, and AI authorities.

The Aragon title game is **a founder-held three-land rulership whose practical unity is stronger than its settled constitutional form**. Ramiro I is the effective opening ruler. The player-facing faction may be called the Kingdom of Aragon. Neither statement converts every land, castle, office, estate, jurisdiction, recognition audience, or future title into uniform royal property.

The strategic promise is to make succession, recognition, title exercise, and regional continuity meaningful without scripting Ramiro's death, guaranteeing one successor, inventing a universal feudal hierarchy, or projecting the later Crown of Aragon backward.

### 10.2 Succession is a case, not a queue

A succession case begins only after an owner-confirmed trigger or an authorized continuity review. Candidate verification must distinguish expected succession, eligibility, designation, recognition, installation, and practical control. The historically later winner is never selected solely because a historical date has been reached.

### 10.3 Ramiro continuity preparation

Before any vacancy, the player may pursue owner-approved preparation around records, seals, household continuity, bounded service for legitimate Sancho, regional contact, institutional witnessing, castle-command review, and external communication. Preparation can reduce specific gaps. It does not guarantee succession or transfer offices and property.

### 10.4 Regional reception

Old Aragon, Sobrarbe, and Ribagorza must provide separate recognition and continuity inputs. Disagreement in one region does not automatically erase recognition elsewhere. Recognition does not automatically transfer property, garrisons, offices, revenues, or local obedience.

### 10.5 Elder Sancho safeguards

The elder Sancho must remain separately identified. His property, testamentary, household, service, command, and potential later-title interfaces require separate owner records. His existence does not automatically create a rival claim, revolt, title, or exclusion from useful service.

### 10.6 Protected younger dynasts

García and Sancha remain meaningful continuity participants within age, development, household, access, property, institutional, and political boundaries. Neither receives an adult office or active title by implication. Sancha’s non-battlefield role does not remove her from dynastic diplomacy, household continuity, patronage, institutional access, property questions, or later succession contingencies.

## 11. Title Exercise, Lordship, and Tenencia Rules

### 11.1 Title exercise

A valid title may exist while exercise is mediated, constrained, disputed, or incomplete. Title exercise must identify audiences, jurisdictions, powers, obligations, access, records, and owner receipts.

### 11.2 Lordship interface

A lordship may connect title, place, property, household, jurisdiction, service, protection, revenue, castle, and community, but none of those identities merges into the lordship record.

### 11.3 Tenencia boundary

Tenencias remain offices or delegations governed by Governance and Delegated Authority. This title authority may describe their title-facing consequences, but it cannot activate them, make them hereditary, convert them into property, or assign holders.

### 11.4 Castle boundary

A castle record does not prove title, lordship, county, hereditary possession, valley government, garrison ownership, revenue ownership, or regional obedience.

### 11.5 Direct domain boundary

The ruler’s title does not make all of Aragon, Sobrarbe, or Ribagorza direct royal property. Every direct-domain claim requires Property, Geography, Governance, Historical, Registry, and Opening State compatibility.

## 12. Recognition, Installation, and Continuity

### 12.1 Recognition audiences

Recognition can differ among the royal household, Old Aragon, Sobrarbe, Ribagorza, ecclesiastical institutions, castle and local actors, Pamplona-facing actors, other Iberian realms, trans-Pyrenean contacts, and papal-facing channels. No audience is synthesized in reverse.

### 12.2 Installation

Installation may require a valid decision receipt, venue, witnesses, records, oath or acclamation where historically and legally appropriate, household and seal handoff, regional reception, ecclesiastical interface, and communications. Missing ceremonial detail remains unknown rather than invented.

## 13. Conquest, Occupation, and Title Settlement

```text
Victory != Control
Control != Government
Government != Recognition
Recognition != Title
Title != Property
Property != Revenue
Revenue != DurableIncorporation
```

Graus, Barbastro, Huesca, Monzón, Alquézar, and other future gains may create military possession, negotiation, capitulation, tribute, custody, or a title question. A settled title requires separate historical, legal, regional, external, governmental, ecclesiastical, property, and Opening State or runtime receipts. No conquest creates an automatic county, kingdom, lordship, or hereditary grant.

## 14. External and Cross-Realm Locks

- Castile-León owns its titles, agreements, interventions, and reverse recognition.

## 21. Decision Cases

### 21.1 Clarify Ramiro’s title language

The player may seek documentary, household, ecclesiastical, regional, or external clarification. A result may improve one audience’s recognition or UI explanation. It does not rewrite all historical documents or create universal recognition.

### 21.3 Clarify elder Sancho’s standing

The player may investigate Aibar, Javierrelatre, Sos, property, service, command, and documentary evidence. Outcomes may separate a property interest from a public delegation or leave uncertainty intact. The system must not force either automatic dispossession or rebellion.

### 21.4 Respond to regional hesitation

If one regional audience hesitates during succession, the player may seek recognition, mediation, guarantees, limited settlement, evidence review, or delay. Military coercion is not the sole remedy and does not create automatic lawful title.

### 21.5 Settle a conquered site

After military control, owners must separately treat capitulation, garrison, supply, property, church, local actors, justice, revenue, title, recognition, administration, and external response. The title authority may open a settlement case but cannot shortcut the other owners.
