## 1. Developed Profile Verdict

Ramiro I is the living sovereign around whom January 1062 Aragon is coordinated. Ramiro's practical rulership across Aragon, Sobrarbe, and Ribagorza, military availability, authority to negotiate and delegate through specialist owners, dynastic position, institutional patronage, and external diplomacy make Ramiro the principal realm anchor. None of those interfaces turns Ramiro into the realm, the three regions, the Jimenez dynasty, the royal household, the court, the army, the treasury, the church, or every castle and route.

**Hold three mountain lands together while converting personal rule into continuity that can survive the ruler.**

Ramiro begins experienced and politically central, but not omnipotent. Aragon's strength is concentrated in a ruler capable of connecting distinct lands, routes, castles, institutions, family members, and frontier choices. Aragon's weakness is that the same concentration exposes the union to absence, defeat, capture, incapacity, death, failed delegation, weak recognition, interrupted reporting, and ill-prepared succession.

The player-facing Ramiro should feel like a main faction leader rather than a placeholder king. Ramiro must be able to govern, travel, negotiate, arbitrate, delegate, command when valid, prepare succession, patronize institutions, respond to frontier pressure, recover from failure, and reshape the political union. Ramiro must also be able to make costly mistakes. Historical death near Graus is a future possibility, not a timer or required destination.

## 7. Core Character Thesis

Ramiro's defining strength is practical coordination. Ramiro can bring royal presence, command eligibility, diplomacy, patronage, grant-making proposals, family authority, and regional mediation to bear where institutions remain thin or uneven.

Ramiro's defining weakness is concentration. If too many functions remain personal, a campaign, illness, captivity, defeat, route failure, or sudden death may expose every region differently. The political problem is not a generic stability penalty. The problem is whether command, reporting, household continuity, regional recognition, property, delegations, institutional relationships, and succession can continue through their own owners.

The profile must remain valid if Ramiro avoids Graus, survives beyond 1063, loses a battle elsewhere, delegates command, abandons a frontier objective, resolves the succession early, leaves it uncertain, strengthens one land at another's expense, or transforms the union into a more durable or more fragmented political order.

### 7.2 Campaign question

The Ramiro campaign asks whether personal kingship can become a durable political order before a crisis removes the person holding the order together. The answer must arise from play. A strong campaign may produce reliable reporting, differentiated regional government, a prepared successor, bounded collateral service, recoverable military pools, institutional cooperation, and credible diplomacy. A failed campaign may preserve Ramiro's title while hollowing out execution, leaving exposed castles, regional disengagement, exhausted pools, unresolved inheritance, weak household continuity, or a frontier possession that costs more than it yields.
