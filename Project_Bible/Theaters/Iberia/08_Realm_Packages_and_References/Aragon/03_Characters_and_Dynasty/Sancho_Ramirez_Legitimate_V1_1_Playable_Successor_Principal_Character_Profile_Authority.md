## 1. Developed Profile Verdict

Sancho Ramirez begins as Ramiro I's living adult legitimate son and expected successor, but not as opening ruler, guaranteed future king, automatic commander, settled husband, or preloaded reformer. Sancho's campaign importance comes from the difficult conversion of dynastic expectation into title-specific eligibility, regional recognition, household continuity, military credibility, institutional relationships, and practical governing capacity.

**Become capable of inheriting a realm whose unity cannot be inherited by label alone.**

Sancho should be among Aragon's most robust profiles because Sancho may become the faction's sovereign during ordinary play. The profile must support meaningful agency before succession, a credible transition if succession occurs, failure or exclusion if recognition and capacity do not develop, and divergent rulership if Ramiro survives longer or transfers responsibility gradually.

Sancho is not a lesser copy of Ramiro. Ramiro's problem is converting personal rule into durability. Sancho's problem is proving that continuity can exist without simply borrowing Ramiro's authority.

## 32. Noninheritance, Partial Succession, Death, and Legacy

Noninheritance must remain fully playable. Sancho may continue through royal service, a bounded regional mandate, military command, an independent household, negotiated property, institutional or diplomatic work, external service, partial recognition, a dormant claim, reconciliation, exile, captivity, or recovery. None automatically creates rebellion.

Partial succession may result in recognition by one or two lands, incomplete title transfer, disputed delegations, household continuity without military command, or military power without institutional acceptance. The game must preserve these mixed states rather than snapping to whole-realm victory or failure.

Sancho's later reign and death do not protect the opening character. Earlier death, long survival under Ramiro, noninheritance, captivity, exile, or a divergent rulership are valid. Legacy arises from observed service, preserved forces, mandates, regional recognition, settlements, institutions, agreements, household continuity, or runtime descendants and remains audience-specific Historical Memory.

## 37. Institutions, Law, Petitions, and Documentary Capacity

Sancho's future capacity to rule depends partly on learning how decisions become records, witnessed settlements, enforceable duties, protected property, institutional cooperation, and remembered precedent. Sancho may witness, petition, arbitrate under mandate, request records, support lawful patronage, negotiate property or immunity disputes, and ask an institution to mediate.

Each monastery, church, episcopal office, chapter, property claim, custodian, witness group, and jurisdiction remains separate. Sancho cannot create a universal church constituency. Documentary participation may improve evidence and continuity while also binding Sancho to promises, witnesses, beneficiaries, and future review.

## 39. Distinctive Player Outcomes and Final Design Summary

A strong Sancho campaign can create a successor recognized differently across the three lands, a disciplined commander with an accountable service record, a household capable of surviving transition, a negotiated settlement with elder Sancho, institution-specific support, and external relationships based on delivered agreements rather than historical assumption.

A compromised campaign may create a militarily prominent but politically narrow successor, a household favorite without regional execution, a regional governor who loses court access, an overextended commander whose victories exhaust the realm, a partial ruler, a claimant without capacity, or a noninheriting principal who remains important through service and family continuity.

A failed campaign may produce capture, early death, broken mandates, alienated contributors, unresolved marriage, depleted pools, weak records, unsupported succession, or a transition Sancho cannot execute. Failure remains recoverable where lifecycle and political conditions permit.

Final design summary: Sancho is Aragon's working successor principal, not Aragon's guaranteed future. Sancho's value comes from building visible and owner-routed capacity before vacancy, carrying meaningful play through transition or noninheritance, and forcing the player to decide whether a successor should be protected, tested, empowered, limited, married, delegated, or allowed to build an independent political future.
