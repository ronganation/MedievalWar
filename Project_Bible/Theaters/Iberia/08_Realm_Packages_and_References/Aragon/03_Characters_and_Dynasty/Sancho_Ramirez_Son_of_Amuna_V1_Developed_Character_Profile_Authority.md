## 1. Developed Profile Verdict

Sancho Ramirez, son of Ramiro I and Amuna, is an opening-compatible adult collateral son whose value lies in the overlap among dynastic standing, property and testamentary evidence, possible western or regional service, command eligibility, and succession-adjacent politics. He is not the legitimate Sancho, not a hidden heir, not a default rival, and not an expendable illegitimate son.

**Turn uncertain grant, property, and collateral status into lawful, useful, reviewable authority without being erased by the legitimate succession or forced into revolt.**

The character must remain strategically meaningful whether serving Ramiro, cooperating with the legitimate Sancho, building a bounded regional role, preserving property, negotiating handback, becoming a commander, withdrawing from court politics, receiving later recognition, or pursuing a divergent nonroyal career.

## 11. Character Fantasy and Strategic Questions

**Character fantasy:** build a durable place in Aragon through lawful property, reliable service, regional usefulness, military credibility, and negotiated family settlement rather than waiting to inherit or rebelling because inheritance is unlikely.

Recurring questions:

- What is actually owned, granted, delegated, promised, or merely associated?
- Is clarification worth losing useful ambiguity?
- Which service produces evidence without surrendering legitimate property?
- Can a temporary regional role be handed back without erasing earned recognition?
- Can cooperation with legitimate Sancho preserve both continuity and collateral standing?
- Does personal command provide leverage or create an alarming independent following?
- When should Sancho refuse an under-resourced or unlawful mandate?
- What future remains if court access declines?
- How can failure be recovered without accepting a rebel identity?

## 25. Workload, Concentration, Absence, and Replacement

Property management, litigation, regional service, command, court attendance, household development, institutional mediation, and diplomacy compete for time, staff, guards, messengers, transport, money, records, and attention.

A command or mandate requires backups, custody of records and resources, reporting routes, local continuity, closeout, incapacity handling, and handback. Workload is not a stamina bar. It creates map-facing choices about which standing Sancho builds and which opportunities are left unattended.

## 26. Failure, Dispossession, Captivity, and Recovery

Failure may include rejected evidence, ambiguous grant, lost property case, failed mandate, local noncompliance, broken promise, military defeat, capture, injury, revocation, court exclusion, or loss of a following. None automatically marks Sancho a rebel or erases the character.

Recovery routes include new evidence, mediation, appeal, negotiated settlement, narrower service, clean handback, reconciliation, regional relocation, external service, ransom, demobilization, household retrenchment, or acceptance of a nonproperty role. Valid death ends agency through Lifecycle and triggers owner-routed consequences.

## 27. Noninheritance, Succession Crisis, and Divergent Futures

Sancho is not the primary expected successor, but succession may still affect property, delegations, command, household access, regional recognition, promises, and security. A new ruler may confirm, review, narrow, revoke, renegotiate, or fail to enforce an arrangement through owners.

Valid futures include loyal collateral service, cooperative settlement with legitimate Sancho, powerful regional delegate, property-centered lord, commander, institutional mediator, independent household principal, external service, dispossessed petitioner, dormant claimant without action, reconciled magnate, captive or recovering dynast, earlier death, or survival beyond historical comparisons. Rebellion is one conditional outcome among many, never the default.
