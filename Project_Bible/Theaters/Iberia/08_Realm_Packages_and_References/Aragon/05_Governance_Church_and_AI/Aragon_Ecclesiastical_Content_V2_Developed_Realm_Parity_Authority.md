# 1. Concise Verdict

Implement the opening Aragonese church as a **multi-center ecclesiastical network** rather than a later settled Diocese of Jaca copied backward into 1062.

The opening content should contain:

- `office.bishop.aragon` as the principal episcopal office identity;
- San Adrián de Sásabe as an opening-valid episcopal residence and monastic institution;
- Jaca as a royal and ecclesiastically relevant center, but not yet a fully settled later cathedral government;
- San Juan de la Peña as a major monastery with royal patronage, records, hospitality, memory, and future burial interfaces;
- San Victorián as a major Sobrarban monastery with property, patronage, mediation, and regional-government interfaces;
- San Pedro de Siresa as a major old-Aragonese institution with route, property, cultural, and episcopal-residence interfaces;
- Roda as an externally owned Ribagorzan bishopric and jurisdiction lock;
- Bishop Sancho as a ReviewOnly person candidate until specialist evidence and the registry promote the identity;
- abbots of San Juan de la Peña, San Victorián, and San Pedro de Siresa as ReviewOnly officeholders;
- the later transfer toward Jaca, Roman-rite reform, papal relationship, cathedral development, and diocesan reorganization as conditional later pathways.

The authority rejects:

- one universal Church Approval score;
- one monolithic “Aragon Church” actor;
- building, institution, office, jurisdiction, property, community, and officeholder collapse;
- Jaca's later cathedral and settled bishopric as opening facts;
- Bishop Sancho inferred from either royal Sancho identity;
- royal patronage as church ownership;
- church immunity as sovereignty;
- automatic papal control;
- automatic Roman-rite conversion;
- legendary Holy Grail gameplay;
- later institutional hierarchy applied retroactively.

Primary classification: **Implement bounded institutions and interfaces. Keep officeholders and exact jurisdiction ReviewOnly. Preserve ecclesiastical mobility, regional variation, and later reform as strategic possibilities rather than scripts.**

# 17. Property and Immunity Boundary

## 17.1 Property

Ecclesiastical institutions may own or use estates, revenues, tolls, mills, fields, forests, settlements, or labor obligations where evidence supports them.

## 17.2 Immunity

- holder;
- subject matter;
- jurisdiction;
- duration;
- recognized authorities;

Military need does not automatically override church property or immunity. The relevant owner validates requisition, consent, compensation, dispute, and consequence.
