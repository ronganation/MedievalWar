## 1. Strong Aragon Legal Verdict

Aragon should operate through overlapping royal authority, regional custom, household and witnessed act, castle and tenencia jurisdiction, ecclesiastical privilege, monastery records, local communities, property and service obligations, negotiated settlement, and practical enforcement. January 1062 Aragon does not possess one codified realm law, a uniform permanent court system, a mature chancery, a complete officer corps, a later municipal constitution, or unlimited royal jurisdiction.

The central legal campaign is **to turn personal rulership, differentiated regional recognition, records, mediation, and bounded enforcement into durable but non-uniform legal order**. Ramiro may hear, mediate, witness, confirm, protect, summon, delegate, settle, and request enforcement where authority, subject matter, jurisdiction, parties, evidence, notice, access, officers, resources, regional reception, and restoration safeguards exist. Ruler preference cannot silently become universal law.

Later Fuero de Jaca, legendary Fueros of Sobrarbe, later cathedral government, later municipal privileges, and the constitutional order of the Crown of Aragon are excluded from the opening state. They may appear only as future, review, quarantined, or historically emergent content through their proper owners.

## 13. Judgment, Enforcement, Compliance, and Restoration

A judgment records findings, reasons, scope, remedy, review, and requested owner actions. It cannot directly change title, property, office, custody, treasury, army, garrison, church, household, relationship, or external state.

Enforcement requires a lawful basis, authorized executor, mandate, target, protected parties, routes, communication, property or financial access, force where permitted, proportionality, stop conditions, monitoring, and restoration. Noncompliance may reflect refusal, inability, dispute, delay, route closure, absent resources, competing jurisdiction, or external protection.

Restoration must identify what changed, what should be returned or corrected, which owners must act, what cannot be restored fully, what compensation or record correction is required, and what remains disputed.
