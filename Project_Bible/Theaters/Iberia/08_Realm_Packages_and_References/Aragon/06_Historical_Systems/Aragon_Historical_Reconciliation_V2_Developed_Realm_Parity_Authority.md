# 1. Concise Verdict

Proceed with the playable **Kingdom of Aragon** label, but do not model January 1062 Aragon as a later centralized kingdom or as an embryonic Crown of Aragon.

The opening polity is best reconciled as:

> A practically independent, founder-dependent union of Aragon, Sobrarbe, and Ribagorza under Ramiro I, whose ruler is treated as royal by important contemporaries while the ruler's own titulature, Pamplonese seniority, regional integration, and administrative reach remain historically uneven.

The following opening facts are approved at Static-Checked Draft level:

- Ramiro I is alive and is the opening ruler on 1062-01-01.
- Ramiro I exercises practical rulership across Aragon, Sobrarbe, and Ribagorza.
- The playable faction may be called the Kingdom of Aragon.
- The data model must preserve uncertainty in Ramiro I's self-styling and external recognition.
- Sancho Ramírez, legitimate son of Ramiro I and Ermesinda, is the expected successor.
- A separate illegitimate firstborn son of Ramiro I is also named Sancho Ramírez.
- The two Sancho Ramírez identities must never merge.
- The illegitimate Sancho has an opening-relevant western authority and inheritance interface associated with Aibar and Javierrelatre; the exact office and control implementation remains specialist-owned.
- Jaca is the leading royal and political center, but later municipal, legal, cathedral, and episcopal developments are not opening facts.
- The bishop of Aragon remains associated with an itinerant or multi-center ecclesiastical structure, with San Adrián de Sásabe opening-valid and Jaca's later settled episcopal role deferred.
- Graus is externally held and strategically important.
- Earlier pressure against Graus may exist as history and memory where validated.
- Ramiro I's later death at or near Graus is not an opening fact or scheduled event.
- Barbastro is externally held and no expedition is active at opening.
- The 1064 expedition remains a shared, optional, cross-realm emergence with disputed papal, leadership, composition, conduct, and crusading interpretations.
- Later papal vassalage, Roman-rite transition, Fuero de Jaca, the 1076 Pamplona union, later conquests, and the Crown of Aragon are excluded from Opening State.

Primary classification: **Implement the reconciled facts through the registry and bounded content authorities. Keep unresolved claims explicit. Quarantine later or legendary material. Reject deterministic historical scripting.**

## 1A. Developed Realm Thesis and Current Strategic Identity

Aragon is **The Founder-Dependent Pyrenean Kingdom**. Ramiro I holds together Aragon, Sobrarbe, and Ribagorza through rulership, mediation, castles, route control, delegated authority, ecclesiastical relationships, military protection, dynastic representation, and repeated recognition. The three lands are joined, but they are not flattened.

```text
Aragon + Sobrarbe + Ribagorza
!= one uniform administration
!= one direct royal domain
!= one recruitment pool
!= one ecclesiastical jurisdiction
!= one succession-recognition surface
```

The opening campaign asks whether the player can convert Ramiro's personal union into durable, transferable capacity without inventing national bureaucracy, guaranteed succession, or compulsory frontier destiny.

5. reliable reference synthesis;
6. web summaries for discovery only.

No source gains authority merely by repeating a familiar narrative. Broad maps do not create ownership. Later chronicles do not automatically create opening facts. A repeated modern statement does not remove a primary-source dispute.

# 3. Realm Identity and Royal Status

## 3.1 Playable label

**Opening disposition:** ConfirmedForOpening as a player-facing faction label.

The faction may be displayed as **Kingdom of Aragon** for recognition and campaign readability.

This label does not establish that every legal, diplomatic, and documentary context used the same unqualified royal formula.

## 3.2 Ramiro I's rulership

**Opening disposition:** ConfirmedForOpening.

Ramiro I must be represented as the effective sovereign ruler of the Aragonese polity on 1062-01-01.

The Government of Aragon's documentary portal states that Ramiro did not style himself king in his own documents but was recognized as such by contemporaries and exercised royal power in practice. The portal specifically points researchers to Eduardo Ibarra's documentary collection and Roberto Viruete Erdozáin's 2013 diplomatic collection. This is stronger for project purposes than flattening the problem into either “not a king” or “fully settled king.”

## 3.3 Titulature

**Opening disposition:** Contested but bounded.

The content model should preserve at least three simultaneous facts:

1. Ramiro I's own documents may emphasize `Ramiro, son of King Sancho` rather than an unqualified royal title.
2. Other actors could call Ramiro I king.
3. Ramiro I exercised regalian power and practical independence.

The following must remain separate:

- player-facing faction name;
- ruler's canonical title holdings;
- self-used documentary style;
- style used by internal subjects;
- style used by external courts;
- remembered descent from Sancho III;
- practical command and administration.

## 3.4 Pamplonese seniority

**Opening disposition:** Contested and ReferencedExternal.

Ramiro's original receipt of Aragon occurred within the dynastic settlement following Sancho III's death. Some evidence and later interpretation preserve a senior position for the Pamplonese line. By 1062, however, practical Aragonese action cannot be modeled as routine Pamplonese administration.

Opening implementation:

- Pamplonese seniority may exist as historical memory, legal interpretation, diplomatic posture, or claim context.
- Pamplona does not directly control Aragon's routine treasury, castles, armies, or appointments.
- No automatic vassal command relationship is approved.
- Reverse recognition and legal claims remain externally owned by the future Pamplona package.

## 3.5 Realm form

**Opening disposition:** StronglySupported.

The realm is a personally consolidated union of three lands:

- Aragon;
- Sobrarbe;
- Ribagorza.

The union is not a federal constitution, one centralized province, or a later kingdom with uniform administration.

# 4. Aragon, Sobrarbe, and Ribagorza

## 4.1 Aragon

**Opening disposition:** ConfirmedForOpening as the ruler's original and politically central land.

Aragon is the strongest core of ruling identity and household authority. Old Aragon should provide the most dependable concentration of royal presence, but exact direct domain still requires site-level evidence.

## 4.2 Sobrarbe

**Opening disposition:** ConfirmedForOpening as part of Ramiro I's practical union; internal authority pattern remains Provisional.

Sobrarbe came to Ramiro after the death of Gonzalo in the mid-1040s. The exact year is often stated as 1045, while summaries sometimes use 1043 or 1044. The opening fact is not the precise anniversary. The opening fact is that Ramiro had ruled Sobrarbe for well over a decade by January 1062.

Sobrarbe must retain:

- regional identity;
- distinct geography;
- castle and valley authority questions;
- separate recognition and delegation surfaces;
- no legendary ancient constitution.

Later myths of an eighth-century Kingdom of Sobrarbe, the Fueros of Sobrarbe, or constitutional oaths are quarantined from the 1062 package.

## 4.3 Ribagorza

**Opening disposition:** ConfirmedForOpening as part of Ramiro I's practical union; internal authority pattern remains Provisional.

Ribagorza is not merely eastern Aragon. The package must preserve:

- its earlier comital history;
- east-facing noble and ecclesiastical connections;
- relationship with Roda, Urgell, Pallars, and frontier actors;
- castle-command importance;
- distinct route and succession concerns.

Later use of the title Count of Ribagorza by the illegitimate Sancho Ramírez must not be projected backward as an opening title unless charter evidence demonstrates it on 1062-01-01.

## 4.4 Acquisition chronology

**Opening disposition:** ReviewOnly for exact date; settled for opening political consequence.

The project does not need to select 1043, 1044, or 1045 as a gameplay variable. The Historical Reconciliation should retain the disputed dating in evidence notes while opening state records only the long-established possession by 1062.

## 4.5 Three-land integration

**Opening disposition:** StronglySupported as incomplete and founder-dependent.

This does not mean every region is disloyal. It means the union relies on:

- Ramiro I's person;
- kinship and delegated commands;
- ecclesiastical mediation;
- castles and routes;
- repeated recognition;
- military success and protection.

No universal `IntegrationScore` is approved.

# 5. Ramiro I: Identity and Opening Lifecycle

## 5.1 Identity

**Opening disposition:** ConfirmedForOpening.

- Person: Ramiro I of Aragon
- Father: Sancho III of Pamplona
- Mother: Sancha of Aibar
- Dynasty: Jiménez dynasty, branch treatment pending registry
- Birth: before 1007 or approximately 1006 to 1007
- Opening state: alive, adult, ruler
- Later death: traditionally 8 May 1063 in connection with Graus

Exact birth date is not required. Use an evidence-bounded age band rather than false precision.

## 5.2 Parentage and legitimacy context

**Opening disposition:** StronglySupported.

Ramiro was born outside Sancho III's principal marriage. That context affects genealogy and the historical partition, but it must not be turned into a universal personal incapacity or an opening legitimacy penalty.

Legitimacy is not capacity. Ramiro's practical power, recognition, and long rule are independently evidenced.

## 5.3 Founder dependency

**Opening disposition:** ConfirmedForOpening as a scenario condition.

Ramiro I is not the founder of the people, church, or all regional institutions. Ramiro is the founder-dependent center of the current royal union.

Functions concentrated in Ramiro include:

- recognized ruler;
- military command;
- distribution of castles and property;
- mediation among regions;
- dynastic representation;
- external diplomacy;
- protection of institutions;
- succession transmission.

The condition evaluates replacement by function rather than applying a generic post-death collapse modifier.

## 5.4 Opening spouse

**Opening disposition:** Provisional.

Ermesinda or Gisberga of Bigorre, mother of the legitimate Sancho, died before the opening in standard chronologies. Ramiro later married Agnes, commonly identified in modern summaries as Agnes of Aquitaine.

Required registry treatment:

- Ermesinda: HistoricalInactive if death before opening is confirmed.
- Agnes: ActiveCandidate only if identity, marriage, and January 1062 survival are sufficiently supported.
- No invented court influence or relationship state.
- Do not confuse Agnes with another Aquitanian or Burgundian Agnes.

## 5.5 Burial and memory

**Opening disposition:** LaterOutcome.

San Juan de la Peña is associated with Ramiro's burial in standard accounts. The burial itself is not opening state. Existing patronage or dynastic association may be opening-valid if independently evidenced.

# 6. Legitimate Sancho Ramírez

## 6.1 Identity

**Opening disposition:** ConfirmedForOpening.

The legitimate Sancho Ramírez is:

- son of Ramiro I;
- son of Ermesinda or Gisberga of Bigorre;
- distinct from the elder illegitimate son of the same name;
- alive on 1062-01-01;
- the expected successor;
- not yet opening ruler.

Approximate birth around 1042 to 1043 is acceptable as an evidence band.

## 6.2 Succession position

**Opening disposition:** StronglySupported.

The legitimate Sancho is the expected successor based on historical succession in 1063 and legitimate filiation. This does not mean:

- every regional oath already exists;
- every title transfers by one identical rule;
- every tenencia continues automatically;
- every external ruler recognizes the transfer;
- no collateral risk exists.

The Opening State should identify `ExpectedSuccessor`, not `GuaranteedFutureKing`.

## 6.3 Opening marriage to Isabella of Urgell

**Opening disposition:** Contested.

Modern summaries give dates ranging from 1062 or 1063 to approximately 1065. The difference matters because the scenario begins on 1062-01-01.

V1 decision:

- Do not initialize the marriage as ConfirmedForOpening.
- Create a ReviewOnly relationship and identity lock for Isabella of Urgell.
- Permit a pre-existing negotiation, intended match, or future emergence only if source research supports it.
- Do not initialize Peter I from the marriage unless birth after opening becomes runtime history.
- Do not treat later Urgell military cooperation as automatic marriage alliance.

## 6.4 Opening command role

**Opening disposition:** Provisional.

The heir is old enough for meaningful adult agency, but a specific independent command on 1062-01-01 requires evidence. Character Authority may initialize command eligibility and court access without inventing an active office.

## 6.5 Later career

**Opening disposition:** LaterOutcome.

Excluded from opening:

- succession in 1063;

- journey to Rome;
- papal relations;
- Roman-rite reform;
- Fuero de Jaca;

# 7. Illegitimate Sancho Ramírez

## 7.1 Identity

**Opening disposition:** ConfirmedForOpening as a separate person.

The elder illegitimate Sancho Ramírez is:

- son of Ramiro I;
- son of Amuña;
- firstborn son in sources that distinguish him from the legitimate heir;
- brother and namesake of the future king;
- alive in 1062;
- opening-relevant because of property, inheritance, and western commands.

## 7.2 Suspect 1049 charter

**Opening disposition:** Quarantined as sole evidence.

A charter placing both same-name sons together in 1049 has been treated as suspect. It may inform research but cannot alone establish office, title, or exact status.

## 7.3 Ramiro I's wills

**Opening disposition:** StronglySupported subject to diplomatic-edition validation.

Modern summaries report:

- a 1059 will including the elder Sancho among heirs under stated conditions;
- a 15 March 1061 will leaving Aibar and Javierrelatre with associated villages.

This supports an opening property and succession interface without making the person co-king or general heir to the kingdom.

## 7.4 Aibar and Sos

**Opening disposition:** Provisional for exact authority type and date span.

Modern prosopographic summaries associate the elder Sancho with:

- Aibar, 1061 to 1062;
- Sos, 1062.

The registry should reserve separate candidates for:

- person;
- Aibar site or jurisdiction;
- Sos site or jurisdiction;
- property rights;
- tenencia or delegated command;
- inherited estate;
- title if any.

Do not collapse the 1061 will, a tenencia list, and direct political sovereignty into one record.

## 7.5 Count of Ribagorza label

**Opening disposition:** LaterOutcome or display disambiguator only.

The later title is useful to distinguish the person in modern writing, but it must not enter opening title state unless dated evidence supports it.

## 7.6 Succession standing

**Opening disposition:** Contested but bounded.

The elder Sancho has meaningful dynastic standing, property, and possible conditional inheritance. The legitimate Sancho remains expected successor.

Do not initialize:

- automatic claimant hostility;
- rivalry;
- disloyalty;
- exclusion from all inheritance;

- hereditary property;
- a county;
- direct domain;
- vassalage;
- governorship over a modern province;
- personal loyalty;

## 12.2 Opening tenencia activation

**Opening disposition:** ReviewOnly until named evidence is validated.

The elder Sancho's Aibar and Sos associations are priority candidates. Other later commands must not be back-projected.

## 12.3 Castle-centered authority

**Opening disposition:** StronglySupported as strategic model.

Castles control movement, refuge, local military concentration, and portions of fiscal or judicial reach. Castle control does not automatically create whole-valley ownership.

## 12.4 Royal itinerary

**Opening disposition:** StronglySupported as governance behavior, not a constant click mechanic.

Ramiro's physical presence may affect assembly, justice, delegation review, and military readiness. The player sets regional priorities; runtime systems resolve routine movement.

# 15. Graus Historical Reconciliation

## 15.1 Opening site state

**Opening disposition:** External and strategically contested.

Graus is a strongpoint in the Cinca or Ésera frontier system. It blocks or constrains Aragonese expansion from Ribagorza toward the plains.

## 15.2 Earlier attack

**Opening disposition:** Provisional historical fact.

Reference synthesis commonly places an earlier failed Aragonese attempt in 1055. This is plausible as Campaign History and holder-specific memory if confirmed in specialist sources.

It must not create permanent revenge, inevitable war, or perfect intelligence.

## 15.3 Castile-Leon and Zaragoza

**Opening disposition:** StronglySupported strategic context.

Ferdinand I received parias from Zaragoza by the late 1050s in standard accounts. Aragonese attack could threaten Castilian revenue and protection interests. This supports a conditional Castilian intervention pathway.

## 15.4 Traditional battle date

**Opening disposition:** LaterOutcome with contested details.

The traditional date is 8 May 1063. Some historiography and later chronicles give different dating. The project may retain 1063 as the leading historical reference while refusing a fixed event trigger.

## 15.5 Ramiro I's death

**Opening disposition:** LaterOutcome, not scheduled.

A short record from the Alaón cartulary tradition reports Ramiro killed by Muslims in battle at Graus. Other accounts elaborate assassination or infiltration. The exact mechanism remains contested.

Runtime rule:

- Ramiro may die if a valid owner-resolved battle or lifecycle event produces that result.
- Ramiro may survive, avoid the expedition, delegate command, negotiate, or attack elsewhere.

## 15.6 Castilian Sancho

**Opening disposition:** Provisional participant in the traditional battle narrative; ReferencedExternal.

No opening commitment exists.

## 15.7 Rodrigo Díaz

**Opening disposition:** Quarantined as a guaranteed participant.

The attribution depends heavily on later or singular evidence and creates chronology and office questions. No opening relationship, command, or Graus commitment should be initialized from later fame.

## 15.8 Force numbers

**Opening disposition:** Rejected without specialist evidence.

Claims such as exactly 300 Castilian knights may remain source notes, not runtime payload or balancing inputs.

## 15.9 Graus opening pressure

Approved content direction:

- threat to southern expansion;
- prior failed pressure;
- possible Castilian protection of Zaragoza;
- uncertain enemy coalition;
- need for supply, siege capacity, and home garrisons;
- succession risk if Ramiro leads personally.

Rejected content direction:

- “Attack Graus by May 1063” mission;
- guaranteed defeat;
- guaranteed royal death;
- guaranteed Castilian or Rodrigo Díaz participation.
