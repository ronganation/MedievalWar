# 1. Concise Verdict

Initialize Aragon on 1 January 1062 as a compact, founder-dependent Pyrenean monarchy under Ramiro I. Jaca is the principal royal center, while Old Aragon, Sobrarbe, and Ribagorza remain distinct governance surfaces inside one realm configuration. The realm is recognized and strategically coherent, but practical authority is uneven, material depth is shallow, castle command remains partly unresolved, and external opportunity cannot be converted into scripted conquest.

This Opening State initializes campaign-start facts, active local records, bounded qualitative pressures, pathway eligibility, and non-runtime external locks. It does not initialize later outcomes, future offices, completed reforms, scripted wars, external private state, or any record whose registry status is `ReviewOnly`, `Quarantined`, `Rejected`, `Deprecated`, or unresolved.

Core player proposition: **Hold three mountain lands together while building enough dynastic, institutional, and material depth to survive the founder.**

The Opening State applies once when a new campaign is created. It never reapplies to, repairs, or overwrites an advanced save.

# 5. Opening Strategic Summary

## 5.1 Strengths

- Ramiro I is an active adult ruler and the principal political link among the three lands.
- Legitimate Sancho Ramírez provides an active dynastic and military-continuity path.
- Jaca provides a legible political center.
- The realm possesses a defensible mountain core and strategically important Pyrenean access.
- The Jiménez identity connects Aragon to a wider dynastic world without turning that world into one faction.
- Monasteries and the bishopric interface provide institutional channels for record, hospitality, patronage, mediation, and legitimacy.
- Historical pressure creates multiple plausible strategic directions rather than a single mission chain.

## 5.2 Pressures

- governance depends heavily on Ramiro's person;
- Old Aragon, Sobrarbe, and Ribagorza are not administratively interchangeable;
- the legitimate heir still requires command, recognition, and succession preparation;
- the elder Sancho has dynastic and property significance without a pre-scripted revolt;
- castle command and tenencia evidence remain incomplete;
- church jurisdiction and several officeholders remain unresolved;
- material depth, route capacity, forces, supply, property, and revenue are not yet validated;
- Pamplona, Castile-León, Zaragoza, Lleida, Urgell, Barcelona, Aquitaine, and papal interfaces remain externally owned;
- Graus and Barbastro remain conditional opportunities, not scheduled outcomes.

## 5.3 Immediate player concerns

- preserve founder continuity without freezing the succession result;
- prepare legitimate Sancho for command and recognition;
- decide how to handle the elder Sancho's standing and possible service;
- maintain workable authority across three governance surfaces;
- secure Jaca and the mountain routes;
- improve church and record capacity without inventing a centralized church;
- avoid military overreach unsupported by supply, access, and coalition consent;
- engage external actors without activating their private state.
