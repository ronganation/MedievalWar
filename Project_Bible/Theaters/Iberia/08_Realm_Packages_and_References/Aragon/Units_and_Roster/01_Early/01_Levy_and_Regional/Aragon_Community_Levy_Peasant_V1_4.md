- **Unit:** Aragonese Community Levy
- **Class:** Tier 1 mixed-weapon peasant infantry
- **Source:** Eligible, unreserved, released lowest-pool contributors from validated Aragonese communities, households, settlements, estates, valleys, camps, or supported sites
- **Availability:** Potentially broad but finite, source-owned, cooperation-dependent, equipment-limited, protected-minimum limited, and recovery-sensitive
- **Battle promise:** Cheap movable bodies that guard, obstruct, occupy low-demand frontage, protect camps and baggage, and hold prepared or constricted positions long enough for better troops to act
- **Best use:** Settlements, camps, baggage, stores, prisoners, labor areas, rear approaches, quiet sectors, prepared obstructions, secondary frontage, and emergency reinforcement
- **Worst use:** Main field line, fresh cavalry, concentrated missiles, disciplined infantry, complex maneuver, pursuit, assault, and distant campaigning
- **Strategic tradeoff:** Preserves qualified military pools but consumes broad household, labor, transport, watch, craft, and local-support capacity
- **Counterplay:** Shock, missiles, flanking, maneuver, isolation, leader loss, fatigue, removal from local support, and prolonged service
- **Roster progression:** Cheaper and weaker than Jaca Muster Spearmen; broader in equipment but less coherent, less durable, and not reliably anti-cavalry

## 5. Player-Facing Description

Aragonese Community Levy is the peasant infantry at the bottom of Aragon's roster: eligible household and community contributors gathered with simple spears, axes, clubs, staves, utility blades, scattered shields, and little protection. Compatible weapons are grouped under a recognizable leader, but the unit remains poorly drilled and fragile. Its ordinary value is useful work: guarding camps, baggage, stores, quiet approaches, and prepared positions while better soldiers handle the decisive fight.

A credible emergency may justify a larger or more hurried call from the same sources. That state raises civilian pressure and can reduce equipment consistency, leadership coverage, morale, and endurance. Emergency service is not the only way to recruit the card. Ordinary safe recruitment does not require an enemy at the gates.

The formation can move, join an army, enter a fort, and return where its service and support permit. Prolonged campaigning is a costly, unreliable choice, not its intended role. Survivors may learn to assemble, guard, rally, and withdraw more effectively, but experience does not turn mixed peasant infantry into spear militia or Serjeants.

High Era stores, inspection, low-cost weapons, clothing, leadership, and records can improve its presentation and usefulness. Poor or damaged communities may retain an Early-style appearance. The family persists under different political outcomes without being duplicated or automatically replaced.

## 23. Strengths

- Fast relative local assembly after credible warning.
- Broad potential emergency source after protected minimums.
- Useful numbers in prepared settlement and constricted positions.
- Low specialist equipment floor.
- Familiarity with local streets, paths, approaches, stores, and fallback points.
- Can absorb secondary tasks and preserve better troops for decisive roles.
- Can protect civilians and supplies during raids or siege preparation.
- Meaningful morale context when defending a visible local objective.

## 24. Weaknesses

- Poor order, drill, facing, and battlefield recovery.
- No reliable anti-cavalry brace or main-line identity.
- Very uneven weapons, shields, protection, and footwear.
- Fragile under missiles, shock, flanking, fire, or leader loss.
- Very low campaign endurance and expeditionary suitability.
- Weak pursuit, assault, and complex maneuver.
- Serious household and civilian cost if service is repeated, extended, or bloody.
- Losses damage future levy, labor, transport, warning, and community recovery.
