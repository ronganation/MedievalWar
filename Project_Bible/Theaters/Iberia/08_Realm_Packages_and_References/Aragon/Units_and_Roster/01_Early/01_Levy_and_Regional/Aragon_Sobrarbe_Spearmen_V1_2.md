Spearmen of Sobrarbe are a regionally sourced spear family designed for passes, steep approaches, constrained frontage, broken ground, and flank anchoring.

The card survives only if Sobrarbe's dispersed sources, assembly friction, formation behavior, terrain mission, and open-ground limitations create a different tactical and campaign decision from Jaca Muster Spearmen and Men of Old Aragon.

## 26A. Early-to-High Pass-Defense Carryover

This is proposed project design, not certification of a historical corps. The family remains useful when the High Era arrives because constrained approaches, crossings, and relief routes still need spear defense. Development improves actual equipment, command, preparation, and support; it does not erase dispersed sources or make every hill favorable.

```yaml
EarlyRegionalBaseline:
  equipment: ServiceableSpearsShieldsAndMixedSourceValidProtection
  organization: LocalFilesStagedThroughActualValleyAndPassConnections
  mission: BlockStepAnchorHandOffAndFallBackByPosition
HighDevelopedRegionalDefense:
  equipment: BetterMaintainedIssueWeatherProtectionAndRepairWhereAvailable
  organization: MoreReliableSignalsStagingReliefPracticeAndReturnSupport
  mission: SameConstrainedApproachRoleWithBetterPreparedPositionCoordination
HighDepletedDefense:
  equipment: OlderOrIncompleteIssueMayPersist
  organization: LostCadresContactsOrRoutesReduceCommonControl
  mission: SmallerSupportedPositionLocalDefenseOrRecoveryAccordingToActualReadiness
calendar_creates_improvement: false
technology_creates_people_equipment_routes_or_knowledge: false
experience_alone_creates_new_card: false
automatic_obsolescence: false
universal_mountain_bonus: false
```

A better-supported High Era formation can prepare and hand off positions more reliably, but a blocked fallback, unfamiliar route, or lost signal still defeats that advantage. Equipment improvements do not grant broad open-field depth, heavy assault, or immunity to missiles and bypass.

Signature cycle:

1. Confirm that the mission genuinely involves a pass, approach, crossing, relief route, strongpoint exterior, or broken-ground flank.
2. Validate primary, secondary, fallback, rally, and return positions.
3. Concentrate dispersed sources by stages instead of demanding impossible instant regional assembly.
4. Assign files to terrain sections whose gaps and flanks are actually understood.
5. Occupy the primary block and preserve javelin, bow, messenger, and withdrawal lanes.
6. Channel the enemy into the defended approach rather than advancing onto broad ground.
7. Reface or step individual files only while communication and mutual support survive.
8. Hand off a threatened section or fall back by position before local isolation becomes general collapse.
9. Reassemble at the secondary or rally position.
10. Pursue only briefly and locally when the route and mission require it.
11. Return people, equipment, guides, route capacity, and local-defense duties through source owners.
