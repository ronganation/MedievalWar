
- **Unit:** Jaca Bowmen
- **Class:** Upper-Early organized bow infantry candidate
- **Source:** Selected qualified bow users and equipment from exact sources that can legitimately assemble, train, receive issue, and serve through a validated Jaca-facing interface
- **Availability:** Narrower than Aragonese Bowmen, dependent on concentration, leadership, stores, compatible equipment, ammunition handling, practice, routes, protection, and recovery
- **Battle promise:** Provide a more reliable protected bow line with better fire control, target discipline, local repositioning, ammunition accounting, and orderly withdrawal than the broad basic bow family
- **Best use:** Main army bow support, prepared approaches, protected sustained defense, controlled volleys, reserve ammunition management, and coordinated withdrawal behind Aragon’s infantry
- **Worst use:** Unsupported skirmishing, cavalry contact, professional long-range dueling, armor destruction as a primary role, close assault, or service after the concentration and support interface fails
- **Strategic tradeoff:** Concentrates scarce shooters, leaders, bows, strings, arrows, stores, carriers, repair, training time, routes, and local defensive capacity into a narrower formation
- **Counterplay:** Cavalry, shields, armor, counterfire, forced movement, wet strings, disrupted stores or routes, broken protection, ammunition exhaustion, and loss of leaders or concentration capacity
- **Special boundary:** Jaca is an assembly and support interface. Each person, bow, arrow stock, leader, craft source, route, and local-defense cost retains its actual owner.

Signature cycle:

1. Select qualified sources and validate Jaca-facing assembly, issue, practice, and return capacity.
2. Inspect and group shooters, bows, strings, arrows, leaders, and carriers.
3. Establish protected sections, firing lanes, reserve ammunition, and withdrawal routes.
4. Hold fire until target and range justify expenditure.
5. Use controlled independent fire or simple volleys according to actual readiness.
6. Concentrate one section briefly against an exposed, compressed, or disrupted target.
7. Shift local sections as lanes close while preserving friendly safety and command.
8. Resupply only from actual reserve lots and carriers.
9. Withdraw by sections before cavalry or formed infantry reaches the bow line.
10. Rally, account, and return surviving sources and property through their real owners.

## 21A. Early-to-High Organized Bow Carryover

This is proposed campaign design, not certification of a uniform historical corps. Jaca Bowmen persist as the organized bow rung alongside broad basic Bowmen and separate Crossbowmen. Their distinguishing investment is recurring concentration, leaders, stores, collective practice, section control, and return arrangements, not an automatic superior bow type.
