- **Best use:** Behind Jaca or regional spears, protected by Aragonese Serjeants, defending bounded approaches, reinforcing strongpoints, supporting sieges, and firing into enemies fixed by terrain or friendly frontage
- **Worst use:** Unsupported open frontage, running missile duels, rapid redeployment, cavalry exposure, interrupted reload, close melee, deep pursuit, or long action without bolt and maintenance support

- **Exact values:** None promoted

### 2A. Player-Facing Description and Total War Identity
Aragonese Crossbowmen are Aragon's deliberately developed High Era crossbow formation, assembled only when a real native adoption path can sustain trained operators, serviceable weapons, compatible bolts, leaders, maintenance, protected reload practice, and field release. They are not upgraded Bowmen, imported specialists wearing Aragonese colors, or an automatic reward for reaching a date or technology.

On the battlefield, the unit rewards preparation rather than constant motion. Establish a clear lane behind spears or Serjeants, inspect the formation, hold the loaded shot for a valuable commitment, loose with discipline, and use the protecting line to survive the reload. If cavalry, fast infantry, counterfire, congestion, or a collapsing frontage closes the safe window, the correct play is to withdraw, reform, and preserve the scarce operators and weapons.

The card's identity is **concentrated native adoption pressure**. It should feel more deliberate, scarce, and materially demanding than Aragonese Bowmen, but more field-flexible than any future pavise-specialist branch. Its strength comes from controlled target selection and combined-arms positioning, not universal armor penetration, elite melee ability, or rapid fire.

Battle rhythm:

1. Secure a protected firing position behind spears, Serjeants, terrain, walls, or a bounded approach.
2. Establish bolts, loading depth, leaders, target priorities, and withdrawal.
3. Load before the decisive enemy commitment where possible.
4. Hold fire against low-value or poorly presented targets.
5. Loose into a dense, protected, fixed, exposed, or otherwise valuable target.
6. Use the protecting frontage to survive the reload phase.
7. Withdraw or reposition before cavalry, skirmishers, or melee collapse the firing cycle.
8. Preserve trained operators and serviceable weapons instead of accepting futile combat.
