## 1. Strong Unit Verdict

**Aragonese Pavise Crossbowmen** are a later protected crossbow formation built around linked operator, pavise bearer, bolt, transport, maintenance, and firing-position capability. The card exists because the player can trade movement, flexibility, deployment speed, transport capacity, and recovery cost for a position that protects crossbow reload and preserves deliberate bolt pressure under missile threat. It is not merely Aragonese Crossbowmen with shields attached.

## 2. One-Screen Total War Player Card

- **Unit:** Aragonese Pavise Crossbowmen
- **Class:** Later protected crossbow infantry
- **Source:** Qualified Aragonese crossbow operators, pavise bearers or handlers, leaders, compatible weapons, bolts, spanning gear, pavises, carts or pack transport, stores, maintenance, deployment practice, protection, and recovery
- **Battle promise:** Establish a directional protected firing position, absorb manageable frontal missile pressure, sustain deliberate bolt fire through the reload cycle, and force the enemy to flank, suppress, assault, or abandon the approach
- **Best use:** Bounded approaches, crossings, streets, gates, siege lines, defended field edges, counter-missile positions, objectives, and battles where Aragon can protect both flanks and the withdrawal route
- **Worst use:** Rapid maneuver, pursuit, scattered skirmishing, cavalry-dominated open ground, surprise contact before deployment, attacks from several directions, broken terrain that prevents setup, and melee after the position is penetrated
- **Strategic tradeoff:** Consumes crossbow operators, pavise handlers, shields, bolts, transport, maintenance, tools, stores, deployment time, and route capacity that ordinary Crossbowmen do not require at the same level
- **Counterplay:** Outflank the protected face, attack during setup or packing, use artillery or heavy missiles where applicable, force repeated displacement, break transport and bolt supply, assault from several directions, use cavalry against exposed sides, and close after suppression
- **Opening State:** Unavailable on January 1, 1062
- **Exact values:** None promoted

## 3. Player Fantasy, Description, and Flavor

**Player fantasy:** Choose the ground, raise the pavises, load behind the shield line, and make the enemy pay for advancing through a controlled lane. Once the formation is established, ordinary missile pressure is no longer enough to silence every reload. The enemy must maneuver, concentrate fire, seize the flanks, or force an assault. The player must also know when to abandon the position. Pavises that cannot be packed or routes that cannot be cleared turn protection into a trap.

**Short description:** Advanced Aragonese crossbow teams who trade mobility and flexibility for directional protection, safer reloads, and sustained bolt pressure from prepared positions.

**Encyclopedia description:** Aragonese crossbow development may eventually produce formations capable of carrying and coordinating large protective shields with operators, bolts, spanning gear, tools, stores, and transport. The pavise does not make the formation invulnerable. It protects a direction and requires ground, deployment, bearers, signals, spacing, and continued flank security. Operators still depend on serviceable weapons, compatible bolts, trained spanning, maintenance, and a safe withdrawal. When these parts work together, the formation can withstand missile exchanges and sustain crossbow pressure that would force ordinary crossbowmen to retire. When the position is bypassed, compressed, turned, or assaulted, the same equipment becomes burdensome.

**Flavor line:** Set the shields. Load behind them. Make the road costly.

## 12. Signature Battle Cycle: Carry, Deploy, Load, Hold, Loose, Recover, Rotate, Pack or Defend

1. Carry or transport pavises, crossbows, bolts, tools, stores, and support to suitable ground.
2. Inspect the position, flanks, firing lane, withdrawal, equipment, and enemy threat.
3. **Deploy** pavises directionally with actual time, people, ground, and spacing.
4. Establish firing, reload, reserve, maintenance, and casualty passages.
5. **Span and load** behind protection using validated methods.
6. **Hold** loaded shots against poor targets while accepting position and fatigue costs.
7. **Loose** by file or controlled volley into a valuable lane or commitment.
8. **Recover and reload** behind surviving protection.
9. **Rotate** files only when spacing, teams, bolts, leaders, and practice permit.
10. **Pack and withdraw** before flank collapse where time and route permit, or defend the position if escape is no longer possible.
