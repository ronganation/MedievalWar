Ricohombres of Aragon are a later concentration of politically released noble riders, retinues, selected horses, equipment, standards, and practiced reserve action. They are neither upgraded Infanzones nor a renamed Royal Mesnada.

### 1A. What the player actually buys

The card is not a generic knight block and not an infantry-killing button. It is a politically assembled, relatively cohesive **noble mounted battle group** able to convert one locally favorable engagement into a sustained decision: absorb or answer a mounted threat, hit a flank or exposed formation, keep enough order to fight briefly after impact, and disengage or reform for a second mission. Its advantage over the Infanzones is not simply higher charge damage. The developed retinue has a deeper leadership layer, more compatible equipment and horse support, and a better chance of remaining useful after first contact. Those qualities must be achieved by actual sources and practice, not bestowed by the unit name.

The player's signature choice is whether to commit the noble files as a **battle-winning wing** now, keep them as an insurance reserve against the enemy's uncommitted horse, or use them to restore a failing sector. A successful charge into prepared spears is not their fantasy; a timed intervention that changes the balance of a contested battle is.

### 2A. Aragonese battlefield personality

A developed Aragonese host has a practical problem: its infantry and missile arms can hold, disorder, and constrain an enemy, while Riders and Jinetes can discover, screen, and provoke, but neither of those lighter mounted families should be asked to win a sustained contest with serious opposing cavalry. Infanzones can meet an earlier or smaller mounted crisis; the Royal Mesnada cannot be spent routinely without exposing the ruler. Ricohombres of Aragon fill the later field-army gap: **a nonroyal, politically expensive mounted striking wing that can fight for a local decision after impact**.

On the map this is a coalition of obligations and households, not a permanent national regiment. On the battlefield it should nevertheless read as one formation because its leaders have established a shared mission, a common signal and standard, and a tested method for aligning, charging, recovering contact, and returning to reserve. If those links fail, the card remains physically present but loses its defining discipline.

### 3A. Relative combat ceiling, without invented numbers

Against ordinary mounted service cavalry, the developed formation should be favored when it can choose the engagement and its rider-horse pairs are ready. Against a well-supported heavy cavalry peer, outcome depends on angle, local support, command, and fatigue; nobility does not grant automatic superiority. Against infantry already engaged by Aragonese spears or disrupted by missiles, it has a meaningful opportunity to deliver shock and then press the breach. Against a braced spear line on its own frontage, it should lose the very advantage for which the player paid.

The **ceiling** is a strong High Era battle cavalry card, not a Late Era gendarme and not an all-purpose elite. The **floor** when service is disputed, retinues are missing, or horses are unsuitable is a costly partial mounted contingent that should not be presented as fully ready.

### 4A. Short in-game description

**Ricohombres of Aragon**: Developed noble cavalry drawn from Aragonese households and their released mounted followings. Held behind the battle line, they countercharge dangerous horsemen, strike exposed flanks, and fight through a local opening before reforming. Strong in a decisive mounted contest, but scarce, politically costly, and vulnerable to prepared spears, missiles, and prolonged isolation.

**Strength labels:** Developed shock; cavalry contest; follow-through; reform. **Limit labels:** Noble-service release; expensive horses and retinues; dangerous overcommitment. These are qualitative design labels, not numeric modifiers.

## 5. Player-Facing Description and Combat Fantasy

Ricohombres of Aragon bring politically concentrated noble riders and household support into a controlled mounted reserve. Their strength comes from timing and cohesion rather than automatic superiority. Hold them behind the line, commit them against a real crisis or exposed target, press only while the local advantage lasts, and reform before prestige turns into overextension.

**Combat fantasy:** Standards rise behind the Aragonese line. The noble files align, countercharge the committed enemy, break the local threat, then pull back through a protected lane to reform for the next decisive moment.

### 5A. Representative battlefield vignette

The Aragonese center has fixed an enemy infantry body, but hostile cavalry waits beyond its right. The player leaves the Ricohombres screened behind the right-center rather than charging the pinned infantry immediately. Jinetes draw the opposing horse toward the flank; Aragonese Riders report the movement and fall away. Once the hostile cavalry is committed, the Ricohombres countercharge from a protected approach, win room for the friendly line, and turn against the already disordered infantry flank. They do **not** chase the first fleeing file across the map. A subleader recalls the files to the standard while the horses still have enough condition to reform. The result is not free kills; it is an opened sector bought with rider, horse, and political risk.

If the hostile cavalry refuses the bait, the player can hold the Ricohombres as insurance or choose a separate, supported flank attack. The enemy has agency; the unit's identity is the quality of the decision, not a scripted reaction.

A candidate source can contribute a complete rider-horse-equipment group, only riders, a leader and his following, or horse and support capacity. These are not interchangeable. ### 12A. Retinue depth as a battlefield mechanism

Retinue support is not a hidden stat bonus. It gives the formation identifiable subleaders who can keep a file together when the leading noble is engaged, attendants who maintain horses and equipment between commitments, and messengers or standards that carry recall and reform signals. A complete retinue can help a noble group maintain its shape through the transition from impact to short melee and back to reserve. Losing that support narrows the safe order set: a damaged formation might still protect a flank but no longer execute a coordinated second charge.

Combatant retainers, if any, count as actual riders with horses and gear; foot attendants do not silently appear as mounted combatants. A lord's household may supply one role but not every role. The card can have mixed provenance without claiming identical noble entourages.

### 13A. Horse quality is achieved, not a breed label

A horse capable of carrying an armored rider into controlled contact must be assessed for condition, load, temperament, training, and tack fit. Better horse access can increase the unit's confidence in alignment and follow-through, but a high-quality animal in poor condition should not perform like a fresh one. Pair familiarity matters particularly during the **break-off and reform** that distinguish this card from a one-impact levy. A replacement horse restores physical mobility first; charge timing and rider-horse coordination return only through integration.

Separate horse losses from rider losses. Captured or surviving horses retain an owner and may be injured, unsuitable, or already reserved. A defeated noble formation cannot simply buy a full remount pool from the treasury.

The intended High Era silhouette is a compact mounted line with lances as the principal charge read where supported, shields and sidearms for contact after impact, and visibly varied protection that reflects actual household resources. Helmet, mail, textile defense, and later period-valid additions are candidates subject to source and era review; no single universal kit is declared here. Damaged tack and lost standards matter because they compromise alignment, recall, and reform.

### 16A. The noble command problem

A multi-household formation has a distinctive vulnerability: capable riders may disagree over precedence or the duration of service even while sharing a battlefield. Before commitment, the army command assigns the objective and the recognized formation leader establishes the charge signal, stop line, reserve position, and succession order. A subleader may preserve a surviving file after the first impact, but cannot conjure absent riders or negate a household recall.

In battle, command failure should produce a **specific loss of coordinated behavior**, not a generic morale penalty: files charge at different moments, press beyond the agreed line, or return to separate household standards. This is the meaningful downside of concentrating noble sources.

### 17A. Formation choices and the moment of contact

**Reserve line:** compact enough to respond, far enough back to avoid unnecessary missile exposure. **Countercharge files:** align to meet an already committed mounted threat without colliding with friendly infantry. **Controlled shock line:** choose an open approach against a flank, rear, or disrupted frontage. **Press formation:** keep a local advantage for a short melee, with subleaders preventing the wing from dissolving into individual pursuit. **Reform on standards:** recover facing, riders, and spacing behind friendly cover.

A wedge, if later supported by evidence and shared formation rules, is not the card's default special ability. A charge cannot be ordered at full quality through a narrow gate, steep broken ground, or a friendly formation occupying the lane.

## 18. Signature Battle Cycle

1. Assess mission, ground, enemy cavalry, spears, missiles, reserves, and friendly line.
2. Hold as a valuable mounted reserve or protected relief force.
3. Move toward the threatened sector without premature exposure.
4. Select countercharge, controlled shock, command protection, or line relief.
5. Form, align, and commit under leaders and standards.
6. Deliver charge or countercharge without guaranteed penetration or rout.
7. Press only while target, support, horse condition, cohesion, and exit remain favorable.
8. Break off, reform, rally, or recommit.
9. Pursue only when the enemy is genuinely broken and reserve risk passes.
10. Recover riders, horses, retinue, equipment, captives, ransom obligations, and political continuity.

### 18A. Two genuinely different winning cycles

**Defensive countercharge:** hold behind infantry, observe enemy horse commitment, strike the committed flank or approach, stop the enemy's mounted advantage, then recover before a fresh reserve can trap the Ricohombres. This is not unique by itself: Infanzones and the Royal Mesnada also countercharge. The Ricohombres' developed distinction is the potential to maintain organized files and **continue into a controlled local follow-through** if the situation supports it.

**Offensive battle-wing action:** allow infantry and missiles to fix or disorder a target while lighter horse screens the approach; bring the noble wing through a viable lane; strike, assess whether the target is actually yielding, and either press a bounded melee or break off. A failed initial impact does not automatically trigger a second charge. Both cycles end with an accounting of horse condition, lance loss, leadership, and political service capacity.

### 20A. Orders should feel different in play

**Hold the wing** protects the expensive option but can allow an enemy to win elsewhere. **Countercharge committed horse** trades reaction speed for certainty that the enemy has already chosen a line. **Shock the exposed flank** can decide the battle, but only if infantry and lighter cavalry have created a genuine opening. **Press locally** is the distinctive High Era option: the formation stays in a short, leader-controlled mounted melee to exploit a breach, at increasing fatigue and flank risk. **Reform on the standard** sacrifices immediate pursuit to recover order and preserve the option of another mission. **Protect withdrawal** turns the card from offensive wing into a rearward shield, reducing its freedom to exploit.

The UI should communicate why an order is weakened rather than simply gray it out. Example: “Charge lane open; retinue leader missing; coordinated press unavailable.”

### 26A. The actual High Era rung

**Aragonese Riders** make contact, screen, escort, and pursue. **Jinetes** pressure through mounted missiles and refusal. **Infanzones** provide the earlier politically sensitive countercharge and limited shock. **Royal Frontier Sergeants** ride to a developing crisis, relieve, rescue, separate a lighter breakthrough, and reform without owning developed noble heavy shock. **Royal Mesnada** protects royal command and is held for an exceptional royal decision. **Ricohombres of Aragon** are the later nonroyal field-army wing: a developed multi-household mounted formation that can countercharge, shock a prepared opportunity, **fight briefly through it**, and reform. **Gendarmes**, if admitted, require a separate later institution and equipment/command constitution.

### 27A. What “shared recruitment” actually means for this card

This is **not** a unit that becomes a Barcelona native unit because a union or conquest banner appears. The Aragonese household source remains in Aragon. Under independent development, Aragon may mature the noble wing without Barcelona. Under negotiated union, a Barcelona-led army might request an Aragonese noble contingent if the exact source agrees, the political compact allows it, and horses, equipment, leaders, route, and return pass. The player sees an **Ricohombres of Aragon** card with Aragonese replacement and recovery, not a Barcelona copy. Conversely, Aragon may use an eligible Barcelona formation under its own source terms.

Under domination, the controlling side may demand service, but a reluctant noble household can limit or refuse effective release, require garrison security, or produce unreliable service. Aragon dominating Barcelona does not create extra Ricohombres of Aragon; Barcelona dominating Aragon does not erase them. If the relationship breaks, future access can close while an already mustered contingent resolves its actual service and return. These are candidate unit interfaces under the controlling three-path authorities, not invented universal unlock thresholds.

### 42A. Explicit pass/fail scenarios

1. **Early countercharge parity:** Infanzones can answer a limited threat; Nobles should justify their greater political and material cost by sustaining an organized local follow-through, not by guaranteed one-hit rout.
2. **Royal reserve parity:** Royal Mesnada protects the ruler and can intervene exceptionally; Nobles should not inherit its protection floor or become its cheaper duplicate.
3. **Heavy-cavalry peer:** a fresh, supported peer can defeat or check the Ricohombres. Nobility alone does not determine outcome.
4. **Prepared spears:** frontal charge fails to provide reliable value and may incur severe horse and leader loss.
5. **Two-stage engagement:** after first contact, a well-led, well-supported noble wing can choose a short press or a disciplined reform; a depleted one cannot.
6. **Union access:** a Barcelona army can receive an eligible Aragonese contingent only when exact source, service, command, and physical capacity pass; donor capacity falls accordingly.
7. **Domination and reversal:** political control can change access and reliability without creating or deleting physical people, horses, or retinues.

If scenarios 1 and 5 do not produce a distinctive decision, the separate card fails irrespective of art quality.

The player holds this politically expensive formation behind the army, commits it against the decisive mounted contest or exposed flank, converts successful impact into a short controlled press, then recalls the household files to their standards before pursuit, rival command, horse fatigue, enemy reserves, or blocked reform turns victory into a strategic loss.

The family is a plausible *gameplay* expression of later noble mounted service, not a certified historical corps. The genealogical source is not an equipment register; the mod preview is not a military primary source. The player-facing card, tactical orders, and scenario examples above are explicit project design proposals. Exact family membership, historical title, lance technique, armor mix, service duration, and chronology remain held.
