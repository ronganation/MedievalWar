**Pioneers of the Royal Host** are selected labor-capable infantry, tool specialists, experienced campaign servants, construction hands, obstacle-clearers, carpenters, diggers, road workers, miners where actually validated, guards, and practical close-protection files organized to make a blocked battlefield usable for the Aragon-led Royal Host.

Their value is not superior melee power. Pioneers prepare an approach, fill or bridge a small obstruction, cut or dismantle a barrier, reinforce a passage, stabilize a breach edge, establish a temporary protected working point, and hand the opened ground to the formation that actually owns the fight. The unit becomes vulnerable when treated as generic assault infantry, held in prolonged melee, or required to solve several engineering problems at once.

## 3. Player-Facing Description

**Short description:** Selected Royal Host workers, practical soldiers, skilled hands, guards, and tool crews tasked with opening approaches and passages under protection. Prepare roads, crossings, fieldworks, barricades, gates, breach edges, and withdrawal routes, then hand the ground to Spearmen, Sergeants, or another proper holding formation.

**Combat fantasy:** Spearmen shield the working frontage while Crossbowmen suppress an exposed lane. The Pioneers mark a blocked approach, move tools and materials forward, dismantle the obstruction in bounded stages, brace the opening, and call the Sergeants to control the handoff. The Royal Host gains a usable passage, but only if the workers, tool train, protection, and withdrawal route survive.

**Flavor line:** **Mark the work. Guard the hands. Open the way. Hand over the ground.**

## 4. One-Screen Player Card

- **Unit:** Pioneers of the Royal Host
- **Class:** Selected fieldworks and passage infantry
- **Battle promise:** Turn one blocked route, obstacle, crossing, field position, or breach edge into usable Royal Host ground
- **Best use:** roads, bridges, fords, camps, siege approaches, gates, barricades, broad streets, breach edges, protected withdrawal routes, and immediate consolidation
- **Worst use:** unsupported assault, cavalry exposure, missile kill zones, prolonged melee, deep pursuit, several simultaneous work sites, or work without a receiving formation
- **Strategic value:** gives the Domination army practical battlefield access and fieldwork capability without granting an elite assault unit
- **Strategic cost:** consumes skilled labor, guards, tools, timber, rope, carts, pack animals, draft capacity, repair, local works, and political compliance
- **Opening State:** unavailable January 1, 1062
- **Exact values:** none promoted

## 14. Signature Work Cycle

```text
INSPECT the obstacle, approach, crossing, field position, or breach edge.
MARK one bounded task and identify the required protection, tools, materials, and exit.
MOVE workers and stores through a controlled passage.
COVER the worksite with infantry, missiles, terrain, or portable protection.
OPEN, FILL, CUT, BRACE, or STABILIZE only the assigned objective.
CONSOLIDATE the immediate result against collapse or enemy reentry.
HAND OFF the opened ground to Spearmen, Sergeants, Wardens, or another proper receiver.
WITHDRAW workers, wounded, tools, carts, records, and recoverable materials.
ACCOUNT for source loss, tool loss, local work debit, and political burden.
```

### 43L. Combined-Arms Mature Scenarios

**Protected ditch crossing:** Royal Host Spearmen establish a guarded frontage while Crossbowmen suppress exposed defenders. The Pioneers fill and brace one infantry lane through a dry ditch. The lane is useful for foot traffic but not automatically safe for cavalry or carts. Sergeants control the first relief movement, and the Pioneers withdraw before the enemy counterattacks.

**Barricaded street:** The Pioneers work against one selected barricade under shield and missile protection. Cutting files open the structure while handling files remove material from the traffic lane. If defenders counterenter before the opening is braced, guards and consolidation files can hold briefly, but sustained fighting requires Aragonese Serjeants, Wardens, or another receiver.

**Breach-edge support:** The breach already exists. Pioneers clear loose rubble, mark unsafe edges, and widen an immediate foot passage. They do not create the breach and do not replace the Companyia de la Santa Creu as the dedicated objective breaker.

**Emergency withdrawal route:** A Royal Host position must be abandoned. Pioneers clear blocked traffic, mark a fallback lane, and reinforce one weak passage while Sergeants separate formations and wounded. The mission succeeds if recoverable bodies pass. Holding the route until every asset escapes may destroy the Pioneers.

**Failed mounted lane:** A passage wide enough for infantry is opened, but wet fill and unstable edges cannot bear formed cavalry. Lancers of the Royal Host must wait, use another route, or accept severe risk. The physical result is not upgraded merely because mounted exploitation is desired.
