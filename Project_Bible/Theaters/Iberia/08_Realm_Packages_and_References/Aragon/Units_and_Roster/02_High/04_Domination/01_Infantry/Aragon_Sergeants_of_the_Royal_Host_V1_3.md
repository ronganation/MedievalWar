**Sergeants of the Royal Host** are the selected foot officers, experienced service soldiers, passage guards, file leaders, and practical close-order infantry who make an Aragon-dominated mixed army function under pressure. They do not replace native Aragonese or compliant Barcelona formations. They stand at threatened passages, missile positions, road junctions, occupation gates, withdrawal points, and weak joins between contingents, then impose enough order for the wider host to fight, move, or disengage.

The card's strength is **local command-backed relief**. The player commits it to one failing sector, one blocked passage, one exposed missile line, or one dangerous withdrawal. Its weakness is scarcity. Every detachment used to reinforce a position leaves fewer sergeants available to organize another sector, protect the Crossbowmen of the Royal Host, control prisoners and stores, maintain an occupation route, or preserve the army's fallback.

### 2A. What the player buys

- a limited professionalizing cadre rather than a broad line unit;
- the ability to repair one local command or passage crisis;
- protection and extraction for expensive missile or compliant formations;
- stronger occupation-line coordination;
- a temporary sector-assumption tool;
- no free morale aura, no universal command bonus, and no replacement for native infantry.

## 3. Player-Facing Description

**Short description:** Selected foot sergeants and experienced service soldiers appointed to keep the Aragon-led Royal Host functioning. Secure firing passages, relieve a threatened sector, separate confused contingents, protect an ordered withdrawal, and reform behind a new line. Dependable in a local crisis, but too scarce to become the army's ordinary frontline.

**Combat fantasy:** A compliant spear body begins to recoil and blocks the crossbow passage behind it. Royal Host Sergeants move into the join, open a lane, hold the pressure long enough for the missile files to withdraw, then turn control of the sector back to a native formation. Elsewhere, another crisis goes unanswered because the sergeant reserve has already been spent.

**Flavor line:** **Open the passage. Take the strain. Bring the host out in order.**

## 4. One-Screen Player Card

- **Unit:** Sergeants of the Royal Host
- **Class:** Selected medium command-and-relief infantry
- **Battle promise:** Restore order, frontage, passage, or withdrawal at one critical point in the domination army
- **Best use:** behind mixed infantry, beside Royal Host crossbows, at gates, roads, bridgeheads, camp entrances, reserve passages, and threatened occupation sectors
- **Worst use:** broad frontage, repeated frontal assault, deep pursuit, prolonged heavy melee, unsupported missile exposure, or several simultaneous crises
- **Strategic value:** turns a politically uneven host into a more usable army without making every contributor professional
- **Strategic cost:** consumes experienced service soldiers, officers, equipment, pay, command attention, garrison capacity, and trusted personnel
- **Opening State:** unavailable January 1, 1062
- **Exact values:** none promoted

## 35K. Field, Siege, Settlement, and Occupation Scenarios

### Mixed field line

The Sergeants remain behind the join between an Aragonese core and a compliant contingent. They intervene only when the shared passage or frontage begins to fail, then return the sector instead of replacing both bodies.

### Crossbow extraction

A Royal Host crossbow sector has released and is reloading when its protecting infantry bends. The Sergeants open the recovery passage, take short contact, and escort operators, weapons, bolts, and records toward a second position. Success depends on timing, not an invulnerability effect.

### Siege-camp entrance

The unit controls engineers, carts, wounded personnel, missiles, and relief infantry moving through a camp entrance. Holding the entrance too long risks trapping the same traffic the Sergeants exist to organize.

### Occupied gate

The Sergeants separate military passage from civilian, supply, prisoner, and administrative traffic. An uprising, sortie, or relief attack may force a choice between holding the gate and preserving the Royal Host reserve.

### Failed bridgehead handoff

Outgoing files and incoming Sergeants collide under missile pressure. The unit may abort, force an emergency insertion, or become intermixed. No relief order guarantees a clean transfer.
