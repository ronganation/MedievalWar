**Spearmen of the Royal Host** are the broad line infantry of an Aragon-dominated composite army. The formation assembles exact Aragonese and compliant spear-and-shield sources into separately led royal sectors. It can cover more useful frontage than a narrow selected formation, protect the Crossbowmen of the Royal Host, hold roads, gates, camps, bridgeheads, and occupation lines, and relieve one source sector with another when passages and command permit.

The card does not turn every contributor into the same soldier. Each source retains its people, equipment, leaders, service terms, home-defense obligations, and recovery needs. The Royal Host provides common mission, sector assignment, signals, passage planning, reserve priorities, and burden accounting. The player gains breadth and coordination, but every extra sector increases incompatibility, leadership, political, supply, and relief risk.

## 3. Player-Facing Description

**Short description:** Mixed spear-and-shield contingents organized into Royal Host sectors. Cover broad frontage, protect crossbows, receive ordinary frontal pressure, relieve strained sectors, and withdraw by source before unequal losses fracture the line.

**Combat fantasy:** Aragonese files anchor the royal standard while two compliant spear sectors extend the frontage around the Crossbowmen of the Royal Host. One sector begins to buckle under sustained pressure. The player can transfer reserve files, ask another source to assume more frontage, call the Sergeants of the Royal Host to organize a clean relief, or yield the threatened ground before the compliant contingent is ruined.

**Flavor line:** **Dress the sectors. Keep the passage. Let no source bear the whole battle.**

## 4. One-Screen Player Card

- **Unit:** Spearmen of the Royal Host
- **Class:** Broad composite spear-and-shield line infantry
- **Battle promise:** Cover several connected sectors and shift pressure between them without pretending the mixed host is one uniform regiment
- **Best use:** conventional frontage, missile protection, roads, camps, bridgeheads, gates, broad streets, second positions, and occupation lines
- **Worst use:** independent assault, rapid pursuit, dense broken ground, unprepared flank contact, prolonged unequal sacrifice, and simultaneous pressure across every sector
- **Strategic value:** converts several exact spear sources into one more usable Royal Host line
- **Strategic cost:** consumes people, spears, shields, leaders, local defense, service compliance, passages, transport, repair, and replacement from every contributing source
- **Opening State:** unavailable January 1, 1062
- **Exact values:** none promoted

## 35Z. Combined-Arms Scenarios

### Protected crossbow frontage

A Balanced Sector protects two Royal Host crossbow lanes while an uncommitted spear reserve remains behind the threatened join. Extending both spear and crossbow frontage may improve coverage but removes the depth needed to survive a mounted or heavy-infantry commitment.

### Sergeant-managed relief

A Serviceable sector becomes Thin after missile losses and repeated contact. Sergeants of the Royal Host open the lane and assume the join while native spear files withdraw. The Sergeants cannot create replacement spears, shields, or rested files; successful relief preserves the source rather than restoring it instantly.

### Mounted threat on the open flank

The threatened sector shortens from Balanced to Narrow Deep while cavalry or native mounted reserves protect the exposed side. A rushed change under contact may produce disorder, so early warning and command links matter.

### Occupation line overstretch

Several Extended Thin sectors cover roads, a gate, stores, and an administrative center. A coordinated attack creates more crises than the common reserve and Sergeants can answer. The player must abandon at least one position or accept likely fracture.
