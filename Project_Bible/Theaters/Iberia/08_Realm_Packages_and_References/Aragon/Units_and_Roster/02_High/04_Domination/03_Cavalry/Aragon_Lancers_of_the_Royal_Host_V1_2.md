**Lancers of the Royal Host** are selected mounted-service contributors organized for a shared Aragon-led battle doctrine. Their distinguishing asset is not superior birth or universal heavy equipment. It is the ability to form behind the Royal Host infantry, pass through a prepared lane, threaten an enemy fixed by spears or crossbows, deliver one controlled impact against a suitable target, disengage before the formation is trapped, and reform for protection, withdrawal, or a second limited mission.

The card concentrates riders, horses, lances or mounted spears, shields, protection, standards, officers, remount support, farriers, handlers, forage, water, and compatible signals from exact contributing sources. The Royal Host does not create these assets. It organizes a bounded mounted institution around common timing and integration.

## 3. Player-Facing Description

**Short description:** Royal Host line cavalry organized for a controlled advance through prepared infantry passages. Threaten a fixed enemy, strike once at the correct angle, break contact, and reform. More coordinated than miscellaneous mounted service, but less individually powerful and politically autonomous than Aragon’s premier noble cavalry.

**Combat fantasy:** Royal Host Spearmen hold the enemy in place while crossbow fire forces shields and formations to turn. The Lancers form behind a protected passage, advance only when the lane opens, deliver one concentrated impact against an exposed or committed target, and disengage toward the reform marker before heavy cavalry, fresh spears, or congestion can trap the formation.

**Flavor line:** **Open the line. Lower the lances. Strike once. Reform on the standard.**

# Part IV. Battlefield System

## 15. Signature Rhythm

```text
FORM behind the Royal Host line with rider-horse pairs sorted for pace and mission.
THREATEN from a position that forces enemy attention without accepting premature contact.
ADVANCE through a prepared passage only when infantry, missiles, and command conditions align.
STRIKE a suitable target once with controlled frontage and a visible disengagement route.
BREAK CONTACT before fresh spears, heavy cavalry, congestion, or fatigue fix the formation.
REFORM on the standard with surviving riders, horses, leaders, and usable equipment.
DECIDE whether a second limited mission is worth spending the remaining line.
```

## 39T. Combined-Arms Success and Failure Scenarios

### Prepared flank strike

Spearmen hold a Balanced sector while Crossbowmen force an enemy formation to turn shields and compress. Sergeants assign a clear outbound and return passage. The Lancers threaten first, commit only when the exposed flank and reform route remain real, strike, and return before the Spearmen close the lane.

### Countercharge behind the spear line

Enemy cavalry commits against a Royal Host spear sector. The Lancers remain behind the line until the attacking cavalry has spent part of its momentum and presented a reachable side or withdrawal path. A premature countercharge risks colliding with friendly spears; a late countercharge may find the sector already penetrated.

### Passage conflict during withdrawal

Crossbowmen, wounded personnel, and carts begin using the same rearward route needed by returning Lancers. Sergeants must assign staggered priority. If the returning mounted line has already committed, trapping that line may be worse than delaying other traffic, but giving cavalry priority transfers risk onto the infantry and wounded.

### Blunted impact and protected reform

Prepared enemy spears blunt the contact. The Lancers break off without pressing while their reserve and Royal Host Crossbowmen discourage pursuit. The unit reforms as Reduced rather than Ready because broken lances, fatigued horses, and separated files remain real.

### Royal Host overstretch

Spearmen face two crises, Crossbowmen are displacing, and Sergeants are committed to extraction. The Lancers cannot simultaneously countercharge, hold the flank, and protect the withdrawal. The player chooses one mission and accepts deterioration elsewhere.
