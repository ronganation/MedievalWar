## 1. Strong Unit Verdict

**Mounted Sergeants of the Royal Host** are an Aragon-led Domination medium contact-cavalry reserve assembled from exact released mounted sergeants, trained service riders, file leaders, suitable horses, contact spears or lances, shields, sidearms, practical protection, standards, handlers, farriers, remount support, and accepted or compelled contributor capacity.

Their role is explicitly reactive. They remain behind or between Royal Host sectors until one recoverable crisis is identified. They then ride through a verified route, check or countercharge the immediate threat, create separation, assume a short contact mission while the endangered formation withdraws or reforms, and break away before the relief action becomes a heavy cavalry or spear grind.

They are not lighter Lancers. Lancers convert preparation into one controlled impact. Mounted Sergeants convert a developing crisis into enough space, time, and order for the Royal Host to survive and continue fighting.

The unit must physically enter danger. Relief is not an aura, teleport, morale bonus, or automatic enemy pushback. The Sergeants create space by arriving in order, directing mounted threat or contact at the part of the enemy that is extending the crisis, and accepting enough combat to let the relieved formation move.

## 5. Collision Firewall

### Lancers of the Royal Host

Lancers own deliberate mounted-line preparation, passage alignment, one controlled impact, and reform. Mounted Sergeants own reactive crisis selection, relief contact, separation, extraction cover, and possible second intervention.

### Royal Frontier Sergeants

Royal Frontier Sergeants retain Aragonese frontier-service providers, strongpoint and route relationships, crisis rescue, and native institutional recovery. Royal Host Mounted Sergeants are a Domination composite field formation assigned to Royal Host sectors through Aragon-led authority and exact controlled contributions.

### Crown Mounted Sergeants

Crown Mounted Sergeants retain reciprocal Negotiated Union field linkage, mixed-contingent handoff, flank support, relief, withdrawal cover, and a disciplined secondary charge. Royal Host Mounted Sergeants operate inside an unequal Domination command system, with imposed crisis priority, controlled-source friction, and a stronger connection to Royal Host passages, extraction, and Lancer return.

### Aragonese Riders

Aragonese Riders remain the broad native screen, escort, reconnaissance, pursuit, and ordinary withdrawal family. Mounted Sergeants are held reserve contact cavalry and should not be spent on routine mounted work.

### Infanzones and Ricohombres

Infanzones own lesser-noble countercharge and ordinary cavalry melee. Ricohombres own the developed noble battle wing, controlled shock, short press, and serious mounted contest. Mounted Sergeants enter only long enough to stabilize, separate, or cover.

### Royal Mesnada

Royal Mesnada retains ruler protection, command continuity, exceptional household intervention, and protected royal withdrawal. Mounted Sergeants serve Royal Host sectors rather than the ruler’s person.

### Negotiated Union lane, heavy, and shock cavalry

Cavallers d’Urgell own outer-lane initiative, pursuit, recall, and repositioning. Cavallers de Montcada own the heavy mounted fight. Cavallers de la Senyera own the highest-gate decisive joint rupture. Mounted Sergeants own none of those ceilings.

## 13. Signature Battle Cycle

1. **HOLD** behind or between Royal Host sectors with routes and crisis priorities known.
2. **OBSERVE** threats, friendly cohesion, passages, receivers, enemy reserves, and horse condition.
3. **SELECT** one recoverable crisis rather than chasing every alarm.
4. **RIDE** through a verified route without blocking missile, infantry, cart, or Lancer traffic.
5. **CHECK** the immediate mobile, flank, or pursuit threat through mounted presence, contact, or countercharge.
6. **RELIEVE** the friendly formation and assume its short pressure or cover mission.
7. **SEPARATE** enemy and friendly bodies enough for withdrawal, contraction, reform, or handoff.
8. **COVER** the passage until the receiver or relieved formation reaches safety.
9. **BREAK CONTACT** before heavy cavalry, spears, fresh infantry depth, or congestion pins the Sergeants.
10. **REFORM** around the standard and reassess whether a second bounded intervention remains physically possible.

## 37. Representative Scenarios

### Crossbow sector under mounted pressure

Enemy light or medium cavalry closes against Royal Host Crossbowmen. The Mounted Sergeants leave reserve through an assigned route, check the cavalry, hold contact long enough for the Crossbowmen to withdraw with usable order, then break away toward Spearmen or a rally point. Chasing the enemy beyond the saved sector is a failure of discipline.

### Lancer return lane contested

The Lancers complete a checked or partial impact while enemy cavalry threatens the return route. The Mounted Sergeants protect the passage and check pursuit. The Sergeants do not become a second shock wave. Their success is preserving the Lancer survivors and keeping the passage usable.

### Spear sector bending

Enemy cavalry or assault infantry begins turning a Royal Host spear flank. The Mounted Sergeants countercharge or threaten the exposed attacker while the Spearmen shorten frontage. The cavalry then leaves before the threat becomes a prepared spear or heavy-infantry grind.

### Failed relief

A friendly formation collapses before the Sergeants arrive and blocks the only exit. The correct decision is to abort, protect the next route, or cover the wider withdrawal. Feeding the reserve into the collapse creates another lost formation.

### Second intervention

After a successful relief, the Sergeants reform with fit horses, working leaders, and usable contact files. A later crisis emerges. A second intervention is available only if actual readiness remains; no cooldown restores the first-action formation.

## 37A. Mature Battlefield Power Floor and Ceiling

The Mounted Sergeants must save recoverable Royal Host actions through real contact. The card fails if relief is only a movement bonus, morale aura, or weaker Lancer charge. Its strength comes from disciplined arrival, short contact, pressure transfer, passage clearance, and timely breakaway.
