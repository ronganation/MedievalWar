## 1. Strong Unit Verdict

**Jinetes of the Royal Host** are the Aragon-led Domination path's controlled mounted-javelin screen. They are assembled from exact released mounted-skirmisher riders, suitable horses, leaders, javelins, shields, light weapons, tack, standards, handlers, farriers, remount support, ammunition stores, and source-specific service obligations.

Their distinction is not superior individual skirmishing and not Negotiated Union file relay. Their power comes from Royal Host command assigning a screen lane, a harassment window, a withdrawal direction, a protected receiving line, and a new angle before the formation spends finite ammunition or horse energy. The Jinetes threaten, cast, turn away, pass behind protected Royal Host elements, and reappear where their remaining readiness still matters.

They are strongest when they make an enemy turn, advance, screen, or expose a reserve without allowing the mounted files to be fixed. They fail when central command treats mobility as immunity, orders several harassment sectors at once, or converts exhausted skirmishers into ordinary melee cavalry.

## 5. Collision Firewall

### Aragonese Jinetes

Aragonese Jinetes remain the Independent path's native mounted-javelin specialists. They own native Aragonese source development, repeated cast-withdraw-reposition discipline, and the strongest native expression where source pairing and support mature. Royal Host Jinetes are a Domination field institution whose access and reliability depend on exact released contributors, Royal Host screen assignments, protected receiving lines, imposed priorities, and source-specific compliance.

### Crown Jinetes

Crown Jinetes remain the Negotiated Union's joint mounted-javelin formation. Their signature is reciprocal file rotation, harassment handoff, shared rally, and mission continuity between source-owned contributors. Royal Host Jinetes use centrally assigned screen lanes and protected Royal Host passage rather than reciprocal political handoff.

### Riders of the Royal March

Riders own proactive outer-lane contact, screen displacement through mounted pressure or short melee, bounded pursuit, recall, and second-lane reappearance. Royal Host Jinetes own ranged harassment, refusal, ammunition economy, and battle shaping without requiring contact.

### Mounted Sergeants of the Royal Host

Mounted Sergeants own held crisis selection, relief contact, separation, extraction, passage clearance, and bounded second intervention. Royal Host Jinetes are proactive screen and harassment cavalry, not a rescue formation.

### Lancers of the Royal Host

Lancers own prepared passage, one controlled impact, disengagement, and reform. Royal Host Jinetes shape facing, movement, screens, and reserves so that a Lancer target may emerge, but they do not deliver the Lancer decision themselves.

### Cavallers of the Crown Standard

Crown Standard Cavallers own precedence, heavy countercharge, peer mounted contest, purposeful short melee, bounded press, and household reform. Royal Host Jinetes must avoid that contest and may support it only through screening, harassment, reserve warning, and pursuit denial.

## 12. Signature Battle Cycle

1. **ASSIGN** one screen lane, harassment purpose, withdrawal direction, receiving line, and rally ground.
2. **SCREEN** the Royal Host while reading enemy cavalry, missiles, Spears, reserves, terrain, and routes.
3. **THREATEN** a flank, route, screen, missile body, or formation without automatically spending ammunition.
4. **HOLD** javelins until range, angle, target, friendly clearance, and exit justify the cast.
5. **CAST** under file and leader control.
6. **TURN AWAY** before heavy contact or interception fixes the files.
7. **PASS BEHIND** protected Spears, missiles, Sergeants, terrain, or another accepted receiving body.
8. **REPOSITION** toward a second useful angle at actual readiness.
9. **REAPPEAR** to threaten, cast, cover withdrawal, or deny pursuit.
10. **EXPLOIT OR REFUSE** according to target exposure, ammunition, horse energy, support, and exit.
11. **RALLY AND ACCOUNT** actual riders, horses, ammunition, equipment, casualties, owners, and grievances.

## 26. Representative Scenarios

### Threat without casting

Enemy Infantry turns to protect an exposed flank while enemy light cavalry moves outward to contain the Royal Host Jinetes. The Jinetes preserve ammunition. The changed facing and displaced cavalry already create value for the main Royal Host.

### Cast and protected withdrawal

A slow enemy formation advances beyond missile support. Jinete files enter a qualified shot window, cast, turn away, and pass behind Royal Host Spearmen. The Spearmen do not magically stop pursuit, but their protected frontage creates a real receiving relationship.

### Heavy cavalry interception

Fresh heavy cavalry appears between the Jinetes and the original rally ground. The formation refuses another cast, uses the nearest valid receiving line, and may fragment by source if no common passage survives. Attempting to prove skirmisher superiority through melee is failure.

### Target preparation for Lancers

Harassment forces an enemy body to turn and compress while avoiding the Jinete angle. If Royal Host passage and Lancer conditions pass independently, the Lancers may exploit the presentation. The Jinetes do not create automatic disorder or a guaranteed impact.

### Ammunition exhausted

The Jinetes retain horses, leaders, and useful order but no qualifying mounted-javelin load. Their mission narrows to screening, reporting, withdrawal cover, selective pursuit, or return. They do not become ordinary Royal March Riders or shock cavalry.

## 26A. Mature Battlefield Power Floor and Ceiling

The Royal Host Jinetes must be materially better than the contact-cavalry portfolio at shaping enemy facing, screening, and forcing reaction without melee. They must remain materially worse at ordinary mounted contact, crisis relief, prepared impact, and the serious heavy-cavalry contest.
