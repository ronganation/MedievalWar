# Aragon High Era Aragon-Led Domination

## Purpose

This folder contains Aragon's High Era Aragon-led Domination roster portfolio. It records the Royal Host institutions Aragon can assemble when Aragon leads the political and military relationship with Barcelona-facing contributors and other eligible sources.

Aragon-led Domination is not a universal upgrade tier, a merged Aragon-Barcelona culture, or a license to duplicate Independent Development and Negotiated Union units. The path creates specific Royal Host institutions only where the Domination political structure produces a distinct battlefield decision, command relationship, source transaction, recovery burden, and player-facing identity.

The current static portfolio is mature enough to stop unit creation for now. Existing profiles and authorities should be maintained, reconciled, and later compared against the full Aragon Early-to-High portfolio before any Late Era continuation, convergence, consolidation, or successor work.

## Path identity

The path's central military institution is the **Royal Host**: an Aragon-led field army that coordinates exact source-owned contributors through centrally assigned sectors, passages, reserves, screen lanes, assault tasks, and recovery relationships.

The Royal Host does not create people, horses, warhorses, retainers, weapons, armor, ammunition, equipment, standards, leaders, recruitment pools, or readiness. Political authority changes eligibility, assignment, command, precedence, reliability, recall, return, and recovery. Every formation still requires real contributors and reduces the capacity of its owning sources while assigned.

The path should feel:

- more centrally directed than Independent Development;
- less reciprocal than Negotiated Union;
- more dependent on Royal Host traffic, passage, receiving-line, relief, and reform relationships;
- powerful when several distinct institutions cooperate;
- brittle when command interfaces, contributor compliance, withdrawal routes, source floors, or recovery systems fail;
- selective rather than universally superior.

## Controlling rules

- Early Era and Independent High Era units persist unless an exact transfer, reassignment, recruitment closure, or replacement relationship says otherwise.
- Negotiated Union, regional, and mercenary formations retain their own political, source, ownership, and recovery authorities.
- Domination never merges all Aragonese and Barcelona-facing contributors into one physical pool.
- The same people, horses, warhorses, retainers, equipment, ammunition, standards, leaders, or support cannot occupy several full assignments at once.
- Every Domination card must own a distinct player decision. Naming, stronger equipment, higher statistics, or political-path symmetry are not sufficient reasons for a unit.
- Practice may improve judgment, signals, cohesion, assignment, recovery, and institutional memory. Practice does not create physical assets.
- Recruitment closure does not delete surviving formations, casualties, captives, equipment, experience, grievances, or source obligations.
- Quick-battle availability does not create campaign recruitment capacity.
- No profile in this folder is automatically present on January 1, 1062.
- No file in this folder authorizes stable IDs, registry changes, Opening State bindings, payload creation, runtime activation, final art promotion, or Unreal implementation.

## Infantry portfolio

### Spearmen of the Royal Host

**Current profile:** `Aragon_Spearmen_of_the_Royal_Host_V1_3.md`

The Royal Host spear line owns assigned frontage, sector holding, burden transfer, relief receiving, anti-cavalry stability, withdrawal control, and reform around a centrally directed field-army relationship.

**Player decision:** Which sector must the Royal Host hold, and when should frontage or burden pass to another formation before the spear line is consumed?

### Sergeants of the Royal Host

**Current profile:** `Aragon_Sergeants_of_the_Royal_Host_V1_3.md`

The Royal Host Sergeants own foot command, protected passage, traffic control, sector assumption, relief coordination, occupation, and ordered withdrawal. They connect specialist formations rather than replacing them.

**Player decision:** Where should the Royal Host spend its most reliable foot command and passage-control capacity?

### Pioneers of the Royal Host

**Current profile:** `Aragon_Pioneers_of_the_Royal_Host_V1_1.md`

The Pioneers own obstacle opening, approach preparation, fieldworks, passage creation, breach support, and immediate consolidation. They prepare and maintain access rather than serving as the primary assault body.

**Player decision:** Which obstacle, approach, or passage is important enough to spend scarce pioneer labor, tools, protection, and recovery capacity?

### Breachmen of the Royal Host

**Current profile:** `Aragon_Breachmen_of_the_Royal_Host_V1_2.md`

The Breachmen own protected assault entry after pioneer preparation, breaking and widening a qualified breach, handing the opening to following troops, and withdrawing or recovering before the assault body is destroyed.

**Player decision:** When is a prepared opening good enough to commit the protected assault formation?

**Current profile:** `Aragon_Jinetes_of_the_Royal_Host_V1_1.md`

The Royal Host Jinetes form the separate Domination mounted-skirmisher branch. They own assigned screen lanes, threat without immediate ammunition expenditure, finite mounted-javelin pressure, controlled shot windows, withdrawal behind a real Royal Host receiving line, and reappearance from another useful angle at actual remaining readiness.

## Royal Host combined-arms logic

The path works through complementary decisions rather than universal upgrades:

1. **Pioneers** prepare approaches, remove obstacles, and create usable passages.
2. **Breachmen** enter and widen qualified assault openings.
3. **Spearmen** hold assigned frontage, receive pressure, and provide rally or cavalry-denial relationships.
4. **Foot Sergeants** manage passage, traffic, sector assumption, relief, occupation, and withdrawal.
5. **Jinetes** screen, threaten, harass, withdraw through receiving lines, and shape targets.
6. **Royal March Riders** contest and reuse the outer mounted lane.
7. **Mounted Sergeants** preserve the army at recoverable crises.
8. **Lancers** convert prepared passage and target conditions into a controlled impact.
9. **Crown Standard Cavallers** answer the serious peer heavy-cavalry contest.

This is a mission-selection map, not a mandatory battle script. An army may lack one or more institutions, refuse a mission, use native or mercenary alternatives, or fight through a different sequence when the actual sources, terrain, enemy, and political state require it.

## Source and ownership boundaries

- Aragon remains the package owner for the Aragon-led Domination path.
- Contributors retain their exact people, property, horses, equipment, standards, service claims, and recovery consequences unless a valid transfer explicitly changes ownership.
- Controlled or compelled service is not the same as full integration.
- An assigned native formation loses availability in its original role while its people and material are committed elsewhere.
- Whole Independent, Negotiated Union, regional, or mercenary cards are not automatically absorbed into Royal Host profiles.
- Repeated service does not automatically create a permanent Domination recruitment pool.
- Physical losses route back to the actual source owners and relevant Royal Host formation state.

## Naming rules

Use flavorful, readable names that communicate the unit's battlefield institution without fabricating a historical corps.

Current naming families are:

- `of the Royal Host` for formations integrated into the Royal Host's central field-army command;
- `of the Royal March` for the proactive outer-lane mounted institution;
- `of the Crown Standard` for the selected multi-house noble heavy wing.

Do not reuse:

- Independent Aragon names for a Domination duplicate;
- Negotiated Union Crown, Senyera, Montcada, or Urgell identities;
- Royal Mesnada language for formations that do not protect the ruler or Royal command continuity;
- generic `Royal`, `Elite`, `Heavy`, or `Guard` labels as substitutes for a real battlefield identity.
