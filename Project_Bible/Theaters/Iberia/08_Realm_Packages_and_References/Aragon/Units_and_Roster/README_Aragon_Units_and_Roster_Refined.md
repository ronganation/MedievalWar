# Aragon Units and Roster

## Purpose

This folder is the canonical roster-development root for Aragon. It contains the Realm's Early Era foundation, High Era native and political-path portfolios, roster research and admission authorities, regional and relationship interfaces, development maps, folder indexes, and future Late Era handoff boundaries.

This README is the entry point for the folder as a whole. It does not replace the detailed authorities and unit profiles below it. It explains where work belongs, which documents control each layer, how unit ownership and political paths remain separated, and what may or may not be treated as current.

## 00 Research, Admission, and Review

`00_Research_Admission_Review` is the home for source studies, candidate comparisons, provider and admission authorities, collision reviews, nonduplication decisions, closeouts, and bounded handoffs that govern whether a card or portfolio should exist.

This folder should contain decision-making evidence and authorities, not ordinary player-facing unit profiles.

Use it for:

- historical and linguistic research boundaries;
- provider, source, equipment, horse, service, and relationship analysis;
- unit-family admission or rejection;
- collision testing against existing cards;
- ownership and nonduplication rules;
- bounded closeouts and owner-review dispositions;
- pre-profile or portfolio-level handoffs.

Do not use it to create parallel implementations, duplicate unit profiles, or administrative receipt chains that no longer serve a live decision.

## 01 Early

`01_Early` contains Aragon's compact campaign foundation and the persistent families from which later development branches.

The Early roster remains active unless a specific authority records transfer, recruitment closure, replacement, or another exact disposition. High and Late Era development does not automatically delete Early cards.

### Current Early identity

Aragon's Early military identity is the **Three Land Mountain Host**:

- defensive spear-centered foundation;
- scarce mounted decision power;
- limited household reserves;
- strongpoint and relief tradeoffs;
- modest missile depth;
- route, broken-ground, and withdrawal utility;
- finite recruitment and shallow replacement depth.

### Controlling Early documents

The current Early overview and roster-reconciliation authorities control membership, persistence, source ownership, role separation, and progression handoff.

Current documentation includes:

- `01_Early/Aragon_Early_Era_Roster_Overview_V1_1.md`
- `Aragon_Early_Roster_Source_Role_and_Progression_Reconciliation_Authority_V1_1.md` at the roster root

The Early overview remains within the Early portfolio. The reconciliation authority remains at the roster root because its scope includes the Early-to-High handoff and parent-level relationships across the roster. Earlier Early overview and reconciliation files remain predecessors, not competing current authorities.

### Early-to-High rule

Early families persist into High Era unless an exact source, role, eligibility, transfer, or closure authority says otherwise. Later political-path portfolios add institutions and assignments; they do not retroactively erase Aragon's native military foundation.

## Shared Aragon High Era core

The shared core holds High Era capabilities that remain native to Aragon or are canonical Theater-owned regional families consumed by Aragon without becoming political-path duplicates.

Examples of current High Era work include native or Aragon-facing institutional Infantry, Crossbow development, mounted service cavalry, Jinetes, noble cavalry, and Aragon's expression or access relationship to the Theater-owned Almughavar family.

A shared core does not mean every political path receives the same availability, reliability, assignment, command, equipment, or recovery. Political paths can change access and institutional expression while the underlying people and owners remain finite.

## Independent Development

`02_Independent_Development` contains High Era institutions developed through Aragon's own people, sources, service relationships, regional capacity, equipment, and campaign choices.

Independent Development is a complete competitive path, not a fallback roster and not a feeder package whose only purpose is to supply later Union or Domination cards.

The folder's entry point is:

- `README_Independent_Development.md`

Independent Development must preserve:

- Aragon ownership unless another canonical owner is explicit;
- native source floors and home-defense obligations;
- coexistence with the Early roster;
- finite people, horses, equipment, ammunition, leaders, and recovery;
- collision boundaries with Negotiated Union, Domination, regional, mercenary, and Military Order content.

## Negotiated Union

`03_Negotiated_Union` contains joint, reciprocal, county-linked, or composite institutions made possible by negotiated Aragon-Barcelona political and military cooperation.

Negotiated Union does not merge all Aragonese and Barcelona-facing people, equipment, horses, institutions, or pools. Each card requires exact contributor eligibility, assignment, command, service, return, compensation, and recovery.

The refreshed structure includes role folders for:

- `00_Research_Authorities_Closeouts`
- `01_Infantry`
- `02_Foot_Archers_and_Skirmishers`
- `03_Cavalry`
- `04_Mounted_Skirmishers`

Current portfolio authorities include developed Foot Archer and Skirmisher and Cavalry progression authorities. The Negotiated Union cavalry authority controls a four-card composite and county-contribution architecture, while mounted-skirmisher work includes Crown Jinetes under Negotiated Union ownership.

No Negotiated Union card automatically becomes native Aragon recruitment, and no repeated joint service creates a universal shared population.

## Regional, Military Order, mercenary, and external access

Not every unit available to an Aragon army is canonically owned by Aragon.

### Regional units

Theater-owned regional families remain canonically stored under the Iberia Theater's Regional Units structure. Aragon receives access through relationship authorities or Realm consumer-integration records.

Aragon's use of a regional family does not create a duplicate Aragon-owned canonical profile.

### Mercenaries

Mercenaries remain company, captain, contract, payment, service-term, equipment, and recovery relationships. They may overlap with native battlefield roles while remaining more expensive and independently governed hired formations.

### Military Orders

Military Order access requires separate chronology, relationship, property, command, recruitment, and recovery authority. Order units do not become ordinary settlement recruitment.

### External allies and contingents

Allied, coalition, subject, conquered-site, or external formations retain their own owner and access logic. Repeated use alone does not convert those people into Aragonese population or permanent recruitment pools.

## Source, assignment, and nonduplication rules

- Every person, horse, warhorse, retainer, weapon, shield, armor item, standard, tool, ammunition load, transport asset, and support relationship has an owner and physical state.
- Political paths change access and assignment; they do not manufacture assets.
- The same physical contributors cannot occupy multiple full unit cards at once.
- A source's field assignment reduces home defense, garrison, household, civic, messenger, transport, labor, breeding, or other source capacity where applicable.
- Shared weapons do not merge institutions.
- Shared unit names do not merge sources.
- Shared tactical practice does not automatically merge cards.
- A unit should remain separate only when source, campaign role, battlefield decision, equipment, formation practice, cost, recovery, and player-facing identity justify separation.
- Do not create symmetry-only cards to fill an empty numbered folder.

## Canonical-file and duplicate policy

Use the project-tree version of a file as canonical when it matches the current folder, document ID, successor chain, and stable footer.

Treat the following as noncanonical unless intentionally restored:

- Copilot chat exports;
- duplicated downloads with `(1)` or similar suffixes;
- shortened 8.3-style names;
- temporary aliases such as `ARAGON-##`;
- stale predecessors superseded by an explicit successor;
- copies stored outside the owning folder.

Do not delete or archive duplicates based on filename alone. Verify content, successor relationship, current project-tree placement, and controlling references first.

## Late Era boundary

The Late Era should not be built as a symmetrical upgrade of every Early or High card.

A future review must decide, card by card, whether a family should:

- persist with source-valid equipment or organization changes;
- remain useful as a lower-tier, regional, local, or emergency card;
- converge tactically while preserving source identity;
- consolidate because separate cards no longer produce distinct decisions;
- receive a true Late Era successor;
- close recruitment while survivors and institutional consequences persist.

No calendar date, era label, research option, building, or treasury balance creates these outcomes automatically.
