## 1. Strong Barcelona Communities Verdict

Barcelona must be represented as a connected but non-uniform comital society spanning Barcelona, Girona, Osona and Vic, Penedès, Olèrdola, rural estates and villages, castles, cathedral and church communities, markets, routes, and Mediterranean and trans-Pyrenean interfaces. The realm is not one city, one people, one language block, one countryside, one church institution, or one population pool.

The faction's social strength should arise from the interaction of a major comital and episcopal center at Barcelona, distinct Girona and Osona communities, frontier Penedès and Olèrdola recovery, documentary and ecclesiastical institutions, rural production, castle networks, markets, specialist labor, household politics, and access to coastal and Pyrenean exchange. This authority must avoid importing later communes, guild republics, standardized Catalan identity, later maritime empire, Crown of Aragon institutions, or exact demographic totals into January 1062.

Population must constrain labor, taxation, settlement growth, institutional capacity, supply, and finite recruitment pools. Population does not become an army manpower currency, and no exact totals or demographic percentages are invented.
