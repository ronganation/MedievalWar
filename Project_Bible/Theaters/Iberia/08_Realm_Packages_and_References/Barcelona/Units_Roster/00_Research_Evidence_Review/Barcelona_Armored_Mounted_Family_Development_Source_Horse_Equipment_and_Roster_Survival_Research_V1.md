## 4. General Armored Men-at-Arms Context

A general reference describes the man-at-arms as a High Medieval to Renaissance military function, typically fully armored heavy cavalry, potentially drawn from knights, nobles, retainers, or mercenaries, and serving through pay or obligation. The same source says the distinction between man-at-arms as a function and knighthood as a social rank was clear by 1300. citeturn27search264

```yaml
armored_mounted_function_general_plausibility: Supported
man_at_arms_equals_knight: false
possible_service_bases_generally:
  - Retinue
  - Obligation
  - Pay
  - MercenaryCompany
direct_Barcelona_source_from_general_reference: none
```

This supports a later functional research lane but does not establish Barcelona personnel, horses, armor, lances, remounts, contracts, command, or a unit name.

## 5. Iberian Light-Horse and Terrain Boundary

A JSTOR search result on mounted warriors of medieval Spain emphasizes light cavalry and raiding in Iberian warfare and says the *jinetes* performed multiple roles. The snippet also states that terrain and low population shaped warfare differently from much of western Europe. This is a broad Iberian argument, not a Barcelona source. citeturn27search269

```yaml
Iberian_light_horse_importance_general: SupportedByLocatedSnippet
Barcelona_native_JinetesCatalans_card: NotSupported
armored_family_should_replace_light_or_regional_access: false
terrain_and_campaign_context_must_affect_mounted_development: true
```

The result argues against treating heavier armor as a universal or inevitable Barcelona progression.
