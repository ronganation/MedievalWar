## 3. Retrieved Barcelona and Catalan Evidence

### 3.1 Ripoll and Rodes manuscript context

The Government of Catalonia cultural-heritage page identifies the Bibles of Ripoll and Rodes as products of the Ripoll workshop in the first quarter of the eleventh century. It describes the Ripoll Bible as a large illustrated manuscript with 465 folios and twenty fully illustrated folios, while the Rodes Bible originated in the same productive first-quarter context. This supplies strong date-compatible Catalan visual context, but the retrieved page does not describe a Barcelona community-archer institution or recruitment system.

The selected Ripoll Bible military-illustration index dates the manuscript approximately to 1027-1032, identifies it as Catalan and eleventh-century, and lists multiple army, siege, and battle scenes. This is useful for period visual comparison and for testing clothes, shields, helmets, weapons, and military presentation. The retrieved index does not itself identify a community-bow formation, and manuscript images based on biblical and diverse iconographic models cannot be treated as a literal roster list.


The strongest shared lesson is the required intersection of service and actual missile capability:

```text
Eligible community service
!= eligible bow users
!= serviceable bows and strings
!= compatible arrows
!= leaders and firing discipline
!= a protected mission-ready formation
```

The Norman and English profiles explicitly separate local obligation from bow capability. Their lower bow families require people who can serve plus real bow familiarity, bows, strings, arrows, leaders, routes, supply, and protection. Their civic or better-supported families remain separate because stores, officers, repeated service, ammunition organization, and prepared defensive positions create a different source and player decision.

Barcelona should inherit that architecture, not the Fyrd, Ceorl, Norman, or French institution.
