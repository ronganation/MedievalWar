The authority expressly rejects an automatic crossbow identity and does not create operators, weapons, ammunition, guilds, or recurring civic military service. ## 4. External Evidence Findings

### 4A. General European Chronology

A general history result states that crossbows reappear in European written sources in 947 at the siege of Senlis and that crossbows and crossbow users held a significant position in European militaries from the eleventh century onward. This supports broad chronological plausibility in Europe, but it does not identify Barcelona, Catalonia, a Barcelona institution, or an opening formation. citeturn22search65

```yaml
general_European_crossbow_plausibility_by_1062: SupportedAtBroadLevel
```

### 4B. Later Catalan Terminology

A modern edition of <File>Crònica de la croada contra Catalunya</File>, based on Bernat Desclot's account of events in 1285, lists *ballesters* among its common terms. This supports the presence of the term in a later thirteenth-century Catalan narrative context. The result does not establish the phrase *Ballesters de la Ciutat*, an urban guild, an opening 1062 unit, or Barcelona-specific recruitment. citeturn22search74

### 4C. Later Barcelona Municipal and Maritime Crossbow Force

A 2022 peer-reviewed article in <External>Gladius</External> studies a paid municipal crossbow force in late medieval Barcelona, the *mantellets*, associated with defense of the maritime frontier from approximately 1356 to 1516. The article says the contingent took its name from the defensive boards used in naval combat and bases the study on royal privileges, municipal ordinances, chronicles, diaries, and records held mostly in Barcelona's city historical archive. citeturn22search81
