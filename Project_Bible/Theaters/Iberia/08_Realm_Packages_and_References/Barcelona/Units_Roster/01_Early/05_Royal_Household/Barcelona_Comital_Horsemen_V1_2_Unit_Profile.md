## 5. Player-Facing Description

Barcelona Comital Mounted Retainers are a finite household cavalry family trusted with the count's protection, escort, command support, and demanding field service. They are not every mounted lord, every castle rider, or a universal body of knights. Their battlefield value comes from greater familiarity, cohesion, readiness, and access to the commander's intent than broader contingent cavalry can normally guarantee.

Keep them behind the main line until the battle presents a real decision. They can secure a threatened flank, counterstrike an enemy fixed by spearmen, rescue a wavering command position, punish exposed missiles, or turn a local retreat into a wider rout. A premature charge wastes their strongest advantage: controlled commitment at the moment when a small reliable mounted force can change the battle.

Their losses are strategically expensive. Dead or wounded retainers weaken the household military core. Lost horses, tack, armor, weapons, trained leaders, and attendants cannot be recreated by a building unlock or treasury payment. The formation should feel powerful because it is trusted and scarce, not because it receives ahistorical immunity or unlimited replenishment.

## 15. Battlefield Rhythm

1. Enter with the command group or immediately behind the principal line.
2. Preserve cohesion, horses, and visibility while the broader host establishes contact.
3. Guard the command position and refuse low-value harassment.
4. Identify a real crisis or exposed target rather than charging the first visible enemy.
5. Commit against a disrupted flank, overextended attacker, threatened command point, or already engaged formation.
6. Break contact before fatigue, spear frontage, missiles, or enemy reserves trap the formation.
7. Rally behind friendly infantry or on a known route.
8. Pursue only when the enemy is already broken and recall remains viable.
9. Preserve retainers, leaders, horses, and equipment for future household readiness.
