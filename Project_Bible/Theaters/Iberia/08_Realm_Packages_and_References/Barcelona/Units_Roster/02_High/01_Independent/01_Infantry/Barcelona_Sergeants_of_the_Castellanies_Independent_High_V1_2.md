## 1. Decisive Unit Thesis

**Sergeants of the Castellanies are Barcelona's developed castle-service relief infantry: a compact spear-and-shield formation assembled from compatible released castellany sections, able to take over a threatened entrance, contain a penetration, counter-enter a short distance, and leave the position under functioning defense.**

They are not another broad spear line. They are the troops committed when a gate, crossing, camp entrance, captured access point, or neighboring strongpoint must remain usable. They can fight ordinary infantry directly and recover limited ground. Their distinguishing achievement is not simply surviving contact, but turning a precarious position into one that friendly troops can actually use and hold.

Their High development is a more repeatable **relief-and-handover formation across cooperating castellanies**, with maintained battle equipment, retained section leaders, receiving-site integration, planned reserve depth, and accountable return. The Early parent already knows post relief and counter-entry. This branch earns its card by making those capabilities dependable beyond one familiar post without pretending Barcelona possesses a uniform national garrison corps.

- **Path:** Independent Barcelona, High Era

## 7. Protected Duties and Release Cost

Before a section leaves, its source must retain viable gate and watch coverage, keys and access control, stores and water custody, signals, command continuity, ordinary relief and rest, emergency response, and a workable local reserve. Prisoner or protected-person custody remains applicable only where the source actually owns that assignment.

Release reduces available people, equipment, leaders, relief depth, and response capacity. Several source contributions never become free aggregation. The same section cannot remain on full castle duty while also populating this unit, Host Spearmen, Osona Shieldmen, or another field card.

## 11. Formation Geometry and Deployment

The default is a compact medium-depth body with a narrow-to-moderate frontage. Front files carry the best compatible spears and shields; supporting files close actual gaps; rear depth provides a finite reserve, relief capacity, and rally control.

| Formation | Benefit | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Compact defensive line | Reliable bounded frontage | Poor broad coverage |
| Gate or passage block | Concentrates reach on a usable approach | Fire, crossfire, bypass, and congestion risk |
| Receiving-post reserve | Preserves intervention timing | Leaves fewer troops on immediate frontage |
| Relief approach column | Uses a real access lane | Vulnerable before deployment |
| Captured-access hold | Secures an entrance for friendly movement | May be isolated from support |
| Normal field line | Useful ordinary defensive infantry | Gives up much of the specialist advantage |

A narrow stair or wall walk may be too constricted for a full body. Sections can relieve posts there, but the whole card does not gain a magical narrow-space advantage. No automatic shieldwall, pike block, omnidirectional brace, or shock column is granted.

## 12. Signature Rhythm: Relieve, Contain, Counter-Enter, Restore

### Relieve

Approach a threatened position through a real lane. Receive its facing, enemy pressure, exit, and protected-objective information. Replace files if the outgoing defenders can yield; otherwise reinforce beside or behind them. Pinned troops cannot phase through one another.

### Contain

Establish a spear-and-shield stop line that prevents attackers spreading into the camp, courtyard, crossing exit, or internal route. Preserve a finite reserve rather than committing every file at the first alarm.

### Counter-enter

Advance a short distance against stalled, divided, or disordered attackers. Recover the threshold or essential space. Halt before the formation becomes an unsupported assault or pursuit body.

### Restore

Reestablish facing, relief depth, friendly access, signals, casualty passage, and an accountable receiving defense. A tactical position is not restored merely because the enemy momentarily retreats. Handback requires actual troops able to hold it.

These are interpretations of ordinary move, attack, halt, formation, and withdrawal controls. Core usefulness does not depend on four special ability buttons.

## 15. Ordinary Fighting Power and Matchups

These are proposed calibration expectations, not tested results.

- **Weak foot:** should win a normal compact frontal fight when serviceable, without requiring castle walls.
- **Ordinary trained infantry:** should contest strongly at a bounded position and use reserve discipline to improve the outcome.
- **Host Spearmen:** better at compact relief and handover; worse at broad coverage and missile-line organization.
- **Osona Shieldmen:** better at receiving-post intervention and access restoration; not automatically better in prolonged open-field infantry grinding.
- **Sword-and-shield companies:** retain the advantage in deliberate field close-pressure tasks.
- **Fresh elite assault infantry:** contain or delay with support; no expectation of an unsupported victory.
- **Cavalry:** prepared frontal spears matter; turning, compression, and exposed flanks remain dangerous.
- **Missiles:** shields are directional; crossbows, crossfire, and sustained exposure remain serious counters.

A position helps only through actual approach geometry, barriers, facing, support, and routes. No blanket castle, urban, bridge, or gate bonus is assumed.

## 16. Reserve Timing and Simultaneous Threats

A relief formation cannot solve several separated crises at once. Committing to a false alarm may leave the real entrance unsupported. Dividing into sections reduces each body's depth and leadership and may remove the capacity for controlled counter-entry.

The player must choose between preserving reserve insurance and saving a threatened post. Information is uncertain; alarms, messengers, visibility, and local command determine what is known. There is no perfect knowledge of the main attack.

## 17. Strengths, Weaknesses, and Counterplay

### Strengths

Compact defensive order, maintained equipment, repeatable relief, receiving-post handover, bounded reserve commitment, controlled counter-entry, captured-access security, and accountable restoration.

### Weaknesses

Narrow availability, source exposure, unfamiliar receiving sites, command friction between sections, missile pressure, fire and smoke, blocked lanes, elite assault, simultaneous threats, poor pursuit, and slow cadre replacement.

### Enemy plan

Threaten two entrances, block relief lanes, use missiles before contact, force turning, draw the reserve away, and attack during assembly or handover. If the unit is deployed far away, pressure its contributing source. None of these effects is automatic; they follow actual position and campaign state.

## 18. Terrain, Weather, and Contact Compression

Broad courtyards and usable entrances permit compact formation and reserve movement. Rubble, stairs, narrow walks, tight breaches, civilian congestion, and blind corners may prevent those advantages. Firm open ground permits ordinary spear fighting but exposes the limited frontage and flanks.

Rain, heat, cold, wind, and poor visibility affect footing, grip, fatigue, signals, routes, shelter, and supply through shared systems. Fire and smoke can make a seemingly defensible passage untenable. The unit is not a firefighting specialist or immune to panic because it serves castles.

As contact compresses, individuals with usable sidearms continue fighting. Reach control, mounted deterrence, relief freedom, and withdrawal ease decline. No synchronized whole-unit weapon swap or automatic armor-piercing trait is proposed.

## 22. Siege, Field, Urban, and Maritime Boundaries

In siege defense, use the unit for gate reserves, post relief, inner containment, and restoration. In siege offense, secure an entrance after assault troops succeed; do not make these troops the default first wave. In field battles, camps, crossings, and bounded objectives suit them, but ordinary compact fighting must remain viable away from masonry.

Temporary urban defense does not grant civic jurisdiction, citywide emergency coverage, or permanent occupation rights. Mounted service requires separate riders, horses, care, training, and role. Embarkation requires actual lift, crews, supply, landing support, and return; no marine or boarding identity follows from castle service.

## 29A. Refined Player Card Authority

- **Weaknesses:** Limited frontage, crossfire, blocked routes, simultaneous threats, unfamiliar sites, recall pressure, elite assault infantry, and slow cadre recovery

- **Player instruction:** Keep them in reserve until one entrance truly matters. Relieve through a real lane, contain before pushing, stop at the recovered threshold, and hand the position back before another crisis empties the castellanies.

## 23A. Final Battlefield Interlock Contract
