## 1. Decisive Emergence Verdict

**The opportunity is to establish common rule, not to unlock a reward faction by reaching a date.**

Barcelona-Aragon is absent from the 1062 opening. Barcelona and Aragon continue as native campaign factions unless a valid recognized settlement creates the combined faction. A negotiated Union can be balanced or give one foundation senior direction. None is a separate faction, occupation branch, or automatic historical script.

```yaml

```

The player gains a larger directly playable realm in its real condition. The price is inherited obligations, disputes, financial commitments and the challenge of sustaining two institutional foundations. This file defines readiness to make that transition; Formation owns the transfer itself.

## 2. Historical Grounding and Adaptation

The user-supplied Crown of Aragon article describes a composite monarchy originating in the dynastic connection between the Kingdom of Aragon and County of Barcelona. It describes constituent realms retaining their own laws and institutions under a shared monarch. These are the limited historical observations used here. [H1]

The article's overview and origin narrative use different formation milestones: the infobox emphasizes 1162, while the origin section traces the arrangement to 1137 and the combination of titles in 1162. This document does not flatten those stages into one compulsory campaign trigger or treat the overview as a fully verified chronology. [H1]

## 4. What Can Produce an Opportunity

An opportunity can arise through dynastic continuity, negotiated common government, mutual protection, or a recognized settlement after conflict. Each needs actual participating authority and workable terms.

| Opportunity | Required political substance | Insufficient shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Dynastic settlement | Valid succession or accepted common-government arrangement | Marriage or shared dynasty alone |
| Mutual protection | Agreed common rule and sustainable protection obligations | Temporary defensive alliance |
| Negotiated integration | Defined government, participating scope and institutional terms | High relationship score alone |
| Post-conflict settlement | Recognized common authority beyond military occupation | Holding both capitals |
| Recognized successor settlement | Supported surviving or restored authority for each foundation | Invented heir or unsupported claimant |

No route automatically chooses a dominant expression. Relative strength informs the settlement but does not execute it. A weakened foundation can negotiate rights; a strong foundation can accept balance.

## 7. Internal Recognition: Sufficient, Not Unanimous

Internal recognition means the settlement has enough valid political and institutional support to operate common rule. It is not unanimous approval, a universal loyalty maximum, or recognition by every foreign power.

```yaml
recognition_evidence_categories:

  - EveryNobleApproves
```

Existing character and governance records determine who can speak for each foundation. Do not fabricate a universal medieval ratification council or require the same institutions in every era. Surviving dissent can become an inherited governance pressure if common rule remains viable.

## 19. Worked Design Cases

1. **Marriage without common government:** relationship opportunity may develop; no faction activates.
2. **Both foundations weak:** a viable balanced settlement can form; no equality or strength minimum is invented here.
3. **Human controls junior Aragon:** accepted Barcelona seniority does not transfer the human campaign to AI.
4. **Human controls junior Barcelona:** accepted Aragon seniority likewise preserves human continuation.
5. **Conquest without recognition:** ordinary territorial control continues; no Union faction or composite grant appears.
6. **Post-war accepted settlement:** actual surviving authorities establish common rule; the Formation Authority resolves the included assets once.
7. **Early compact:** negotiation can mature, but no High Era faction or military institution is awarded before the controlling chronology permits it.
8. **Ruler dies before commitment:** the Character Authority resolves continuity; materially changed terms require revalidation.
9. **Foreign ruler refuses recognition:** the refusal has supported diplomatic consequences, not an automatic universal veto.
10. **One region remains enemy-occupied:** it remains occupied; supported claims do not grant control.
11. **Repeated acceptance notification:** the completed transition is shown, not executed again.
12. **Later expression shift:** Governance changes precedence within the same faction without repeating inheritance.
13. **No acceptable settlement:** Barcelona and Aragon remain legitimate independent campaign choices.
14. **Two human players:** unsupported absorption is blocked rather than silently removing a controller.

These are proposed design fixtures, not executed tests or historical examples.
