## 1. Distinctive Purpose and Player Promise

**Decide which places the joined realm can afford to hold, which sieges it can sustain, and which threatened holdings justify a relief army.**

This proposed specialist addresses competition between actual strongpoints and field operations across the two foundations. A larger realm can coordinate defense in more places, but the same army, repair capacity, stores and treasury cannot be fully committed everywhere. Its strategic reward is a wider choice of defensible positions and concentration options, not a free wall, garrison or siege train.

The distinctive question is not whether an army can receive an order. It is whether keeping, relieving, reducing or abandoning a fortified position is worth the displaced campaign opportunity. A functioning defensive arrangement can remain unchanged. The player need not repeatedly approve every garrison or manage every sack of grain.

## 5. Strongpoint Functions, Not Universal Bonuses

A site may serve several functions only where actual geography and facilities support them.

| Possible function | Decision value | Required limit |
|---|---|---|
| Approach defense | Supports a protected route or local position | Does not automatically seal an entire region |
| Settlement protection | Helps defend an actual population and service center | Does not create acceptance or a field army |
| Concentration support | Provides a supported assembly or recovery location | Requires actual capacity and access |
| Storage support | Holds existing supplies | A building does not create goods |
| Port-related defense | Protects an admitted port function | Does not create ships or sea control |
| Frontier presence | Supports a current territorial commitment | A title does not prove control |

A site can be strategically useful without having the highest fortification level. Conversely, a large fortress can be poorly placed for the current objective. Exact topology and control effects remain with the shared map and siege systems.

## 6. Defensive Posture Portfolio

| Choice | Strategic purpose | Principal cost |
|---|---|---|
| Maintain current defense | Preserve a working arrangement | Continue actual upkeep and exposure elsewhere |
| Reinforce one priority site | Improve the chosen defense | Reduce field or other garrison availability |
| Hold in depth | Retain several viable positions and a mobile response | Requires supported routes and sufficient distinct capacity |
| Concentrate on fewer holdings | Avoid thin commitments everywhere | Accept disclosed exposure or loss of lower-priority functions |
| Repair before expanding | Restore damaged useful capability | Delay another campaign or investment |
| Relieve a threatened site | Restore a viable position or protect a force | Commit an actual relief army and its sustainment |
| Reduce or withdraw a commitment | Preserve forces for a better objective | Accept actual territorial, political and material consequences |

These are alternatives, not new posture buttons required by every settlement. Existing army, construction and campaign interfaces should carry them where possible.

## 8. Garrison, Field Force and Mobile Reserve

Use Military Command's distinction between actual field forces, garrisons, local defense, pool capacity and recovering formations. A garrison cannot simultaneously appear as a complete expeditionary army. A recruitment pool is not a mobile reserve.

A relief plan can use a real available force, reassign a force with disclosed consequences, or await actual recruitment and recovery. It cannot turn the nearest map marker into a reinforcement. A supporting relationship is not a second full assignment.

Releasing a garrison does not automatically demolish a site or erase its local rights. It changes actual defensive capacity through the owning systems. No fixed minimum garrison or universal abandonment penalty is invented here.

## 10. Offensive Siege Commitment

Before sustaining a siege, compare the objective with available forces, admitted access, actual siege readiness, supply continuity, known relief risk and home-defense exposure. The decision can be to continue, assault where supported, negotiate, narrow the objective, lift the siege or withdraw.

A siege is not automatically the best use of a larger Union army. Keeping an army tied to one objective can expose another part of the realm. No forced historical conquest sequence or universal preference for capturing every fortress is introduced.

Shared siege rules determine investment, assault availability, damage, attrition and resolution. This file does not add a second siege-progress meter or change battle statistics.

### 10A. Siege Phase Transition and Commitment Atomicity

The shared siege system may use different internal phases. At this authority's boundary, preserve the player-facing distinctions among approach, investment or containment, active siege, assault where admitted, negotiation, relief contest, withdrawal, capitulation, and resolved disposition.

A battle, opened route, negotiation proposal, or elapsed time changes only the states its owner confirms. No single event silently completes the entire siege portfolio.

## 11. Sustainment and Partial Isolation

Treat land access, maritime access, supply delivery, communication and reinforcement as related but distinct. A site may be cut off from one function while another remains usable under the shared systems. Do not infer complete blockade from a single reported disruption.

Actual stocks remain at their locations and keep their reservations. The same supplies cannot fully sustain the field army and the besieged garrison twice. A successful delivery does not automatically replenish all losses or restore every service.

A port does not prove maritime resupply. Ships, crews, capacity, access and functioning facilities remain required where modeled. No exact ration, endurance, starvation or attrition rate is assigned.

## 12. Relief and Rescue Objectives

Distinguish defeating the besieger, opening a supported route, delivering supplies, extracting a force and permanently restoring the position. A plan may accomplish one without the others.

These are proposed objective distinctions, not new mission types already implemented. Use only the operations the shared campaign supports. A rescue can be worthwhile even if holding the site afterward is not feasible.

## 13. Negotiation, Capitulation and Withdrawal

Negotiation is a valid alternative where diplomacy and siege systems support it. A proposal is not acceptance, and acceptance is not completed performance. Preserve actual terms affecting garrisons, captives, property, access and control.

Capitulation, occupation, annexation, recognition and recovery remain distinct. Taking control does not repair damage, refill stores, pacify the settlement or unlock its full recruitment roster.

Withdrawal preserves surviving state and completed costs. Do not refund consumed supplies, restore casualties or teleport survivors. Civilian movement or evacuation, if supported, belongs to population, settlement, access and public-order owners; this file creates no automatic displacement or replacement population.

## 14. Campaign and Battle Handoffs

Use the actual site condition, participating units, current strength, admitted siege assets, terrain and supported arrival conditions supplied by the producing owners. Do not generate missing equipment or fresh troops to match the presentation.

Return casualties, structural damage, control, surviving stores and continuing commitments through their owners once. Manual battle and autoresolve must preserve equivalent conservation rules, without requiring identical tactical outcomes.

A battle victory can resolve an immediate engagement while leaving access, repair, occupation or supply problems unresolved. Report those remaining conditions rather than marking the entire defensive plan complete.

## 15. Recovery and Captured Sites

After a siege or relief operation, choose between restoring the site, maintaining a reduced function, extracting surviving assets where permitted, or retiring an unsupported commitment. Preserve damage, casualties, local response and recovery demand.

Captured facilities may provide useful surviving capacity, but possession does not create trained operators, intact records or available supplies. Knowledge Adoption can support a justified transfer of practice; it does not bypass construction, recruitment or material owners.

Repairing one site must not produce a realm-wide defensive bonus unless a separate shared rule explicitly supports that effect. No compulsory recurring decay meter is added.

## 16. Information, AI and Player Presentation

Information supplies actor-visible threats, confidence, age and contradictions. A rumor of a relief army does not become a confirmed force or exact strength. AI cannot use concealed enemy orders to choose the perfect siege response.

AI compares the current plan with feasible reinforcement, repair, relief, concentration, negotiation and withdrawal. It must account for the force or resources no longer available elsewhere. Reassess after material evidence, battle results, access change or objective invalidation, not every panel opening.

The player sees the site's current purpose, known condition, actual committed forces, major sustainment blocker, displaced opportunity and feasible alternatives. Use text equivalents for icons and color. Suppress unchanged warnings; a working defense does not require recurring decisions.

## 17. Persistence and Union Lifecycle

Preserve site, army, siege, repair and commitment identities. Repeated reports or completion events create no duplicate cost, benefit or control transfer. An ambiguous transaction result is reconciled with the producing owner before retry.

Expression changes do not reset sieges or refresh garrisons. Dissolution routes actual sites, forces, stores and obligations once through Lifecycle; it does not clone a fortress or army into both successors. Reformation consumes current surviving state.

Later starts author their actual site and siege conditions. No opening template overwrites an advanced save. The three time tracks remain independent; date alone does not repair a site, deliver relief or complete an operation.
