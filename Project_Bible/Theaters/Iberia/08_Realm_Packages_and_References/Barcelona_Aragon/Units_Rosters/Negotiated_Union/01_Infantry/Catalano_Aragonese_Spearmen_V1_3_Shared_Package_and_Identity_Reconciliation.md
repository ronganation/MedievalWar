## 1. Definitive Strong Unit Verdict

**Catalano-Aragonese Spearmen** are a High Era joint Crown battle line created by repeated Aragonese and Catalan service under one practiced field command. They are selected medium-to-heavy spear-and-shield infantry, not common levies pressed together and not a later pike block.

On the battlefield they form a broad, layered center that receives cavalry or infantry contact, protects the Union's mixed missile arm, replaces a failing section with prepared reserve files, and then makes a short coordinated counterpush once the enemy is fixed and disordered. Their strength is not raw elite melee power. It is the ability to keep the center functioning through the moment when an ordinary allied line would split into separate contingents.

## 2. What the Player Actually Buys

The player is buying a dependable **joint battle center**, not a political paperwork mechanic. This unit stands above broad Catalan Spearmen and ordinary regional musters in equipment concentration, formation depth, repeated service, and common drill. It does not exceed native household guards, elite assault infantry, or later specialist pike formations in their own roles.

The key battlefield choice is whether to:

- hold the formation in a deep defensive center;
- spread it into a broader protective frontage for crossbows and archers;
- commit its rear files to relieve a failing section;
- or spend cohesion and stamina on a short counterpush after the enemy's attack loses momentum.

A player who simply orders a frontal attack wastes the card. A player who keeps the formation faced, supported, and deep enough to relieve its own front can make the entire mixed Union army work.

## 4. Player Fantasy, Description, and Flavor

**Player fantasy:** Build the spear line worthy of a working Crown. Catalan files extend and sustain the frontage; Aragonese files add protected depth and relief where their sources support it. Crossbows and archers fire behind the shields. Cavalry waits behind the wings. When the enemy charge loses force, reserve files step through, restore the line, and drive the disordered attackers back several deliberate paces before the officers call the formation to order again.

**Short description:** Selected Aragonese and Catalan spear infantry trained for a common High Era battle line. Strong at receiving charges, sheltering missiles, relieving damaged files, and executing a bounded counterpush.

**Encyclopedia description:** The union of Aragon and Barcelona does not erase their separate lands or military traditions. Where repeated campaigns, recognized contributor rights, compatible equipment, shared signals, and accepted commanders create a durable field institution, selected spear infantry may be trained to operate as one Crown formation. These soldiers retain native officers, property, and source obligations, but battle through a common frontage and relief plan. Their deep line can absorb ordinary pressure and protect missile troops better than an improvised allied host. Their weakness is the cost of keeping that line aligned. Broken ground, conflicting commands, missile crossfire, unequal casualties, and attacks during relief can turn the formation's mixed strength into disorder.

**Flavor line:** **Two realms, one line, and no room for the center to fail.**

## 8. Signature Battle Cycle: Cover, Set, Receive, Relieve, Counterpush

1. **Dress the Crown line.** Establish facing, contributor sectors, missile passages, flanks, relief lanes, and rally points.
2. **Cover.** Raise shields and preserve working space for crossbows, archers, and withdrawing skirmishers.
3. **Set.** Prepare the spear front when mounted or infantry contact is credible and ground permits.
4. **Receive.** Absorb the first collision without treating spear reach as immunity.
5. **Hold the pressure.** Maintain shield contact and controlled spear thrusts while leaders identify damaged files.
6. **Relieve.** Fresh files step into a threatened section while exhausted or depleted files pass rearward.
7. **Counterpush.** If the enemy is fixed, compressed, disordered, or recoiling, the formation advances a short controlled distance to restore space or seize ground.
8. **Halt.** Stop before the line outruns missile, cavalry, flank, or command support.
9. **Reform.** Rebuild shield contact, spear spacing, contributor sectors, and relief access.
10. **Withdraw in order.** Retire before encirclement, severe crossfire, elite breakthrough, or total exhaustion destroys the expensive joint formation.

### Practical matchup behavior

- Against ordinary cavalry, set the line and force the mounted player to find a flank or spend missiles first.
- Against heavy cavalry, survive through depth and support rather than claiming immunity.
- Against ordinary infantry, use reach, protection, and relief to win the longer contest.
- Against elite assault infantry, delay and support. Do not grind unsupported.
- Against missiles, raise shields only with return fire or a plan. Remaining static under sustained bolts should lose.
- Against skirmishers, advance only while the rest of the army prevents endless withdrawal.

## 13. Representative Battlefield Scenarios

### Scenario A: Crossbows behind the Crown line

Barcelona and Aragonese Crossbowmen occupy firing lanes behind the formation. Crown Spearmen maintain shielded gaps for shooting and reloading. When enemy infantry closes, the gaps shut, spears set, and the line receives contact. If one section weakens, relief files replace it while the crossbows shift to another passage. Cavalry waits behind the wing for the enemy's flank attempt.

### Scenario B: Failed enemy cavalry charge

The formation holds on firm ground with exposed but protected flanks. Enemy cavalry charges the front after insufficient missile preparation. The spears receive the impact at cost. The player does not immediately pursue. Relief restores the damaged file, then **Advance the Crown** pushes back supporting infantry while friendly cavalry threatens the mounted retreat.

### Scenario C: Enemy heavy infantry assault

Elite assault foot approaches under missile cover. The Crown line raises shields and preserves depth rather than advancing. Friendly crossbows and archers reduce the attackers. Once contact compresses, the formation holds only long enough for household foot, serjeants, axemen, or cavalry to intervene. An unsupported prolonged grind should eventually defeat it.

### Scenario D: Union line begins to split

One contributor sector has taken far greater casualties. Native leaders dispute another relief commitment. Advanced orders narrow. The player can hold in place temporarily, withdraw the depleted sector behind a fresh native formation, or separate the composite line in order. Forcing continued counterpush risks turning a battlefield success into a campaign grievance.

## 32. Decisive Roster Gap Ownership

The Negotiated Union already has access to separate Aragonese and Barcelona spear families. A composite card survives only if the player needs something those parallel contingents cannot provide by merely occupying adjacent ground.

**Catalano-Aragonese Spearmen own the prepared central battle line for a mixed Crown army.** They are the formation that converts partner coexistence into one dependable battlefield center: a protected frontage wide enough to cover the Union's missile arm, deep enough to replace damaged files, and controlled enough to launch one bounded counterpush without dissolving into two separate commands.

```yaml
roster_gap: MixedUnionArmyLacksOnePreparedCentralFormationWithSharedMissileLanesInternalReliefAndBoundedCounterpush
unit_owns:
  - MainOpenFieldCenterForNegotiatedUnionArmy
  - SharedAragoneseBarcelonaMissileFrontage
  - InternalReliefAcrossContributorSectors
  - OneBoundedCounterpushAfterSuccessfulReception
  - RallyReferenceForNativeWings
unit_does_not_own:
  - CheapestSpearMass
  - BestDifficultGroundInfantry
  - BestArmoredNativeSpearFront
  - BestCloseCombatReserve
  - BestAssaultInfantry
  - BestPikeOrLongWeaponDoctrine
  - BestGarrisonOrCastleCustodyTroops
why_player_recruits_it: BuildOneReliableCenterSoTheUnionArmyCanUseBothRealmsMissilesAndMountedForcesWithoutTheMiddleSplittingUnderPressure
why_player_may_refuse_it: NativeContingentsAreCheaperMoreAvailableAndBetterForRegionalTerrainRaidsGarrisonsOrPoliticallyStrainedCampaigns
```

### Thirty-second player read

- Put the Crown Spearmen in the center.
- Place crossbows and archers behind or between protected lanes.
- Keep enough depth for **Relief Through the Ranks**.
- Receive the enemy rather than racing forward.
- Use **Advance the Crown** only after the target is disordered or yielding.
- Guard both flanks with native infantry, cavalry, terrain, or reserves.

If the card cannot communicate that use immediately, the design has failed regardless of its campaign detail.
