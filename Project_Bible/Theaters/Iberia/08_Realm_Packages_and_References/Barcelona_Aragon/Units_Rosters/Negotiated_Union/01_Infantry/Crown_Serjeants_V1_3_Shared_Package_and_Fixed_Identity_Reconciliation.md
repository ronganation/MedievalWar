## 1. Mature Definitive Unit Verdict

**Crown Serjeants** are the Negotiated Union's recurring medium-heavy sword-and-shield infantry. They are selected from actual Aragonese and Barcelona serjeant, household-adjacent, castle, civic, urban, lordship, or other validated service sources and trained to fight under a common Crown command without erasing their native providers.

On the battlefield they advance behind substantial shields, relieve spear or missile formations under close pressure, enter the sustained melee after specialist troops create an opening, drive ordinary infantry back through disciplined shield-and-sword work, and secure the ground for the Crown army. They are not merely troops who arrive after the interesting action. **They are the formation that turns a local opening into a position the army can keep.**

The card is strong by default. Crown Serjeants should defeat low and ordinary infantry in direct close combat, carry a selected frontage against peer service infantry, and pressure protected opponents when shields, formation depth, fatigue, and combined arms deny those opponents their ideal fight. Their combat identity is not an invisible professional bonus. It is visible shield pressure, purposeful file rotation, disciplined passage through friendly lines, a controlled several-pace advance, and the ability to reform on gained ground. They sit below the Companyia de la Santa Creu in concentrated assault violence and below the Royal-Comital Household Guard in crisis cohesion and command protection, but they are broader, more repeatable, and better suited to winning the sustained infantry battle than either elite formation.

## 2. What the Player Buys

The player buys the negotiated army's dependable close-infantry backbone.

Crown Spearmen can hold the center and receive the first pressure, but spear geometry loses value once formations compress, weapons break, flanks fold inward, or objectives require entry and occupation. Santa Creu can break the hardest prepared opening, but should not remain in ordinary attrition after the decisive assault. The Crown Household Guard can save the commander or one critical collapse, but is too scarce and politically valuable for routine close fighting.

Crown Serjeants fill the space between those cards. They can:

- advance under shields through controlled frontal danger;
- replace spear files in compressed combat;
- attack ordinary infantry with sustained medium-heavy pressure;
- reinforce a threatened street, gate, courtyard, camp, or field sector;
- occupy the foothold opened by Santa Creu;
- secure standards, missiles, wounded, and routes inside the captured position;
- resist the immediate counterattack;
- reform and remain useful after the first clash.

The player should feel comfortable committing them to an important infantry fight. Their purpose is not to wait forever for a perfect target. Their limitations prevent elite or universal dominance, but do not make them hesitant or weak.

## 4. Player Fantasy, Description, and Flavor

**Player fantasy:** The Crown Spearmen have received the enemy attack, but the center is compressing into shield-to-shield melee. The player commits the Crown Serjeants through a prepared relief lane. The front files lock shields, replace exhausted spearmen, and take the close fight. Sword files work around shields while selected axes and maces strike at protected opponents. The formation presses several disciplined paces, restores the frontage, and secures the position. If Santa Creu has opened a breach, the Serjeants pass through behind the assault company, spread into the captured space, and take responsibility for keeping it.

**Short description:** Joint Aragonese and Barcelona sword-and-shield service infantry trained for close press, line relief, sustained melee, and objective security.

**Flavor line:** **The Crown takes ground with its chosen few. The Serjeants make it hold.**

## 6. Direct Collision Firewall

### Catalano-Aragonese Spearmen

Crown Spearmen own broad frontage, charge reception, missile protection, internal spear relief, and the bounded counterpush. Crown Serjeants own compressed melee, sword-and-shield relief, sustained close pressure, and objective security.

A Spearman formation normally receives the enemy. A Serjeant formation normally enters when the fight must be continued at close range or moved onto contested ground.

### Companyia de la Santa Creu

Santa Creu owns the protected final approach and the elite entry against the hardest bounded objective. Crown Serjeants cannot match its initial assault violence, armor-breaking concentration, or prestige.

Crown Serjeants are more available, broader in routine use, and better suited to occupy, defend, and continue fighting after the opening is made.

### Royal-Comital Household Guard

The Household Guard owns shared-command continuity and one finite crisis intervention. Crown Serjeants are not tied primarily to the commander and should not receive household succession, extraction, or standards-recovery authority.

The Guard saves the battle at one critical point. The Serjeants perform the strong recurring infantry work that prevents every difficult fight from becoming a household crisis.

### Aragonese Serjeants

Aragonese Serjeants remain an Aragonese institutional infantry family with native providers, obligations, recruitment, equipment, command, and recovery. Crown Serjeants become possible only through repeated joint service and real contribution from Barcelona as well as Aragon.

### Barcelona Castle Sergeants

Barcelona Castle Sergeants retain castle custody, permanent post relationships, and controlled field release. Crown Serjeants are a mobile Crown field formation and cannot claim permanent site personnel without actual release.

### Barcelona Comital Household Foot Retainers and Royal Mesnada Foot

Native household formations retain command protection, court relationships, and exceptional reserve identity. Crown Serjeants may include qualified released contributors only where actual providers permit it, but do not inherit household status.

## 11. Signature Battle Cycle: Advance, Relieve, Press, Secure

1. **Advance behind shields.** Approach the threatened or contested sector without pretending immunity to missiles.
2. **Read the contact.** Identify whether the friendly line needs reinforcement, replacement, or an offensive close-infantry push.
3. **Open the relief lane.** Friendly Crown Spearmen, missile troops, or damaged infantry shorten frontage or disengage by signal.
4. **Take the press.** Crown Serjeants enter the existing close fight in compact order.
5. **Lock shields and fight.** Sword files sustain pressure while selected striking files address dense or protected resistance.
6. **Replace files.** Relief depth moves forward as fatigue, casualties, shield damage, and disorder accumulate.
7. **Advance several controlled paces.** Push ordinary or wavering infantry from the immediate ground when the opportunity exists.
8. **Secure for the Crown.** Reform the frontage, protect passages, standards, wounded, missiles, and incoming support.
9. **Hold the counterattack.** Defend the newly secured position as a strong compact body.
10. **Reform or withdraw.** Preserve enough organization to continue the battle or leave before elite opposition and fatigue destroy the formation.

## 11A. Physical Relief Lane and Passage-of-Lines Contract

Relief is the unit's signature tactical transaction, but it is never a healing pulse or instantaneous model swap. A Crown Serjeant relief requires actual ground, signals, leaders, and cooperation from the formation being relieved.

```yaml
relief_lane_elements:
  approach_lane: RouteFromReserveToFriendlyRearWithoutCrossingBlockedBodies
  receiving_frontage: ExactSectionTheSerjeantsWillAssume
  yielding_frontage: FriendlyFilesAbleToShortenTurnOrWithdrawByOrder
  passage_space: PhysicalGapForTwoFormationsToExchangeWithoutInterpenetration
  casualty_route: PathForWoundedBrokenEquipmentAndDisorderedFilesToLeave
  flank_cover: FriendlyOrTerrainProtectionDuringTheExchange
  reform_space: GroundBehindOrBesideTheNewFrontageForTheRelievedFormation
relief_states:
  Prepared: LeadersSignalsLaneAndFrontagePass
  Opening: YieldingFilesBeginControlledMovement
  Passing: BothFormationsShareAHighRiskTransferSpace
  Assumed: SerjeantsOwnTheFrontageRelievedFilesClearContact
  Contested: EnemyPressureInterferesButTransferMayStillSucceed
  Congested: BodiesWoundedEquipmentOrFriendlyRoutBlockPassage
  Collapsed: NoOrderedExchangeRemainsPossible
```

The relieved unit retains its casualties, fatigue, equipment state, morale history, and formation damage. Crown Serjeants inherit the actual enemy pressure and terrain. They do not erase either formation's condition.

## 16. Combined-Arms Transactions

### Catalano-Aragonese Spearmen

The Spearmen receive, fix, and protect. Crown Serjeants relieve a compressed section, enter the close fight, or secure ground after the spear line advances. The two cards form the basic negotiated infantry partnership.

### Companyia de la Santa Creu

Santa Creu breaks the most difficult local position. Crown Serjeants follow, widen the usable frontage, occupy the objective, defeat remaining ordinary opposition, and release the assault company for recovery.

### Royal-Comital Household Guard

Crown Serjeants handle recurring close-infantry problems so the Household Guard remains fresh for command protection or a genuine crisis. If the Serjeants fail and command continuity becomes threatened, the Guard may intervene.

### Crown Crossbow Sergeants

Crossbows suppress armored or dense targets while Crown Serjeants protect the firing line, close after volleys, or hold the objective that missile pressure helps create.

### Crown Mounted Sergeants

Mounted Sergeants protect flanks, intercept mobile threats, and exploit enemies displaced by Crown Serjeants. Foot Serjeants secure the ground; mounted troops extend control beyond it.

### Cavallers de la Senyera

The noble cavalry delivers shock against a fixed or disordered enemy while Crown Serjeants hold the local infantry fight and prevent the cavalry's gains from being lost.

### Native infantry

Native spears, serjeants, household foot, castle troops, regional infantry, and civic forces remain valid partners. The joint card does not absorb native rosters or create free contributors.

## 27. Representative Battle Scenarios

### Relief of the Crown center

Catalano-Aragonese Spearmen receive an infantry assault and hold, but the contact compresses into sidearm fighting. Crown Serjeants approach through a marked lane, take over the most threatened frontage, and allow damaged spear files to withdraw. The Serjeants then press the ordinary enemy infantry back and secure enough space for the Spearmen to reform.

### Following Santa Creu

The Companyia de la Santa Creu breaks a defended gate interior. Crown Serjeants follow through, defeat remaining ordinary defenders, spread into the courtyard, protect the flanks and passage, and hold against the immediate counterattack. Santa Creu withdraws before its elite assault cadre is consumed.

### Field counterattack

Crossbow fire and the Crown Spearmen disorder an enemy sword unit. Crown Serjeants advance under shields, enter the fixed formation, win the close press, and move several controlled paces. The player then activates Secure for the Crown rather than pursuing into fresh enemy reserves.

### Missile line threatened

Enemy light infantry penetrates toward Crown Crossbow Sergeants. Crown Serjeants intercept, take the close fight, and create time for the crossbows to shift position. The Serjeants remain to hold the recovered sector while the Household Guard stays near command.

### Failed relief

Crown Serjeants are committed after the spear frontage has already routed. Friendly fugitives block the lane, the Serjeants arrive disordered, and fresh enemy assault infantry strikes during the transfer. The card is strong, but relief timing and physical space still matter.

### Elite opposition

Fresh elite household infantry meets Crown Serjeants in open combat. The Serjeants fight well but begin losing the longer contest. Success requires crossbow pressure, spear support, cavalry threat, terrain, or a timely withdrawal rather than inflated universal superiority.
