## 1. Mature Definitive Unit Verdict

**Union Wardens** are joint Aragonese and Barcelona custody infantry assigned to fortified Crown sites that neither realm can safely or politically hold alone. They guard gates, arsenals, magazines, camps, bridges, ports where applicable, fortified administrative centers, shared treasuries, assembly grounds, and other recognized Union positions under a negotiated custody charter.

They are not generic garrison troops and not Crown Serjeants standing still. Their battlefield strength comes from knowing the assigned site, preparing its approaches, dividing posts, retaining a local reserve, controlling access, maintaining signals, rotating watches, and responding to a breach without abandoning the rest of the position.

On the campaign map, recruiting or assigning Union Wardens creates a real choice: concentrate reliable joint custody in an important Crown site, or leave the relevant Aragonese and Barcelona troops available to their native defenses and field armies.

## 2. What the Player Buys

The player buys an infantry formation that is strongest when defending a prepared, politically important position for which it has actual custody responsibility.

Union Wardens can:

- occupy several connected posts without treating every soldier as an independent detachment;
- protect a gate, passage, standard, arsenal, camp entrance, bridge approach, or defended courtyard;
- maintain one finite local reserve rather than committing the whole body at first contact;
- reinforce the threatened post by signal;
- counterattack troops that penetrate a controlled entry;
- conduct an ordered transfer between watches or relief companies;
- preserve access routes for friendly troops and supplies;
- continue custody after the immediate battle rather than disappearing when the field objective is captured.

The formation is less suitable than Crown Serjeants for sustained offensive field combat and less violent than Santa Creu in assault. Its value is that prepared custody turns a strategically important position into a difficult, organized problem for the enemy.

## 3. One-Screen Total War Player Card

- **Unit:** Union Wardens
- **Class:** Strong prepared-defense and joint garrison infantry
- **Battle promise:** distribute posts, guard the approaches, reinforce the threatened entry, and counterattack before the enemy converts a breach into control of the site
- **Best use:** gates, camps, bridges, arsenals, courtyards, fortified administrative sites, port approaches where applicable, breaches under defense, and connected strongpoints
- **Worst use:** open pursuit, broad offensive field frontage, unsupported assault, rapid raids, several isolated objectives beyond signal and relief range, and cavalry-dominated open ground
- **Strategic value:** protects shared Crown infrastructure without assigning permanent custody to only one realm
- **Strategic cost:** removes real castle, civic, urban, serjeant, and related custodial capacity from native institutions
- **Counterplay:** attack several posts, cut signals, block the reserve route, create fire or missile pressure, isolate the command post, feint one entry and strike another, or force the Wardens to fight outside prepared ground
- **Opening State:** unavailable on January 1, 1062
- **Exact values:** unresolved

## 4. Player Fantasy and Flavor

The alarm begins at an outer approach. The first Warden post closes the gate lane and signals the command position. Missile troops and defenders at other posts remain in place rather than rushing toward the first noise. The commander releases part of the local reserve through a protected route. When attackers force the threshold, the Wardens counterattack from within the site, restore the barrier, recover the standard and wounded, and reopen the passage for friendly relief.

Elsewhere, a feint draws attention toward one wall section. The player resists the temptation to abandon every post. A second alarm reveals the true attack at the arsenal courtyard. Because the reserve was preserved, the Wardens can reinforce the correct point without surrendering the gate.

**Short description:** Joint Aragonese and Barcelona custody infantry trained to control Crown sites, distribute posts, reinforce threatened entries, and recover local breaches.

**Flavor line:** **Two realms may share the Crown. No enemy shares its gates.**

## 6. Collision Firewall

### Crown Serjeants

Crown Serjeants are mobile professional field infantry. They attack, relieve, counterattack, secure battlefield ground, and reform for continued service. Union Wardens own long-duration custody, post distribution, access control, watch rotation, stored resources, prepared routes, and site-specific reserve response.

Crown Serjeants may secure a captured gate for the battle. Union Wardens maintain that Crown gate as an institution after the army moves on.

### Castle Wardens of Aragon

Castle Wardens retain Aragonese site custody, native appointments, local knowledge, obligations, and provider relationships. Union Wardens exist only at recognized shared Crown sites under a joint charter and require real Barcelona participation as well as Aragonese contribution.

### Barcelona Castle, Civic, and Urban Custodial Troops

Barcelona custodial formations retain their native sites and obligations. Union Wardens cannot draw them automatically or replace urban and comital defense throughout Barcelona.

### Royal-Comital Household Guard

The Household Guard protects rulers, command continuity, and shared standards. Union Wardens protect a site and its custody system. A ruler entering the site does not convert all Wardens into household guards.

### Companyia de la Santa Creu

Santa Creu attacks defended entries. Union Wardens defend and recover them. The cards form opposing mission specialists without becoming mirrored stat packages.

## 10. Signature Battle Cycle: Post, Signal, Reinforce, Recover

1. **Assign posts.** Distribute files across the actual approaches without stripping the reserve.
2. **Prepare the site.** Use existing barriers, routes, stores, signals, and defensible geometry.
3. **Watch and report.** Outer posts identify the real attack and avoid dying silently where withdrawal is possible.
4. **Delay.** Entry files hold long enough for the command post to understand the threat.
5. **Preserve the reserve.** Do not commit the entire body to the first alarm.
6. **Reinforce by signal.** Move the appropriate reserve or nearby post along a real internal route.
7. **Counterattack the breach.** Strike attackers whose formation is compressed by entry geometry.
8. **Recover the post.** Restore barriers, standards, casualty routes, and access control.
9. **Rotate watches and files.** Replace fatigued defenders without pretending losses disappear.
10. **Receive relief.** Conduct an ordered custody transfer if another recognized formation assumes the site.

## 15. Combined-Arms Transactions

### Crown Serjeants

Crown Serjeants can serve as field relief outside the site or reinforce a broad frontage. Union Wardens preserve post geometry, signals, access, and the local reserve. During a major siege, Serjeants may act as mobile field reinforcement while Wardens remain responsible for custody.

### Crown Spearmen

Spearmen can reinforce an exposed open approach or protect Wardens from cavalry outside the site. Wardens control the entry, internal routes, and connected posts.

### Santa Creu

When defending, Wardens oppose the kind of assault Santa Creu performs. If the Crown is retaking its own site, Santa Creu may break the occupied entry while surviving Wardens provide local knowledge, signals, and post restoration after recovery.

### Household Guard

The Household Guard protects the ruler and command. Wardens protect the site. The two may cooperate at a Crown center, but their custody and bodyguard authorities remain separate.

### Crossbows and other missiles

Wardens protect firing posts, ammunition access, withdrawal routes, and local reserves. Missile troops provide the ranged weight the Warden card does not automatically contain.

### Mounted forces

Mounted troops patrol and contest exterior approaches. Wardens control the fixed position and receive mounted reports or survivors through protected routes.

## 25. Representative Battle Scenarios

### Gate assault

An enemy attacks the main gate while pressure begins at a secondary passage. The player holds the local reserve until the true commitment becomes clear, then sends part of the reserve to the gate. The Wardens counterattack attackers compressed inside the threshold and restore the post without abandoning the secondary passage.

### False alarm and true breach

A visible attack against the outer wall draws attention, but signals from the arsenal court reveal the true assault. Because the reserve was preserved, Union Wardens reinforce the arsenal route and prevent the enemy from converting surprise into interior control.

### Camp defense

Wardens hold the camp entrance, stores, standards, and a secondary exit. Crown Spearmen support the open approach while Crown Serjeants act as mobile field reserve. Wardens remain focused on custody and internal access.

### Failed reserve commitment

The player sends the entire reserve to the first alarm. A second attack captures the command-post route. The gate may still hold, but shared signals and whole-site control fail. Native posts continue locally without coordinated reserve response.

### Retaking a Crown site

A former Crown post has been captured. Santa Creu breaks the occupied gate while surviving or reassembled Wardens use site knowledge to identify routes and likely post positions. After entry, the Wardens restore custody rather than replacing Santa Creu's assault role.

### Open-field misuse

Union Wardens are drawn away from their assigned position and deployed as general offensive infantry. They fight competently but lose the post, signal, reserve, and prepared-ground advantages that justify the card.
