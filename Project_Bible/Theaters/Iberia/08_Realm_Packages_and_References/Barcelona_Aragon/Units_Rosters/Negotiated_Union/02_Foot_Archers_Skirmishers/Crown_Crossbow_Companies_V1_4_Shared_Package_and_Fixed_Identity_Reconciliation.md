## 1. Final Static Unit Verdict

**Crown Crossbow Companies are the Negotiated Union’s ordinary mixed-realm crossbow battle line. Trained Aragonese and Barcelona companies operate under a common Crown plan to cover several lanes, maintain protected bolt pressure, relieve a weakening contributor sector, and retire by company toward another defensible position.**

Their advantage is organizational breadth and contributor redundancy, not professional supremacy. Separate contributor companies can cover adjacent or different sectors, preserve local fire when whole-line command degrades, and support a bounded company withdrawal. Mixed service also carries real disadvantages: slower whole-line response, equipment compatibility burdens, passage congestion, political friction, and uneven recovery.

The distinction from Crown Crossbow Sergeants is now explicit. Crown Crossbowmen own broader ordinary availability, sector coverage, ordinary file fire, contributor relief, and company retirement. Crown Crossbow Sergeants own selected controlled salvoes, counter-missile discipline, stronger position endurance, and sergeant-led reform and withdrawal. The ordinary card therefore survives as a positive battlefield role rather than merely a lower-stat version of the selected formation.

## 4. Player Fantasy, Description, and Flavor

**Player fantasy:** Stretch a dependable crossbow line across the Union center without spending the scarce Sergeant company. Aragonese and Barcelona companies cover separate lanes, combine their loaded strength when armor commits, and relieve one another when return fire or casualties weaken a sector. The formation can retire by company and reappear behind a second frontage, but a rushed whole-line movement risks tangled passages, disputed orders, and separated contributors.

**Short description:** Ordinary Aragonese and Barcelona crossbow files organized for durable mixed-realm field service under accepted Crown command.

**Encyclopedia description:** Repeated joint campaigning can create more than temporary allied contingents. Where both realms release real operators, weapons, bolts, leaders, maintainers, stores, and service rights into a durable ordinary formation, Crown Crossbow Companies may emerge. The unit offers broader field availability than Crown Crossbow Sergeants, but it lacks the selected professional cadre, internal security, counter-missile discipline, and controlled withdrawal of that higher formation. Its success rests on practical compatibility, basic common commands, a working protective frontage, and a political agreement strong enough to survive casualties and unequal contribution.

**Flavor line:** **Set the line. Hold the bolts. Loose for the Crown.**

## 16. Signature Battle Cycle: Form, Cover, Concentrate, Relieve, Retire

**FORM** the Aragonese and Barcelona contributor companies into workable weapon, bolt, command, and passage groups behind a valid frontage.

**COVER** separate lanes with recognized companies and common target boundaries. Use ordinary file fire while ready shares remain uneven.

**CONCENTRATE** both companies only when a dense, armored, mounted, fixed, or committed target creates a window worth the coverage loss and reload lull.

**RELIEVE** a suppressed, depleted, or moving company with the contributor that still has useful ready files. Relief preserves pressure but creates exposure and passage burden.

**REFORM** after casualties, movement, weapon failure, command disruption, or equipment mismatch.

**RETIRE** by company toward a second position before cavalry, fresh infantry, or a collapsing frontage reaches the reload depth. This is a bounded ordinary withdrawal, not Sergeant-secured extraction.

## 34. Tactical Scenarios

### Union center versus armored infantry

Catalano-Aragonese Spearmen hold the frontage. Crown Crossbowmen use deliberate volley when the armored formation compresses against the line, then return to ordinary file fire as loaded files become available. The formation ceases fire when friendly contact blocks the lane.

### Routine field battle

The Crown line supplies repeated ordinary pressure while native archers shape movement and Crown Crossbow Sergeants remain in reserve for counter-missile or high-consequence target work.

### Contributor command disruption

The Union captain falls and common orders slow. Native subleaders keep their files functioning, but coordinated volleys and whole-line reform degrade. The player either restores accepted command or withdraws contributor groups separately.

### Cavalry threat

The formation fires before contact while spears hold. If cavalry bypasses the frontage, Crown Crossbowmen withdraw. No internal security-sergeant component exists to rescue a failed deployment.

### Siege or gate lane

The formation controls a known approach with adequate bolts, reload depth, and relief. If the lane becomes a melee, close infantry must take over.

### Company relief under counterfire

Barcelona files are suppressed and their loaded share falls. The Aragonese company expands into the threatened lane while Barcelona files retire behind the second frontage. The Crown line keeps partial pressure, but the covering company becomes more exposed and cannot maintain every sector.

### Second-position defense

The first spear frontage becomes untenable. One company retires while the other preserves loaded threat. The rear company deploys behind the fallback line, then covers the second company's movement. Success is preserving a functioning crossbow line, not firing continuously during the retreat.
