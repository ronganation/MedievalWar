## 1. Final Static Unit Verdict

**Crown Vanguard Bands are the Negotiated Union’s single composite foot-skirmisher card. Mixed-realm light bands spread ahead of the Crown army, contest enemy screens, support one another with brief finite casts, contract before isolation, and disengage by bounds toward a prepared Union line.**

They are fighting skirmish infantry first. They can drive away weaker scouts, delay comparable light troops, work broken approaches, harass exposed flanks of already engaged enemies, cover Crown missile movement, protect a withdrawal, and rally for another bounded task. Their advantage comes from frontage control, mutual support, contraction, and recovery. It does not come from superior individual lethality, elite melee, unlimited missiles, or hidden information.

Observed threat marking remains a limited supporting effect. It may pass a current lane or target category to a nearby receiving arm, but it creates no shared vision, accuracy, damage, facing change, ambush, or perfect location. Almocadens retain sharper selected cast-close-disengage action. Almughavars retain operational raiding, disruption, exploitation, and campaign persistence. Native and regional light troops retain cheaper local service, source coherence, and ownership.

## 4. Player Fantasy, Description, and Flavor

**Player fantasy:** Send several Crown bands ahead of the army. One presses enemy skirmishers off a crossing while another threatens the edge of an engaged formation. When cavalry appears, the outer bands contract. A supporting band casts to cover the first withdrawal, then follows through a prepared passage while Crown archers and spears take over the fight. The Vanguard rallies behind the line instead of dissolving into a rout and can later screen a flank or cover another reposition.

**Short description:** Mixed Aragonese and Barcelona light bands trained to contest the forward fight, support one another across broken approaches, and withdraw in stages toward the Crown army.

**Encyclopedia description:** A durable Union requires more than placing native light troops under one banner. Contributor bands must recognize common signals, protect one another’s movement, know when frontage has become overextension, and return through friendly lines without collapsing the formations behind them. Crown Vanguard Bands embody that repeated joint practice. They remain light, finite, and vulnerable, but they give the Union army a dependable composite screen without replacing native javelinmen, selected Almocadens, or operational Almughavars.

**Flavor line:** **Spread the screen. Cast for the next band. Return to the Crown.**

## 13. Signature Battle Cycle: Spread, Skirmish, Support, Contract, Disengage, Rally

**SPREAD** contributor bands across useful approaches, but keep each inside a workable mutual-support and withdrawal plan.

**SKIRMISH** with finite missiles to drive away weaker screens, delay comparable light troops, pressure exposed flanks, or protect friendly movement.

**SUPPORT** a pressured band with a brief cast, route protection, warning, or local relief instead of feeding the entire screen into one fight.

**CONTRACT** before outer bands become isolated, trading coverage for local cohesion and safer passages.

**DISENGAGE** by bands before cavalry or formed infantry closes the route. One band moves while another briefly covers.

**RALLY** behind spears, missile lines, or another prepared reception point. Reform for flank warning, rear-guard work, missile reposition cover, or a second screen when strength permits.

## 32. Tactical Scenarios

### Enemy screen across broken ground

The contributor bands spread across separate approaches and contest enemy skirmishers with brief casts. One band begins taking heavier pressure. The nearest supporter casts across its movement lane long enough for it to give ground. The player does not chase when the enemy screen withdraws.

### Contraction before cavalry contact

Light cavalry appears beyond the outer band. The player surrenders the widest lane, contracts both flanks toward the spear frontage, and preserves the central passage. A late outer band risks being pinned if contraction begins too slowly.

### Flank harassment beside the Crown line

Enemy infantry is already fixed against friendly spears. One Vanguard band applies a bounded cast into an exposed flank while another protects its return path. The attack ends before the enemy can turn fresh troops onto the skirmishers.

### Crown missile reposition cover

Crown Archer Companies or Crown Crossbowmen move toward a second position. Vanguard bands occupy the broken approach, cast against pursuing light troops, and disengage through the newly established line. Their purpose is to preserve the transition, not win a prolonged missile duel.

### Failed screen

Fast cavalry closes one passage while a supporting band is already low on missiles. One contributor reaches the reception point, another becomes isolated, and the composite screen fragments. Surviving groups retain actual ownership and losses; the Crown label creates no free recovery.

### Rally and second screen

After an orderly band withdrawal, surviving leaders regroup the formation behind the main line. If fatigue, casualties, missiles, routes, and reception still pass, the unit shifts to a flank and establishes a narrower second screen.
