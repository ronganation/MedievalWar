**Cavallers d’Urgell** are the Negotiated Union’s active frontier line cavalry: a county-identified formation assembled from exact Urgell riders, mounted retainers, file leaders, horses, lances or mounted spears, shields, sidearms, attendants, and support released into the joint political path. They are faster and more aggressive than Crown Mounted Sergeants, less armored and less melee-dominant than Cavallers de Montcada, and less committed to one decisive impact than Cavallers de la Senyera.

The unit exists to **take and keep the outer cavalry lane**. Urgell files advance ahead of the elite reserve, drive away hostile screens, intercept light and ordinary medium cavalry, punish exposed missile troops, pursue a genuinely broken mounted enemy, and wheel back toward the next useful flank before heavy reserves close the battle. Their strength is sustained mounted initiative, not missile harassment or elite shock.

### 1A. What the player actually buys

- claim a useful flank, road, valley approach, or open lane;
- force hostile light cavalry to yield or commit;
- defeat ordinary mounted screens through spear-and-shield contact;
- exploit broken cavalry or exposed missiles with bounded pursuit;
- recall before pursuit becomes isolation;
- wheel toward another flank while elite cavalry remains preserved.

## 5. Player-Facing Description and Combat Fantasy

Urgell’s riders appear where the battle is loosest and most mobile. The formation advances along a flank road or open valley lane, pushes back hostile scouts and mounted screens, and forces the enemy to protect spaces it hoped to use freely. When enemy light or medium horse commits, the Urgell files meet it with spear or lance, shields, and supporting depth. When the enemy genuinely breaks, Urgell pursues hard enough to prevent an easy rally, then recalls and wheels toward the next exposed flank.

**Combat fantasy:** get there first, force ordinary enemy horse to yield, turn retreat into battlefield space, and return before the elite cavalry battle begins.

### 12A. Retinue depth as a battlefield mechanism

Retinue support is not a hidden stat bonus. It gives the formation identifiable subleaders who can keep a file together when the leading noble is engaged, attendants who maintain horses and equipment between commitments, and messengers or standards that carry recall and reform signals. A complete retinue can help a noble group maintain its shape through the transition from impact to short melee and back to reserve. Losing that support narrows the safe order set: a damaged formation might still protect a flank but no longer execute a coordinated second charge.

Combatant retainers, if any, count as actual riders with horses and gear; foot attendants do not silently appear as mounted combatants. A lord's household may supply one role but not every role. The card can have mixed provenance without claiming identical noble entourages.

### 13A. Horse quality is achieved, not a breed label

A horse capable of carrying an armored rider into controlled contact must be assessed for condition, load, temperament, training, and tack fit. Better horse access can increase the unit's confidence in alignment and follow-through, but a high-quality animal in poor condition should not perform like a fresh one. Pair familiarity matters particularly during the **break-off and reform** that distinguish this card from a one-impact levy. A replacement horse restores physical mobility first; charge timing and rider-horse coordination return only through integration.

Separate horse losses from rider losses. Captured or surviving horses retain an owner and may be injured, unsuitable, or already reserved. A defeated noble formation cannot simply buy a full remount pool from the treasury.

### 16A. The noble command problem

A multi-household formation has a distinctive vulnerability: capable riders may disagree over precedence or the duration of service even while sharing a battlefield. Before commitment, the army command assigns the objective and the recognized formation leader establishes the charge signal, stop line, reserve position, and succession order. A subleader may preserve a surviving file after the first impact, but cannot conjure absent riders or negate a household recall.

In battle, command failure should produce a **specific loss of coordinated behavior**, not a generic morale penalty: files charge at different moments, press beyond the agreed line, or return to separate household standards. This is the meaningful downside of concentrating noble sources.

### 17A. Formation choices and the moment of contact

**Reserve line:** compact enough to respond, far enough back to avoid unnecessary missile exposure. **Countercharge files:** align to meet an already committed mounted threat without colliding with friendly infantry. **Controlled shock line:** choose an open approach against a flank, rear, or disrupted frontage. **Press formation:** keep a local advantage for a short melee, with subleaders preventing the wing from dissolving into individual pursuit. **Reform on standards:** recover facing, riders, and spacing behind friendly cover.

A wedge, if later supported by evidence and shared formation rules, is not the card's default special ability. A charge cannot be ordered at full quality through a narrow gate, steep broken ground, or a friendly formation occupying the lane.

## 18. Signature Battle Cycle

- Assess outer lanes, hostile screens, mounted reserves, missiles, ground, and return routes.
- Advance before the heavy cavalry commits and claim a useful cavalry lane.
- Intercept scouts, pursuers, and ordinary cavalry attempting to control that lane.
- Charge only when alignment, ground, target, support, and recall route pass.
- Drive enemy horse from the sector without accepting an unnecessary heavy-cavalry grind.
- Pursue only a genuine break, preserving enough horse condition and file control to recall.
- Recall before fresh reserve, prepared infantry, blocked ground, or missile crossfire closes the route.
- Wheel toward another flank, exposed missile body, retreat route, or reserve lane.
- Reposition and decide whether another action remains possible.

### 18A. Two winning cycles

**Lane control:** displace screens, hold the outer approach, and preserve space for Union infantry, missiles, or elite cavalry.

**Pursuit and return:** prevent easy enemy rally, recall under leaders and standards, and reappear as a second flank threat.

### 20A. Orders should feel different in play

**Hold the wing** protects the expensive option but can allow an enemy to win elsewhere. **Intercept committed ordinary horse** trades position for a chance to deny the lane. **Attack the exposed flank** is worthwhile only when the route remains open and heavier reserves cannot trap the formation. **Drive off and separate** preserves the county line-cavalry role by ending contact before it becomes a heavy grind. **Recall on the standard** sacrifices some pursuit to recover files for the next lane. **Protect withdrawal** turns the card from offensive wing into a rearward shield, reducing its freedom to exploit.

The UI should communicate why an order is weakened rather than simply gray it out. Example: “Charge lane open; retinue leader missing; coordinated press unavailable.”

### 27A. What “shared recruitment” actually means for this card

This is not a native Aragon or Barcelona unit merely because the file is stored in the shared Barcelona-Aragon package. Urgell sources retain exact ownership. Under independent development, this negotiated composite card is unavailable unless a separate native or regional authority admits a successor expression. Under negotiated union, a Barcelona-led army might request an Urgell noble contingent if the exact source agrees, the political compact allows it, and horses, equipment, leaders, route, and return pass. The player sees a **Cavallers d’Urgell** card with Urgell replacement and recovery, not a Barcelona copy. Conversely, Barcelona may use an eligible Barcelona formation under its own source terms.

Under domination, the controlling side may demand service, but a reluctant noble household can limit or refuse effective release, require garrison security, or produce unreliable service. Aragon dominating Barcelona does not create extra Cavallers d’Urgell; Barcelona dominating Aragon does not erase them. If the relationship breaks, future access can close while an already mustered contingent resolves its actual service and return. These are candidate unit interfaces under the controlling three-path authorities, not invented universal unlock thresholds.

## 42. Tactical Calibration Requirements

Test against Infanzones, Royal Mesnada, Aragonese Riders, Aragonese Jinetes, Barcelona Comital Horsemen, ordinary medium and heavy cavalry, mounted serjeants, spears, pikes, bows, crossbows, armored infantry, and future Gendarmes.

The decisive test is whether Cavallers d’Urgell remain county line cavalry for outer-lane control, bounded pursuit, recall, and reposition rather than replacing native Aragon, Barcelona, Montcada, or Senyera formations.

### 42A. Explicit pass/fail scenarios

1. **Early countercharge parity:** Infanzones can answer a limited threat; Nobles should justify their greater political and material cost by sustaining an organized local follow-through, not by guaranteed one-hit rout.
2. **Royal reserve parity:** Royal Mesnada protects the ruler and can intervene exceptionally; Nobles should not inherit its protection floor or become its cheaper duplicate.
3. **Heavy-cavalry peer:** a fresh, supported peer can defeat or check the Nobles. Nobility alone does not determine outcome.
4. **Prepared spears:** frontal charge fails to provide reliable value and may incur severe horse and leader loss.
5. **Two-stage engagement:** after first contact, a well-led, well-supported noble wing can choose a short press or a disciplined reform; a depleted one cannot.
6. **Union access:** a Barcelona army can receive an eligible Urgell contingent only when exact source, service, command, and physical capacity pass; donor capacity falls accordingly.
7. **Domination and reversal:** political control can change access and reliability without creating or deleting physical people, horses, or retinues.

If scenarios 1 and 5 do not produce a distinctive decision, the separate card fails irrespective of art quality.
