**Cavallers de Montcada** are a High Era Negotiated Union noble cavalry battle wing anchored by an admitted Montcada contribution assembled from a historically admitted Montcada household expression and its actually released nobles, mounted retainers, leaders, standards, selected horses, armor, lances, sidearms, attendants, and support. They are not generic Catalan knights and not Barcelona Comital Mounted Retainers with a famous name. The unit exists to answer serious cavalry with serious cavalry: hold behind the army, countercharge an enemy mounted commitment, strike a fixed or exposed flank, remain organized through a bounded mounted melee, and reform before a local victory becomes the destruction of the house's mounted strength.

The player's purchase is **a cohesive named-house battle wing**. Compared with broad high-status mounted contingents, it is narrower and rarer but more internally coherent. Compared with Comital Mounted Retainers, it is less concerned with the count's immediate escort and more capable of sustained field combat. Compared with Cavallers de la Senyera, it lacks the dual-realm formation's highest joint first-impact concentration but is better suited to an extended native mounted contest, controlled local press, and credible second mission after reform.

## 1A. What the player actually buys

The player buys a powerful but finite cavalry formation with a complete job:

- preserve one high-quality mounted wing until the decisive sector is known;
- answer committed enemy cavalry with a disciplined countercharge;
- deliver controlled shock into a flank or reserve already fixed by Barcelona infantry and missiles;
- continue briefly with sidearms while leaders and standards preserve file control;
- break away under command rather than dissolve into individual pursuit;
- reform enough surviving riders and horses to remain a real reserve.

The unit is strong because it concentrates better riders, deeper household leadership, more suitable horses, stronger equipment, and more practiced battle-wing behavior than ordinary mounted service. It is not balanced by making it timid. It is balanced by scarcity, political release, horse and retinue cost, prepared-spears counterplay, missile exposure, difficult terrain, capture risk, and very slow recovery.

## 3A. Relative combat ceiling

Against ordinary medium cavalry, a ready Montcada formation should be favored in an open mounted contest. Against peer noble or heavy cavalry, result depends on timing, angle, local support, horse condition, file depth, command, and fatigue. Against infantry already engaged or disrupted, the formation can deliver a battle-changing flank action and press the local opening. Against prepared spears or pikes from the front, the card should lose the advantage for which the player paid.

The ceiling is a top Montcada-anchored Negotiated Union High Era cavalry card. It is not a universal super-unit, not a late gendarme, and not the highest single-impact charge in the political union portfolio. Its strength is the complete mounted action: **countercharge, shock, press, break off, and reform**.

## 5. Player-Facing Description and Combat Fantasy

The Montcada standard rises behind the Barcelona line while the house's mounted files wait out of premature contact. Scouts and lesser cavalry identify the enemy wing. Infantry and missiles establish the battle. When the opposing horse commits or an infantry flank opens, the Montcada Knights align, countercharge or strike, and fight long enough to decide the local contest. The formation does not vanish into a one-impact script. Front files break lances and reach for sidearms; supporting retainers close gaps; file leaders keep the wing moving; the standard provides a physical rally reference. The player then chooses whether to press, break away, or protect the reform.

**Combat fantasy:** Montcada's cavalry does not merely arrive with prestige. It arrives with enough riders, horses, armor, leadership, and formation depth to defeat a serious enemy wing and return as a reduced but still dangerous reserve.

## 5A. Representative battlefield vignette

Barcelona's center has fixed the opposing infantry, but enemy noble cavalry waits beyond the right. The player keeps Cavallers de Montcada screened behind the right-center rather than spending them against the infantry. Barcelona Riders provoke the hostile wing and withdraw. Once the enemy cavalry commits, the Montcada files countercharge from a protected angle. Leading lances check the enemy momentum, the main noble body adds weight, and household retainers prevent the contact from dissolving into scattered duels.

The enemy begins to give ground. The player can press briefly with sidearms and attempt to break the wing, or break off before a fresh spear reserve arrives. If the player withdraws in time, standards and subleaders recover enough files for a second mission. If the player pursues every fleeing rider, the unit may win the first clash and still be destroyed strategically.

### 12A. Retinue depth as a battlefield mechanism

Retinue support is not a hidden stat bonus. It gives the formation identifiable subleaders who can keep a file together when the leading noble is engaged, attendants who maintain horses and equipment between commitments, and messengers or standards that carry recall and reform signals. A complete retinue can help a noble group maintain its shape through the transition from impact to short melee and back to reserve. Losing that support narrows the safe order set: a damaged formation might still protect a flank but no longer execute a coordinated second charge.

Combatant retainers, if any, count as actual riders with horses and gear; foot attendants do not silently appear as mounted combatants. A lord's household may supply one role but not every role. The card can have mixed provenance without claiming identical noble entourages.

### 13A. Horse quality is achieved, not a breed label

A horse capable of carrying an armored rider into controlled contact must be assessed for condition, load, temperament, training, and tack fit. Better horse access can increase the unit's confidence in alignment and follow-through, but a high-quality animal in poor condition should not perform like a fresh one. Pair familiarity matters particularly during the **break-off and reform** that distinguish this card from a one-impact levy. A replacement horse restores physical mobility first; charge timing and rider-horse coordination return only through integration.

Separate horse losses from rider losses. Captured or surviving horses retain an owner and may be injured, unsuitable, or already reserved. A defeated noble formation cannot simply buy a full remount pool from the treasury.

Horse armor is an exceptional, evidence-gated later expression, not a defining baseline.

A broken lance should change what a rider can do next. The formation may continue a brief melee with sidearms, protect command, or withdraw, but it does not regenerate a lance for another full-strength shock. Damaged tack and lost standards matter because they compromise alignment, recall, and reform.

### 16A. The noble command problem

A named-house battle wing with admitted attached contributors has a distinctive vulnerability: capable riders may disagree over precedence or the duration of service even while sharing a battlefield. Before commitment, the army command assigns the objective and the recognized formation leader establishes the charge signal, stop line, reserve position, and succession order. A subleader may preserve a surviving file after the first impact, but cannot conjure absent riders or negate a household recall.

In battle, command failure should produce a **specific loss of coordinated behavior**, not a generic morale penalty: files charge at different moments, press beyond the agreed line, or return to separate household standards. This is the meaningful downside of concentrating noble sources.

### 17A. Formation choices and the moment of contact

**Reserve line:** compact enough to respond, far enough back to avoid unnecessary missile exposure. **Countercharge files:** align to meet an already committed mounted threat without colliding with friendly infantry. **Controlled shock line:** choose an open approach against a flank, rear, or disrupted frontage. **Press formation:** keep a local advantage for a short melee, with subleaders preventing the wing from dissolving into individual pursuit. **Reform on standards:** recover facing, riders, and spacing behind friendly cover.

A wedge, if later supported by evidence and shared formation rules, is not the card's default special ability. A charge cannot be ordered at full quality through a narrow gate, steep broken ground, or a friendly formation occupying the lane.

## 18. Signature Battle Cycle

1. Assess mission, ground, enemy cavalry, spears, missiles, reserves, and friendly line.
2. Hold as a valuable mounted reserve or protected relief force.
3. Move toward the threatened sector without premature exposure.
4. Select countercharge, controlled shock, command protection, or line relief.
5. Form, align, and commit under leaders and standards.
6. Deliver charge or countercharge without guaranteed penetration or rout.
7. Press only while target, support, horse condition, cohesion, and exit remain favorable.
8. Break off, reform, rally, or recommit.
9. Pursue only when the enemy is genuinely broken and reserve risk passes.
10. Recover riders, horses, retinue, equipment, captives, ransom obligations, and political continuity.

### 18A. Two genuinely different winning cycles

**Defensive countercharge:** hold behind infantry, observe enemy horse commitment, strike the committed flank or approach, stop the enemy's mounted advantage, then recover before a fresh reserve can trap the Nobles. This is not unique by itself: Infanzones and the Royal Mesnada also countercharge. The Nobles' developed distinction is the potential to maintain organized files and **continue into a controlled local follow-through** if the situation supports it.

**Offensive battle-wing action:** allow infantry and missiles to fix or disorder a target while lighter horse screens the approach; bring the noble wing through a viable lane; strike, assess whether the target is actually yielding, and either press a bounded melee or break off. A failed initial impact does not automatically trigger a second charge. Both cycles end with an accounting of horse condition, lance loss, leadership, and political service capacity.

### 20A. Orders should feel different in play

**Hold the wing** protects the expensive option but can allow an enemy to win elsewhere. **Countercharge committed horse** trades reaction speed for certainty that the enemy has already chosen a line. **Shock the exposed flank** can decide the battle, but only if infantry and lighter cavalry have created a genuine opening. **Press locally** is the distinctive High Era option: the formation stays in a short, leader-controlled mounted melee to exploit a breach, at increasing fatigue and flank risk. **Reform on the standard** sacrifices immediate pursuit to recover order and preserve the option of another mission. **Protect withdrawal** turns the card from offensive wing into a rearward shield, reducing its freedom to exploit.

At a glance, the Nobles should read heavier and more ordered than Riders or Jinetes, but less uniform and less late-period than Gendarmes. Their banner and leader positions should make the press-and-reform behavior intelligible. Variation in shields, helmets, mail and textile protection, and horse tack is purposeful household variation, not a random cultural collage. Under the Negotiated Union, the card retains Montcada visual identity; a joint command marker may be shown separately without repainting the riders as generic Union cavalry or suppressing their Barcelona-rooted source.

## 42. Tactical Calibration Requirements

Test against Infanzones, Royal Mesnada, Aragonese Riders, Aragonese Jinetes, Barcelona Comital Horsemen, ordinary medium and heavy cavalry, mounted serjeants, spears, pikes, bows, crossbows, armored infantry, and future Gendarmes.

The decisive test is whether Cavallers de Montcada remain a developed Negotiated Union Montcada-anchored reserve rather than replacing Infanzones, Royal Mesnada, or Barcelona formations.

### 42A. Explicit pass/fail scenarios

1. **Early countercharge parity:** Infanzones can answer a limited threat; Nobles should justify their greater political and material cost by sustaining an organized local follow-through, not by guaranteed one-hit rout.
2. **Royal reserve parity:** Royal Mesnada protects the ruler and can intervene exceptionally; Nobles should not inherit its protection floor or become its cheaper duplicate.
3. **Heavy-cavalry peer:** a fresh, supported peer can defeat or check the Nobles. Nobility alone does not determine outcome.
4. **Prepared spears:** frontal charge fails to provide reliable value and may incur severe horse and leader loss.
5. **Two-stage engagement:** after first contact, a well-led, well-supported noble wing can choose a short press or a disciplined reform; a depleted one cannot.
6. **Union access:** a Barcelona army can receive an eligible Montcada contingent only when exact source, service, command, and physical capacity pass; donor capacity falls accordingly.
7. **Domination and reversal:** political control can change access and reliability without creating or deleting physical people, horses, or retinues.

If scenarios 1 and 5 do not produce a distinctive decision, the separate card fails irrespective of art quality.
