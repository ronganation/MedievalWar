## 1. Final Static Unit Verdict

**Cavallers de la Senyera** are the Negotiated Union's scarce composite noble shock reserve. Selected Aragonese and Barcelona mounted contributors align beneath one recognized Senyera, accept one release authority for a bounded field action, and concentrate their best available riders, contact horses, lances, leaders, and supporting files into a charge body designed to break one prepared opportunity.

The unit's distinction is not that joint knights are inherently stronger than native nobles. Native noble cavalry can possess equal or superior individual riders, horses, equipment, local cohesion, or household loyalty. The composite advantage is **joint concentration at the decisive point**: two contributor bodies agree on one target, one approach, one release, one rally reference, and one politically shared outcome.

The player preserves the formation while the rest of the army creates the opening, commits only after the target and lane pass, and then chooses whether to widen the rupture briefly or recover the formation before impact success becomes attritional failure.

## 3. One-Screen Total War Card

- **Unit:** Cavallers de la Senyera
- **Class:** Scarce joint noble shock cavalry
- **Battle promise:** Convert one combined-arms opening into a concentrated mounted rupture, then recover around the Senyera
- **Best use:** exposed flanks, turning infantry, dense troops disrupted by bolts, enemy cavalry already committed, recoiling heavy infantry, and high-value reserves without protected spear depth
- **Worst use:** prepared spear fronts, fresh deep heavy infantry, unsupported frontal charges, crowded lanes, repeated charges without reform, prolonged melee, and deep pursuit
- **Strategic value:** gives the Negotiated Union one visible decisive mounted reserve while preserving native cavalry as separate and competitive choices
- **Strategic cost:** consumes real noble riders, leaders, suitable horses, lances, armor, remounts, court trust, and recovery capacity from both realms
- **Counterplay:** deny or clutter charge lanes, preserve fresh spear depth, bait early release, screen with expendable troops, strike the rally route, shoot the waiting formation, or pin it after impact

- **Exact values:** unresolved

### Native noble cavalry remains preferable when

- one realm must retain independent command;
- local household or baronial loyalty matters more than joint concentration;
- the mission requires repeated smaller actions rather than one major charge;
- contributor trust is weak;
- suitable horses, leaders, or equipment cannot be grouped compatibly;
- loss of native mounted defense would cost more than the composite opportunity provides.

### Composite cavalry becomes preferable when

- a major mixed army already has infantry, missiles, screening horse, and a protected reserve lane;
- both contributors accept the target and command relationship;
- the army needs one concentrated blow rather than several independent cavalry actions;
- the political value of visible joint victory justifies the shared risk.

## 6. The Charge-Lane Game

The V1 concept correctly required a clear approach. V1.1 makes lane creation, preservation, denial, and abandonment the unit's defining battlefield problem.

```yaml
lane_states:
  Unselected:
    effect: NoChargePreparationBeyondGeneralReserveReadiness
  Observed:
    effect: PossibleTargetAndApproachIdentifiedButNotCommitted
  Screened:
    effect: FriendlyHorseInfantryOrTerrainProtectsAssemblyAndApproach
  Clear:
    effect: FormationCanAlignAndAccelerateWithoutFriendlyCollision
  Contested:
    effect: EnemyScreenMissilesTerrainOrFriendlyMovementThreatensChargeQuality
  Blocked:
    effect: ReleaseUnavailableOrLikelyCatastrophic

    effect: BreakawayAndRallyRiskRiseEvenIfImpactSucceeds
lane_quality_depends_on:
  - Width
  - FirmnessAndSlope
  - Visibility
  - FriendlyCongestion
  - EnemyScreensAndMissiles
  - FlankSecurity
  - TargetMovement
  - ExitAndRallyRoute
```

### Friendly lane creation

- Crown Mounted Sergeants intercept mobile screens and protect the outer approach.
- Native light riders identify hidden threats and pull skirmishers away.
- Catalano-Aragonese Spearmen fix the target or protect the reform area.
- Crown Crossbow Sergeants suppress armor, dense support, enemy missiles, or cavalry occupying the lane.
- The Royal-Comital Household Guard protects the command and standards so the shock reserve can remain focused on the mounted decision.

### Enemy lane denial

The enemy can:

- place expendable screens in the approach;
- maintain fresh spears behind an apparently vulnerable first line;
- move light cavalry across the lane;
- concentrate missiles on the assembly area;
- retreat before release and reopen distance;
- crowd the target with friendly bodies that absorb or redirect impact;
- threaten the Senyera or rally route;
- force the joint cavalry to choose between an imperfect charge and losing the opportunity.

## 7. Alignment, Commitment, and Abort Window

A strong charge begins before acceleration. The formation must settle contributor order, pace, target, standard position, release signal, and rally direction.

```yaml
commitment_phases:
  Reserve:
    player_decision: PreserveOrRelocate
  LaneObserved:
    player_decision: BeginAlignmentOrWait
  Aligning:
    player_decision: ContinueCancelOrChangeTargetAtCohesionCost
  CommittedApproach:
    player_decision: AbortStillPossibleButDisorderAndFatigueIncrease
  FinalRelease:
    player_decision: ChargeCannotCleanlyCancelBeforeContact
  Impact:
    player_decision: BreakThroughPressBrieflyOrPrepareBreakaway
  PostImpact:
    player_decision: WidenRuptureProtectStandardOrWithdraw
  Rally:
    player_decision: ReformHoldAsReserveOrAttemptReducedSecondAction
```

### Valid abort

An abort is the correct order when:

- the target reforms or turns prepared spears toward the lane;
- friendly troops obstruct the approach;
- a hidden reserve appears;
- the ground proves worse than observed;
- contributor alignment breaks;
- the standard or release authority is lost;
- an enemy countercharge threatens the formation before useful impact.

Abort preserves lives and horses but costs fatigue, alignment, opportunity, and possibly prestige. It should never be free, yet it must remain better than knowingly completing a catastrophic charge.

## 22. Representative Refined Scenarios

### Coordinated rupture

The Crown spear center fixes ordinary heavy infantry while controlled crossbow fire disrupts the supporting reserve. Crown Mounted Sergeants drive enemy light horse from the outer lane. Cavallers de la Senyera align, release into the exposed reserve flank, achieve a local break, and immediately rally while infantry advances into the opened space.

### Correct abort

The target appears disordered, but a fresh spear reserve steps forward during alignment. The player aborts before final release. The formation loses some fatigue and opportunity but preserves the elite reserve for another sector. This is successful command, not failed aggression.

### Countercharge decision

Enemy noble cavalry commits against the Union flank. The Senyera formation can meet it head-on, angle into the engaged body, or wait behind Crown Mounted Sergeants. Equal social status does not determine the result; freshness, alignment, horse condition, mass, angle, ground, leadership, and supporting depth do.

### Impact succeeds but press is wrong

A charge breaks the first enemy line, but untouched spears move from the second line. The player orders immediate breakaway instead of chasing. Crown infantry occupies the rupture while the cavalry rallies. Preserving the card is the stronger outcome.

### Standard crisis

The Senyera falls during breakaway. Nearby files can attempt recovery, rally on a recognized fallback, or withdraw under native leaders. Continuing pursuit while the composite command reference is endangered risks converting tactical success into political fracture.

## 9. Signature Battle Cycle

1. **Wait behind the line.** Preserve horses and formation while the Union army develops the battle.
2. **Observe the opening.** Identify the target, supporting enemies, approach lane, exit route, and probable countercharge.
3. **Clear the lane.** Crown Mounted Sergeants, native light horse, infantry, and missiles remove screens or fix threats.
4. **Align beneath the Senyera.** Native files acknowledge the joint release, settle pace, dress formation, and confirm the rally point.
5. **Commit.** Advance only when the target is fixed, exposed, turning, disordered, recoiling, or committed elsewhere.
6. **Deliver shock.** Lances, horses, formation mass, angle, ground, and target condition determine the result.
7. **Break through or bounce.** Penetration is never guaranteed. Stable deep formations may absorb the charge.
8. **Fight briefly.** Use sidearms only long enough to widen the rupture, defeat an immediate mounted opponent, or protect withdrawal.
9. **Withdraw from the grind.** Leave before spears, reserves, missiles, or superior numbers trap the formation.
10. **Rally on the Senyera.** Recover surviving files, leaders, horses, and standards.
11. **Choose the remainder.** Hold as a reduced reserve, protect withdrawal, or make a lesser second action only if genuine readiness remains.

## 13. Combined-Arms Transactions

### Catalano-Aragonese Spearmen

The spear line fixes enemy infantry, protects the cavalry's reform area, and denies hostile horse a clean countercharge. Cavallers de la Senyera punish a flank or reserve exposed by the prolonged central fight.

### Crown Crossbow Sergeants

Controlled salvos disrupt armor, horses, dense reserves, or supporting spears before the charge. The cavalry must not cross occupied firing lanes or assume missiles have erased prepared spear resistance.

### Royal-Comital Household Guard

The Household Guard remains the foot crisis reserve near the joint command. Cavallers de la Senyera are the mounted decisive reserve. One should not be committed carelessly merely because the other remains fresh.

### Crown Mounted Sergeants

Mounted Sergeants clear screens, protect the approach, relieve vulnerable troops, and cover the post-charge rally. They do not merge into the shock formation or provide free horses and riders.

### Native Light Riders and Jinetes

Light horse scouts, screens, reports, harasses, and draws enemy response. The Senyera cavalry should not replace those wider operational functions.

### Native Noble Cavalry

Native noble formations may charge independently, support another sector, preserve realm-specific command, or remain unavailable because contributors entered the composite body. The joint card does not double-count them.

## 25. Representative Battle Scenarios

### Crown center creates the opening

Catalano-Aragonese Spearmen receive the opposing center while Crown Crossbow Sergeants punish the dense supporting line. When an enemy reserve turns to reinforce the center, Cavallers de la Senyera strike the exposed side. Success is a local rupture followed by rally, not a charge through the entire army.

### Cavalry counterstroke

Enemy heavy cavalry commits against Crown Mounted Sergeants or native horse. The Senyera formation waits until the hostile cavalry loses clean alignment, then countercharges at an angle. If the enemy retains fresh depth, the joint noble formation must withdraw rather than enter a prolonged duel.

### Failed frontal temptation

An enemy spear line appears shaken but retains formation and reserve depth. A premature Release the Lances order produces a costly checked charge. The correct play was to preserve the reserve until missiles, infantry contact, or a turning maneuver created a real opening.

### Siege relief battle

The unit operates outside the siege works on open ground, targeting the relief army's exposed reserve or cavalry. It does not lead ladder, breach, or gate attacks.

### Union command fracture

One contributor disputes the target after alignment. The charge launches unevenly, reducing mass and widening gaps. The player may cancel, accept a weaker release, or preserve the formation. Political stability does not automatically override battlefield disagreement.
