## 1. Final Static Unit Verdict

**Crown Mounted Sergeants** are a developed Negotiated Union formation of trained, non-noble or lower-status mounted service personnel, selected retainers, mounted sergeants, file leaders, and compatible horses contributed by Aragonese and Barcelona service institutions. They provide the mixed Union army with dependable medium cavalry able to protect a flank, reinforce a threatened sector, relieve engaged friendly troops, cover a controlled withdrawal, and exploit a limited opening without behaving like noble shock cavalry.

The formation's signature strength is **field linkage**: mounted handoff, sector reinforcement, and mission transfer between Union contingents that differ in source, pace, command language, and battlefield role. Crown Mounted Sergeants can arrive where one Union contingent is losing contact, assume the screen or pressure mission for a short period, and allow that formation to disengage, reform, resupply, or return to its proper role. They connect the army rather than attempt to win through one decisive charge. Cavallers de la Senyera own the highest-gate first-class shock commitment. Cavallers de Montcada own the heavy mounted contest, and Cavallers d’Urgell own outer-lane initiative. Crown Mounted Sergeants own preservation, relief, withdrawal cover, mission transfer, and the disciplined second action.

## 3. Player Fantasy and Flavor

**Player fantasy:** Keep the Crown Mounted Sergeants behind a flank or between the center and missile line. When enemy light cavalry appears, a crossbow position is threatened, or friendly infantry needs room to disengage, send the sergeants into the gap. They arrive in reasonable order, absorb the immediate mounted or light-infantry pressure, escort friendly troops clear, then either reform or make a short exploitation against an already disordered enemy.

**Short description:** Dependable Aragonese and Catalan mounted service files trained to secure flanks, reinforce threatened sectors, relieve friendly troops, cover withdrawal, and exploit limited openings.

**Flavor line:** **Where the Union line thins, the Crown rides.**

## 8. Signature Battle Rhythm

1. Screen the chosen flank, route, reserve lane, or missile position.
2. Observe enemy cavalry, exposed light troops, friendly fatigue, and likely withdrawal routes.
3. Intercept a manageable mobile threat or reinforce a developing fight.
4. Establish enough local pressure to prevent immediate pursuit or overrun.
5. Pass the protected friendly unit through the relief lane.
6. Maintain contact only until the handoff succeeds.
7. Exploit a limited opening if the enemy is already disordered and the route remains safe.
8. Halt before pursuit separates the formation from Union support.
9. Reform horses, files, leaders, and contributor bodies.
10. Remain available for flank security, escort, or a reduced second intervention.

## 30Z. Representative Tactical Scenarios

### Crossbows threatened by light cavalry

The Sergeants move from flank guard, check the cavalry, and open a route for Crown Crossbow Sergeants to withdraw or reform. The Sergeants do not chase far enough to expose the missile line again.

### Spear center bending

The Sergeants threaten the pursuing flank while Catalano-Aragonese Spearmen shorten or reform their frontage. The mounted formation does not replace the spear center.

### Noble cavalry preparing decisive shock

The Sergeants intercept light support or pressure an exposed flank. Cavallers de la Senyera or valid native noble cavalry retain the decisive charge mission.

### Controlled withdrawal

The Sergeants alternate between visible threat, short contact, and breakaway while the army retires by formations. Success is preserved order, not maximum kills.

### Failed relief

A friendly formation collapses before the handoff, clogging the route. The correct order is recall or withdrawal, not feeding the mounted formation into an unrecoverable melee.
