## 3. Player-Facing Encyclopedia

Calatrava Knight Brethren are professed mounted members of a later Iberian military-religious institution whose earliest campaign identity grows from fortress custody, disciplined service, and a finite community of brethren, clergy, horses, equipment, and support. The unit is not recruited from a church or castle tier. A valid contingent requires an existing Calatrava institution, eligible brethren, mounted competence, horses, equipment, leadership, an available house or fortress, and a mission that does not strip the order below its protected local obligations.

On the battlefield, the brethren are built around a disciplined mounted commitment. The player forms the contingent, selects a worthwhile target and route, charges or counterattacks, and then must recover the formation. Success is not measured only by the first collision. A victorious but scattered company may lose riders, horses, standards, equipment, prisoners, and the ability to reform for another battle. The order pays those losses at its houses and fortresses.

The unit changes visually across the campaign. The foundation-era expression uses regional twelfth-century Iberian knightly equipment and does not assume the familiar later red floriated Calatrava cross. Later expressions may become more coherent and more visibly institutional only when chronology, property, craft, stores, and sources permit.
