## 4. Source Findings

The probable <Person>Ian Heath</Person> extract identifies an early Santiago brother knight from a manuscript associated with the reign of <Person>Alfonso VIII of Castile</Person> and distinguishes the early espada device from a later form. The same extract describes a 13th-century Santiagan knight in war harness from the Cantigas de Santa María. That later figure is reported with a faded red surcoat, white helmet and shield, and a red cross with fleur-de-lis ends and five white cockleshells. Some mounted figures are reported with similarly marked housings and red leather chanfrons, while others have mail bards. These are expression-specific visual leads rather than one uniform issue.

The institutional package records the supported direction that Santiago knights could marry under conditions and that Santiago included clerics, a master, general chapter, branches, houses, and commanderies. These facts affect the unit source and recovery system: a knight-brother may carry household, property, spouse, dependent, inheritance, branch, and patron obligations in addition to personal military readiness.
