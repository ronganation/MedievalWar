## 4. Player-Facing Encyclopedia

Santiago Knight Brethren are professed mounted members of a later Iberian military-religious order that develops from a knightly confraternity into a network of branches, houses, castles, commanderies, clerics, households, estates, and political relationships. A battlefield contingent is not produced by a church, pilgrimage route, castle tier, or calendar date. A valid unit requires actual admitted brethren, leaders, standards, suitable horses, equipment, support, a branch capable of releasing them, and a mission that does not abandon the order’s protected local, household, and institutional duties.

On the battlefield the brethren are elite heavy cavalry capable of a main or supporting charge, countercharge, controlled mounted melee, protected escort, and reinforcement of a threatened branch, allied force, or order element. Santiago’s signature is not a supernatural pilgrimage bonus. The unit’s distinctive strength is the ability to gather a valid cross-realm branch contingent, impose heavy cavalry deterrence around a protected movement or mission, respond decisively when that mission is threatened, then rally and return people, horses, standards, captives, claims, and household consequences to the owning institution.

The unit changes across the campaign. The first confraternity expression uses regional Iberian knightly equipment and practical brotherhood cues. An early Santiago device must remain distinct from later espada forms. A mature thirteenth-century expression may use the reported red-and-white war-harness direction, cockleshell-bearing device, marked horse housings, leather chanfrons, or some mail barding only when the exact date, company, equipment, and institutional capacity support them.
