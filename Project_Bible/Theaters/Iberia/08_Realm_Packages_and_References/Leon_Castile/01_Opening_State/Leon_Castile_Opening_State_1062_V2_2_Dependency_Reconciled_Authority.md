## 6A. Shared Dynastic Stakeholder Campaign Engine

The first review correctly protected the opening from invented kingdoms, armies, offices, and grants, but it did not expose enough of the playable machinery that makes the three brothers distinct. The opening must therefore initialize one shared stakeholder engine with three different routes through it. The engine begins with eligibility, recognition surfaces, service opportunities, and blocked authority. It does not begin with autonomous rule.

```yaml
SharedStakeholderEngine:
  SovereignGatekeeper: person.ferdinand.jimenez.muniadona_son
  PlayableStakeholders:
    - person.sancho.ferdinand_sancha_son

  AutomaticConversionToTitleOrOffice: false
```

These are routed state categories, not free numeric bonuses created by this file. Their definitions, mutation rules, visibility, thresholds, decay, and consequences remain with Character, Network, Dynasty, Governance, Military, Economy, Information, AI, Momentum, Emergence, Title, and Save owners. Opening State may bind the starting absence, known eligibility, and available route definitions.

### 6A.1 Royal service and reward cycle

Each brother's early campaign should repeatedly present a bounded cycle:

1. Discover or receive a valid service opportunity.
2. Evaluate the sovereign's request, regional need, institutional sponsor, risks, and required resources.
3. Petition for a mandate, office, mission, command, access, funds, or logistical support through the owning system.
4. Accept, negotiate, refuse, delay, or redirect the opportunity.
5. Perform under seasonal, geographic, military, political, and informational constraints.
6. Generate owner-observed performance, costs, grievances, obligations, reputation, and historical pressure.
7. Seek confirmation, reward, renewal, expanded authority, restitution, or a different route.
8. Convert sustained recognition into a power base only when the relevant owner validates the transition.

A successful task does not automatically grant a kingdom. A failed task does not end the campaign. Failure may narrow access, increase supervision, damage recognition, create debt, expose rivals, or open recovery service.

### 6A.2 Sibling competition without scripted rivalry

The brothers compete for scarce royal attention, credible service, appointments, resources, regional sponsors, institutional recognition, command opportunities, and succession relevance. Competition is an emergent allocation problem, not an opening hostility edge.


Owner-observed comparison may consider service delivered, reliability, costs imposed, regional breadth, institutional support, military credibility, administrative performance, household mediation, refusal history, recovery, and the sovereign's changing needs. Private attitudes, promises, and preferences remain unknown until validly observed or communicated.

### 6A.3 Multi-route viability

No brother is locked to one profession. Their primary identities establish asymmetrical strengths and opening opportunities, while secondary routes preserve strategic flexibility.

- Sancho may pursue military service first, but can also build court access, negotiated reward, regional administration, household alliances, and succession recognition.
- Alfonso may pursue Leonese political and administrative service first, but can also seek diplomacy, military credibility, institutional sponsorship, household influence, and territorial responsibility.
- Garcia may pursue Galician coordination, routes, institutions, and the western frontier first, but can also seek court presence, diplomacy, command, administrative consolidation, and continuity outside immediate inheritance.

Cross-route development requires demonstrated access and performance. It cannot erase each brother's regional and political circumstances.
