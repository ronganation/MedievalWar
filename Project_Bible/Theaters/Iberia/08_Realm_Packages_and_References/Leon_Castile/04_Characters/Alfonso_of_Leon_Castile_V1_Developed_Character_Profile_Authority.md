## 1. Developed Profile Verdict

Alfonso begins in January 1062 as a living adult royal son and one of the three selectable Dynastic Stakeholder perspectives under Ferdinand I. His strongest opening route is Leonese political and administrative recognition, reinforced by institutional access, diplomacy, household position, and conditional military service. He does not begin as King of Leon, administrator of Leon, Ferdinand's chosen heir, independent diplomat, holder of a Toledo mission, army commander, or universal institutional favorite.

**Convert recognition, access, and service into durable map-facing authority without becoming dependent on court favor or a scripted Leonese crown.**

Alfonso's strength is the ability to join political access, administration, institutions, diplomacy, information, and negotiated service. His weakness is that recognition without an executable mandate, office, revenue, force, route, or territorial base leaves him dependent on Ferdinand and vulnerable to displacement. His campaign must reward coalition-building and administrative competence while preserving military credibility and alternate futures.

## 7. Core Character Thesis and Main-Player Fantasy

Alfonso's defining tension is **recognized political relevance versus executable independence**.

He can become persuasive without being obeyed, institutionally welcomed without controlling an institution, administratively useful without holding office, diplomatically capable without treaty authority, and succession-relevant without owning a crown. His campaign is strongest when the player must convert these advantages into real mandates, staff, revenues, places, routes, forces, agreements, and title-specific recognition.

**Main-player fantasy:** Build a Leonese political center that can govern, negotiate, and survive beyond court access, while retaining enough military and regional credibility to remain viable in a contested future.

## 12. Court Access Without Court Dependence

Court access is a route, not a resource owned forever. Alfonso's proximity to Ferdinand, Sancha, officers, petitioners, clergy, messengers, and ceremonies may improve information and proposal access when location and household state support it. It may also create dependence, political exposure, and weak map-facing capacity.

The campaign should force choices between remaining near the royal center and accepting distant responsibility. Travel can reduce immediate access while building administrative, military, diplomatic, or regional credibility. Return to court may require reports, witnesses, records, results, and negotiation rather than an automatic audience.

## 14. Administration, Justice, Records, and Enforcement

Alfonso may seek a bounded mandate involving petitions, records, revenue supervision, dispute referral, justice support, institutional coordination, route administration, or royal representation. Every function requires jurisdiction, law or custom, officers, records, treasury or revenue authority, enforcement, reporting, duration, review, revocation, and reversion.

Administrative success depends on whether decisions can be communicated and enforced. A well-written order without officers, money, witnesses, routes, or regional acceptance is not completed government. Alfonso's profile therefore distinguishes proposal, judgment, record, execution, compliance, appeal, and memory.

## 28. Political Blind Spots and Failure Risks

Conditional failure risks include:

- confusing court access with durable power;
- collecting recognition without gaining staff, revenue, jurisdiction, force, or enforcement;
- accepting a mission whose negotiating authority is unclear;
- promising outcomes controlled by Ferdinand or external owners;
- relying on institutional access while neglecting magnates, settlements, officers, or military followers;
- remaining so close to court that no independent map-facing base develops;
- accepting office without records, routes, or treasury support;
- interpreting Leonese reception as universal claim support;
- allowing Sancho to monopolize military credibility or dismissing Garcia's regional route;
- treating Urraca and Elvira as predetermined allies;
- projecting later Toledo or kingship outcomes backward;
- failing to preserve a viable continuity route after noninheritance or exile.
