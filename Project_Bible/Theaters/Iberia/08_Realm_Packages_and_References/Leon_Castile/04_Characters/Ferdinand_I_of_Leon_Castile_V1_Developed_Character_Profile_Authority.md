## 1. Developed Profile Verdict

Ferdinand I is the living sovereign around whom the January 1062 Leon-Castile composite monarchy is coordinated. Ferdinand's recognized Leonese kingship, Castilian title, military and diplomatic experience, royal household position, institutional standing, and taifa-facing leverage make Ferdinand the Realm anchor. None of those facts turns Ferdinand into the Realm itself or gives Ferdinand universal practical possession of every settlement, military contribution, office, institution, treasury, agreement, route, or political relationship.

**Hold together a composite monarchy whose unity is real, personal, and unfinished.**

Ferdinand's campaign function is founder dependency. The opening state is powerful because Ferdinand is present, but durable success requires institutions, regional channels, household continuity, bounded delegation, military logistics, valid agreements, and succession preparation that can survive absence, incapacity, capture, defeat, or death. The player does not control Ferdinand directly in the three stakeholder openings. Ferdinand remains the AI sovereign while Sancho, Alfonso, or Garcia attempts to serve, persuade, bargain with, resist, outgrow, or survive the royal center.

## 6. Core Character Thesis

Ferdinand's strength is the personal coordination of authorities that are legally, regionally, institutionally, and operationally distinct. Ferdinand's weakness is that the same concentration makes the Realm dependent on one living person.

The strategic problem is therefore twofold:

1. Use personal sovereignty to produce real action across Leon, Castile, Galicia, the western frontier, ecclesiastical institutions, and taifa-facing diplomacy.
2. Build enough durable structure that delegation, succession, and crisis do not dissolve the political order or mechanically reproduce later history.

A successful Ferdinand may create resilient channels and multiple viable continuity options. A failed Ferdinand may leave a strong title claim but weak execution, favored children without accepted authority, regional command without reversion, agreements without continuity, or a household unable to mediate transition.

## 22. Failure, Recovery, Captivity, Incapacity, and Removal

### 22.1 Failure is not removal

Defeat, failed diplomacy, mandate failure, regional resistance, succession disagreement, or treasury shortage creates evidence and consequences through owners. Ferdinand remains the same person unless Lifecycle state changes.

### 22.2 Recovery

Recovery may require demobilization, ransom, route restoration, treasury repair, reconciliation, mandate revision, household mediation, institutional recognition, regional concession, or succession renegotiation. No generic cooldown replaces these systems.

### 22.3 Captivity or incapacity

Captivity or incapacity may change availability, communication, presence, command, and governance requirements. It does not automatically transfer titles, dissolve agreements, create regency, or distribute the Realm.

### 22.4 Death

Ferdinand's death is Lifecycle-owned and not scheduled by Historical date. Death triggers separate reviews of:

- each title succession;
- Realm and provisional government;
- household leadership and residence;
- dynasty and branch headship;
- army commands and forces;
- offices and mandates;
- property and treasury access;
- agreements, tribute, and protection;
- institutions and patronage;
- directed relationships, promises, grievances, and knowledge;
- each stakeholder perspective and campaign objective.

No single person receives Ferdinand's whole portfolio automatically. No historical partition is applied without valid owner decisions and current world-state support.
