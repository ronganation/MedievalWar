## 1. Developed Profile Verdict

Sancho begins in January 1062 as a living adult royal son and playable Dynastic Stakeholder inside Ferdinand I's still-unified composite monarchy. He has a military-forward and Castilian-facing route, but he does not begin as king, independent ruler, army owner, commander, governor, claimant with an active claim, or holder of recruitment rights.

**Earn military authority, Castilian recognition, and a durable political future without mistaking battlefield usefulness for sovereignty.**

Sancho's campaign is the conversion of bounded royal service into credible, executable, and reviewable power. His success must come through actual mandates, forces, finite recruitment pools, routes, supply, officers, disciplined performance, regional reception, negotiated reward, and title-specific recognition. His central danger is that visible military strength can outpace legal, household, institutional, and dynastic acceptance, alarming Ferdinand or isolating Sancho from the wider monarchy.

## 7. Core Character Thesis

Sancho's central tension is **earned military authority versus incomplete political recognition**.

Sancho can become indispensable to the monarchy without becoming sovereign. He can win battles without securing Castilian government, earn Castilian recognition without gaining Leonese acceptance, receive command without owning the force, and gain reward without receiving the historical kingdom later associated with him. The strongest campaign keeps those achievements connected but never collapses them.

His identity remains valid if he becomes Ferdinand's leading commander, a bounded Castilian delegate, a force-preserving frontier organizer, a negotiated appanage holder, a noninheriting royal servant, a recognized claimant, a defeated or captive stakeholder, a reconciled brother, a subordinate ruler, or an alternate sovereign through valid campaign outcomes.

## 8. Character Fantasy and Strategic Questions

**Character fantasy:** Build a military and regional reputation strong enough to shape the future, while proving that strength can be disciplined, governable, and politically legitimate.

Sancho's recurring strategic questions are:

- What exact authority has Ferdinand granted, and what remains only expectation?
- Which force is actually available, from which finite pools, under whose officers, and for how long?
- Does the proposed operation protect the composite monarchy or merely increase Sancho's exposure?
- Will success generate evidence that Castilian, Leonese, household, and institutional audiences can recognize?
- Can Sancho accept review, limits, revocation, and handback without losing accumulated credibility?
- Is a promised reward deliverable, title-specific, documented, and protected against reversion?
- Does personal military concentration strengthen Ferdinand or make Sancho appear uncontrollable?
- Are siblings competitors, partners, mediators, or separate interests in this specific matter?
- If inheritance does not occur, which valid continuity route preserves meaningful play?

## 15. Recruitment, Replenishment, and Force Preservation

Sancho's military credibility should depend partly on how he uses scarce people, horses, equipment, money, and political obligations.

Potential force sources may include royal household contingents, title-bound levies, regional contingents, garrisons released for field use, allied contributions, mercenaries, and volunteers only when validated by their owners. None belongs to Sancho by identity.

Sancho should evaluate:

- whether a pool can recruit now or only after recovery;
- which region bears the burden;
- whether removing a garrison exposes a settlement or route;
- whether allied contingents accept Sancho's hierarchy;
- whether losses damage future defense and recognition;
- whether horses, equipment, pay, and replacement officers exist;
- whether demobilization and return obligations are defined;
- whether victory is worth exhausting the force base.

## 16. Tactical and Operational Identity

Sancho's military identity should emerge from decisions rather than a universal combat bonus. Potential authoring emphases include decisive action, concentration of force, frontier responsiveness, disciplined pursuit, territorial pressure, and insistence that service produce visible recognition. These are candidate tendencies, not fixed Traits.

The profile must support multiple styles:

- aggressive concentration when intelligence and supply justify it;
- defensive preservation when the force base is fragile;
- relief and reinforcement of threatened positions;
- limited operations tied to mandate objectives;
- withdrawal when continued action would destroy political capacity;
- coordination with regional or sibling-controlled functions;
- negotiated demobilization after success;
