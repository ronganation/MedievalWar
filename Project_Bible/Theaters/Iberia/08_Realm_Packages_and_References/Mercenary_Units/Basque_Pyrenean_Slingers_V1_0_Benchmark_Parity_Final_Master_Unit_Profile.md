- **Unit:** Basque-Pyrenean Slingers
- **Internal family:** Pyrenean Honderos
- **Class:** regional hand-sling skirmishers with conditional contract service
- **Quality:** practiced company-specific skirmishers, not automatically elite

- **Primary weapon:** hand sling
- **Ammunition:** selected stones as the baseline candidate; shaped dense shot or lead only where manufacture, ownership, supply, chronology, and company practice pass
- **Sidearm:** knife, light spear, club, or source-approved local sidearm
- **Protection:** light and source-variable
- **Primary role:** range, cast, reposition, and pressure exposed formations from usable broken ground
- **Best use:** slopes, ridges, pass approaches, broken flanks, screens, crossings, rocky edges, and protected elevated positions with real withdrawal routes
- **Major weakness:** cavalry, bows and crossbows, close infantry contact, blocked casting arcs, ammunition exhaustion, poor visibility, fatigue, and loss of screening support
- **Opening State:** rejected for January 1, 1062
- **Exact values:** none promoted

## 2. Player-Facing Description

Basque-Pyrenean Slingers are a proposed regional skirmisher family drawn from exact western Pyrenean providers rather than from every shepherd, mountain resident, or Basque-speaking community. Their sling is cheap to manufacture compared with a bow or crossbow, but battlefield competence is not cheap: useful range, timing, ammunition selection, spacing, observation, and safe displacement require practiced people, experienced leaders, prepared shot, carriers, routes, and supporting troops.

The company seeks ground that opens a release arc while complicating enemy approach. It ranges targets, casts controlled volleys or looser harassment, shifts before missiles or cavalry fix the formation, and uses ridges, folds, rocks, woodland edges, and pass approaches as geometry rather than magical concealment. The unit can participate in an army ambush when the army's information, planning, terrain, deployment, and secrecy succeed. It does not add a flat ambush percentage and does not remain invisible while firing.

Stone and dense shot serve different campaign and battlefield needs. Readily selected stone may be locally available but still requires suitable size, shape, collection, carriage, and issue. Dense shaped shot or lead may offer compact carriage and different impact behavior, but it requires actual material, manufacture, custody, compatibility, and resupply. Neither ammunition automatically bypasses mail, slows victims, or turns the unit into an anti-knight weapon.

## 3. One-Screen Design Card

### Battlefield

- Screen the army and identify usable casting ground.
- Choose stone or approved dense-shot load before the engagement or at an actual supply point.
- Range an exposed target and preserve a safe release arc.
- Cast, observe, and displace before missile or cavalry response fixes the company.
- Use broken ground to complicate approach, not to ignore terrain.
- Pressure skirmishers, light infantry, exposed missile troops, animals, and disordered formations.
- Participate in planned ambushes without receiving automatic concealment or success.
- Withdraw behind spears, terrain, or another screen before close contact.

### Campaign

- Draw only from exact validated regional providers or existing companies.
- Preserve domestic source, patron, leader, personnel, ammunition, route, and replacement.
- Choose between regional duty, allied service, owner-released service, and independent contract service where supported.
- Pay for food, water, quarters, shot preparation, sacks and slings, transport, medicine, route security, and return.
- Accept that local materials do not create trained slingers, a company, or an endless ammunition pool.
