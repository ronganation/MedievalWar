Iberian Mercenary Light Lancers are contract mounted companies hired for direct work that skirmish cavalry cannot finish and heavy knights may be too scarce, slow, expensive, or excessive to perform. They approach through usable ground, form for a bounded light-lance or melee commitment, strike an exposed or already engaged target, fight briefly with swords, spears, or compatible sidearms, wheel out before heavier opposition fixes them, reform around the captain, and recover riders, horses, tack, equipment, and company order.

The card is not Mercenary Jinetes after their javelins are gone, not discounted knights, and not a universal pool of Caballeros Villanos. Its identity comes from direct mounted melee, light-lance impact, rapid local response, finite company service, lighter horses and protection, quicker disengagement, and a lower shock and staying-power ceiling than heavy cavalry.

## 3. Player-Facing Description

Iberian Mercenary Light Lancers are hired mounted companies built for direct intervention rather than prolonged skirmishing. Their riders threaten an exposed flank, intercept enemy light cavalry, overrun a vulnerable missile formation, reinforce a local cavalry fight, rescue an isolated friendly element, cover withdrawal, or turn a genuine rout into controlled pursuit. The company is most effective when speed, route selection, timing, and a viable exit matter more than raw armor and horse mass.

A valid unit requires an actual company, accepted captain and subleaders, qualified riders, suitable horses, compatible rider-horse pairings, serviceable lances or spears, sidearms, shields and protection, saddles, bridles, tack, handlers, farriers, supply, medical and veterinary support, a contract, a mission, an approach lane, and a return or recovery route. A regional or social label never creates those inputs.

The unit must choose fights carefully. A successful light-lance commitment can unsettle exposed or disordered opponents, but it cannot reproduce the weight of a fully equipped knightly charge. A brief melee can defeat light cavalry or vulnerable support troops. A prolonged fight against heavy cavalry, deep infantry, or prepared reach weapons turns mobility into entrapment and exposes both riders and horses to losses the company may not be able to replace.
