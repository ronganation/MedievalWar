## 3. Evidence Register and Confidence

### 3.1 Ian Heath synthesis

The reviewed synthesis identifies Bidets, Bidsaux, or Bibaldi as Spanish javelin-armed light infantry, especially Aragonese, Navarrese, and Basque, employed in France from the mid-twelfth century until the fourteenth. It reports a thrusting spear, two darts, and a coutel at the waist, and describes them generally as shieldless and unarmored while allowing possible light protection.

```yaml

  SpanishRegionalIdentity: StrongWithinReviewedSynthesis
  JavelinArmedLightInfantry: StrongWithinReviewedSynthesis
  AragoneseAssociation: ExplicitInSynthesisUnderlyingSpecificCitationNotYetExposed
  NavarreseAssociation: ExplicitInSynthesisAndReinforcedByGuiartQuotation
  BasqueAssociation: ExplicitInSynthesisUnderlyingSpecificCitationNotYetExposed
  EmploymentInFrance: ExplicitInSynthesis
  ExactInstitutionRecruitmentPoolOrFormation: NotSpecified
```

### 3.2 Guiart-derived evidence

Retrieved quotations of Guillaume Guiart associate Bidaux with Navarre and the direction of Spain and describe two darts, a lance, and a coutel. Retrieved later commentary interprets their combat use as light harassment and dart throwing rather than standing firm against heavy attack.

```yaml
Guiart_derived_confidence:
  NavarreReference: StrongestCurrentOwnershipEvidence
  BroaderSpanishFrontierDirection: Supported
  TwoDartsLanceAndCoutel: Supported
  ExactAragoneseSource: NotEstablishedByRetrievedQuotation
  ExactBasqueSource: NotEstablishedByRetrievedQuotation
  ExactRecruitmentInstitution: NotSpecified
```

### 3.3 Cantiga visual corroboration

The reviewed Cantiga pages explicitly note people equipped with both spear and javelin. They support the plausibility of the weapon combination in Iberian visual material but do not by themselves identify a Bidet institution, Navarrese ownership, or a standardized card.

### 3.4 Later Bidower evidence

The reviewed Bidower synthesis reports light-armed Gascon infantry in a 1346 Brittany-service context, connects “bayouney” with Bayonne, and describes spear-and-buckler equipment. It separately repeats the Navarrese and Spanish Bidaux description of two darts, lance, and coutel.
