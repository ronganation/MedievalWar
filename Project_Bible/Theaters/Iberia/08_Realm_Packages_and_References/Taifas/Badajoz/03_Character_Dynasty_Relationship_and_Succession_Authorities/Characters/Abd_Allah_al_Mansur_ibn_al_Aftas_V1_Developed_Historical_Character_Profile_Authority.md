## 3. Developed Historical Character Verdict

Abd Allah al-Mansur ibn al-Aftas is the historical person through whom Aftasid rule and the inherited political order of Badajoz become genealogically and institutionally legible. His profile should explain what current actors inherited, what claims require validation, what memories may differ, and what structures remain dependent on a founder whose personal agency has ended.

**Historical-character fantasy:** Understand and contest the founder's legacy through living descendants, institutions, records, memories, claims, and political choices without turning the dead founder into an invisible buff or scripted command source.

**Central design question:** Can the campaign make the founder consequential through evidence, inherited structures, and contested memory while preserving complete separation between historical causality and living agency?

The profile remains valid if the Aftasid realm strengthens, fragments, reforms, loses territory, changes its succession practice, rejects parts of the founder's model, magnifies his memory, disputes his legacy, or ends under campaign-produced conditions.

## 6. Historical Basis and Evidence Boundary

### Supported or strongly bounded

- Founder of the Aftasid dynasty in Badajoz.
- Former ruler before al-Muzaffar.
- Deceased before the January 1062 opening.
- Father-reference candidate for al-Muzaffar.
- Historical dependency for Aftasid genealogy and political continuity.
- Suitable subject for dynasty history, memorial presentation, claims evidence, and audience-specific memory.

### Unknown or owner-gated

- Exact birth date and age.
- Exact death date if not locked by specialist historical authority.
- Exact burial site and material memorial state.
- Exact offices, household, property, treasury, commanders, ministers, scholars, jurists, and institutional relationships.
- Exact private motives, successor preference, relationship dimensions, and intended constitutional legacy.
- Exact surviving documents, seals, coins, inscriptions, grants, or legal acts available in the opening world state.

### Prohibited invention

- A speaking posthumous actor.
- A founder Trait inherited by descendants.
- Automatic legitimacy for al-Muzaffar, Yahya, or Umar.
- Automatic obedience from institutions or settlements.
- An invented testament, succession settlement, treasury, army, tomb, relic, or archive.
- A scripted restoration of founder-era borders or practices.

Living actors know only what they can access through records, teaching, memory holders, objects, institutions, correspondence, or public presentation. The dead founder supplies no omniscience.

## 19. Founder Legacy Decision Portfolio

Living Badajoz actors may encounter bounded strategic decisions concerning the founder:

1. Verify a genealogy or predecessor claim.
2. Preserve, copy, translate, or compare a record.
3. Decide whether to cite founder precedent in a current dispute.
4. Investigate whether an institution or regional arrangement claims founder-era authority.
5. Fund or decline a memorial practice after evidence and ownership review.
6. Reconcile conflicting accounts of the founder's rule.
7. Distinguish inherited procedure from obsolete custom.
8. Use founder memory to support continuity without manufacturing obedience.
9. Protect archives or material evidence during crisis.
10. Reject a forged or unsupported founder claim.
11. Allow multiple audience memories rather than imposing one official legacy.
12. Reform a founder-linked structure while preserving historical provenance.

Each decision belongs to living actors and current owners.

## 22. Divergent-History Contract

The founder's established past remains historical, but its future significance may diverge.

Campaigns may produce:

- stronger or weaker founder commemoration;
- recovered or lost records;
- accepted or rejected precedents;
- peaceful or contested succession narratives;
- reforms that reduce founder dependency;
- institutions that reinterpret his legacy;
- dynasty survival, displacement, fragmentation, or extinction;
- borders and governments unlike later history.

No repair script restores an expected legacy or dynasty outcome.

## 23. Failure, Loss, Recovery, and Falsification

Founder-related systems can fail through archive loss, forged documents, false attribution, broken genealogy, damaged memorials, political misuse, contradictory testimony, inaccessible evidence, or migration corruption.

Recovery may require provenance review, comparison, retranslation, conservation, owner adjudication, genealogy repair, direct-ID migration, public correction, or acceptance that certainty cannot be restored.

Discovery of error must not delete the person identity if the identity remains valid. It should revise the affected evidence, dependency, memory, claim, or presentation record.
