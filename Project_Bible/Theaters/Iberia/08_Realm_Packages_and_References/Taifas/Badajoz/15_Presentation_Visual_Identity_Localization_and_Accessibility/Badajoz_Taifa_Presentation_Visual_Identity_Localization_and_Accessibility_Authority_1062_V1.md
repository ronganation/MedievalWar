This authority defines how the Taifa of Badajoz is presented to the player in faction selection, opening briefing, campaign map, realm overview, decision panels, historical-system interfaces, localization-ready source text, accessibility states, and later visual-production handoffs.

## 5. Badajoz Visual Language

Badajoz should look broad, connected, administratively active, and frontier-conscious. The visual composition should combine:

- warm stone, lime plaster, earth, timber, ceramic, leather, textile, parchment, wax, iron, and river-water textures;
- walled cities, bridges, ferries, guarded roads, storehouses, markets, courtyards, and river landings;
- court authority expressed through seals, records, witnesses, messengers, and guarded access;
- military preparedness expressed through garrisoned nodes, mobile response, baggage, and route dependence;
- Atlantic opportunity at Lisbon without naval-faction exaggeration;
- Coimbra as a guarded frontier rather than a doomed countdown;
- a visual gradient from the administrative center to long-distance frontiers without turning uncertainty into absence.

### 5.1 Composition Layers

```yaml
foreground:
  - road and river traffic
  - pack animals and guarded baggage
  - market goods and administrative movement
  - crossings ferries and landing activity
middle_ground:
  - city walls gates towers bridges and stores
  - regional garrisons and mustering spaces
  - court and institutional compounds
background:
  - river valley and plateau transitions
  - distant frontier approaches
  - Lisbon Atlantic light where appropriate
political_layer:
  - ruler princes officers seals petitions envoys and records
military_layer:
  - regional concentration defensive depth and supply visibility
```

## 8. Campaign Map Presentation

### 8.1 Map Thesis

**A broad southwestern network of cities and corridors whose strategic coherence depends on routes, crossings, garrisons, and regional response.**

### 8.2 Site Hierarchy

#### Badajoz

Capital, court, principal administrative center, central military coordination point, and strongest faction-identity anchor.

#### Mérida

Historic and geographic depth node on the Guadiana system. Present its antiquity and strategic position without inventing exact monument use, Property rights, or institutional control.

#### Lisbon

Atlantic-facing commercial and Port-operation interface. It must appear distinct from Badajoz's inland court identity and must not create an opening fleet, customs receipt, shipment, or naval command by icon alone.

#### Santarém

Tagus crossing and corridor-control node. Its crossing status must be visible through bridge, ferry, guarded approach, and route markers rather than a generic settlement bonus.

#### Évora

Southern-interior node supporting depth, communication, defense, and regional economic presentation. Do not invent specific opening Property or revenue rights.

#### Coimbra

Northern frontier city and major military-pressure node. Present preparedness, distance, and vulnerability without a countdown to later conquest.

#### Viseu and Lamego

External historical Sites. Show them through historical context, knowledge, or external objectives only when owners authorize those states. They are not controlled opening Sites.

### 9.1 Abu Bakr Muhammad al-Muzaffar

Present the ruler as an established sovereign at the center of the Aftasid court, administrative coordination, diplomacy, and military responsibility. Do not invent exact appearance, age, regalia, traits, private intent, or guaranteed historical choices.

Visual direction may emphasize:

- calm court authority;
- documents, seals, maps, petitions, and envoys;
- awareness of distant frontiers;
- the weight of maintaining multiple major cities;
- dynastic continuity without deterministic succession.

### 9.2 Yahya al-Mansur

Present as a dynastic prince and possible political actor. Do not label him formal heir, guaranteed successor, future ruler, or predetermined rival unless runtime owners establish those states.

### 9.3 Umar al-Mutawakkil

Present as a dynastic prince and possible political actor with separate identity from Yahya. Do not visually encode later territorial division, later rivalry, later rule, or guaranteed succession.

This authority does not invent names, portraits, marriages, battlefield commands, or Offices to fill presentation space.

The court should read as an operating political household rather than a flat portrait gallery. Use:

- controlled audience space;
- scribes, petitions, seals, account bundles, and route reports;
- military and diplomatic messengers;
- distinct Office channels;
- dynastic consultation;
- institutional and legal interfaces;
- Unknown or vacant presentation where personal holders are unresolved.
