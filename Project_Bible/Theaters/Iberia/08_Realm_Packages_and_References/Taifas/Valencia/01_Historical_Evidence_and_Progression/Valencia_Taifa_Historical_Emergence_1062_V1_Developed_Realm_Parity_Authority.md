### 0.1 Developed-Realm Verdict

Historical Emergence gives Valencia historically recognizable possibilities without turning known later history into a schedule. The campaign may produce renewed Amirid rule, government by inherited officials, a resilient divided administration, a negotiated Balansiyan settlement, hydraulic cooperation or crisis, lawful or extractive government, commander politics, mercantile influence, Toledan dependency, an eastern coalition, a fortress-centered state, outward Amirid ambition, protected plurality or communal fracture, and later historical-character appearances. None is guaranteed, mutually exhaustive, or activated by date alone.

The core rule is simple: history supplies identities, precedents, windows, pressures, and recognizable pathways. Current campaign state supplies the cause.

## 23. Cross-Realm Consent and Intervention Rules

Any Toledo, Denia, Alpuente, Tortosa, Zaragoza, Murcia, Leon-Castile, Barcelona, Aragon, North African, or other external pathway requires the external realm owner's current state. ## 24. Crisis, Battle, Siege, and Deposition Firewall

The reported later Valencia crisis is a historical reference, not a script. There is no fixed 1065 trigger, mandatory attacker, guaranteed battle site, fixed army, required siege, predetermined betrayal, forced rescue, inevitable deposition, or automatic annexation.

A valid crisis requires actual actors, information, motives, diplomatic state, armies, recruitment pools, equipment, horses, commanders, routes, supply, season context, objectives, and owner decisions. A battle requires forces meeting. A siege requires a defended place and siege state. Deposition requires a valid political and military outcome plus succession and recognition handling.

## 25. Research, Decision, and Resource Non-Creation Rule

Research may reveal an option, improve doctrine, clarify a historical candidate, or unlock an owner-authorized capability. A decision may initiate a proposal. Neither creates people, communities, officers, equipment, horses, ships, labor, money, food, fodder, siege engines, fortifications, roads, ports, information, consent, authority, or recruitment pools.

## 26. Failure, Dormancy, Expiry, and Alternative History

Failure must be substantive. Negotiations may fail, preparations may exhaust resources, participants may withdraw, routes may close, legitimacy may collapse, or military action may be defeated. Failure does not schedule the nearest historical outcome as a replacement.

Dormancy preserves a candidate without pressure to activate. Expiry reflects genuine incompatibility, such as a dead candidate, destroyed institution, impossible route, superseded political order, or world state that removes the subject. Alternative history is valid when produced by campaign causality and recorded by owners.

## 27. AI Observation, Proposal, and Abstention

AI may observe owner-validated facts, memories, Momentum, threats, opportunities, capacity, and uncertainty. It may explain eligibility, compare pathway variants, propose a stage transition, or abstain. It cannot accept on behalf of another owner, invent external consent, issue owner commands, create resources, or select historical outcomes to imitate real history.

Every proposal must identify candidate, current stage, enabling conditions, blockers, missing information, costs, affected owners, external consents, divergent outcomes, and rollback point.

## 28. Player Presentation

Expose emergence as possibility and process, not prophecy. The player should see why a pathway is imaginable, which current state enables it, what it requires, who must agree, what it costs, what can block it, and how it may fail or diverge.
