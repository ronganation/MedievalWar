## 1. Historical opening and confidence

### Opening position

Abd al-Malik succeeded his father Abd al-Aziz al-Mansur in 1061. Abd al-Aziz had ruled Valencia for roughly four decades, giving the taifa unusual continuity during the fragmented politics of eleventh-century al-Andalus. The new ruler inherits more than a title. He inherits appointments, grants, service households, military obligations, records, diplomatic habits, public expectations, and a capital associated with competent Amirid protection.

Valencia had emerged as an independent polity during the collapse of caliphal authority. Its earliest taifa rulers were connected to the Amirid household and irrigation administration. Abd al-Aziz later established a durable Amirid dynastic settlement centered on Balansiya. By January 1062, Valencia is a serious eastern Andalusi court, not a provisional city government.

Abd al-Malik is married to a daughter of Yahya al-Mamun of Toledo. The marriage provides access to a stronger court and a potential protector. It also gives Toledo privileged knowledge of Valencian politics and a plausible reason to intervene if the ruler, household, or succession appears endangered.

### Confidence register

#### Established

- Abd al-Malik rules Valencia in January 1062.
- He succeeded Abd al-Aziz in 1061.
- Abd al-Aziz is his father and is deceased before the opening.
- The ruling house belongs to the Amirid lineage.
- Balansiya is the capital.
- Abd al-Malik's reign is represented through sovereign coinage.

#### StronglySupported

- Abd al-Aziz's long reign created substantial administrative and dynastic continuity.
- Abd al-Malik married a daughter of al-Mamun of Toledo.
- Balansiya was a major urban and political center.
- The city possessed important eleventh-century Islamic defenses.
- The huerta and lower Turia were essential to production and capital supply.
- Valencia had active judicial, fiscal, religious, market, military, and mint functions.

#### Supported

- Abd al-Malik's personal authority was less established than his dynastic legitimacy.
- A senior inherited administrator possessed unusual practical influence.
- The Toledan marriage created meaningful household and diplomatic access.
- Valencia's outer authority weakened with distance from Balansiya.
- Christian and Jewish communities contributed to the realm's urban and rural life.

#### Plausible

- Valencia could recruit or hire Andalusi, Berber, Christian, and other contract forces through regional military markets.
- Neighborhood mosques, craft concentrations, transport communities, and local watches formed part of the capital's institutional landscape.
- Murviedro, Alzira, Cullera, Lliria, and Xativa occupied different places within Valencia's strategic system.

#### Contested

- Abd al-Malik's exact age and legal minority.
- A formal regency after his accession.
- The exact identity and title of the principal senior administrator.
- Direct January 1062 ownership of important outer sites.

#### Unknown

- Abd al-Malik's children, heir, siblings, and collateral Amirids.
- The personal name of the Toledan consort.
- Names of most officials and commanders.
- Exact population, troops, garrisons, treasury, revenues, stocks, prices, and tribute obligations.

### Opening historical rule

Unknown is not zero. Where quantity is unknown, the faction uses capability bands, confidence, readiness, relationships, and site functions. Later history is excluded from opening certainty.

## 2. Faction identity and political form

### Identity: court of water, walls, and markets

Valencia is a concentrated court-state with four connected identities:

1. **Amirid monarchy:** The ruler claims recognized lineage, courtly status, and inherited service networks.
2. **Urban capital polity:** Merchants, jurists, officials, military households, artisans, neighborhoods, and protected communities sustain practical sovereignty.
3. **Irrigation realm:** Food, labor, revenue, mills, and recovery depend on channels and locally understood obligations.
4. **Diplomatic balancer:** Marriage, gifts, tribute, contracts, trade access, mercenaries, and selective alliances can preserve independence or consume it.

### Political form

The ruler is sovereign, but power is exercised through offices and negotiated institutions. Valencia is personal, literate, fiscal, judicial, urban, and locally mediated.

Abd al-Malik reserves authority over:

- War and peace
- Formal alliance and tribute
- Foreign military access
- Senior appointments
- Supreme field command
- Major confiscation
- Succession and dynastic marriage
- Permanent extraordinary taxation
- Sovereign coin and titulature
- Surrender of a fortress or permanent local autonomy

He depends on others for:

- Accurate reports
- Daily collection
- Payment execution
- Contract enforcement
- Market provisioning
- Water allocation and repair
- Garrison discipline
- Credit
- Foreign intelligence
- Local compliance

### Gameplay identity

- One exceptional capital matters more than several poor outer territories.
- Control follows roads, crossings, tax delivery, garrison obedience, market access, and communication.
- Characters matter because offices control real capabilities.
- A military victory that devastates the huerta may be a strategic defeat.
- A foreign protector may conquer Valencia without first declaring war.
- Short, prepared operations suit the faction. Prolonged war does not.

## 5. Government, offices, law, and delegation

### Government condition

Valencia inherits a capable but concentrated palace administration.

- **Administrative Continuity:** Strong and Inherited
- **Personal Oversight:** Developing
- **Ministerial Concentration:** High
- **Fiscal Confidence:** Functional
- **Legal Confidence:** Functional
- **Urban Order:** Stable
- **Provincial Compliance:** Uneven

### Core offices and functions

- Senior administrator
- Chief secretary and record staff
- Fiscal superintendent
- Treasurer or physical custodian
- Mint master
- Qadi
- Market inspector
- Capital prefect or wali
- Palace guard commander
- Huerta administrator
- Settlement governors
- Fortress commanders
- Customs and toll officers
- Preacher and congregational-mosque functions

Names and exact titles remain Unknown or ReviewOnly where unsupported.

### Separation of power

The realm is safest when no one subordinate controls all four:

1. Information
2. Money
3. Lawful recognition
4. Armed force

Concentration improves speed. Separation improves oversight and resilience.

### Secretariat and records

Records concern land, grants, appointments, customs, arrears, garrison pay, endowments, market obligations, water responsibilities, diplomatic correspondence, and stores.

The ruler can choose:

- Unified archive
- Duplicate palace register
- Provincial copies
- Qadi-witnessed records
- Parallel reports

Officials can shape policy through delay, omission, classification, and selective framing without openly falsifying records.

### Fiscal administration

Separate assessment from physical custody to reduce fraud and coup risk. Fiscal officers consolidate revenue and expenditure; the treasurer controls coin and sealed stores; the ruler authorizes extraordinary uses.

### Law

Valencia's legal order combines:

- Broad Maliki jurisprudence
- Judicial practice
- Ruler ordinance
- Contract and testimony
- Endowed property
- Protected communal practice
- Local custom, especially in water and village obligations

### Emergency authority

In siege, flood, fire, shortage, unrest, or garrison crisis, the ruler may close gates, release grain, requisition transport, redirect labor, suspend tolls, appoint commissioners, and require extraordinary reporting.

Every emergency measure must identify who pays, who gains authority, what institution is bypassed, whether compensation is promised, how long the measure lasts, and how normal government returns.

### Delegation

#### Routine

Correspondence, records, ordinary collection, market inspection, patrol, and minor repair.

#### Conditional

Extraordinary collection, local revenue assignment, foreign negotiation, military requisition, emergency labor, and governor replacement.

#### Reserved

War, peace, succession, foreign troop access, supreme command, major confiscation, and sovereign coin.

A valid delegation defines task, jurisdiction, duration, funding, reporting, oversight, revocation, emergency discretion, and prohibited actions.

## 6. Ecclesiastical, religious, and juristic institutions

### Religious landscape

- **Public religion:** Sunni Islam
- **Legal tradition:** Maliki, StronglySupported in broad form
- **Christian community:** Supported, scale Unknown
- **Jewish community:** StronglySupported in broad form, scale Unknown

Muslim Valencia has no Christian-style centralized clergy. Authority is distributed among qadi, preacher, jurists, teachers, mosque personnel, witnesses, patrons, endowments, and respected communities.

### Congregational mosque

- Active and Serviceable
- Principal public religious setting
- Supports Friday worship, teaching, preaching, charity, announcement, and public recognition
- Not palace property

### Friday recognition

Abd al-Malik begins with **Secure but Recently Renewed Recognition**. This supports orderly sovereignty but does not guarantee military, provincial, or unconditional political obedience.

### Jurists and scholars

Jurists, teachers, reciters, transmitters, grammarians, notaries, witnesses, students, and visiting scholars form a learned constituency. Court patronage can attract talent; endowment support protects independence; coercion can cause withdrawal or scholarly flight.

### Preacher

The preacher addresses the congregation. The qadi judges. Do not merge their functions without evidence.

### Endowments

Awqaf may support worship, teaching, charity, water, fountains, burial, travelers, and family purposes. Protecting them supports legal and institutional confidence. Auditing disputed claims is possible through law. Confiscation provides immediate resources and severe long-term damage.

### Charity

Relief can be provided by dynasty, consort, mosques, endowments, merchants, jurists, neighborhoods, and protected communities. It supports poor households, dependents, travelers, burial, ransom, flood and fire victims, and displaced rural families.

### Christian and Jewish communal institutions

Protected communities may maintain bounded worship, charity, burial, family records, and internal practice while remaining subject to public order, lawful dues, property rules, and cross-community jurisdiction.

Community identity is never automatic evidence of treason, espionage, revolt, or extraordinary fiscal liability.

## 7. Communities, culture, population, and institutions

### Population model

- **Total population:** Exact Count Unknown
- **Balansiya urban population:** Large for faction
- **Huerta population:** Dense and productive
- **Dependent-town population:** Moderate and site-dependent
- **Upland population:** Dispersed
- **Coastal and wetland population:** Localized

Population supplies separate capacities: revenue, labor, production, recruitment, transport, information, credit, skilled service, legitimacy, institutional staffing, and recovery.

### Muslim urban communities

Dominant in Balansiya and socially diverse: officials, jurists, merchants, artisans, laborers, property holders, tenants, soldiers, servants, dependents, migrants, and poor households.

Their principal concerns are food, work, law, coin, worship, fire protection, disciplined soldiers, and defense.

### Huerta communities

Cultivators, tenants, landholders, estate agents, canal users, mill workers, garden laborers, transport keepers, village intermediaries, and local witnesses.

They can cooperate, petition, delay labor, conceal output, hide animals, redirect trade, neglect maintenance, or resist prolonged service.

### Neighborhoods

Support night watch, fire response, charity, petitions, local worship, emergency militia, funerals, and small-scale maintenance. Their leaders derive influence from wealth, law, craft, military clients, property, religion, or charity rather than a fixed municipal office.

### Merchants

Local retailers, wholesale traders, agricultural brokers, caravan merchants, coastal traders, money changers, warehouse operators, and transport contractors.

They provide credit, grain imports, storage, route intelligence, diplomatic goods, and mercenary-payment access. Their leverage rises during shortage or war finance.

### Artisans

Broad sectors include textiles, leather, footwear, pottery, metalwork, woodworking, building trades, food processing, milling, rope and container production, saddlery, arms repair, and book crafts.

Fair contracts preserve long-term capability. Requisition accelerates immediate military output and damages Artisan Vitality.

### Laborers and poor households

Provide portering, construction, water carrying, market labor, domestic service, stable work, warehousing, garden labor, street maintenance, and emergency response. They are highly vulnerable to price shocks, forced labor, fire, lost wages, quartering, and patron collapse.

### Military households

Soldiers are also social groups with dependents, housing, food, fodder, workshops, honor, and legal-discipline needs. They provide coercive power while consuming treasury and urban capacity.

### Christian communities

Contribute farming, crafts, construction, transport, trade, translation, local continuity, taxable production, and possible contract service. Protection depends on predictable dues, worship, property, appeal, and troop restraint.

### Jewish communities

Contribute in varied ways to craft, agricultural marketing, trade, brokerage, medicine, scholarship, records, and individual court or fiscal service. Do not impose automatic financier, spy, or royal-servant roles.

### Women and households

Households organize property, labor, agriculture, craft, education, charity, military dependency, inheritance, and crisis survival. Women participate in household property, production, markets, family networks, patronage, and relief without unsupported named additions.

### Captivity, unfreedom, and clientage

Historically plausible, Exact Count Unknown. Captivity, ransom, manumission, and clientage connect war, law, household, charity, and diplomacy. People are not treated as generic commodity stock.

### Resistance ladder

1. Petition
2. Delay
3. Concealment
4. Noncompliance
5. Work or market withdrawal
6. Flight
7. Support for a rival patron
8. Armed disorder
9. Revolt

The campaign does not jump directly from dissatisfaction to rebellion.
