### 0.1 Legacy Verdict

Abd al-Aziz al-Mansur is dead before Valencia's January 1062 opening, but he is not absent from the campaign. His historical importance survives through the Amirid succession, the long duration of his government, inherited personnel and obligations, Balansiya's fortification tradition, monetary and administrative continuity, court expectations, and conflicting memories held by the new ruler, officials, military households, merchants, communities, and external courts.

He must never appear as a living opening character, ghost adviser, autonomous AI actor, hidden regent, or posthumous owner of current state. His correct campaign function is to explain inherited capacity and inherited constraint. Valencia begins stronger because of the predecessor's reign, and Abd al-Malik begins under pressure because that strength was not personally created by him.

Abd al-Aziz is a historical person. He is not identical to the Amirid dynasty, Valencia, Balansiya, the walls, government, mint, treasury, household, or inherited officials. Each legacy carrier requires its own current record and owner.

### 3.2 Death Firewall

It cannot instantiate him as living, move him, assign him an office, accept commands from him, age him, injure him, capture him, marry him, or give him current knowledge.

## 4. Historical Position and Reign Interpretation

### 4.1 Long-Reign Context

The project treats Abd al-Aziz as having ruled Valencia for roughly four decades before his death in 1061. That duration supports a powerful continuity premise: institutions, relationships, expectations, records, and obligations had time to become embedded. Duration alone does not prove uniform administration or uninterrupted success.

### 4.2 Peace and Prosperity Reference

A general reference characterizes the reign as a period of peace and prosperity stabilized through protection and friendship. This is useful as a broad orientation, not as proof that every constituency prospered equally, every year was peaceful, or every policy was competent. The campaign should therefore represent durable capacity and confidence alongside entrenched commitments and unequal interpretations.

### 4.3 Fortification Association

Broad reference and project authority associate Abd al-Aziz with Balansiya's major eleventh-century fortification. This supports the predecessor as a fortification legacy subject. It does not settle exact walls, gates, towers, dates, repair state, ownership, garrison, or art assets.

### 4.4 Monetary Association

Coin records are attributed to his reign. This supports sovereign monetary expression and institutional continuity. It does not establish exact coin issue at the opening, physical mint operation, bullion supply, treasury reserves, or market acceptance.

### 4.5 Succession Achievement and Incomplete Transfer

His greatest opening relevance is paradoxical. A functioning state passes to his son, but practical authority remains distributed among people and institutions formed under the predecessor. The succession is recognized, while personal control must still be negotiated and performed.

## 18. Player-Facing Presentation

### 18.1 Short Legacy Profile

Abd al-Aziz ruled Valencia long enough for his government, walls, obligations, and reputation to outlive him. His son inherits a functioning realm, but also officials, clients, expectations, and dependencies built for the father. The player must decide which parts of that inheritance deserve confirmation and which must be changed before continuity becomes control by the dead ruler's system.

### 18.2 One-Line Legacy Promise

**Inherit the predecessor's strength without allowing his unfinished obligations to rule the successor.**
