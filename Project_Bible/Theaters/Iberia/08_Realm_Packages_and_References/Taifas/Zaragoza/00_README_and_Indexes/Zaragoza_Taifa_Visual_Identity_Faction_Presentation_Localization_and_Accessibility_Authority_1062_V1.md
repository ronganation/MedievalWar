## 0.1 Strong Presentation Verdict

Zaragoza should be presented as the **Frontier Court at the Ebro Crossroads**: a capital-centered Banu Hud polity whose courtly, commercial, military, diplomatic, and intellectual capabilities are real but constrained by uncertain peripheral control, multi-actor frontier pressure, finite liquidity and forces, route dependence, incomplete information, succession risk, and the opportunity costs of patronage and Aljaferia development.

The player-facing identity must avoid five flattening errors: generic Taifa opulence, generic desert imagery, permanent religious-bloc presentation, later Aragonese or Spanish symbolism projected backward, and a completed Aljaferia palace treated as the January 1062 baseline. Presentation must distinguish capital certainty from frontier uncertainty, economic capacity from cash, diplomacy from submission, historical potential from predestination, and historical evidence from later memory or legend.

## 0.2 Benchmark and Noninheritance Rule

The developed Valencia presentation authority states that a Realm presentation file should connect visual identity to established faction identity while avoiding invented heraldry, later symbolism, and generic Taifa imagery. Valencia's presentation also separates prosperity, productive capacity, military uncertainty, external access, and political pressure. Zaragoza adopts that methodological rigor without copying Valencia's huerta, maritime profile, Amirid identity, palette, heraldry, architecture, player text, or private state.
