## 1. Purpose

This file begins Phase 1 of the Italia Theater workstream. It establishes a bounded assessment of geographic scope, political fragmentation, maritime and cross-Theater interfaces, opening-boundary research questions, candidate Realm and actor classes, dependency order, evidence requirements, and stop gates. It does not assume that a modern Italian border, a later Kingdom of Sicily, or a single imperial frame correctly describes January 1, 1062.

The assessment is intentionally broader than a Southern Italian Norman faction package. Italia must first be understood as a Theater of multiple political forms, territorial claims, maritime systems, ecclesiastical jurisdictions, urban powers, island societies, imperial interfaces, and contested conquest fronts.

```text
Italia Theater != Modern Italy
Italia Theater != One Italian Realm
Northern Italia != Direct Imperial Ownership
Papal Authority != Uniform Papal Territorial Control
Maritime City != Automatically Mature Later Republic
Sicily In 1062 != Later Kingdom Of Sicily
Muslim Sicily != One Assumed Unified Realm Without Source Reconciliation
Byzantine Interface != Byzantine Control Of Every Greek Community
Theater Association != Political Allegiance
Historical Pressure != Guaranteed Scripted Outcome
```

### 3.3 External orientation evidence

The Metropolitan Museum chronology describes the Italian peninsula from 1000 to 1400 as politically fragmented. It identifies Holy Roman Empire and developing autonomous city-state contexts in the north and center; Byzantine, Lombard, Norman, Papal, and autonomous ducal contexts in the south; and Kalbid and later Norman contexts in Sicily. It also states that Norman princes captured Bari in 1071 and that Genoa and Pisa contested Sardinia and Corsica. This supports a fragmented Theater model and confirms that later results must not be back-projected into January 1062.  
Source: [Metropolitan Museum, Italian Peninsula 1000–1400](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/07/eust.html)

Britannica describes Norman service among southern Italian rulers, Aversa and Capua, Hauteville expansion, and the interaction of papal, imperial, and Byzantine interests. This supports treating Southern Italia as a contested multi-owner environment rather than a completed Norman state.  
Source: [Britannica, The Papacy and the Normans in Italy](https://www.britannica.com/place/Italy/The-papacy-and-the-Normans)

The Medieval maritime-republic orientation identifies Venice, Genoa, Pisa, and Amalfi as important but institutionally nonidentical maritime powers. That supports separate candidate reviews rather than one generic maritime-republic template. The evidence is broad chronology and does not by itself prove exact 1062 autonomy, holdings, offices, or playable status for each city.  
Source: [ItalyScapes, Italian Maritime Republics](https://www.italyscapes.com/blog/italian-history/italian-maritime-republics/)

The Roger I chronology states that the Sicilian expeditions began in 1061 and that a 1062 agreement divided Calabria holdings between Roger and Robert. This is a major opening-boundary research lead, but the exact treaty terms, effective dates, and territorial consequences require stronger source reconciliation before Opening State use.  
Source: [Roger I of Sicily overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_I_of_Sicily)

The Muslim Sicily overview describes the post-1044 island as divided among competing emirates and notes Muslim rule extending until the Norman conquest was completed. This supports investigating multiple Sicilian political centers and relationships rather than assuming one unified island faction. It remains an orientation source and must be checked against scholarly and primary-source material.  
Source: [Muslim Sicily overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_Sicily)

## 4. Theater Identity and Design Opportunity

Italia is suitable as a Theater because its systems interact without collapsing into one political constitution.

```yaml
ItaliaTheaterIdentity:
  political_fragmentation: Core
  maritime_competition: Core
  ecclesiastical_secular_overlap: Core
  imperial_association_without_uniform_control: Core
  norman_conquest_and_consolidation: Core
  byzantine_residual_and_adriatic_interfaces: Core
  muslim_sicilian_political_plurality: CoreResearchQuestion
  lombard_principalities_and_duchies: Core
  autonomous_city_and_communal_development: EmergingAndRegionSpecific
  island_and_strait_geography: Core
  cross_mediterranean_trade_and_warfare: Core
  cultural_religious_and_language_plurality: Core
```

The desired player experience is a Modern Medieval II Theater in which political and military options differ by actor:

- conquest and coalition-building in Southern Italia;
- contested legitimacy and investiture;
- Papal, imperial, Byzantine, Lombard, Norman, civic, monastic, and maritime interfaces;
- island invasion and Strait of Messina operations;
- regional recruitment shaped by actual communities and resources;
- naval access and transport as capacities rather than free movement;
- cities whose institutional development cannot be back-projected from later centuries;
- alternate-history outcomes constrained by January 1062 conditions rather than guaranteed later unification.

## 6. Opening Historical Problem: January 1, 1062

### 6.1 Southern Italia and Norman political structure

The opening assessment must establish:

- Robert Guiscard's exact titles, recognized claims, controlled territory, disputed territory, and obligations on January 1, 1062;
- Roger de Hauteville's exact authority, holdings, dependences, and relationship to Robert at that date;
- whether the reported 1062 Calabria division occurred before or after the scenario opening and how it should be represented if later;
- Richard of Capua and Drengot power;
- Lombard principalities, including Capua, Benevento, Salerno, and any other relevant rulers or fragments;
- autonomous or semi-autonomous duchies and cities, including Naples, Amalfi, Gaeta, and their exact statuses;
- Papal territory, claims, protection, investiture, alliance, hostility, and Benevento interfaces;
- Byzantine controlled, claimed, supplied, or contested territory, including Bari and other Apulian positions;
- exact war, truce, vassalage, tribute, and recognition relationships.

### 6.2 Sicily

Sicily is a core opening conflict laboratory, not a later completed Norman Realm.


The initial design hypothesis is that Sicily may require more than one political actor, with island-wide conquest represented as a long campaign opportunity rather than an opening unified faction. This is a hypothesis only. The research must determine whether the game needs:

- multiple playable Sicilian emirates;
- one playable Realm plus internal emir or provincial actors;
- a coalition or civil-conflict structure;
- external Zirid military and diplomatic support;
- non-playable local powers;
- dynamic Norman foothold and conquest mechanics;
- conditional alliance, surrender, tribute, or collaboration paths.

### 6.3 Byzantines in Italia and Sicily

The Byzantine Empire must eventually receive its own full treatment. Italia Phase 1 may define only the owner interfaces necessary to avoid fabrication.

```text
Byzantine Rule In Southern Italia != Byzantine Rule In Sicily
Greek-Speaking Community != Byzantine Political Control
Ecclesiastical Tradition != Territorial Sovereignty
Claim != Garrison
Garrison != Province-Wide Control
Later Loss Of Bari In 1071 != Loss Already Complete In 1062
```

The Metropolitan Museum chronology places Byzantine rule in South Italy through 1071 and separately describes Sicily under Kalbid governors followed by Norman rule. That supports retaining a Byzantine territorial interface in Southern Italia while not assuming Byzantine political control of Sicily in 1062. Exact settlement-level control still requires dedicated evidence.  
Source: [Metropolitan Museum chronology](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/07/eust.html)

### 7.2 Maritime powers

Venice, Genoa, Pisa, and Amalfi must not be made symmetric. Each requires evidence for:

- political institutions in 1062;
- territory and jurisdiction beyond the city;
- ports, fleets, merchant networks, and naval capacity;
- access rights and foreign privileges;
- island or overseas holdings actually present in 1062;
- local aristocratic, civic, ecclesiastical, and military organization;
- route dependence and shipbuilding capacity;
- war, piracy, convoy, transport, and blockade capabilities;
- later developments that must not appear at opening.

The broader maritime-republic evidence establishes that these cities differed institutionally and rose on different chronologies. It does not support copying a mature later republic template into every 1062 city.  
Source: [ItalyScapes maritime republic overview](https://www.italyscapes.com/blog/italian-history/italian-maritime-republics/)

### 7.3 Papal package boundary

The Papal-centered package would require strict separation among:

- the Pope as a Character and officeholder;
- the Papacy and ecclesiastical institution;
- Papal territorial administration;
- Roman local political actors;
- bishops, abbeys, reform groups, and juristic authorities;
- Papal claims, recognition, sanctions, mediation, and investiture;
- military forces, allies, vassals, patrons, and protected actors;
- relations with Normans, Empire, Byzantium, Lombards, and cities.

The Papal package should not be the first Realm built until these boundaries are mapped.
