## 1. Purpose and Verdict

This preflight compares Venice, Genoa, and Pisa as distinct January 1062 maritime Realm candidates. It determines whether each candidate has enough independent authority, strategic base, campaign agency, military and economic differentiation, continuity, and source potential to justify a dedicated dossier and possible full Realm package.

## 2. Design Doctrine

```text
Maritime Importance != Mature Later Republic
Shared Sea != Shared Faction
Commercial Contact != Political Union
Temporary Cooperation != Permanent Alliance
Competitive Interest != Guaranteed War
Port != Fleet
Fleet != Transport Capacity
Expedition != Territory
Claim != Control
Imperial Association != Direct Imperial Administration
Papal Jurisdiction != Uniform Papal Territorial Control
Named City != Playable Realm
Historical Importance != Full Realm Package
```

The required Modern Medieval II outcome is a readable strategic campaign with strong faction identity, finite maritime and recruitment capacity, meaningful ports and routes, and limited player micromanagement. Detailed receipts, ownership, cargo, crew, fleet, and access state remain behind the campaign layer.

## 16. Stop Conditions

Stop the affected candidate if:

- a later republic, duchy, signoria, or territorial empire is projected into 1062;
- Venice, Genoa, and Pisa receive cloned government, military, economy, or fleet structures;
- maritime fame creates opening fleets, Trade income, or island ownership;
- imperial association creates direct control;
- Papal office and territorial government are merged;
- Tuscany is unified without an opening owner;
- Milan is backcast as its later duchy;
- every named city becomes playable;
- Registry, Opening State, payload, runtime, or Unreal work begins.
