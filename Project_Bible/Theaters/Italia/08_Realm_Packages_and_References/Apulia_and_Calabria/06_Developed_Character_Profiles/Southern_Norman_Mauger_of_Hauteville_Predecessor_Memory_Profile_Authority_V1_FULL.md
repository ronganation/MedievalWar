## 4. Core Historical-Memory Thesis

**Mauger matters not because the dead can act, but because living people may inherit, contest, misremember, document, suppress, or exploit claims about Mauger's service, regional position, household, followers, property, and succession.**

Key questions:

- Which evidence identifies Mauger rather than another named person?
- What was Mauger actually granted, recognized for, or able to perform?
- Did Capitanate association concern title, delegated responsibility, possession, control, property, or later narrative?
- Who knew of Mauger's death, when, and with what confidence?
- Which living actors claimed continuity from Mauger?
- Did a transfer pass through William to Geoffrey, and what exactly transferred?
- What followers, dependents, obligations, records, or disputes survived?
- Can the campaign preserve Mauger's importance even if the exact chain never closes?

## 24. Conditional Memory and Provenance Paths

- **Capitanate Predecessor Memory:** preserves regional provenance without opening ownership.
- **Verified Transfer Tradition:** supports a bounded historical chain only after subject, authority, recognition, and delivery close.
- **Disputed Transfer:** keeps competing claims and local interpretations visible.
- **Military-Service Memory:** affects living expectations without creating forces or traits.
- **Household Continuity:** protects verified dependents, records, and property through living owners.
- **Institutional Record Path:** preserves charters, burial, or commemoration without institutional ownership.
- **Suppressed or Fragmented Memory:** remains valid when publicity or evidence is weak.
- **Referenced Dependency Only:** retains Mauger in provenance without an activated memory.
