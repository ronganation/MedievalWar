## 2. Core Character Thesis

**Can <Person>William of the Principate</Person> preserve a credible lordship, household, marriage network, military contribution, and freedom of judgment while remaining inside a Southern Norman political order led by <Person>Robert Guiscard</Person> but dependent on associated lords who are not owned by the duke?**

William's value is not that William provides another sword. William provides the clearest test of whether composite rule can distinguish kinship, service, homage, alliance, lordship, property, and political support. William may strengthen Robert, support Roger, mediate, bargain, refuse, reconcile, or pursue a bounded regional interest. None is guaranteed.

## 5. Opening Identity and Exclusions

### William begins as a qualified candidate for

- adult associated-lord agency;
- a separate Hauteville household and lordly interest;
- a Principate lordship relationship pending owner validation;
- Lombard, Sorrento, and Salerno-facing marriage politics;
- conditional military contribution and command;
- support for Roger, disagreement with Robert, and later reconciliation as world-state possibilities;
- lordly coalition, mediation, and regional diplomacy.

### William does not begin as

- an automatically active character;
- confirmed dead or certainly alive without chronology receipt;
- owner of an exact Principate territory package;
- a permanent vassal, rebel, ally, or enemy;
- commander of a free contingent;
- Robert's obedient brother;
- Roger's guaranteed supporter;
- possessor of Sorrento, Salerno, Maria's rights, or any external household.
