## 1. Purpose

This preflight defines the strongest historically responsible and strategically enjoyable January 1, 1062 opening interpretation for Drengot Capua. It preserves the approved playable faction, Richard I as its opening ruler, Aversa as its established dynastic base, Capua as its princely center, and Capuan Consolidation as an active opening problem.

It does not pretend that every dimension of authority has been reconciled. Instead, it separates title, recognition, military predominance, city access, garrison, administration, property, revenue, jurisdiction, local participation, and effective territorial control so that the later Opening State can represent a meaningful transition without invented precision.

```yaml
PreflightPurpose:
  - ProtectPlayableOpening
  - DefineBoundedHistoricalHaircut
  - SeparateAuthorityDimensions
  - EstablishSafeOpeningAssumptions
  - QuarantineUnresolvedHistoricalClaims
  - PrepareKeysOfCapuaEmergence
  - RouteExactBindingsToOpeningStateOwners
```

## 2. Strong Preflight Verdict

Drengot Capua should begin as a fully playable Realm under Richard I. Aversa should function as the established Drengot base. Capua should function as the Realm's princely center and primary consolidation problem. The faction should begin with recognized authority and military leverage sufficient to act as a major regional power, but without assuming complete direct control of every Capuan institution, property, garrison, office, claimant network, or local interest.

## 3. Evidence Position

### 3.1 Supported at design level

The current evidence supports the following design-level positions:

- Richard I held Aversa and used the princely title before the scenario opening.
- Papal recognition of Richard's princely position belongs to the opening political background.
- Aversa predates Capua as the Drengot political base.
- Sources distinguish the earlier acquisition of title or conquest from a later possession, submission, or keys tradition connected with 1062.
- Drengot Capua was politically distinct from the Hauteville power.
- Capua retained a deep Lombard political inheritance relevant to its transition under Norman leadership.
