## 1. Purpose

This authority establishes Richard I of Capua as the leading faction character of Drengot Capua. He should occupy the same level of faction-defining importance that Medieval War reserves for rulers whose personal authority, military reputation, dynastic project, and political decisions shape the entire opening campaign.

This is a statement of design importance, not a claim that Richard and William of Normandy possessed identical careers, institutions, resources, or historical roles. Richard receives a leading profile because Drengot Capua's opening position depends heavily upon him and because the source record presents a ruler whose career joins military command, dynastic advancement, Papal recognition, Aversan lordship, and Capuan princely ambition.

```yaml
AuthorityPurpose:
  - EstablishRichardAsLeadingFactionCharacter
  - PreserveVerifiedHistoricalCareer
  - BuildStrongPoliticalAndMilitaryAgency
  - ConnectCharacterToAversaCapuaPapacyHautevillesAndLombardContinuity
  - MakeCharacterStrengthCausalRatherThanDecorative
  - MakeRealmDependenceOnRichardVisibleButNotAbsolute
  - PrepareConditionalHistoricalEmergence
  - PrepareSuccessionAndRealmContinuity
```

## 2. Strong Character Verdict

Richard I should begin as a high-agency ruler, experienced commander, forceful dynastic founder, and dangerous political operator. His profile must make Drengot Capua feel capable of changing the balance of Italia from the first turn.

His strength must not be implemented as a flat package of oversized bonuses. It should emerge through relationships, command credibility, household loyalty, recognition, fear, patronage, access, information, and his ability to convert temporary advantage into durable authority.

## 8. Historical Career Foundation

### 8.1 Adventurer and claimant background

Richard's early career should inform his character identity as a man who arrived without an established southern principality and advanced through service, kinship, opportunism, coercion, and political timing.

The exact sequence must remain subject to the full reading of Amatus and diplomatic evidence. At design level, the background supports adaptability, ambition, confidence among followers, and willingness to use both service and force.

### 8.2 Aversan ascendancy

Aversa represents Richard's conversion from mobile opportunity-seeker into established territorial ruler. This transition supports his understanding of patronage, followers, landed reward, local acceptance, and the danger of disputed succession.

### 8.3 Civitate and command reputation

Richard's reported command of the Norman right wing at Civitate provides a strong basis for military reputation and aggressive command design. Exact battlefield traits, numbers, and tactical modifiers remain deferred.

### 8.4 Princely advance

Richard's assumption of the Capuan princely title and Papal recognition support a ruler able to convert military pressure into political status. The incomplete Capuan consolidation prevents this from becoming a finished success story at opening.
