## 1. Purpose

This authority defines the religious and ecclesiastical environment of Drengot Capua as a field of independent institutions, jurisdictions, communities, properties, patronage relationships, sacred spaces, mediation channels, records, and Papal authority. It prevents religion from becoming one faction-wide piety score or an automatic extension of princely government.

It does not publish exact January 1062 bishops, abbots, church holdings, appointments, cathedral chapters, monastic dependencies, grants, jurisdictions, sanctions, rites, or Papal obligations.

```yaml
AuthorityPurpose:
  - DefineChurchAndMonasticInstitutionsAsIndependentActors
  - SeparateFaithEcclesiasticalJurisdictionPropertyAndPatronage
  - EstablishPapalRecognitionServiceSanctionAndMediationInterfaces
  - PreserveAversaCapuaAndMontecassinoInstitutionalDistinctness
  - ConnectSacredSpaceRecordsMemoryPropertyAndPublicOrder
  - PrepareExactEcclesiasticalOpeningStateAndInstitutionProfiles
```

## 2. Strong Religious-System Verdict

Drengot Capua should experience religion through institutions and relationships rather than a passive legitimacy bonus. The prince may receive recognition, protect churches, patronize monasteries, negotiate appointments, confirm property, seek mediation, or provoke sanction, but no religious institution becomes Realm property and no act of patronage creates universal ecclesiastical obedience.

```yaml
ReligiousSystemVerdict:
  model: LayeredInstitutionalJurisdictionalAndPatronageChurchSystem
  primary_strength: PapalAccessAndDenseEcclesiasticalNetworks
  primary_constraint: IndependentJurisdictionsPropertyAndInstitutionalMemory
  Papal_relationship: RecognitionServiceSanctionMediationAndCanonicalAuthority
  monastic_relationship: PatronageProtectionPropertyMediationAndIndependence
  one_realm_piety_score: rejected
  ruler_owns_church: false
  exact_opening_bindings: Deferred
```

## 3. Evidence Boundary

The controlling source inventory identifies princely diplomas, Montecassino material, Capuan ecclesiastical records, Aversan diplomatic material, and Papal documentation as critical future sources. External research identifies G. A. Loud's Church and Society in the Norman Principality of Capua, 1058 to 1197 as a major study and its published contents explicitly include Montecassino and the Archbishopric of Capua, the Papacy and Capua, ecclesiastical tensions, administration, property and benefactors, and spirituality and standards. A separate chapter explicitly studies contested sacred space in Norman Capua and describes later conflict involving Capuan cathedral clergy, Cassinese dependencies, and Papal intervention. Those sources justify robust institutional and jurisdictional systems, but they do not settle exact January 1062 actors or holdings.
