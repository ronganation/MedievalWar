## 1. Verdict, rationale, and playable promise

The existing Genoa military-maritime authority defines integrated capabilities, candidate land roles, command, muster, transport, and recovery. This child portfolio does a different job: it specifies a **playable land-roster design surface** with distinct candidate formations, battlefield jobs, providers, material dependencies, weaknesses, counters, and conditional development. It neither replaces nor narrows the parent. The Pisa land-roster authority is a *methodological* comparator, not a source of Genoese units, opening facts, or Pisan river geography.

Genoa's land force should feel like a compact harbor polity defending a steep coast and hard inland approaches. Its potential strengths are defensive concentration, flexible urban service, coastal and route knowledge, and a limited ability to combine land fighters with maritime transport. Its weaknesses are small and competing labor pools, difficult sustained field operations, uncertain access to neighboring communities, costly mounted service, and a high cost of losing skilled workers. **These are design propositions and role candidates, not verified 1062 force descriptions.** The player can develop a recognizable army without receiving an opening state navy, a prince's host, a formal communal army, or later famous crossbow companies.

The land portfolio comprises **ten distinct functional families**, with narrow variants only where they alter a meaningful source, role, or burden. The names below are working design labels, not claimed medieval Genoese titles.

### 4.1 Urban watch and emergency defenders

**Function:** observation, alarm, gate and harbor-approach security, protection of households and records, and emergency defense of a threatened site. **Source:** qualified urban, neighborhood, and harbor-adjacent service providers with a valid temporary mandate. **Equipment:** actual available hand weapons, shields or tools, not an assumed standardized kit. **Battlefield:** strong situational awareness and short-distance defensive response; weak in prolonged open-field fighting and pursuit. **Campaign cost:** pulls people from port handling, craft, markets, and household care. **Development:** improve signals, assembly, protection, equipment storage, and relief rotation; a watch remains distinct from a trained standing infantry force. **Gate:** a building or district label cannot summon watchmen.

### 4.2 Mustered urban spear-and-shield foot candidate

**Function:** potential line-holding core for gates, urban approaches, harbor landings, and limited field defense. **Source:** owner-validated urban provider pools, service rules, equipment access, and collective authorization. **Equipment:** spear and shield are *candidate functional equipment*, subject to 1062 material review; protective gear varies with owned stock. **Battlefield:** defensive frontage and protection for lighter troops; weaker when cohesion breaks, supply fails, or field operations exceed training. **Counterplay:** maneuver, missile pressure, disrupted assembly, and separation from command. **Civilian cost:** concentration of economically active households. **Development:** trained or better-equipped urban foot requires practice, officers, shields, pay or compensation, and a real institution; no automatic formal civic company at opening.

### 4.3 Selected urban close foot candidate

**Function:** compact reserve for a gate, landing, narrow approach, or contested urban space; not a generic superior version of all infantry. **Source:** already eligible experienced urban defenders, possibly supported by valid household or public contracts. **Equipment:** only owner-held weapons and protection. **Strength:** greater cohesion for a bounded high-risk task when trained and led. **Weakness:** small finite provider base, high equipment cost, and reduced availability for other service. **Counterplay:** isolation, attrition, open-field overextension, and loss of experienced leaders. **Development:** selection and drill may improve a current formation, but cannot mint a new elite pool. **Gate:** historical office or title and actual selected-company practice require evidence.

### 4.4 Coastal watch and landing-response foot

**Function:** warning, local shore security, anti-raid response, escort between coastal sites, and limited landing protection. **Source:** qualified coastal communities under their own access and obligation terms; urban service may supplement but does not own those communities. **Strength:** local shoreline and transfer knowledge. **Weakness:** narrow operating range, weather dependence, and competing fishing or transport work. **Counterplay:** surprise at an unobserved landing, blocked communication, or inability to reinforce. **Development:** better signals, maintained access, boats and cooperation; neither coast nor boat creates a permanent marine unit. **Gate:** do not treat all Ligurian shore settlements as Genoese recruitment districts.

### 4.5 Valley and pass service contingent candidate

**Function:** guide, screen, escort, hold a route chokepoint, protect a supply movement, or support relief. **Source:** a particular owner-validated valley, mountain, or route community, not a generic 'Ligurian highlander' population. **Strength:** site-specific movement and warning when local cooperation exists. **Weakness:** limited transferable skill, uncertain political access, and dependence on local supplies and return terms. **Counterplay:** deny local support, bypass, isolate, or interrupt provisions. **Development:** negotiated service, improved warning and routes, and repeated practice; region ownership alone does not provide recruits or a terrain bonus. **Gate:** exact places and community obligations are research-held.

### 4.6 Household or institutional armed following candidate

**Function:** escort, guarded travel, reserve, defense of validated property or person, and command support. **Source:** a *specific admitted* household, institution, office, or contract with actual maintained personnel. **Strength:** potentially quicker coherent service and restricted equipment access. **Weakness:** scarce, politically entangled, and not necessarily available for collective policy. **Counterplay:** patron absence, contested mandate, payment failure, or competing loyalties. **Development:** recognized service may become a bounded public contribution, but no anonymous later famous house is inserted into 1062. **Gate:** no named opening Genoese household or commander has been admitted by the character and household decisions; this is a conditional family, not an opening retinue.

### 4.7 Missile-support foot candidate

**Function:** defensive ranged support from protected urban, shore, route, or siege positions. **Source:** qualified users and training, equipment and ammunition, not 'rural population' or an occupational label. **Weapon:** bow, sling, thrown weapon, or crossbow form only after individual chronological and local evidence review; no assumed uniform equipment. **Strength:** pressure on exposed approaches when supplied and protected. **Weakness:** ammunition, training, weather and cover dependence, vulnerability in close action. **Counterplay:** cover, movement, pressure on supply and screen, and forced close engagement. **Development:** later organized crossbow specialization is a separate evidence-gated lineage, not an opening Genoese corps or automatic recruitment branch.

### 4.8 Mounted messenger and escort candidate

**Function:** movement of orders, observation, route contact, limited screening and pursuit where terrain permits. **Source:** actual trained riders, horses, tack, feed, and a political or contractual service basis. **Strength:** speed on usable routes, not universal battlefield shock. **Weakness:** horse scarcity, maintenance, slope and pass restrictions, high cost. **Counterplay:** narrow terrain, denied forage, route disruption, and dismounted pressure. **Development:** better route intelligence and escort coordination; heavy mounted shock remains a separate evidence and material gate, not a compulsory Genoese core. **Gate:** a messenger on horseback is not automatically a cavalry formation.

### 4.9 Shipboard and landing fighting contingent candidate

**Function:** protection of embarked people and cargo, boarding defense where battle owners admit it, first-shore security, and return protection. **Source:** specifically eligible armed personnel; essential mariners, pilots, handlers, and propulsion workers cannot be double-counted. **Strength:** can support a combined land-sea mission if vessels, crews, weather, landing, supply, and command align. **Weakness:** very limited lift, vulnerable transfer, and civilian-maritime opportunity cost. **Counterplay:** denied landing, interrupted reinforcement, damage to transport, or lack of safe withdrawal. **Development:** repeated practice may justify a specialist contingent; the term 'marines' does not prove a permanent corps. **Gate:** no free shipboard fighters, ships, or crew from the roster label.

### 4.10 Fieldwork, siege, and logistics support contingent

**Function:** route clearing, camp and defensive works, handling, emergency repairs, siege support, and return recovery. **Source:** finite craft specialists, general labor, transport handlers, and authorized command, each still owned by their population and economic systems. **Equipment:** actual tools, timber, fastenings, animals, and stores. **Strength:** enables other forces rather than replacing fighting infantry. **Weakness:** scarce skilled labor and exposure when unprotected. **Counterplay:** disrupt materials, access, time, security, or the labor base. **Development:** training and equipment may improve work, but the siege owner retains works and operational outcomes; no siege engine appears from a roster slot.

## 5. Conditional supplements, not mandatory roster slots

**Contracted foot or missile support:** a valid individual or company, terms, source region, equipment, payment, route, command, and termination must exist. Genoa does not receive an infinite mercenary market. **Regional contingents:** only a particular admitted community or external owner can provide its people. **Ecclesiastical protection:** institutional property and mandate do not make clergy an army or church building a military provider. **Better-equipped mounted service:** requires evidence of actual riders, war-capable horses, feed, and institutional or household backing. **Later formal crossbow specialists:** require chronology, material production, training, organization, provider pools, and political support. **Later professional service:** requires sustained finance and personnel rather than a technology toggle. None of these supplements is an automatic opening option.

## 6. Finite recruitment, equipment, and double-commitment contract

A provider pool is attached to real people and a place or institution. A person can have several skills but cannot simultaneously perform incompatible full-time military, crew, port, voyage, craft, transport, and household-care commitments. Recruitment eligibility consumes owner facts: mandate and obligation; population and availability; actual equipment, animals, money, food and water; training and officers; route and site access; public-order response; and command. Recruitment remains finite even when a settlement develops. No fixed numeric regeneration, universal recruitment cooldown, or direct population-to-army conversion is specified.

## 7. Mission-based composition and battlefield differentiation

These are *decision patterns*, never free force packages or prescribed unit ratios.

- **Urban-harbor defense:** watch and eligible defensive foot, with qualified missile support, a reserve, warning, and engineers if available. Protect households, stores, access, and withdrawal rather than treating a harbor icon as a fortress.
- **Coastal landing response:** local warning and shore responders, a bounded mobile reserve, boats only if owned and staffed, and a clear distinction between defense ashore and fighting at sea.
- **Valley/pass escort:** route-informed contingent, protected transport, compact infantry, scouts or messengers if available, and a plan for denied access or weather change.
- **Limited field force:** cohesive foot, available ranged support, command, supply, a return route, and explicit acceptance of weaker sustained reach than a large territorial army.
- **Embarked contingent:** land fighters plus *separate* ship crews, pilots, handlers, vessel capacity, safe landing, and reembarkation. Losing a vessel does not automatically delete all persons aboard; battle and custody owners resolve outcomes.
- **Siege relief or defensive works:** qualifying troops, labor, tools, supplies, civilians and site access; a roster does not perform engineering or determine surrender.

Genoa may choose to delay, contract, cooperate, defend, or abstain when it cannot field a credible formation. A recognizable faction roster includes the option **not** to overextend.
