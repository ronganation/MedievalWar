Stradioti are later Balkan-source light cavalry whose value lies in operational mobility and controlled disruption. They scout, raid, test routes and flanks, threaten weak targets, tempt an undisciplined pursuit where conditions pass, disengage before fixation, and counterattack disorder. Their card also carries campaign consequences that other light cavalry do not necessarily share: refugee movement, settlement, families, land, privileges, garrison duty, Venetian overseas service, reassignment, and later company circulation between employers.

## 3. Player-Facing Description

Stradioti provide the later Italian and Venetian battlefield with fast light cavalry whose purpose is to create uncertainty and operational pressure rather than win a frontal cavalry contest. Their riders reconnoiter, attack exposed support, raid communications and baggage, screen friendly movement, threaten flanks, and repeatedly disengage before heavier forces can fix them.

The unit is strongest when the player has reliable information, open withdrawal lanes, a disciplined captain, suitable horses, and another force ready to exploit the disorder. A feigned withdrawal is a risky operation rather than a guaranteed ability. An opponent may refuse pursuit, block the lane, counter with missiles or faster cavalry, or turn the maneuver into a real rout.

The campaign card can represent different historically grounded entities. Some are exact companies serving for agreed terms. Others may be authorized Venetian contingents, garrison formations, settled military colonists, refugees maintained through land or privileges, or transferred formations. The card never erases those distinctions.
