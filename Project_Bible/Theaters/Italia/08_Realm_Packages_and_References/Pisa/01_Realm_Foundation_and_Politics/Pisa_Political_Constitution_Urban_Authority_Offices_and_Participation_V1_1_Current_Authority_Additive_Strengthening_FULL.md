## 1. Purpose

This authority develops Pisa's political identity without flattening the city into either a monarchy or a mature later republic. It defines the political questions, actor classes, participation channels, command relationships, legitimacy pressures, decision procedures, and transformation routes needed for a historically bounded January 1062 campaign.

The central design problem is not merely who rules Pisa. It is how a rising maritime city converts overlapping urban, aristocratic, episcopal, merchant, military, neighborhood, regional, and nominal superior claims into decisions that can sustain fleets, trade, defense, diplomacy, taxation, expeditions, and internal compliance.
