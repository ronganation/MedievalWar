## 1. Executive Finding

Pisa's western Mediterranean interfaces in January 1062 must be represented as actor-specific, partially known, and structurally interconnected rather than as a single Pisan sphere of influence. General references support earlier Pisan and Genoese intervention connected with Sardinia and Corsica, growing Norman power in southern Italy during a prolonged and fragmented conquest, continuing Muslim political authority in Sicily before complete Norman conquest, and wider contact across the western Mediterranean. These sources do not establish Pisa's exact opening treaties, island possessions, Papal mandates, Norman agreements, Sicilian enemies, North African partners, fleet access, trade routes, or military plans.
