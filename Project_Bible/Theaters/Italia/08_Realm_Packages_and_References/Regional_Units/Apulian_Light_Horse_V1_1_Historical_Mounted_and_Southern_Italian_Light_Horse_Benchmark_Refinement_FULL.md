Apulian Light Horse represent exact regional mounted sources capable of scouting, screening, javelin harassment, route patrol, pursuit, and rapid battlefield communication. The unit’s identity is mobility and judgment rather than armor or charge power. Riders probe, cast, turn away before heavy contact, re-form behind protection, and exploit troops that have already lost cohesion.

The formation may serve local rulers, regional powers, Norman, Lombard, Byzantine, papal, or other employers only where actual political access, obligation, release, or contract exists. Changing employers does not erase the source community, horses, leaders, equipment, or replacement relationships.

## 2. One-Screen Player Card

- **Unit:** Apulian Light Horse
- **Class:** Regional light mounted scouts and javelin skirmishers
- **Core verb:** Scout, Cast, Withdraw
- **Source:** Exact Apulian or validated adjacent southern-Italian mounted community, household, following, company, or service source with riders, horses, leaders, equipment, and replacement
- **Battle promise:** Reconnoiter the field, screen the army, harass exposed troops with limited mounted missiles, and disengage before heavier enemies establish contact
- **Best use:** Open or rolling ground, advance screens, flanks, route security, counter-skirmishing, pursuit, isolated missiles, and disordered troops
- **Worst use:** Heavy cavalry contact, stable spear frontage, dense woodland, deep mud, blocked retreat, prolonged missile exchange, and unsupported melee
- **Strategic value:** Supplies regional information, screening, communication, and light mounted pressure without replacing heavy cavalry
- **Strategic cost:** Finite riders, suitable horses, tack, javelins, leaders, remounts, fodder, water, veterinary care, political access, and local-defense opportunity cost
- **Signature decision:** Continue harassment, attempt a controlled lure, pursue a broken target, or preserve horses and riders before the withdrawal lane closes

## 3. Player-Facing Description

Apulian Light Horse are regional mounted troops trained or experienced in scouting, screening, patrol, pursuit, and mobile javelin pressure. The company is most effective when the player preserves distance, uses terrain and friendly troops to protect withdrawal, and chooses exposed targets rather than charging formed infantry.

A controlled feigned withdrawal may tempt an undisciplined enemy into pursuit, but success depends on the company’s cohesion, leaders, horses, ground, visibility, enemy command, and a prepared support position. The order is not mind control. A disciplined opponent may refuse pursuit, screen with missiles, block the escape route, or counter with faster cavalry.
