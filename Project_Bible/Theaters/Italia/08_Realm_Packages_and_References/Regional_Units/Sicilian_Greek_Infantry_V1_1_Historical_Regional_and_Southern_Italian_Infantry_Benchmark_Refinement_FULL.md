Sicilian Greek Infantry represent surviving Greek-speaking military communities in Sicily and related southern Italian locations whose service could continue through political change. The profile’s center is disciplined regional shield-and-spear infantry rather than a generic Byzantine transplant or mercenary stereotype. The troops form a stable local line, protect urban and coastal approaches, support regional armies, and counterattack disordered enemies while remaining dependent on actual communities, leaders, equipment, institutions, obligations, and political access.

The unit may enter mercenary or contract service when an actual company, captain, employer, agreement, route, and replacement relationship exist. That service channel does not redefine every Sicilian Greek infantry source as mercenary.

## 2. One-Screen Player Card

- **Unit:** Sicilian Greek Infantry
- **Class:** Regional shield-and-spear infantry
- **Core verb:** Form, Hold, Counter
- **Era:** Eleventh-century source direction with exact opening and later availability owner-gated
- **Source:** Surviving Greek-speaking communities, local institutions, households, veterans, leaders, equipment, and recognized service arrangements in Sicily or validated southern Italian expressions
- **Battle promise:** Hold a coherent shielded frontage against direct pressure and deliver a controlled counterattack when the enemy loses order
- **Best use:** Gates, streets, walls, crossings, settlement approaches, guarded coastal sites, stable field frontage, and support beside missile troops or heavier formations
- **Worst use:** Open flanks, missile saturation, deep mud, broken formation, prolonged pursuit, heavy shock attack without support, and distant campaigns without replacement access
- **Strategic value:** Preserves and mobilizes a real regional military population through changing political regimes without converting the population into faction property
- **Strategic cost:** Draws from finite communities, households, local defense, leaders, shields, spears, armor, transport, and political cooperation
- **Signature decision:** Maintain the shielded line, open spacing for movement, or counterattack without dissolving regional formation cohesion

## 3. Player-Facing Description

Sicilian Greek Infantry are regionally sourced troops from Greek-speaking military communities that endured political change in Sicily and parts of southern Italy. Their battlefield identity draws upon locally preserved Eastern Roman or Byzantine-influenced equipment and military practice while allowing the actual formation to absorb local Sicilian, Muslim, Latin, Italian, Norman, and Mediterranean influences where evidence supports them.

The unit is strongest when maintaining a coherent frontage with shields and spears, protecting a settlement approach, or supporting a mixed army. The formation can threaten a direct mounted approach and can counterattack a disordered enemy, but it is not an ancient phalanx, a pike block, or an invulnerable shield wall. Missile pressure, flanking, command loss, poor ground, and forced pursuit can break its cohesion.
