## 1. Purpose and refined verdict

This inventory converts the broad Sicily Muslim political-field assessment into claim-level research and design controls. Its central problem is not whether the Norman conquest existed. The invasion began before the January 1, 1062 opening. The unresolved problem is which Sicilian political centers were already in directed war, alliance, accommodation, rivalry, or uncertainty relationships with the Southern Normans and with one another at the exact opening boundary.

```yaml
RefinedVerdict:
  Norman_invasion_began_in_1061: StronglySupported
  Sicily_is_active_conflict_theater_on_January1_1062: StrongOpeningInterpretation
  universal_formal_war_between_all_Sicilian_centers_and_Normans: NotEstablished
  one_island_wide_Muslim_belligerent: NotEstablished
  Norman_victory_predetermined: false
  difficult_early_campaign_identity: StrongDesignRecommendation
  AI_survival_requires_coordination_diplomacy_and_real_capacity: true
  Zirid_assistance_path: HistoricallyGroundedCandidate
  Fatimid_direct_opening_intervention: NotEstablished
  western_North_African_intervention: AlternateHistoryCandidateOnly
  next_owner_decision: DirectedWarRelationshipAndRealmStructureAdmission
```

The historical conquest is an allowed campaign outcome, not a script. A human or AI Sicilian polity must be able to survive, coordinate, bargain, secure aid, contain the invasion, recover, fragment, accommodate, or lose according to current world state.

The modern military study by Georgios Theotokis states that the Norman invasion took place in 1061 and was not completed until 1091. It also identifies the first phase as running from the invasion through the unsuccessful siege of Palermo in 1064. The study warns that Geoffrey Malaterra wrote later, was not an eyewitness, relied mainly on unidentified oral sources, and has dating and detail limitations. These limits require claim-level chronology rather than a simple conquest narrative.
