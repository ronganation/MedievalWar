## 2. Diplomatic thesis and player promise

**The central question is not simply whom to ally with.** A Sicilian center may need help against another Sicilian center, a truce with the Normans, a defensive coalition, transport from Ifriqiya, commercial access, or a mediated settlement. These objectives can conflict. A single banner must not erase that conflict.

```yaml
DiplomaticForm: CenterBoundNegotiationWithinOnePlayableCompositeRealm
OpeningPressure: NormanInvasionAlreadyUnderway
UniversalOpeningWar: NotAdmitted
AutomaticCommonDefense: NotAdmitted

FatimidAid: DistantOwnerAndCapacityGatedAlternative
PlayerPromise: WinTimeBuildCoordinationOrSecureAidWithoutBeingGivenUnityOrForeignArmiesForFree
HistoricalNormanVictoryGuaranteed: false
```

For the human player, the reward is assembling an effective response through persuasion, concessions, intelligence, and actual logistics. For the AI, the same decisions must be possible using the same information, costs, permissions, and failure conditions. A campaign can also end in negotiated survival, separate peace, fragmentation, or defeat.

## 6. Sicilian internal diplomatic field

**Ibn al-Thumna's sphere.** Norman cooperation is a strong candidate for historical reconciliation, not a perpetual faction-wide alliance. The playable tension is whether assistance against rivals can be bounded without surrendering future autonomy, access, hostages, or local support. The Sicilian side may seek a narrowed mandate, delay, renegotiation, or separation; Norman response remains Norman-owned.

**Ibn al-Hawwas's sphere.** Rivalry with Ibn al-Thumna and opposition to Norman operations are strong research candidates. The political options include holding fortified centers, seeking Zirid support, proposing a common defense, negotiating a truce, or rejecting a coalition that gives rivals command. No single option is compulsory.

**Palermo.** Its institutional importance does not establish a named ruler or automatic leadership of an island coalition. Palermo may receive proposals, finance a bounded contribution, defend its own interests, bargain over port access, or decline a common mandate. Exact opening positions remain held.

**Western and southeastern centers.** Mazara, Trapani, Noto, and other centers require separate owner dispositions. Their maritime links or geographic exposure create opportunities, not automatic alignment. The same applies to locally governed or contested northeastern communities under Norman pressure.

Written or oral reports can be intercepted, stale, contradictory, forged, or misunderstood. No player or AI omniscience follows from selecting the faction.

```yaml
ContactLifecycle:
  - IdentifyIssueAndKnownCounterparty
  - VerifyMandateAndAvailableRepresentative

  - AuthenticateReceiptAndUnderstanding
  - ReceiveReplyCounterproposalRefusalOrSilence
  - UpdateActorSpecificKnowledgeAndReview
```

The population authority owns language skills and finite interpreters. The government authority owns credentials and office competence. A future information authority may deepen provenance, forgery, secrecy, and counterintelligence without taking over diplomatic consent.

## 9. Norman pressure, war, truce, and separate peace

The invasion began before the scenario opening, but exact January 1 bilateral war records must be reconciled actor by actor. This authority supports **directed active-war candidates**, theater hostility without universal formal war, and conditional escalation. It rejects an island-wide automatic war solely because the player controls one container.

```yaml
NormanInterface:
  IbnAlThumna_to_Normans: CooperationCandidateExactTermsHeld
  IbnAlHawwas_to_Normans: WarOrHostilityCandidateExactBoundaryHeld
  Palermo_to_Normans: Unknown
  WesternCenters_to_Normans: Unknown
  SoutheasternCenters_to_Normans: Unknown
  Norman_private_intent_and_capacity: SouthernNormanOwnerOnly
  universal_war_flag: NotAdmitted
```

A truce must specify parties, effective place and time, permitted movements, protected persons and property, access, exchange, violations, reporting, and end conditions. A separate peace can preserve a center while weakening a coalition, or prevent needless destruction; it is not automatically treason. Likewise, a Norman offer can be useful to one center and unacceptable to another. A Norman invasion order is not evidence that all Sicilian centers agreed to fight it.

## 14. Commercial, port, and safe-conduct diplomacy

A merchant, vessel, port, or road may be useful to an agreement without being owned by the negotiating center. Terms may address port access, customs treatment, passage, cargo inspection, pilots, rescue, warehouses, escort, food purchase, credit, compensation, and dispute jurisdiction. The government and economy owners control lawful assessment, accounts, goods, and payments; geography and maritime owners control practical access and movement.

```yaml
TradeAccessTermsRequire:
  - ActualPortRouteOrLanding
  - PartiesAndValidAuthority
  - ScopeAndEffectiveWindow
  - OwnershipUseAndCustody
  - SecurityAndInformation
  - CustomsOrPaymentBasis
  - CapacityAndOperatingPeople
  - DisputeAndTerminationRoute
```

Trade privileges do not generate cargo or revenue; safe conduct does not guarantee safety; a port-access agreement does not grant a fleet or territorial title.

## 15. Hostages, prisoners, guarantees, and compensation

Hostages and sureties may be negotiated only with person-specific standing, lawful custody, care, communication, duration, release, and review. Captivity does not establish guilt, submission, or a ransom tariff. Guarantees may be performed, breached, renegotiated, or released. Law, character, custody, economy, and external owners each retain their own records. An exchange agreement is not a completed exchange, and compensation promised is not compensation delivered.

## 16. Capitulation, accommodation, and postwar settlement

Jeremy Johns's study emphasizes surrender on terms as an important process during the long Norman conquest. That is a **future scenario design family**, not proof that a particular center had already capitulated on January 1, 1062. A center may bargain over security, office continuity, religious institutions, property, taxes, garrison conduct, hostages, return, trade, and appeal. The settlement must identify which actors are bound and what was actually performed.

```yaml
SettlementRecordRequires:
  - AuthorizedPartiesAndDirectedScope
  - MilitaryCessationAndAccessTerms
  - LocalGovernmentAndInstitutionalContinuityTerms
  - CivilianPropertyAndReligiousProtections
  - PrisonerHostageAndSafeConductTerms
  - FiscalContributionAndPaymentTerms
  - GarrisonAndOccupationLimits
  - VerificationBreachRemedyAndReview
```

Surrender does not grant blanket title, local consent, or administrative capacity. Negotiated survival is a possible political outcome for a center, not an automatic faction defeat or guaranteed preservation.

## 17. Coalition bargaining and internal distribution

A coalition requires explicit participating centers, common objective, authorized leadership, separate force and resource contributions, permitted targets, compensation, and exit terms. Its leader cannot spend another center's treasury or redirect another center's forces. If one center receives Zirid support or Norman terms, the others may seek consultation, contest the mandate, counteroffer, or withdraw; consequences depend on the actual agreement and their current interests.

## 18. Diplomacy under leadership loss

Ibn al-Thumna's reported death during 1062 cannot be backfilled into the opening or guaranteed in a divergent campaign. If any principal dies, is captured, defects, or loses effective authority, each personal mandate and center-specific relationship must be reviewed. A hostage, envoy, contract, safe conduct, or truce may survive the principal only if its terms and receiving institutions support continuity. No person automatically inherits all agreements or their performance.

## 20. Player-facing diplomacy loop

A player considering help should see **who can ask**, **whom the request binds**, **what is known**, **what the other side could plausibly supply**, **what must be paid**, **where the aid would land**, **when it might arrive**, **which local center may object**, and **what happens if the request fails**. Present investigation, smaller contribution, negotiation, defensive-only support, money, access, truce, and abstention as distinct alternatives. Never label an unaccepted proposal an alliance or show a future contingent as a present army.
