# PART I. HISTORICAL AND TERMINOLOGY DECISION

## 1. What the research directly supports

The general crossbow chronology source states that crossbows reappear in European sources in 947 and became significant in European militaries from the eleventh century onward. It also states that pre-medieval European military use is unclear. This supports an eleventh-century crossbow development question, not a specific 1062 Sicilian Muslim formation. citeturn34search123

The Southern Italian archery discussion states that crossbowmen were increasingly used in early twelfth-century Southern Italy, while Sicilian Muslim archery remained an important exception and continued in Norman-Swabian armies. That supports a later development lane and continued coexistence with Muslim bow traditions, not replacement of Khaffaf al-Thaghr in 1062. citeturn34search122turn34search125

The Muslim mechanical-bow discussion says true hand-held crossbows pose a difficult historical question. This is useful caution against treating Fatimid, North African, or Muslim crossbow use as settled from a broad cultural connection alone. citeturn34search149

## 2C. Player-facing description

Qawwasun al-Rijal are Sicily's developing crossbow infantry, created only when real crews, weapons, spanning methods, bolts, repair, officers, training, protection, and service institutions come together. Early formations may be narrow, mixed, and tied to a particular town, fortress, ruler, workshop, contract, or captured-knowledge program. Mature High Era service may become more consistent, but never appears simply because the faction survives to a date.

Crossbows give Sicily a slower and more deliberate missile rhythm than its bow families. Crews seek protected firing lanes, hold fire for worthwhile targets, release, recover behind the screen, reload, and withdraw before cavalry or assault infantry breaks the position. Bows remain essential for sustained volume, mobility, and faster response.
