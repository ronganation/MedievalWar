# PART I. PLAYER EXPERIENCE

## 1. Unit identity strip

- **Unit:** Khayyālat al-Rāmī
- **Class:** trained mounted-bow specialist cavalry
- **Quality:** trained, horse-dependent, bow-dependent, ammunition-dependent, and institutionally demanding
- **Source:** qualified riders and suitable horses combined with real mounted-bow instruction, bows, arrows, leaders, support, political authority, and recovery
- **Availability:** narrow, conditional, regionally uneven, and never created by culture or a calendar date
- **Primary role:** apply mobile bow pressure from shifting angles, force facing and movement decisions, and disengage before enemy cavalry or infantry fixes the formation
- **Best use:** open or rolling ground, broad flanks, protected maneuver corridors, enemy support lines, and combined-arms battles where spears and heavy cavalry constrain pursuit
- **Major weakness:** finite arrows, demanding rider-horse-bow intersection, weak prolonged melee, concentrated missiles, aggressive enemy cavalry, prepared spears, bad ground, and blocked withdrawal
- **Opening State:** not an opening default
- **Exact values:** none promoted

## 3. Player-facing description

Khayyālat al-Rāmī are specialist mounted archers developed only where Sicily can unite qualified riders, suitable horses, mounted-bow technique, compatible bows and arrows, officers, trainers, horse care, ammunition support, service terms, and sustained practice. The formation circles, shifts angle, looses at worthwhile targets, pressures exposed flanks and support troops, and forces enemies to change facing or formation.

The unit is not Kalbid Light Horsemen with bows. Mounted javelin service works at shorter range with different ammunition, contact, pursuit, and training demands. Khayyālat require a distinct mounted-bow intersection and retain separate spacing, fire-control, horse-conditioning, ammunition, withdrawal, and replacement burdens.

## 49. Comparative battle vignettes

### Vignette 1: pressure against a heavy flank
Khayyālat move along an open flank and loose against a heavy formation from changing angles. The goal is facing changes, shield redistribution, fatigue, disorder, and openings for Zardīyah or Fursān al-Amīr, not automatic casualties or rout.

### Vignette 2: mounted counter-skirmish
Enemy mounted skirmishers threaten the army’s flank. Khayyālat use bow range and mobile fire while preserving spacing and withdrawal. Kalbid Light Horsemen remain better suited to javelin exchange, rapid response, and closer pursuit.

### Vignette 3: protected missile counter
Enemy archers occupy protected ground. Counterfire, shielded targets, restricted lanes, arrow expenditure, and horse fatigue may make withdrawal or another target the correct choice.

### Vignette 4: cavalry interception failure
Khayyālat overextend while firing and fresh enemy cavalry closes the retreat corridor. The specialist formation should suffer heavily if horse condition, signals, spacing, support, or withdrawal fail.
