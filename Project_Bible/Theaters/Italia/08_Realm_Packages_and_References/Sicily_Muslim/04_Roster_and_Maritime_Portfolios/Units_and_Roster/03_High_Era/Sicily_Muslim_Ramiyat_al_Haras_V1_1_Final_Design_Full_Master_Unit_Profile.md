## 3. Player-facing description

Rāmiyāt al-Ḥaras are selected Sicilian Muslim archers maintained around the citadel, ruler, arsenal, gates, towers, and other protected centers of command. They represent the successful maturation of Sicily's foot-archery tradition into a narrow institution able to preserve veteran skill, inspect equipment, control ammunition, train replacements, and hold prepared stations under pressure.

The formation is not simply Rammāt Siqilliyya with higher statistics. Rāmiyāt al-Ḥaras draw from a smaller and more politically protected source, require stronger patronage and equipment support, carry a more demanding station-defense and command-protection duty, and recover more slowly from the loss of veterans, officers, bows, armor, and instructors.

On the battlefield, the unit selects targets carefully, counters enemy missiles, pressures exposed officers and valuable formations when visibility and doctrine allow, punishes troops moving through defended approaches, and protects the ruler's or commander's position. If battle reaches the station, selected protection and sidearms let the formation defend gates, stairs, towers, courtyards, mantlets, stores, or withdrawal points long enough to hold, rotate, or extract. The unit remains an archer formation, not medium sword infantry in disguise.

## 30A. Comparative battle vignettes

### Vignette 1: citadel approach defense

Rāmiyāt al-Ḥaras occupy prepared wall, tower, gate, and courtyard stations with inspected arrows, relief files, fallback points, and local frontage. The player holds fire until attackers enter valuable approach lanes, counters enemy missiles, rotates depleted files, and preserves a withdrawal into the inner position. If stores burn, signals fail, or a second breach bypasses the station, the unit must reposition or extract rather than receive an invisible citadel bonus.

### Vignette 2: prepared hill or crossing

The formation deploys behind Sicilian spear frontage on a defended bank or hill. Rāmiyāt pressure enemy missiles and selected advancing formations while Khaffaf or Rammāt provide broader volume. The elite formation succeeds through target priority, protected lanes, and ammunition control. If the spear line breaks or an open flank permits cavalry access, the correct response is passage, withdrawal, and reform.

### Vignette 3: counter-missile contest

Enemy archers begin damaging the army's approach. Rāmiyāt take a protected counter-missile posture, select worthwhile targets, rotate files, and conserve specialized lots. The exchange remains conditional on cover, observation, range, ammunition, and casualties. The card fails its design if elite status automatically defeats every enemy missile formation regardless of circumstances.

### Vignette 4: wrong-ground failure

The formation is ordered across exposed open ground without frontage or fallback. Cavalry threatens during relocation while counterfire disrupts spacing. Armor and sidearms may preserve some personnel during escape, but do not turn the unit into medium sword infantry or cavalry-resistant skirmishers. If Rāmiyāt win this situation routinely, their implementation is too strong.
