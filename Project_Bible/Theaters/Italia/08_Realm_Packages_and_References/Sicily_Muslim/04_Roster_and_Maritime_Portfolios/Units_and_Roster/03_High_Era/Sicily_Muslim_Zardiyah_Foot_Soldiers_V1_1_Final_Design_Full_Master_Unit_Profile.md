## 3. Player-facing description

Zardīyah Foot Soldiers are trained Sicilian Muslim axemen equipped for aggressive but controlled close fighting. Shields and maintained protection help the formation reach and survive the decisive local contest; axes give the unit a different contact rhythm from spearmen and swordsmen. The player should commit Zardīyah after missiles, cavalry, terrain, or ordinary frontage has checked the enemy, then use the formation to widen disorder and create a gap.

The unit is not a universal spear-line breaker. Fresh spears with room, cohesion, and protected flanks remain dangerous. Zardīyah succeed when timing, support, facing, fatigue, and withdrawal are managed. If allowed to chase too deeply after an initial success, the unit can become isolated, enveloped, exhausted, or destroyed.

## 46. Comparative battle vignettes

### Vignette 1: checked spear line
Friendly spearmen fix an enemy frontage while trained archers and slingers disrupt the nearest files. Zardīyah advance into the local fight, apply short axe pressure, open a gap, and reform. The unit succeeds through timing and combined arms, not an automatic spear-breaking trait.

### Vignette 2: gate interior relief
A friendly gate defense begins to buckle. Zardīyah move through a protected passage, execute a controlled local counterattack, stabilize the immediate interior, and hand the position back to holding infantry. The formation is not a wall-scaling or first-wave assault unit.

### Vignette 3: armored infantry contest
Zardīyah engage moderately protected foot already tired and disordered. Axes and shields provide useful pressure, but armor interaction remains contextual. Against fresh elite heavy infantry in an even prolonged fight, the medium formation should lose efficiency and require relief.

### Vignette 4: open-ground failure
Zardīyah pursue too far after a successful push. Enemy cavalry attacks the flank while missiles strike during reform. The correct implementation punishes the overextension despite the unit's armor and shields.
