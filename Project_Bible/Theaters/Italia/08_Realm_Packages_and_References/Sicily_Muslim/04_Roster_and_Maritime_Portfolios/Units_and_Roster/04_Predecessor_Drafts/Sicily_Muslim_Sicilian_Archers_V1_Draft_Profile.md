# PART I. HUMAN-READABLE CURRENT PROFILE

## 1. Unit identity strip

- **Unit:** Sicilian Archers
- **Realm:** Sicilian Emirates working package
- **Class:** center-bound foot missile infantry
- **Quality:** ordinary trained archery candidate with uneven provider and equipment quality
- **Source:** qualified, available, and unreserved foot archers contributed through validated center, urban, household, rural, strongpoint, or contracted access
- **Availability:** finite, provider-bound, ammunition-dependent, leadership-dependent, and politically uneven
- **Primary role:** sustained missile support and approach control
- **Best use:** behind defended frontage, beside secure flanks, around real strongpoints, and where clear firing lanes and withdrawal routes exist
- **Major weakness:** poor prolonged melee, cavalry exposure, ammunition depletion, disruption during movement, and dependence on protection and command
- **Development:** parent family may improve through provider, equipment, training, storage, and command development without automatically splitting into a superior second card
- **Opening State:** unknown and not initialized
- **Exact values:** none promoted

## 2. One-screen design card

### Battlefield

- Delivers sustained bow fire against exposed, advancing, disordered, or otherwise worthwhile targets.
- Shapes enemy approaches and supports defensive infantry rather than replacing the line.
- Rewards fire discipline, target selection, protected lanes, and planned withdrawal.
- Suffers when cavalry threatens the flank, visibility collapses, ammunition is wasted, or friendly troops obstruct fire.
- Remains a weak melee formation even when individual members carry sidearms or limited protection.

### Campaign

- Draws from real provider networks rather than a universal Sicilian Muslim population pool.
- Requires qualified shooters, bows, strings, arrows, quivers, leaders, practice, storage, transport, protection, and recovery.
- Competes with local wall and gate defense, strongpoint assignments, household service, commercial and craft activity, and future missile formations.
- Can remain available in some centers while unavailable in others.
- May persist after its original recruitment lane closes if actual formations, veterans, equipment, and support survive.

### Access

- A center, fortress, workshop, bow range, technology, or cultural label does not create archers.
- Access requires one accountable provider or an explicitly reconciled combination of contributors.
- External or contracted archers remain provider-owned unless a valid transfer changes the relationship.
- Strongpoint archery is an assignment of the parent family unless an independent population and role are proven.

### Development

- Training, fire control, ammunition discipline, equipment maintenance, and provider depth may improve unevenly.
- A selected specialization may emerge later only if it gains a distinct provider, training program, equipment architecture, replacement burden, battlefield role, and player decision.
- The family does not automatically become mounted archers, crossbowmen, guard archers, longbowmen, or incendiary troops.

## 3. Player-facing description

Sicilian Archers are qualified foot shooters drawn from the island's politically divided centers and provider networks. Protected by spearmen, urban defenders, strongpoints, terrain, or other real cover, the formation can shape an enemy approach, pressure exposed troops, contest opposing skirmishers, and support a controlled withdrawal.

The formation is not an unlimited national archer pool. Every unit requires practiced people, serviceable bows, usable strings, finite arrows, internal leaders, safe practice, protected assembly, transport, storage, and an authority able to release the required people from local duties. Pulling archers into a field army may weaken walls, gates, routes, households, strongpoints, or commercial centers.

On the battlefield, Sicilian Archers are most effective as part of a combined-arms position. The formation should conserve arrows when targets are poor, reposition before cavalry reaches contact, and withdraw behind friendly protection rather than pretending to be line infantry.

## 34. Melee and withdrawal boundary

Sidearms and limited protection permit emergency defense and withdrawal. They do not convert the formation into line infantry. The correct response to closing heavy infantry or cavalry is protected withdrawal, repositioning, passage behind friendly troops, or defense of a real nearby objective.

## 35. Counterplay

- Threaten or break the protective frontage.
- Use cavalry or mobile infantry to force early withdrawal.
- Outshoot with superior range, protection, cadence, or ammunition.
- Use shields, armor, cover, and dispersed movement.
- Force relocation through multiple threats.
- Block retreat lanes or attack during reform.
- Exhaust arrow stocks.
- Disrupt leaders, signals, or ammunition transport.
- Attack when the formation is isolated from its provider and support chain.
