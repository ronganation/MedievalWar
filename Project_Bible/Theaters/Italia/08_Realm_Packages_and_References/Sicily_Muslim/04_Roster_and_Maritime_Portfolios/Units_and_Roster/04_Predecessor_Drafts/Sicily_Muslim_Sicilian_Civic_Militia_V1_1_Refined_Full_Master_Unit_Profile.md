Sicilian Civic Militia are not a standing island-wide regiment, a free settlement garrison, or a permanent pool. The card appears only when a real center or provider can release eligible people from protected urban duties, issue compatible equipment, establish leadership and signals, sustain a bounded mission, and fund return and civic recovery.

The prior working name **Sicilian Urban Defenders** described the system accurately but blurred the distinction between the Early Era civic card and its later maintained garrison successor. Other developed faction portfolios separate those layers more clearly. <File>Aghmat Militia</File> is an Early Era civic militia foundation, while <File>Aghmat Garrison Infantry</File> is a High Era maintained urban and fortified family. <File>Norway Local Drengjar</File> and <File>Norway High Era Bymenn</File> likewise distinguish organized local settlement defense from later urban militia institutionalization. The Sicily branch now follows the same portfolio grammar without copying those factions' institutions.

**Sicilian Civic Militia** is therefore the Early Era player card. It represents recurring but finite and locally bounded civic service, emergency muster, and short defensive field support where a real provider and service cycle exist. **Sicilian Garrison Infantry** is the conditional High Era successor family. It requires maintained personnel, officers, stores, quarters, watch routines, relief schedules, prepared positions, repair, provision, sanitation, and replacement capacity. It is not created by date, settlement level, walls, or a building alone.

- Quality: uneven, locally trained, provider-dependent, and non-elite

- Development: civic organization can improve in the Early Era; a recurring town guard or maintained urban infantry family is a conditional High Era development, not a free upgrade
- Opening State: unknown and not initialized

## 3. Player-facing description

Sicilian Civic Militia are drawn temporarily from the civic services that keep the island's centers functioning. Watchmen, gate and wall personnel, quarter or neighborhood contributors, port and market security, strongpoint personnel, and other valid providers may assemble under recognized authority when a real threat justifies mobilization. They are not every resident with a weapon and they do not appear because a wall, city, or barracks exists.

On the battlefield, Sicilian Civic Militia protect a bounded objective. They can reinforce a gateway, hold a street or broad lane, protect Sicilian Archers, secure stores or a camp edge, cover a local withdrawal, or delay an enemy long enough for Sicilian Spearmen and other field formations to stabilize the fight. The formation should avoid open pursuit, prolonged maneuver, unsupported shock action, and distant campaigning.

Mobilization has an immediate civic price. Pulling people from gates, ports, markets, warehouses, water, records, fire response, warning, or public security weakens the same center the formation is meant to defend. Survivors must return, equipment must be accounted for, and damaged civic coverage must recover. Victory does not erase the cost.

The user-facing intuition is correct: this family naturally points toward urban and civic development as the campaign moves into the High Era. The progression is not that every Sicilian Urban Defender turns into a better unit because time passed. The progression is that some centers may develop stable civic institutions, recurring providers, maintained stores, regular inspection, recognized officers, drill schedules, relief systems, and longer service capacity. Where those inputs do not develop, the Early Era emergency expression persists, declines, localizes, or disappears.
