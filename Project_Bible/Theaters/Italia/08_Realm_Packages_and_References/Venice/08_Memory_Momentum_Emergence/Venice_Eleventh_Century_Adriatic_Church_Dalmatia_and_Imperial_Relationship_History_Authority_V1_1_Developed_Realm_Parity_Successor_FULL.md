## 1. Purpose, Scope, and Ownership

This authority reconciles the historical relationships that shaped Venice immediately before and at the January 1062 opening. Its subject is not every fact in Venetian history. Its subject is the interface system through which Venice exercised and contested authority across the lagoon, northern Adriatic, mainland approaches, church jurisdictions, and Byzantine-connected commercial world.

### 1.1 This authority owns

- Historical propositions about the character of Venice-facing external interfaces.
- The distinction among relationship types.
- Opening-safe, ReviewOnly, ExternalLock, and LaterHistoryExcluded dispositions.
- Historical chronology seeds and contradiction-routing requests.
- Handoffs to specialist historical, diplomatic, ecclesiastical, geographic, economic, military, Registry, and Opening State owners.

## 3. Historical Thesis

Venice in 1062 is best understood as a ducal lagoon polity whose authority is maritime, negotiated, documentary, ecclesiastically entangled, and geographically discontinuous. Venice possesses a durable political center and a strong capacity to connect sea routes, but relevance across the Adriatic cannot be translated into a smooth territorial state. The doge may claim, negotiate, protect, receive recognition, organize shipping, or intervene without owning every city, church, island, port, road, or resource implicated in the relationship.

The historical design question is therefore not whether Venice already commands an empire. The question is whether lagoon security, ducal legitimacy, church alignment, merchant access, fleet service, and local agreements can be converted into repeatable influence without confusing influence with ownership or importing later institutions backward.

## 8. Ecclesiastical Topology: Grado and Aquileia

The Grado-Aquileia conflict must be represented as a layered jurisdictional and political problem, not a two-color religious war. Grado and Aquileia are institutions with officeholders, suffragan relationships, properties, patrons, documentary claims, and external connections. Venice may support Grado strongly without owning the patriarchate or converting every ecclesiastical judgment into ducal policy.

## 9. Byzantium Interface
