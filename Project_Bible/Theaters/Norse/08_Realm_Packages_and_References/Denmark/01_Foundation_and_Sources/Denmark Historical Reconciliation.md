## 1. Verdict

Denmark enters the January 1062 scenario as a surviving, Christian, maritime kingdom under King Sweyn Estridsen, not as an extension of Norway and not as a centralized later-medieval monarchy.

The realm is strategically coherent but politically negotiated. Royal action depends upon regional acceptance, assemblies, landholding powers, fleet and levy contribution, church cooperation, and the crown's ability to move across Jutland, the islands, and the eastern Danish lands.

The prolonged conflict with Harald Hardrada is the dominant external pressure. The Battle of Nisa has not yet occurred at the January 1062 opening and must remain an emergent campaign possibility. The historical battle, its tactical details, the survival of Sweyn, and the later peace may inform pressure, AI, and counterfactual validation, but none is a forced runtime outcome.

Denmark's dynastic strength and danger are the same fact: Sweyn has a large and politically consequential offspring field, but succession is not automatic primogeniture and later kingship cannot be preloaded into any child.

The church is a structural pillar of the realm. The recent organization of Danish dioceses, the crown's gifts and patronage, the metropolitan authority of Hamburg-Bremen, and Sweyn's interest in stronger Danish ecclesiastical standing create strategic cooperation and institutional friction without reducing the conflict to nationality.

## 5. Realm and Crown Reconciliation

### Opening Realm Thesis

Denmark is a Christian maritime kingdom whose crown survives through negotiated recognition, regional support, church partnership, and control of movement between the North Sea and Baltic.

### Crown Strengths

- established reign since 1047;
- royal descent through Estrid and the older Danish royal line;
- demonstrated ability to survive repeated Norwegian pressure;
- broad dynasty and household network;
- cooperation with the organizing church;
- access to multiple regional assemblies and power bases;
- towns, mints, ports, crossings, and agricultural resources;
- strategic position between western and Baltic theaters.

### Crown Constraints

- succession is not secured by simple primogeniture;
- assemblies and regional powers matter to recognition and action;
- maritime war requires contributed ships, crews, provisions, and regional cooperation;
- a large offspring field can generate alternatives rather than guaranteed continuity;
- Hamburg-Bremen holds formal metropolitan authority;
- eastern and southern frontiers/interactions cannot be reduced to static borders;
- prior raids create recovery and security burdens.

### Governance Rule

Do not create one “Crown Authority” score. Represent:

- lawful kingship;
- recognition by assemblies;
- crown administrative reach;
- regional contribution capacity;
- political cooperation;
- church support and jurisdiction;
- military/fleet readiness;
- legitimacy narratives;
- policy performance.

## 6. House Estridsen Historical Legacy

### Dynastic Basis

House Estridsen takes its political identity from Sweyn's maternal descent through Estrid Svendsdatter, daughter of Sweyn Forkbeard and sibling of Cnut the Great. Ulf Jarl provides the paternal magnate and regency legacy. The dynasty's authority is therefore neither a simple agnatic continuation nor a new house detached from earlier Danish kingship.

### Historical-Legacy Records

#### `legacy.denmark.estrid_royal_continuity`

- effect domain: legitimacy narrative, church patronage memory, dynasty continuity;

- effect domain: crown-magnate caution, household memory, regional power narratives;
- later embellished stories remain evidence/narrative records.

#### `legacy.denmark.cnutian_north_sea_order`

- basis: living memory of the wider realm of Cnut the Great;

#### `legacy.denmark.sweyn_survived_exile_and_defeat`

Reliable founding ancestry should stop at evidence-supported persons. Legendary descent through Styrbjorn or other unsupported lines is prohibited from authoritative genealogy and may appear only as a later claim or quarantined tradition.
