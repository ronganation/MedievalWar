## 3. Realm Development Thesis

### Working Identity Formula

**A maritime Christian kingdom surviving through assemblies, regional landholding power, dynastic legitimacy, church construction, and control of the passages between the North Sea and Baltic.**

### Working Central Question

**Can the Danish crown convert survival against Norwegian pressure into durable kingship without breaking the assemblies, regional elites, church relationships, and maritime obligations that make the realm governable?**

### Strategic Character

Denmark should feel like a major Medieval II faction through:

- a recognizable crown and faction identity;
- Jutland, the Danish islands, and the eastern Danish lands as connected but distinct strategic spaces;
- cities, ports, crossings, sea lanes, assemblies, bishoprics, estates, and military gathering points;
- fleets and maritime movement as core strategic tools;
- armies, levies, regional support, and political settlement;
- competition with Norway and future balancing with Sweden;
- contact with England, Flanders, the Empire, Slavic/Baltic frontiers, and church authorities;
- succession and dynasty continuity as major long-term pressures;
- deep internal simulation that remains summary-first and campaign-map centered.

## 4. Grand Campaign and Alpha Separation

### Grand-Campaign Authority

The Denmark V1 design package must define the complete major-realm foundation required for later expansion.

### Alpha Implementation Subset

The first implemented slice may limit:

- active characters;
- authored settlements and routes;
- army and fleet detail;
- fully processed institutions;
- player-facing decisions;
- active historical pressure chains.

The Alpha subset may not:

- reduce Denmark to one ruler and one war;
- omit future expansion interfaces;
- invent temporary IDs or ownership;
- merge Jutland, islands, eastern lands, offices, church institutions, or dynasties into one object;
- encode later historical outcomes;
- create non-migratable saves or Alpha-only AI.

## 5. Opening-Date Research Questions

The Historical Reconciliation must resolve or explicitly quarantine:

- exact scenario opening relative to the 1062 campaign season and the Battle of Nisa;
- the opening state of the Danish-Norwegian war;
- the scope and strength of Sweyn Estridsen's kingship;
- the role of things/assemblies in recognition, law, levy, and political settlement;
- regional power in Jutland, Zealand, Funen, Scania, Halland, Blekinge, and other justified areas;
- the crown's itinerant centers and principal residences;
- the opening status of Roskilde, Lund, Ribe, Odense, Aarhus, Viborg, Schleswig/Hedeby, and other candidate strategic sites;
- church dioceses, bishops, metropolitan dependence, royal patronage, and contested ecclesiastical authority;
- the identities, ages, households, status, and political relevance of Sweyn's children and other dynasts;
- named regional magnates only where evidence supports opening participation;
- Danish relations with Norway, Sweden, England, Flanders, Saxony/Empire, Slavic neighbors, and the papal/Hamburg-Bremen church sphere;
- fleet mobilization, levy obligations, trade, ports, crossings, and maritime supply;
- evidence and narrative uncertainty surrounding the war and its participants.

## 11. Validation Gates

### Fatal

- duplicate Norway-owned person or institution;
- later epithet, title, sainthood, death, office, marriage, or victory loaded at opening;
- one object used as person, title, office, dynasty, institution, settlement, or realm;
- Norwegian claim treated as ownership;
- kinship treated as loyalty;
- assembly recognition treated as unlimited crown authority;
- church office and institution merged;
- exact chronology invented from uncertain evidence;
- a major realm reduced to a single ruler object;
- static definitions containing mutable war, holder, relationship, or succession outcomes;
- noncanonical IDs written to new saves.

### Warning

- major region has no political or military interface;
- crown has no regional dependencies;
- fleet power exists without ports, crews, obligations, routes, or supply;
- church content has no recognition or jurisdiction model;
- succession field is roster inflation rather than strategic content;
- Denmark AI has only one scripted war path;
- Alpha subset omits required grand-campaign migration or extension points.

## 12. Save, Migration, and Determinism

- New saves write canonical IDs only.
- Opening State initializes once.
- Every cross-realm lock records the owning registry.
- Review and quarantine states cannot instantiate authoritative actors.
- Later Denmark package versions require explicit migration.
- Process by canonical ID, record type, effective date, stable event/command ID, and content version.
- Identical state, inputs, version, and seed must produce identical outcomes.

## 15. Completion Gates

### Static package gate
All authorities parse, IDs resolve or remain explicit review, ownership is unique, hashes close, schemas validate, and preflight creates zero runtime state.

### Compile gate
UE5 modules, structs, DataAssets, generated headers, and importer bindings compile with retained logs.

### Runtime gate
Opening State applies once, all Network activation gates behave correctly, external reverse directions remain absent, AI proposes commands only, and owners validate mutations.

### Save and determinism gate
Advanced saves preserve divergence, migrations use canonical IDs, equal replay produces equal hashes and traces, and revised defaults never overwrite progressed state.
