## 1. Concise Verdict

Denmark's AI should behave as the strategic leadership of a negotiated Christian maritime kingdom, not as an omniscient national actor and not as a script that reproduces later history.

The realm personality should prioritize recognized kingship, regional and assembly support, maritime readiness, household continuity, church cooperation, dynastic resilience, route access, credible defense, and selective external opportunity. Denmark may act boldly when authority, information, resources, access, and support align, but should prefer negotiation, staged commitments, or bounded delegation when those foundations are weak.

Core rule:

**Realm personality shapes evaluation and proposal order. Realm personality does not own facts, relationships, resources, offices, forces, commands, or outcomes. AI proposes commands. Specialist owners validate and execute.**

## 4. Realm Personality Thesis

Denmark should present a coherent but conditional strategic character:

- **Maritime:** sea access, fleet contribution, ports, crossings, and transport matter.
- **Negotiated:** Crown action depends on assemblies, regional support, relationships, communication, and enforceable mandates.
- **Dynastic:** House Estridsen provides continuity and competition without a fixed heir.
- **Christian and institution-building:** church cooperation, diocesan structure, patronage, and metropolitan tension matter.
- **Regionally distributed:** Jutland, the islands, and the eastern Danish lands cannot be treated as one frictionless space.
- **Externally alert:** Norway, Hamburg-Bremen, the southern Baltic, and wider northern politics create simultaneous pressures.
- **Pragmatic:** Denmark should avoid expensive commitments without routes, supply, contributions, and political backing.
- **Capable of consolidation:** repeated successful governance may strengthen authority, but consolidation is an outcome rather than an opening assumption.

## 6. Evaluation Principles

### 6.1 Use current owner state

AI evaluates only owner-approved data available to the acting authority.

### 6.2 Separate capability from preference

A preferred action remains unavailable when authority, resources, access, knowledge, command, or validation is absent.

### 6.3 Preserve uncertainty

Unknown is not zero. AI may seek information, delay commitment, choose a reversible proposal, or decline to evaluate.

### 6.4 Avoid historical hindsight

Later success, failure, kingship, sainthood, death, reform, and peace cannot influence opening or runtime proposals before they are known in simulation.

### 6.5 Prefer explainable proposals

Every ranked proposal carries visible strategic reasons, blockers, risks, prerequisites, and expected owner commands.

### 6.6 Allow strategic adaptation

The same realm personality may produce different choices under different rulers, relationships, resources, threats, routes, institutions, and campaign histories.

## 7. Core Strategic Priorities

### Priority A: Preserve recognized kingship

Protect the ruler's ability to communicate, travel, summon, appoint, delegate, negotiate, and command.

### Priority B: Maintain regional and assembly support

Address issue-specific support rather than assume permanent obedience. Prefer consultation when enforcement is weak or legitimacy costs are high.

### Priority C: Preserve maritime capability

Maintain access to ships, crews, ports, crossings, routes, supply, and commanders before committing to major maritime operations.

### Priority D: Contain Norway-facing danger

Monitor claim, war, readiness, intelligence, routes, allies, contributions, and diplomatic options. Do not force the historical battle or peace.

### Priority E: Protect dynastic continuity

Maintain multiple viable continuity paths without preselecting the later historical successor.

### Priority F: Sustain church cooperation

Support viable Danish institutions, protect valid office processes, and manage Hamburg-Bremen relations without assuming Crown ownership of the church.

### Priority G: Avoid simultaneous overextension

Limit concurrent major commitments when governance, information, treasury, fleet, army, supply, household, or regional support is insufficient.

### Priority H: Use selective opportunity

Exploit southern Baltic, ecclesiastical, diplomatic, dynastic, or regional opportunities only when the opportunity is known, legal, affordable, and strategically connected.

## 8. Strategic Restraints

Denmark AI should resist:

- unsupported centralization;
- universal taxation or contribution assumptions;
- automatic military or fleet availability;
- simultaneous major war, church, succession, and regional campaigns without capacity;
- assigning offices or commands by kinship alone;
- treating marriage as automatic alliance;
- treating bishops as Crown officials;
- treating assemblies as permanent approval sources;
- duplicating external people or reverse relationships;
- using private or unknown information;
- deterministic pursuit of later history.

## 15. Crown and Governance Behavior

The Crown-facing AI should:

- distinguish recognition from enforceable control;
- seek assembly or regional support for high-cost actions;
- use mandates with clear scope and reporting;
- select delegates by eligibility, access, capability, relationships, and current readiness;
- avoid assigning permanent authority through temporary crisis;
- review delayed or partial compliance before interpreting disloyalty;
- preserve communication and itinerary access;
- balance publicity, consultation, and enforcement.

The AI should not create an office, mandate, contribution, or obedience record directly.

## 16. Regional and Assembly Behavior

Regional strategy is issue-specific.

A region may:

- support defense but resist extraordinary contribution;
- recognize the ruler but dispute an officeholder;
- attend an assembly but oppose a proposal;
- provide partial fleet or levy support;
- delay because of route, season, capacity, or local danger;
- seek conditions or compensation.

Assembly AI should evaluate competent subject matter, participant access, custom, enforcement, regional interests, and consequences. One approval cannot become a permanent realm modifier.

## 23. Crisis Postures

### Ruler incapacity or death

Prioritize continuity, communication, household security, title and succession process, assembly and regional recognition, force control, and prevention of unsupported office transfer.

### Regional refusal

Diagnose authority, communication, capacity, local threat, relationship, custom, and enforcement before escalation.

### Fleet or army loss

Prioritize recovery, defense, contribution review, supply, command accountability, and diplomatic alternatives.

### Church vacancy or dispute

Preserve institution continuity, separate office from holder, use valid process, and avoid placeholder people.

### External identity failure

Quarantine dependent proposals only. Do not block unrelated Denmark strategy.

### Succession pressure

Evaluate multiple viable paths. Do not select the later historical ruler by hindsight.

---

## 24. Opportunity Postures

AI may pursue opportunity when:

- the opportunity is known;
- authority and owner routes exist;
- prerequisites are satisfied;
- costs and opportunity costs are visible;
- regional, household, church, military, or maritime capacity is adequate;
- the action does not violate a higher critical priority;
- the commitment is appropriately reversible for the uncertainty level.

Opportunity does not override existential defense, ruler continuity, or severe internal fragmentation without explicit policy.

## 36. Validation Catalogue

### Fatal

- AI directly mutates owner state;
- AI reads hidden or owner-inaccessible information;
- realm personality stores mutable facts;
- identity, office, institution, household, relationship, region, army, fleet, route, or resource types are merged;
- review or quarantine identity enters routine AI;
- external person is duplicated;
- external reverse direction becomes Denmark-owned;
- Eric receives adult strategy or command agency;
- Sigrid bypasses lifecycle, marriage, household, location, route, chronology, or external gates;
- William bypasses Roskilde occupancy;
- later kingship, sainthood, death, Nisa, peace, or archbishopric influences an earlier proposal;
- preference bypasses authority, legality, resources, access, or owner validation;
- unknown becomes zero;
- advanced save or player policy is overwritten by revised defaults;
- proposal ranking is nondeterministic;
- compile or runtime status is claimed without evidence.

### Warning

- Denmark behaves identically under all rulers and campaign states;
- all regions and assemblies respond identically;
- every Norway signal produces escalation;
- maritime preference ignores fleet, route, contribution, and supply state;
- dynasty preference selects the historical successor;
- church strategy becomes Crown control;
- proposals lack reasons or alternatives;
- too many low-value prompts reach the player;
- AI repeatedly proposes blocked actions without seeking prerequisites.

### Static acceptance tests

- every priority and restraint has owner-facing inputs;
- all proposal classes route to specialist commands;
- no owner state appears in personality definitions;
- the 20-direction Network surface is consumed without duplication;
- external identities retain owners;
- underage, office, household, route, and chronology gates remain intact;
- dry evaluation produces zero runtime mutations.

### Runtime scenarios

- Norway threat rises while fleet readiness is low, producing preparation or negotiation rather than automatic attack;
- assembly support is missing, producing consultation or a bounded alternative;
- a route closes, changing proposal rank deterministically;
- William loses Roskilde occupancy, suppressing office-dependent proposals;
- Gottschalk remains unresolved, blocking only dependent southern Baltic proposals;
- Sweyn dies in a divergent campaign, producing continuity proposals without selecting the historical successor;
- player policy favors caution, changing valid proposal order without legalizing blocked actions;
- save/load preserves policy, pending proposals, cooldowns, and deterministic replay;
- equal replay produces equal hashes and traces.

## 37. Risks and Mitigations

### Risk: personality becomes a stereotype

Mitigation: personality shapes priorities but current ruler, relationships, information, resources, threats, and player policy determine proposals.

### Risk: AI recreates history

Mitigation: forbid future facts and validate proposal provenance against simulation date.

### Risk: hidden omniscience

Mitigation: knowledge scopes, message provenance, and information-confidence reasons.

### Risk: direct mutation bypasses systems

Mitigation: proposal-only outputs and owner validation.

### Risk: repetitive behavior

Mitigation: crisis posture, opportunity posture, strategic focus, cooldown, reversibility, and overextension checks.

### Risk: excessive CPU cost

Mitigation: event-driven summaries, bounded candidates, stable caching, and no routine full-world scan.
