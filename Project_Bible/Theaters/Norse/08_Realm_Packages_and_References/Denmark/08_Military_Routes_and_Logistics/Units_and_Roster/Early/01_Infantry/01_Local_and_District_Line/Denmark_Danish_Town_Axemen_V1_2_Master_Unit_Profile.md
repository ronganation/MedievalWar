## 3. Player-Facing Description

Danish Town Axemen are community fighting men assembled for a recognizable local defense duty. They bring usable one-handed axes, shields where supplied, basic protection, and enough familiar leadership to act as a company rather than a crowd. Their equipment varies with the actual source and its stores.

Use them where the frontage is manageable and the purpose is clear. They can defend an approach, protect bowmen, reinforce a gate, or counterattack ordinary light troops after a stronger line contains the threat. They can also accompany a nearby army for a supported short task; local does not mean incapable of leaving a settlement tile.

Their limits become clear on an exposed battlefield or a long expedition. Cavalry, intact spears, concentrated missiles, stronger infantry, and exhaustion can break them. Preserve a rally point and a route back. Losing the company also costs the community defenders, leaders, equipment, and future recovery.


### 3A. Stronger Playable Militia Promise

**Recruit these axemen to win an ordinary local infantry fight, not merely to occupy a gate until better troops arrive.** A serviceable company can receive comparable light foot, protect a firing position, advance a short distance into a viable local contest, and counterattack an overextended enemy before that enemy is routing. Its practical value is affordable close combat with basic organization and meaningful shields.

Local does not mean permanently immobile. Nearby field support and camp defense remain normal useful applications when the source and support permit. The limits are sustained offensive practice, exposed approach, broad frontage, fatigue, and long-service burden, not a compulsory inability to fight outside streets. The company can stay in a useful supported melee until the local task is settled; no tiny scripted attack burst or mandatory cycle-charge loop is imposed.

The player decision is whether ordinary community infantry can handle this sector while selected axemen and the Hird remain available for harder tasks. A weak source can produce a degraded company; it must not define every healthy company as helpless. No universal victory against another faction's militia is implied.

## 15. Frontage and Formation Geometry

Use a manageable local frontage, not the width expected of a broad spear line. Streets, yards, gates, stores, and camps can constrain approach and reduce the ground to cover, but also crowd movement, expose missiles, and block retreat.

Understrength veterans should shorten frontage. Experience cannot cover missing bodies. Excessive depth crowds axes and shields; excessive width weakens mutual support and leader reach. The unit should feel organized but less dependable in repeated turning or passage than selected field troops.

Proposed postures: route column, ordinary local line, short defensive frontage, supported reserve, loose movement, short counterattack, and withdrawal/reform. No pike block, spear brace, permanent shieldwall bonus, or instant all-round defense. Orders acknowledge immediately; execution visibly depends on condition and space.

## 16. Signature Battle Cycle

1. Gather around an actual local objective and recognized leader.
2. Establish a manageable frontage with supporting troops, obstacles, or a clear fallback.
3. Guard the approach, store, firing position, camp, or access point.
4. Receive comparable light foot or support a stronger line under ordinary pressure.
5. Counterattack a local overextension or disrupted target when support and exit remain viable.
6. Stop after recovering useful ground or containing the threat.
7. Regroup behind support or at a known rally point before fatigue and flank pressure isolate the company.

Gather, Guard, Counter, Regroup is a design mnemonic, not an attested Danish drill. Familiar movement, attack, halt, and withdrawal controls carry it. The player does not need a separate button for each verb.


## 17. Close Combat and Counterattack

Town Axemen should fight comparable ordinary light foot credibly when assembled and supported. They can hold a short sector or make a useful local attack before the opponent is routing. Lower tier does not mean incapable of winning any contact.

A short counterattack can clear immediate pressure, recover a yard, protect bowmen, or help contain a penetration. It is not the planned sustained offensive task of Selected Danish Axemen. A fresh intact spear frontage or stronger household infantry can stop and punish it.

Shield protection is directional and changes during attacks, turning, and disorder. Axe contact does not guarantee shield destruction or armor penetration. Sustained unsupported melee, repeated rushes, and deep pursuit waste limited endurance and expose the source's ordinary defenders.


## 18. Relief, Withdrawal, and Pursuit

Relief needs actual space and a viable handoff. A fresh company cannot pass through engaged spearmen without congestion. Friendly bowmen may need to stop or redirect fire. Internal rotation is not a default professional countermarch.

Withdrawal can fail when pinned, surrounded, or blocked. Support, suppression, a reserve, or a costly retreat may be needed. Regroup requires surviving leaders, time, space, and reduced pressure. A rally click does not restore missing shields or lost experience.

Short follow-up can secure an immediate objective after a genuine break. Deep pursuit is poor normal use. Halt when the enemy reforms, reserves appear, cavalry threatens, fatigue rises, or the route back becomes unsafe. Saving the company and local objective can be a successful result without a large kill count.

## 20. Morale, Cohesion, and Fatigue

Familiar leaders, repeated local assembly, a clear purpose, support, and a known rally point can help cohesion. Home defense does not grant a fixed morale aura or immunity. The same concern for homes can make an exposed flank or blocked retreat more consequential.

Rushed assembly, incompatible equipment, prolonged service, hunger, missile losses, leader loss, fire, cavalry, and isolation can degrade control. No automatic national cowardice, heroic last stand, or guaranteed rout is prescribed.

Close fighting, shield posture, marching, slopes, poor footing, and repeated turning consume stamina. Practice helps pacing but not infinite endurance. Fatigue should visibly reduce useful counterattack and reform. Rest can improve condition; it cannot replace people or repair all kit.


## 21. Danish Combined-Arms Applications

**Settlement defense:** spears secure a mounted approach where available; town axemen hold a secondary street or yard; bowmen cover usable sectors; selected troops or the Hird remain a more dependable reserve. Walls and gates exist only where actual site state supports them.

**Nearby field battle:** keep town axemen on a protected secondary sector or behind the main frontage. They can protect a firing position or counterattack exposed light foot without pretending to be the army's assault core.

**Selected assault:** Town Axemen handle ordinary local protection while Selected Danish Axemen concentrate on the planned attack. Similar axes do not make their service and battle decisions identical.

**Mounted crisis:** spears and Mounted Retainers answer the serious mounted threat. Town Axemen do not inherit brace or countercharge because they stand nearby.

**Withdrawal:** use a bounded local stand or counterattack to buy movement, then withdraw toward support. Do not make destruction of the militia the default requirement for success.

## 23. Garrison, Siege, and Maritime Limits

Possible garrison support reflects actual stationing, accommodation, supply, and duty. Shared rules may allow reduced or free upkeep; exact slots and eligibility remain unresolved. Such support does not create a unit, refill casualties, or erase source reservation.

Useful siege roles include gate or yard defense, stores and camp protection, reserve behind a secured breach, and local withdrawal cover. The card is not a siege engineer, fire-resistant troop, ladder specialist, or automatic breach winner. Ordinary axes do not create demolition capacity.

Embarkation needs real ships, crews, lift, supply, landing, and return. Coastal origin does not grant seamanship or boarding mastery. The same person cannot be counted as crew and a full-strength land fighter simultaneously. Long overseas service is a supported extension, not the baseline.


### 23A. Prepared Defense Without Custody or Free Engineering

Town Axemen may reinforce an actual gate, yard, wall-access route, camp entrance, or internal fallback. Their local knowledge does not give them keys, permanent post responsibility, siege machinery, free barricades, or trained archers. A continuing custody source remains separate from the community field company.

A threatened entrance is not the only possible danger. Fire, smoke, blocked lanes, missiles from another angle, and simultaneous approaches can make a compact defense untenable. Moving the whole company to one gate may leave another ordinary approach exposed. Relief and fallback must use real available space; a fresh reserve does not teleport through engaged files.

On an offensive siege, guarding stores, camps, access, or captured ground is useful. Supported follow-through after stronger troops secure entry may be possible. First-wave ladder or prepared-breach assault remains poor normal use. Coastal origin provides no automatic boarding, landing, or crew skill.


## 24. Service Endurance and Danish Campaign Choices

The ordinary service scope is settlement defense, immediate local response, or a short regional contribution. Duration and distance are not assigned exact values. A supported town company can serve beyond its origin when actual terms, supply, command, and return permit.

Delay, source labor, harvest demand, local threat, and depleted support can make an extension costly. The player can release contributors, arrange relief, support continued service, or narrow the operation. These are proposed shared campaign choices, not implemented unit-specific controls.

The Canute example discussed earlier motivates the distinction between successful assembly and enduring service. This profile does not recreate a historical revolt, make Danish troops inherently unreliable, or remove the unit during battle because a seasonal condition changed. Refusal, inability, delay, and disputed departure remain different outcomes.


## 25. Cost, Availability, and Fun

The proposed card should be accessible enough to build meaningful local defenses and useful small armies. It is not an elite recruitment rarity. Healthy communities with equipment and leadership should not face arbitrary extreme cooldowns.

Its intended direct equipment and training burden is lower than Selected Danish Axemen, but local labor, defense, care, and recovery still matter. Exact price, upkeep, recruitment time, and free-upkeep rules remain unresolved. Lower gold cost does not make losses inconsequential.

Finite source-informed pools prevent infinite militia spam. Multiple companies can exist where actual capacity permits. One defeat should not permanently destroy settlement military play; recovery can be practical when survivors, replacements, kit, leaders, and support are healthy. Explain the dominant blocker rather than demand an exhaustive receipt trail from the player.

### 26A. Source Loss, Displacement, and Return

Losing or occupying the source may close recruitment, disrupt return, scatter contributors, capture stores, or require support elsewhere. It does not automatically delete every surviving field company, erase its experience, or transfer its loyalty and equipment to the conqueror. A surviving company can remain temporarily in service if command and support exist, while replacement remains limited or closed.

Retaking a settlement restores access for evaluation, not full people, equipment, cooperation, or readiness. Displaced survivors may return, remain elsewhere, join a compatible source under actual terms, or demobilize. Any transfer consumes the same real people and kit rather than generating a duplicate pool.

## 27. Development Without Forced Promotion

Better stores, repairs, leadership, local practice, equipment consistency, and survivor continuity can improve the same local family. Buildings and decisions enable support; they do not generate people, kit, or veterans.

Selected Danish Axemen are not a compulsory upgrade. A qualified individual may enter a selected source after actual release, equipment, training, and service review; that reduces the original availability. The whole militia does not become selected merely because it survives a battle.

Later civic military development may justify a new formation or a stronger expression, but no High Era work is initiated here. ## 28. Campaign and Battle AI

Recruit when a valid local source exists and the mission needs ordinary settlement defense or short support. Consider watch coverage, source recovery, equipment, leaders, and the need for spears or selected troops. Do not replace every field unit with cheap militia or empty a settlement's essential duties.

In battle, defend manageable frontage, protect support, counterattack a real local opening, and preserve a fallback. The AI must not hold a fresh company idle through a decisive local threat, nor rush it into fresh heavy infantry because it carries axes.

Recognize cavalry, missiles, flank exposure, fatigue, blocked withdrawal, and command loss. Higher difficulty does not create free equipment, morale, recruitment, or recovery. All AI behavior here is proposed, not tested.


### 28A. AI Commitment and Source Preservation

Campaign AI compares the local mission against the cost to watch, labor, source recovery, and remaining defense. It should recruit a useful company when ordinary local melee is needed, not only after all selected troops are unavailable. It should avoid making militia the entire open-field army when cavalry, reach, missiles, or campaign endurance demand other families.

Battle AI holds a manageable sector, protects missile access, recognizes a viable local counterattack, and commits before the opportunity disappears. It must not conserve fresh militia until the objective is lost. It must also avoid repeated attacks into intact spears, chasing mobile skirmishers, or treating the axe label as permission for heavy assault.

Failed passage, new cavalry pressure, concentrated missiles, leader loss, exhaustion, or a blocked fallback should trigger reassessment. A necessary stand may remain the least bad choice; withdrawal is not always physically possible. No difficulty-only free equipment, hidden shield coverage, instant rally, or extra source capacity is proposed.
