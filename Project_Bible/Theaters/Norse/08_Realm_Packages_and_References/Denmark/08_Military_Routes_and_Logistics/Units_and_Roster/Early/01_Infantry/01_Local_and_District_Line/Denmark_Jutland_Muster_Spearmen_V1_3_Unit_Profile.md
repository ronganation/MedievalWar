Jutland Muster Spearmen are Denmark's broad Jutland regional line infantry. ## 3. One-Screen Player Contract

### Battlefield

- Assemble into usable frontage before entering decisive contact.
- Anchor a defensive sector with spear points, shields, depth, and local leadership.
- Endure ordinary pressure while bowmen and aggressive infantry perform their own roles.
- Deter cavalry only when facing, cohesion, footing, and spear readiness are intact.
- Relieve or withdraw before fatigue, flank exposure, or equipment disorder destroys the line.

### Campaign

- Draw the formation from valid regional contributors rather than a universal Danish pool.
- Accept assembly time, local-defense loss, labor loss, maritime competition, and political friction.
- Replenish from the original or separately compatible Danish source.
- Demobilize when continued field service costs the source more than the army gains.

### Player warning

A promised muster is not a deployed unit. The line exists only after people, equipment, leaders, assembly, route, reception, and readiness all succeed.

## 4. Player-Facing Card

### Short description

Jutland spear-and-shield infantry who provide dependable western Danish frontage and dependable defense when properly assembled, led, and supported.

### Encyclopedia description

Jutland supplies Denmark with the western peninsula's regional communities, landed and household contributors, roads, crossings, coastal approaches, and assembly relationships. Jutland Muster Spearmen represent valid contributors from that regional source gathered into a practical field formation, with Aarhus serving as a recognizable campaign-facing concentration point where actual settlement, route, and authority conditions permit. Their spears and shields make them suitable for defending crossings, coastal approaches, camps, roads, settlement access, and the ordinary battle line.

They are not a permanent national army. Every formation depends on actual people, equipment, leaders, compliance, routes, supply, and time to assemble. Some contributions arrive ready and experienced; others require reorganization before battle. Properly concentrated, the spearmen can protect bowmen, steady a vulnerable sector, and resist cavalry approaching from the front. Disordered, tired, flanked, or divided, they are ordinary local fighters far from the conditions that make a spear line effective.

### 14A. Frontage, Depth, and Understrength Footprint

Match frontage to actual strength, spear coverage, leadership, ground, and support. Stretching a depleted unit across its original width creates gaps; shortening it preserves density but leaves more ground for neighbors to cover. Deepening can improve local reception while reducing coverage and increasing turning or congestion costs.

Understrength veterans may retain better judgment but cannot cover missing ground through experience. A full replacement body may recover numbers before familiar files and timing. Show these differences through frontage, cohesion, fatigue, and reform rather than a hidden all-stat penalty.

A narrow approach can reduce the burden of holding width, but also traps retreat, exposes dense targets to missiles, and risks congestion. Crossing defense is a useful role, not automatic superiority.

### 24A. Brace, Receive, and Counterpush Sequence

The proposed useful sequence is: face the approach, dress the line, ready spears and shields, receive contact, hold or shorten the sector, and counterpush only when the enemy is disrupted and support remains viable.

Brace requires warning and actual formation preparation. A unit that is turning, moving quickly, crossing difficult ground, or already broken cannot gain full reception simply because the player clicked Brace. Basic orders still acknowledge immediately; the visible formation takes time to execute them.

Spear points, density, facing, cohesion, ground, and the attacking cavalry's own condition create deterrence. No automatic damage reflection, charge cancellation, cavalry immunity, or all-round anti-mounted tag is added. Flank or rear contact remains dangerous even while the front is prepared.

### 34A. Reconciliation with the Refined Danish Roster

**Spears and District Bowmen:** establish useful frontage and a clear firing or passage plan. Bowmen stop or redirect fire when friendly movement masks the lane. Spears do not confer a passive protection aura.

**Spears and Hirdmen:** the muster owns broad holding contact; the Hird owns scarce shielded close-combat intervention. Preserve a usable approach and rally route. A Hird counterattack does not automatically restore the spear unit's lost leaders, equipment, or cohesion.

**Spears and Mounted Retainers:** the refined mounted card is a higher-quality household countercharge and melee reserve, not merely light pursuit cavalry. Spears provide a stable reception reference; retainers answer a mounted flank crisis or strike a suitable exposed sector. Both require actual separate personnel and equipment.

**Spears and selected assault foot:** the muster fixes or contains ordinary contact while a separately justified aggressive unit attacks a chosen point. No unverified axeman opening card is created by this relationship.

**Domestic and hired spears:** overlapping battlefield roles are acceptable. Actual ready-company access, hire terms, source burden, and recovery distinguish the campaign choice. Domestic service is not always cheaper overall, and a mercenary label does not guarantee immediate arrival.

### 41A. Layered Recovery and Reassembly

Separate killed, wounded, missing, captured, dispersed, early-returned, and fit survivors. Missing is not dead; captured is not available. Equipment returned without people restores property, not a fighter. People returned without usable spears and shields do not restore the original line standard.

Recovery includes health, equipment, surviving leaders, formation memory, household labor, local defense, route access, and service confidence. Reassembly can draw on survivors and compatible new contributors, but new numbers do not instantly restore veteran collective timing.

A valid reduced-strength veteran formation may persist while new recruitment is blocked. Do not erase it on an era change or restore its pool merely because the calendar advances. Depletion, equipment shortage, service refusal, or institutional change can close recruitment without automatically deleting surviving units.

### 62A. Relief, Internal Rotation, and Withdrawal Limits

The normal player command is relief, reform, or withdrawal at unit scale. Internal file rotation is a conditional future behavior requiring room, signals, suitable contact pressure, and practiced personnel; it is not a default professional countermarch or an individually managed subsystem.

Relief requires a real lane and handoff. A fresh formation cannot pass through an engaged one without congestion or risk. Narrow the tired frontage, stabilize the contact, move the replacement into a viable sector, and withdraw only when the actual battle permits. These are design criteria, not a claimed implemented procedure.

Breaking contact can fail. Spears may need friendly pressure, missile suppression, a reserve, or a costly retreat to escape. Reform requires surviving leaders, space, time, and reduced pressure; neither a rally click nor the Hird's presence guarantees recovery.

Refuse pursuit means preserve the line by default. A short local follow-up may recover ground after a genuine break when facing, support, and recall remain viable. It must stop before it becomes deep pursuit or shock assault.
