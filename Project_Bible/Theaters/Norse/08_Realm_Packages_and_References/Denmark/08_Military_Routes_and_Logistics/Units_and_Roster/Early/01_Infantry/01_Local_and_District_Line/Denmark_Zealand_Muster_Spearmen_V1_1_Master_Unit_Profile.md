## 4. Player-Facing Description

Zealand Muster Spearmen are regional contributors assembled from valid Zealand sources into practical spear-and-shield infantry. They protect bowmen, settlements, routes, stores, assembly points, and exposed approaches while giving Denmark another source of ordinary line infantry beyond Jutland.

The unit's distinction is not a magical island bonus. Zealand's compact geography and water boundaries create a different campaign problem: contributors can concentrate within the island only where actual routes, command, and assembly work, while deployment elsewhere may require secure crossings, ships, crews, ports, landing capacity, supply, and a return plan. Taking the company away can expose the same island sources that support royal movement, local defense, transport, and recovery.

In battle, use the formation as ordinary spear frontage. Maintain facing, protect the flanks, manage fatigue, and keep a relief or withdrawal route. The spearmen can receive ordinary pressure and deter a careless mounted approach when prepared. They should not become heavier or more professional merely to differ from Jutland.

## 6. Why the Card May Deserve to Exist

Denmark's current three-card local and district folder has a genuine structure:

- Community Levy: lowest-pool mixed-weapon task infantry.
- Town Axemen: organized settlement axe militia.
- Jutland Muster Spearmen: Jutland regional field spear frontage.

Zealand Muster Spearmen add a possible second regional source rather than another quality tier. The card may broaden Denmark's playable field strength while making regional depletion matter. It gives the player a choice between concentrating Jutland's peninsula contribution and drawing upon Zealand's island communities, routes, transport interfaces, and home-defense capacity.

The card is not justified by folder symmetry, Norway's four-unit count, a desire for another spear skin, or a blanket claim that capital-region troops are better.

## 19. Formation Geometry

The formation uses moderate frontage and depth with enough spacing for spear projection and shield cooperation. It should look like ordinary regional infantry, not an elite compact guard or an unorganized crowd.

Regional subgroups may remain visually present, but battlefield command must integrate them sufficiently to function as one unit.

## 22. Spear Reception and Cavalry Boundary

Frontal mounted deterrence exists only when:

- the threat is seen;
- the unit faces it;
- spears remain serviceable;
- depth and cohesion remain intact;
- ground supports the formation;
- fatigue remains manageable;
- flanks are protected.

Turning, movement, missiles, broken files, soft ground, leader loss, flanking, or rear contact can remove the advantage. No cavalry immunity, reflected damage, automatic charge cancellation, or all-round brace is authorized.

The receive should have a visible sequence: identify the approach, face the threat, dress files, settle spear points and shields, receive or deny contact, close gaps, then either hold, shorten frontage, counterpush locally, or seek relief. A late turn or interrupted deployment should not receive the same result as a settled line.

## 23. Infantry Combat and Counterpush

Zealand Muster Spearmen can fight ordinary infantry on controlled frontage and can advance methodically when the army must take limited ground. They are not restricted to passive delay.

The preferred offensive use is a short ordered advance or counterpush after an enemy attack loses cohesion. Push far enough to recover a route, firing position, landing perimeter, or damaged sector, then halt and redress.

Swordsmen or axemen become more dangerous after entering broken spear files. Selected assault infantry, Huskarle, household troops, missiles, and cavalry retain separate roles.

The unit may initiate ordinary contact against comparable foot when the approach is controlled and support remains present. It should not need the enemy to be routing before receiving an attack order. Its offensive ceiling remains lower than Landsmaend, Selected Danish Axemen, Huskarle, and Hirdmen because spear geometry, mixed preparation, and regional service are optimized for frontage rather than breakthrough.

## 24. Friendly Passage, Relief, and Withdrawal

Relief requires a real lane and an actual handoff. A fresh unit cannot phase through engaged files. Narrow ground, baggage, bowmen, frightened contributors, and damaged subgroups can cause congestion.

Zealand Spearmen can open passage for Hirdmen, Landsmaend, Selected Axemen, or a fresh spear unit when space, control, and pressure allow. Withdrawal may need missile suppression, reserve action, or a costly covered retreat.

Rally requires leaders, space, time, and reduced pressure. A rally order does not restore lost spears, shields, people, or morale.

A relief can fail through excessive frontage, an enemy surge, blocked lanes, bowmen occupying the passage, baggage, a damaged landing perimeter, leader loss, or panic among withdrawing files. If relief fails, both formations may become congested and vulnerable. This is a physical battle risk, not an abstract permission gate.

### 27A. The Distinct Zealand Campaign Choice

The decision is regional force allocation, not a scripted bonus. The player should be able to compare **ready source, arrival burden, home exposure, and recovery** directly between Jutland and Zealand.

## 29. Settlement, Camp, Siege, and Strongpoint Use

Useful assignments include settlement entrances, broad streets, camp perimeters, stores, baggage, route nodes, gate reserve, wall-access support, breach containment after stronger troops secure the position, and withdrawal cover.

The unit does not create walls, gates, ferries, ships, ladders, earthworks, barricades, or engineering skill. It does not own permanent custody merely because the unit reinforces a strongpoint.

In offensive sieges, camp and route security are more appropriate than first-wave ladder or breach assault.

## 30. Terrain and Weather

Best use comes from firm controlled frontage, settlement approaches, roads, fields with manageable obstacles, camp perimeters, actual crossings, and established landing areas.

Dense woods, marsh, soft floodplain, steep ground, offensive crossings, narrow congested streets, exposed beaches, and open flanked ground can disrupt spear geometry and command. Familiarity may improve navigation or planning but grants no combat multiplier.

Rain affects footing, shield grip, equipment, visibility, routes, and transport. Fog affects warning and signals. Snow and ice affect movement and concentration. Heat affects water and fatigue. No Denmark or Zealand climate immunity is created.

The strategic interface should compare Zealand and Jutland through a small number of readable outputs: units currently available, readiness, arrival burden, home-defense exposure, competing duty, and recovery. This preserves regional causality without turning recruitment into paperwork.

## 32. Availability, Multiple Companies, and Anti-Spam

A valid Zealand source can potentially support more than one formation when people, equipment, leaders, support, and recovery permit. Finite does not mean singular.

Availability narrows through heavy casualties, home threat, missing leaders, equipment loss, broken routes, insufficient transport, service refusal or dispute, competing bow or maritime duty, damaged settlements, occupation, and unrecovered households.

Do not impose one arbitrary cooldown on every Zealand source. Show the main blocker. Do not permit infinite recruitment queues merely because the region is populous or contains a major settlement.

## 33. Ordinary Muster and Emergency Overdraw

Ordinary muster preserves essential home functions and aims at the serviceable field standard.

Emergency overdraw may increase available bodies while reducing shield coverage, equipment consistency, leadership, integration, endurance, and willingness. It may increase civilian damage, unauthorized return, resentment, delayed recovery, and exposure to a second threat.

Emergency overdraw is a state of the same card, not a permanent second unit and not free manpower.

Release is not return; return is not recovery. A surviving veteran formation may persist while new recruitment closes. Replacements can restore numbers before familiar timing, leaders, equipment, and confidence.

### 34A. Formation Continuity

A surviving formation may retain company history, recognized leaders, regional subgroup familiarity, and veteran file control. Reinforcement from compatible Zealand sources can rebuild it gradually. A destroyed company does not automatically respawn with the same identity, and one veteran cadre cannot train several full replacements simultaneously.

## 35. Source Loss, Occupation, and Displacement

Occupation or loss of a Zealand source can close recruitment, disrupt return, capture stores, scatter contributors, alter cooperation, and block replacement. It does not automatically delete surviving field companies or transfer them to the conqueror.

Retaking a source restores access for evaluation, not full readiness. Survivors may return, remain with the army, be reassigned under valid terms, or demobilize. People and equipment cannot be duplicated through displacement.

A surviving company cut off from Zealand may continue temporarily if command, supply, and service remain valid, but its recovery and replacement become more difficult.

## 36. Morale, Cohesion, and Rally

Morale improves through complete concentration, familiar subgroups, recognized leaders, a clear mission, protected flanks, nearby bowmen or reserves, successful defense, and a visible return route.

Morale deteriorates through piecemeal arrival, disputed service, missile losses, blocked return, leader loss, exposed homes, fatigue, abandonment, flanking, and repeated failed orders.

Rally is strongest near the assigned objective, reserve, settlement approach, camp, landing perimeter, or recognized marker. Regional identity does not create fearless morale.

Concern for an exposed home source can strengthen immediate defense but weaken prolonged distant service. This should follow campaign state and mission rather than a universal home-region morale aura.

## 37. Era Persistence and Regional Development

The family can persist through Early and High eras where Zealand's source and service remain viable.

Possible improvements include better records, assembly, route support, equipment inspection, shield and helmet availability, limited mail distribution, subleaders, combined-arms practice, port and crossing coordination, stores, medical recovery, and veteran continuity.

Calendar time does not automatically upgrade every company. The unit does not automatically become professional spearmen, royal guards, dismounted knights, or another spear card. Development must follow actual institutions, people, equipment, and service.

## 38. Regional Presentation

The visual identity is a coherent but nonuniform Zealand spear-and-shield line:

- mixed practical clothing and weather gear;
- serviceable but varied shields;
- visible spear frontage;
- limited helmets and mail where supported;
- restrained local or command markers;
- source and equipment variation without a fantasy island uniform.

Avoid identical royal livery, universal mail, horned helmets, fur-barbarian stereotypes, later plate, naval costumes, and a palette swap of Jutland.

Zealand and Jutland may share weapon silhouettes. Honest differentiation should come from regional equipment mixtures, weathering, source-supported clothing, shields, command markers, deployment context, and card presentation. It must not depend on making Zealand richer, brighter, or more armored by default.

## 39. Card Art, Animation, and Audio

Card art should show an assembled Zealand spear line protecting a recognizable island-region approach, coastal route, settlement access, camp, or established landing perimeter. The image should emphasize practical regional infantry, not royal elite or amphibious assault specialists.

Animations require spear-and-shield march, dressing, facing, guarded advance, conditional reception, short counterpush, passage, withdrawal, rally, equipment loss, fatigue, and reform after transport or crossing where shared systems support them.

Audio emphasizes assembly, facing, hold, mounted warning, protect the bows, keep the route, relief, withdrawal, and rally. Exact Danish language requires localization review.

## 40. Campaign AI Recruitment

AI recruits when:

- a valid Zealand source exists;
- line frontage or home defense is needed;
- Jutland capacity is insufficient, distant, committed, or better preserved;
- people, shields, spears, leaders, support, and route pass;
- Zealand can bear the extraction cost;
- the objective fits defensive or combined-arms infantry.

AI abstains when Zealand is depleted, exposed, transport cannot support the mission, the same people are committed elsewhere, the army already has sufficient frontage, or another unit better fits a mobile or assault task.

## 41. Battle AI and Autoresolve

Battle AI should form controlled frontage, protect bowmen, face observed mounted threats, preserve flanks, avoid repeated turning, counterpush only after a useful receive, coordinate relief, refuse deep pursuit, and withdraw before encirclement where possible.

Autoresolve considers source readiness, leaders, equipment, formation, mission, support, terrain, weather, fatigue, missiles, cavalry, flanks, and retreat. The labels Zealand, island, capital region, or coastal grant no hidden bonus.

Difficulty creates no free people, ships, equipment, morale, information, recovery, or combat modifiers.

## 44. Rework, Merge, and Rejection Triggers

Rework or merge if:

- Zealand is merely Jutland with different colors;
- the card is justified only by the folder count;
- the unit receives unexplained superior morale or equipment;
