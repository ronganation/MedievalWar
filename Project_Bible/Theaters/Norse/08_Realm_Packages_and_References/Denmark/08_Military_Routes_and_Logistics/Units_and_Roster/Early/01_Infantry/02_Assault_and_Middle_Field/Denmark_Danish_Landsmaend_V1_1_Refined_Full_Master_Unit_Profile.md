## 3. Player-Facing Description

Danish Landsmaend are practiced domestic foot assembled around swords, shields, recognized leaders, and recurring field service. Their proposed company is better equipped and more coherent than a local alarm or militia muster, but does not require every fighter to belong to the royal household or hold a noble title.

They provide a useful middle infantry body for a Danish army. Deploy them beside the spear line, advance them into an ordinary contest, or use them to relieve a tiring sector. Their shields and close-combat practice support a continuing fight rather than a single spectacular charge. A short counterattack can restore ground or exploit a viable flank without turning the company into heavy shock infantry.

The company remains vulnerable. Spears can keep it at reach, cavalry can threaten exposed sides, missiles can wear down protection, and fresh elite infantry can outfight it. Preserve support and a route to reform. Returning veterans, repaired swords, and surviving leaders matter to the next campaign as much as the current victory.


### 3A. The Practical Field-Infantry Promise

**These are the swordsmen the player can put into the ordinary battle line and expect to keep working.** Their proposed advantage over local militia is coherent sword-and-shield equipment, practiced leaders, useful field endurance, and the ability to hold or advance without requiring the Hird to solve every infantry contest.

Supporting frontage does not mean permanently second-line. A serviceable company can form a main infantry sector when cavalry reception and flanks are covered. It can attack suitable ordinary foot before that enemy is disordered, continue necessary supported melee, and secure ground after a local success. Relief is one valuable application, not its entire identity.

Its versatility has limits: shorter reach than spear frontage, lower concentrated assault pressure than a suitably equipped specialist, and lower crisis reliability than the royal core. These are proposed roster preferences, not fixed sword-versus-axe laws. Shared roles are welcome; actual equipment, source, practice, availability, and campaign cost make the choice meaningful.

## 13. Signature Battle Cycle: Form, Engage, Relieve, Reform

1. Form beside or behind the main line with support and a known rally point.
2. Assess a useful ordinary-infantry task rather than wait for an enemy rout.
3. Advance behind actual shields over a viable route; coordinate friendly fire and passage.
4. Engage and maintain a supported close fight while the objective remains worthwhile.
5. Relieve a tired sector or counterattack an exposed local threat when space permits.
6. Halt after securing ground, meeting fresh reserves, losing support, or becoming too fatigued.
7. Handoff or break contact where feasible, then reform before recommitment.

The rhythm is a gameplay summary, not an attested Danish drill or four compulsory ability buttons. Familiar movement, attack, halt, and withdrawal controls carry it. The company can attack and hold; it is not limited to cleanup or tiny scripted bursts.


## 14. Melee, Advance, and Relief

The intended combat reward is dependable ordinary shielded melee. A fresh serviceable company can contest ordinary infantry with support and hold a useful sector. Its charge is controlled infantry impact, not heavy shock. It need not wait for complete enemy disorder before committing.

Spears can keep it at reach; turning or already engaged spear files may offer an opening. Selected axes can apply more aggressive pressure, but do not automatically defeat every sword formation. Fresh Hirdmen should retain a higher reliability ceiling through actual kit, leadership, and practice.

Relief requires a real lane, handoff, facing, and enemy pressure that permits movement. A fresh company cannot pass through engaged allies without congestion. Bowmen may need to stop or redirect fire. Internal rotation is conditional, not an infinite endurance system. A pinned outgoing unit may still need suppression, a reserve, or a costly withdrawal.


### 14A. Advance, Hold and Counterpressure Decisions

- **Advance:** move into a suitable ordinary infantry contest when ground, facing and support make contact worthwhile. Enemy disorder is helpful, not compulsory.
- **Hold:** maintain a supported sector when it protects missiles, fixes an enemy or secures ground. Holding does not grant spear brace.
- **Counterpressure:** recover a local sector or attack an exposed side while the objective remains bounded.
- **Handoff:** let another actual formation take frontage when space and pressure permit.
- **Reform:** restore usable facing and command after disengagement, not magically restore kit or casualties.

A useful melee can continue beyond initial impact. Avoid both compulsory instant withdrawal and endless attrition. Reassess fresh enemy reserves, cavalry, missile angles, leader loss, fatigue, isolation and the route back. Breaking contact can fail; support, suppression or a costly retreat may be needed.

The intended distinction from selected axes is steady general field work, not an absolute sword defensive bonus. The intended distinction from Hirdmen is ordinary service and achieved quality, not a ban on bravery or effective counterattack. A fresh well-supported company can outperform a depleted higher-tier formation without reversing the roster's design ceiling.
