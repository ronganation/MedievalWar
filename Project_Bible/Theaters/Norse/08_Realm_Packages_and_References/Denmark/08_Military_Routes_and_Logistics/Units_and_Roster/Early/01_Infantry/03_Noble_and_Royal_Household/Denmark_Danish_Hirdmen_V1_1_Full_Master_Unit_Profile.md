Danish Hirdmen are the Kingdom's scarce, experienced royal household infantry. They protect the ruler and command structure, hold critical ground, steady less cohesive regional troops, close dangerous gaps, and deliver a controlled commitment when ordinary formations can no longer solve the battle.

Their strength is reliable shielded close combat under pressure, not limitless shock power. They are fighting household infantry, not merely a command-security record: against ordinary infantry they should deliver a credible, forceful local counterattack when cohesion, protection, and support hold. The player should feel the cost of committing them away from the ruler, court, camp, fleet concentration, succession security, or another threatened army. A Danish field army with Hirdmen nearby is more dependable because an experienced reserve exists. A kingdom that spends its Hird recklessly loses military skill, political security, personal loyalty, and institutional continuity that cannot be replaced by money alone.

## 3. One-Screen Player Contract

### Battlefield

- Guard the ruler, standard, command group, or decisive reserve position.
- Steady nearby formations through presence, relief, leadership, and controlled intervention.
- Commit against a real crisis or exploitable weakness, not every ordinary target.
- Recover behind a protected line before elite fatigue and losses become irreversible.

### Campaign

- Recruit or maintain Hirdmen only from actual royal household capacity.
- Reserve the same people honestly across foot, mounted, maritime, court, camp, and protective assignments.
- Accept high support, equipment, prestige, and recovery costs.
- Preserve veterans because death removes experience and relationships, not only bodies.

### Player warning

A title does not create a Hirdman, and assigning Hirdmen to several missions does not duplicate the household.

## 4. Player-Facing Card

### Short description

Scarce Danish royal household infantry who protect command, stabilize the line, and deliver a disciplined commitment at the decisive point.

### Encyclopedia description

Danish Hirdmen represent experienced members of the ruler's close military following gathered for foot service. Better supported and more practiced than the ordinary regional muster, they protect the Crown's person and command, preserve standards and communications, reinforce unstable sectors, and form a dependable reserve for the moment when the battle demands more than common troops can provide.

The Hird is not one uniform weapon regiment. The proposed field card nevertheless needs a clear first read: shielded close-combat retainers with swords and one-handed axes, rather than an equal blend of every household weapon. Members may fight with sword and shield, spear and shield, or axes according to source, equipment, assignment, and chronology. This card represents the broad household foot formation before any narrower equipment profile proves a separate battlefield and campaign decision.

Hirdmen can defeat ordinary infantry through cohesion, protection, leadership, and disciplined contact. They should not be thrown into every fight. Prolonged melee, missiles, encirclement, and heavy casualties can destroy the experienced core that keeps Denmark's distributed armies and royal authority connected.

### Flavor line

**The levy holds the line. The Hird decides whether it breaks.**

### 16A. Field Formation, Guard Detachment, and Understrength Footprint

A full field card is one coherent concentration, not the entire royal household. A smaller personal escort or gate detachment does not automatically become another full unit. Detaching real personnel reduces the parent formation's frontage, relief depth, and leadership coverage.

An understrength veteran card may retain excellent judgment while lacking enough bodies to hold a wide sector. Reinforcing it with accepted entrants restores headcount before it restores familiar files and collective timing. The player should see reduced frontage and endurance rather than a mysterious all-stat penalty.

### 16B. Relief and Passage Geometry

- Deploy close enough to reach the threatened sector, but behind cover or friendly frontage where practical.
- Identify an actual entry lane and a rally position before commitment.
- Move into one short sector; do not stretch a small Hird formation across the whole army.
- Relieve spearmen only when space, facing, signals, and enemy pressure permit the exchange.
- Keep bowmen's firing lane clear or stop their fire during passage.
- Preserve a route for wounded, standards, and withdrawing files.
- Hand broad frontage back to ordinary infantry once the immediate crisis is solved.

Congestion can mix files, expose shields to the wrong direction, block withdrawal, or interrupt command. Elite status does not permit passage through friendly soldiers or instant disengagement from contact.

### 23A. Equipment Interaction and Condition

Shield protection is directional and depends on the actual shield, facing, spacing, and the fighter's current action. Turning, pursuit, damaged grips, flank fire, and broken files reduce protection. Sword and one-handed axe use remain compatible with a carried shield; active two-handed axe use does not retain full normal shield benefits.

Weapon changes require carried equipment, practice, room, and transition time. A spear minority does not grant the whole formation a brace state. Axes may create useful pressure but do not automatically destroy shields or ignore armor.

Track equipment at unit-readiness scale: serviceable, worn, damaged, incomplete, or lost. Repair and replacement improve readiness without forcing individual-item maintenance clicks. Better armor helps survival but adds heat, fatigue, fit, transport, and repair burdens.

### 27A. Commitment Window, Stop Conditions, and Extraction

The strongest proposed offensive window is a short counterattack against infantry already fixed, exposed, compressed, tired, or disordered. The Hird is not restricted to waiting until the army collapses: a deliberate local commitment may secure a decisive point before a crisis develops.

Stop or reassess when the target recovers, fresh reserves arrive, the flank becomes exposed, the commander needs protection elsewhere, fatigue compromises control, or the route back closes. Success means securing the local purpose, not chasing every fleeing soldier.

Breakaway is conditional. A pinned formation may need spear relief, missile suppression, a local counterstroke, or a withdrawal under pressure. Extraction can fail and cause casualties. Do not grant a safe-disengage button.

### 32A. Command Loss, Reputation, and Royal Presence

A recognized leader or standard gives a visible rally reference and clearer orders. The ruler's presence can sharpen purpose while limiting pursuit and increasing the consequences of failure. The formation remains trained infantry when the ruler is absent; a personal-guard instance and a field Hird card are not automatically the same record.

On leader loss, surviving file leaders attempt to maintain facing, reduce mission complexity, and rally at a known point. If accepted command cannot be restored, withdrawal, fragmentation, capture, or rout become possible. Neither automatic rout nor automatic heroic last stand is prescribed.

Reputation may influence local confidence only when identity and outcomes are perceived through the shared morale and information systems. A successful counterattack can become a rally opportunity; visible Hird defeat can deepen a crisis. No universal fear aura, guaranteed rally, or forced political allegiance is created.

### 37A. Danish Army Applications

**Regional field army:** Regional Muster Spears provide width; District Bowmen pressure the approach; the Hird waits behind a usable lane and restores one threatened sector. The Hird must not become an expensive replacement for every spear unit.

**Planned counterattack:** Spears fix an enemy, bowmen force movement or disorder, and Hirdmen attack a short exposed sector. Mounted Retainers, when actually available, secure the outer flank or exploit withdrawal rather than being conjured by the combination.

**Royal movement and embarkation:** Use a bounded Hird concentration for command, camp, and access security. Land, court, and maritime assignments compete for the same personnel. No specific 1062 voyage or formation is asserted.

**Gate or camp crisis:** Local defenders retain ordinary custody and frontage. Hirdmen reinforce the entry, protect command movement, or counterattack a penetration, then hand the position back.

**Army withdrawal:** Preserve a route for command and wounded; cover one threatened passage; withdraw by controlled sections when feasible. A costly rearguard may be justified, but destruction is not the default success condition.
