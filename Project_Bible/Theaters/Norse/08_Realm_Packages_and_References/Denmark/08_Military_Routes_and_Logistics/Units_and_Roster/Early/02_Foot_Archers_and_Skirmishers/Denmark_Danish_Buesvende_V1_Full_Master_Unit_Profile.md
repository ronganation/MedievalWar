## 1. Strong Unit Verdict

**A dependable trained bow company for Denmark's main field army: more consistent shots, better target judgment and a steadier firing line than broad district service, without the cost or identity of an exceptional royal missile corps.**

Danish Buesvende are the proposed second native bow tier. They reward the player who wants more reliable missile work behind spears and swordsmen, especially when one useful firing window must prepare a local infantry or cavalry action. They can sustain ordinary pressure, contest suitable enemy missiles, concentrate on a worthwhile target and withdraw before the protective line fails.

Their better training must have visible combat value. This is not a second card whose only benefit is paperwork or a more elaborate supply receipt. With comparable equipment and a serviceable cadre, the intended advantage includes more consistent release and target tracking, fewer wasted shots, steadier cadence and quicker recovery of firing order after movement. Exact accuracy, cadence and morale values require calibration; no claim of guaranteed superiority at every range is made.

They remain vulnerable to cavalry, heavy melee, counterfire, poor angles, fatigue and exhausted arrows. Better helmets or textile protection help survival but do not make archers into sword infantry. Their recruitment and recovery are narrower and more expensive than ordinary district bows, but broad enough to function as a useful field-army tier rather than another tiny royal reserve.

## 3. Encyclopedia and Player Hints

Danish Buesvende are selected bowmen prepared for recurring field service. Their proposed formation combines practiced shooters, recognized leaders, inspected bows, adequate arrow stocks and collective fire training. The company is not a royal guard and does not require every archer to be noble, wealthy or a full-time soldier.

Denmark's ordinary District Bowmen provide broad missile coverage. These trained companies offer a more dependable line when the army needs sustained useful fire, a controlled target change or a timely concentration against an exposed enemy. Their equipment is better maintained and their movement more coherent, but arrows still need a clear path and a worthwhile target.

Protect them with spear frontage, supporting swordsmen, terrain or a reserve. They can make an approach costly and help prepare a local counterattack, but cannot stop formed cavalry on their own. Their sidearms offer a chance to escape light contact, not a reason to send them into a prolonged infantry fight.

**Battle hint:** Choose the firing lane before the target. Concentrate briefly when it matters, stop when friendly troops enter the lane, and relocate before the enemy reaches you.

**Campaign hint:** These companies are scarcer and more demanding than ordinary district bows. Preserve instructors, veterans and compatible equipment; a refilled headcount is not automatically a restored trained company.

## 20. Signature Battle Cycle: Aim, Pressure, Concentrate, Reposition

1. Choose a useful firing position with a viable target sector and escape route.
2. Form, inspect readiness and hold fire until range and target justify expenditure.
3. Apply steady pressure to exposed or suitable approaching troops.
4. Concentrate briefly when a target threatens the line or offers a combined-arms opening.
5. Cease or shift fire when friendly troops enter the lane or the target ceases to be worthwhile.
6. Reposition before counterfire, cavalry or melee makes the current ground untenable.
7. Reform, reassess ammunition and resume if the mission still makes sense.

**Aim, Pressure, Concentrate, Reposition** is a player-readable summary, not an attested Danish drill or four mandatory special abilities. Familiar fire-at-will, target, hold-fire, move and withdrawal controls should carry the main behavior.

The unit should shoot usefully without waiting for a perfect scripted opportunity. Concentration is an important option, not its only permitted fire. A clear ordinary target at useful range deserves sustained engagement while protection and ammunition remain adequate.

## 22. Range, Accuracy and Cadence Calibration

Use long, useful, close and minimum-safe bands rather than invented exact meters. Useful range is the main reliable pressure band. Long-range shots can harass but may waste arrows; close range can improve effect while exposing the company to rapid contact.

The intended trained accuracy advantage is conditional consistency under comparable kit, range and conditions. Better tracking, release and target selection can reduce dispersion and waste. Exact projectile modeling and hit probabilities remain unresolved. Do not make every shot accurate merely because the name is trained.

Cadence depends on draw effort, equipment, fatigue, arrows, command and target. A trained company can maintain a more coherent useful rhythm and resume more reliably after movement. It is not guaranteed to fire faster than every basic veteran or specialist.

The design must avoid two extremes: forbidding all statistical improvement so the tier has no combat value, or giving elite range and penetration without supporting equipment. Ordinary calibrated improvements are welcome when tied to the intended achieved standard.

## 24. Line of Fire, Friendly Safety and Arc Behavior

A bow can use direct or arcing trajectories only where the shared projectile model supports the shot. Friendly frontage is not always an absolute wall, but neither is it permission to fire through occupied bodies. Elevation, range, arc, target motion and intervening troops determine risk.

Trained leaders should cease or shift fire more reliably when Landsmaend, selected axes, Huskarle or cavalry enter the commitment lane. They do not receive friendly-fire immunity. Shooting into a mixed melee may be unsafe even when the enemy target is valuable.

Use a clear unsafe-lane indicator and familiar hold-fire response. Do not require the player to approve every arrow. An actual obstacle, smoke or a moving friendly formation can invalidate a previously good lane.

The company can relocate to a flank or elevation for a better angle, but movement interrupts useful fire and may expose it. A perfect safe arc should not be fabricated to preserve output when terrain and troops block the shot.

## 25. Movement, Section Relief and Withdrawal

Short controlled movement and re-formation are central trained advantages. The company can shift a sector, pass behind protected frontage and resume with better order than an unprepared basic expression. It remains ordinary foot, not a fast woodland skirmisher.

Section relief is a conditional shared-system proposal. Some files may cover while others move or receive actual arrows when space, leaders and protection permit. It does not guarantee continuous fire, remove fatigue or create ammunition. If unsupported by the initial battle system, use whole-unit movement without redesigning the card.

Withdrawal needs warning, space and a real route. Spears or swordsmen can protect passage; friendly congestion can defeat it. Cavalry, blocked exits, fatigue or leader loss can make extraction fail. Sidearms may buy time, not guarantee escape.

Avoid unrestricted shooting while running, automatic backward evasion and repeated perfect leapfrogging. The player should recognize a coherent trained company, not an invulnerable kiting unit.

## 26. Morale, Cohesion, Fatigue and Emergency Contact

Recognized leaders, familiar practice, useful protection, arrows and a clear task can support steadier morale. Counterfire, cavalry access, officer loss, empty containers, blocked withdrawal and prolonged exposure can break it. No Danish courage aura or unbreakable trait is added.

Repeated drawing, rapid fire, ammunition load, long marches, cold or heat exposure, relocation and emergency melee consume stamina. Fatigue can reduce cadence, aim consistency, movement and rally. Training improves management, not immunity.

If light troops reach the line, the company can use actual sidearms briefly while seeking relief. Formed infantry or cavalry should force serious losses or rout when protection fails. A carried shield may help after a real transition, but cannot coexist with full bow operation.

Empty arrows do not turn the unit into good swordsmen. It can retreat, protect a safe support position or assist limited non-missile duties. Its trained cadre remains valuable even when current missile output is gone.

## 29. Crossings, Coasts and Maritime Assignments

Defensive bridges, fords, causeways and landing approaches can suit protected fire when lanes and withdrawal exist. An offensive crossing is a movement problem first: the company cannot shoot as a perfect line while transiting a bottleneck or water obstacle.

Denmark's island and strait context can make delivery and assignment meaningful. Embarkation requires actual vessels, crews, lift, equipment protection, supply, landing and return. The bow company creates none of those resources.

A person with both bow skill and maritime obligation cannot fill two simultaneous full assignments. Actual overlap should be conserved without assuming every archer is a sailor. Shipboard fire needs a suitable tactical surface and equipment handling; it is not a default boarding or naval-archer bonus.

The current work remains a land unit. Future naval systems can adapt valid assignments without retroactively treating this profile as an implemented ship or crew card.

## 30. Settlement, Siege and Camp Use

Useful defensive tasks include protected wall or elevated fire, gate approaches, broad streets, store and camp perimeters, and reinforcement of an existing missile position. Actual access, arrows, relief and exits determine value.

Useful offensive tasks include suppression from secured ground, approach support and camp defense. The company is poor as first-wave ladder infantry, breach assault or an unsupported street-clearing formation. Better sidearms do not create siege-assault expertise.

A wall position can improve angle or protection where geometry permits; it does not grant a magical accuracy multiplier. Crowding, smoke, rooflines and enemy access can make it unsafe. Narrow firing positions may restrict the company's output.

Permanent garrison status, free upkeep and civic rights are separate policy questions. Assignment to a settlement does not automatically convert the company into militia or erase its service and supply burden.

## 31. Weather, Seasons and Campaign Endurance

Rain, wind, fog, snow, ice and heat affect achieved shooting through material condition, visibility, footing, fatigue and routes. Do not impose one flat Norse weather immunity or a universal wet-bow failure.

Winter can increase shelter and transport burdens; spring can affect roads and agriculture; summer can support movement while increasing water and extended-service needs; autumn can create harvest and return pressure. These are proposed qualitative interfaces, not fixed calendar penalties.

The trained company is intended to support a real field campaign when arrows, repair, leaders and service are maintained. It is not restricted to a few scripted shots, but long operations consume material and people. A distant campaign with no compatible replacement can preserve veterans while steadily reducing readiness.

Mission endurance should be readable through ammunition, fatigue, service and recovery feedback. Do not turn every season into a separate approval document or make trained archers unusable without a perfect logistical chain.

## 32. Supply, Resupply and Maintenance

Supply includes food, water, shelter, clothing, bows, strings, arrows, containers, sidearms, protection, repair, leaders and return. The company should be maintainable through the game's shared army and settlement systems rather than a bespoke archer economy.

Battlefield resupply is conditional on actual compatible arrows, safe access, carriers, time and command, and on whether the shared battle system supports it. It is not required for the initial card to function. A normal finite carried allowance must already support useful battle play.

Post-battle recovery of arrows is partial and condition-dependent. Safe field access, inspection and compatibility matter. No fixed recovery percentage is invented. Captured stores are not automatically compatible or ready to issue.

Maintenance payment finances real work; it does not instantly restore damaged equipment or practice. Long inactivity can degrade collective readiness, but should not erase all individual skill on a date tick.

Recover health and return first, then equipment, arrows, leaders and collective practice. These layers can progress unevenly. Full headcount does not imply full trained performance; a surviving cadre can rebuild more effectively than a company that lost its leaders.

Replacements need qualified people, compatible kit, actual support and integration. Original or separately compatible Danish sources are the strongest access. A treasury or settlement alone cannot replenish every layer.

Losses can reduce ordinary district capacity where sources overlap, but unrelated sources should not be penalized. Preserve veterans, property and service history through return and reassignment. Release is not return, and return is not full recovery.

## 34. Source Loss, Transfer and Veteran Persistence

Occupation or source disruption can close recruitment while a surviving field company remains supported elsewhere. Retaking a source restores access for evaluation, not instant cooperation, instructors or arrow stores.

Transfers preserve actual people, equipment and experience. A trained company reassigned to local defense is not duplicated into a fresh district company. Demobilized veterans may support later training when available, but cannot teach several simultaneous formations without actual capacity.

A name change or later localization does not reset history. Merging depleted compatible companies requires real integration and preserves losses rather than refilling them. Splitting a company divides actual personnel and kit; it does not create two full cards.

Recruitment can become replacement-only or close while veterans persist. A calendar transition does not delete them, promote them to royal archers or convert their bows into crossbows.

## 35. Independent Development and Crossbow Coexistence

Potential development includes better bow and arrow provision, more reliable practice, stronger cadres, improved protection, repair, stores, records and supported service. These channels can advance independently and unevenly.

Research or decisions enable programs; people, materials and support deliver them. No universal binary cultural fork is imposed. A trained Danish bow company can remain recognizably Scandinavian while adopting useful equipment or organization from contact and experience.

Crossbows remain a separate weapon and training family. They need actual weapons, bolts, maintenance, operators and service. A bowman can retrain if real inputs and capacity support it, but the old bow company must not remain simultaneously full-strength.

The future crossbow role can favor deliberate pressure against protection, while trained bows remain useful for ordinary cadence, flexible target changes and broad field support. This file does not initiate High Era authoring or guarantee crossbow admission.

## 36. Campaign AI and Roster Composition

Recruit when the army needs more dependable bow support than broad district service provides and actual trained capacity can support the cost. Consider available basic bows, infantry protection, cavalry threat, ammunition, cadre and source recovery.

Do not replace every District Bowmen company simply because the trained tier exists. Broad cheap coverage can be the better choice. Conversely, do not refuse the trained card because it shares bows and familiar tactics with the basic family; its maintained quality and service standard are legitimate roster value.

The AI should balance missile depth with spears, swords, axes and mounted response. It should not strip every instructor or local defense source to spam trained archers. No arbitrary global cap is required if real qualified capacity and costs work.

Difficulty must not create free trained shooters, arrows, equipment, perfect service or hidden morale. Campaign planning uses available information, not an omniscient forecast of enemy targets.

## 38. Autoresolve, Difficulty and Fairness

Autoresolve should consider achieved training, bows, arrows, leaders, protection, terrain, friendly frontage, enemy cavalry and missiles, and withdrawal. It should recognize the trained standard without treating the name as an automatic damage multiplier.

A serviceable protected company should be more dependable than a poorly prepared basic company in suitable conditions. A depleted or exposed trained company should not retain full value. Empty arrows and lost cadres must matter.

Do not reward repeated low-value attrition as though instructors and experienced shooters are disposable. Strategic value includes recovery and preservation, not only immediate kills. Exact autoresolve weights remain unresolved.

Difficulty and multiplayer rules should apply the same achieved equipment and ammunition logic. No hidden Danish accuracy, coastal bonus, free replacement or friendly-fire immunity is added.

## 39. Recruitment UI, Battle UI and Accessibility

The first read is trained bow infantry: better shooting consistency and fire control, narrower availability and greater cost. The player should understand that bargain without reading source administration.

Recruitment shows the card, source or region, pool, readiness, cost, replacement outlook and dominant blocker. Optional details can explain people, kit, cadre and service. No manual household selection, arrow crafting or individual bow inspection is required.

Battle UI prioritizes ammunition, range, line of fire, target suitability, fatigue, formation and immediate threats. Advanced details can expose cadre or equipment condition. Use text, icons, posture and sound alongside color.

Useful feedback includes: lane obstructed, arrows low, cavalry approaching, reforming, protection reduced and trained cadre rebuilding. Avoid dozens of simultaneous warnings. The shared UI owner determines exact presentation and accessibility implementation.

## 40. Visual Silhouette and Danish Expressions

The distance read should be a coherent trained bow line with maintained equipment, visible arrow containers, practical protection and recognizable leaders. It should look more prepared than broad district service, less royal than the Hird and less terrain-specialized than Northern Woodland Bowmen.

Proposed kit includes source-appropriate bows, practical clothing, more consistent helmets and textile protection, restrained mail among supported members, and actual sidearms. Shields may be carried or stowed where supported but not active while shooting.

Jutland, Funen, Zealand, southern-island and Scanian expressions may vary in clothing, containers, equipment finish and approved markers. These are design directions, not certified regional uniforms or hidden ethnic bonuses. Exact art requires review.

Avoid fantasy rangers, universal fur, horned helmets, elite English longbow staging, steppe composite-bow silhouettes, uniform royal livery and ragged peasant caricatures. Shared Norse visual language is acceptable without copying Norwegian source identity.

## 41. Animation, Audio and Card Art

Animation priorities include bow inspection, nock, draw, aim, release, recovery, hold-fire, target shift, movement, re-formation, arrow depletion, sidearm transition, withdrawal and rally. Trained timing should be readable without machine-perfect synchronized volleys.

A damaged string or empty container should not coexist with normal full output. A slung shield should not secretly supply active frontal protection. Movement and passage need believable interruption of fire. No acrobatic shooting or unrestricted fire while running is added.

Audio emphasizes target, hold, loose, cease, shift, arrows, cavalry warning, withdrawal and reform. Danish or appropriate shared Norse voice direction remains a localization task; no recorded assets or exact historical phrases are claimed.

Card art should show a prepared bow company near a protected field line or crossing approach, with useful spacing and maintained kit. The composition should distinguish trained ordinary archers from both a levy crowd and an elite royal guard. No image asset is generated by this text deliverable.

## 42. Flavor, Chronicle and Player Examples

**Short card:** Trained Danish bowmen who deliver dependable field fire, concentrate on useful targets and reposition under protection. More consistent than broad district service, but vulnerable to cavalry, melee, counterfire and arrow loss.

**Flavor line:** A bow can be bought. A company that knows when to loose is harder to replace.

**Example: the spear line holds.** Use sustained fire against exposed approaching troops, then shift to a useful flank. Do not waste the whole allowance into strong shields at a poor angle.

**Example: the swordsmen counterattack.** Cease or redirect fire as Landsmaend enter the lane. The company can resume from a new sector after actual movement and reform.

**Example: cavalry appears.** Withdraw early behind actual reception rather than rely on sidearms or a final volley. A trained company can still be caught.

**Example: the battle is won but arrows and leaders are lost.** Preserve survivors, repair kit and integrate replacements. Victory does not instantly restore the trained standard.

Chronicle events can record a held crossing, disciplined withdrawal or surviving cadre. They report earned outcomes and do not spawn free equipment or permanent accuracy buffs.
