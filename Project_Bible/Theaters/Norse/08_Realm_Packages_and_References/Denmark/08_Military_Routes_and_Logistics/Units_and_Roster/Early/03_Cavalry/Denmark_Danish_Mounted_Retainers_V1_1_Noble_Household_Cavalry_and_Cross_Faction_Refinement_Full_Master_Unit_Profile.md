## 1. Direct Unit Verdict

**A scarce, better-equipped noble-household cavalry core that countercharges serious mounted threats, delivers controlled shock, fights a purposeful cavalry melee, and rallies before its riders and horses are spent.**

Danish Mounted Retainers fill the upper Early household mounted-combat lane beside Regional Muster Spears, Danish District Bowmen, and Danish Hirdmen. The infantry establishes the fight; the retainers change its direction. Their proposed reward is better-supported protection, selected combat-capable mounts, credible lance or spear impact, mounted melee skill, and enough cohesion to rally after contact. They should be a serious mounted reserve rather than light utility riders with a prestigious name.

They are not merely couriers on horses. A recruitable combat card must possess actual mounted weapon practice, compatible military mounts, formation leadership, and a useful strike-and-recall loop. Nor are they a cheap version of later armored knights. Prepared spears, concentrated missiles, stronger cavalry, difficult ground, and exhaustion remain serious counters.

## 2. One-Screen Player Card

- **Class:** Upper-Early noble-household cavalry.
- **Source:** Qualified mounted retainers from individually identified Danish household sources.
- **Battle promise:** Meet a committed mounted threat, add real melee weight, strike a suitable infantry flank, and rally for another considered decision.
- **Best use:** Countercharge reserve, supported cavalry melee, controlled shock into a fixed or exposed flank, important escort, and limited exploitation.
- **Strengths:** Strong Early household cohesion, meaningful countercharge, selected mounts, better-supported protection, cavalry melee, and rally control.
- **Weaknesses:** Scarce riders and suitable mounts; expensive horse support; poor frontal use against formed spears; vulnerable to prolonged melee and blocked withdrawal.
- **Availability:** Finite and household-specific; actual opening access unresolved.
- **Campaign bargain:** A useful mounted answer purchased with local protection, escort, horse, and recovery capacity.
- **Development:** Persistent where its source survives; later armor or knightly service requires actual new capability.

**Flavor line:** The spears hold the enemy. The retainers find the opening.


## 3. Player-Facing Description

Danish Mounted Retainers represent selected fighters from noble or otherwise high-status military households and their qualified mounted followings. Noble-household describes the source and player-facing quality, not proof that every rider holds a noble title. Their formation brings military riding, weapons, maintained tack, leaders, and compatible horses together into a scarce maneuver force. Household connection may support repeated association and accepted command, but neither a noble title nor a horse alone produces cavalry.

Use them to protect important movement and shape the battle around the infantry. A flank threat can force an opponent to turn or keep reserves back. When arrows, spears, terrain, or enemy overextension create a real opening, the retainers can strike and exploit it. Their purpose is to supply a credible mounted combat answer: countercharge, controlled impact, and a supported melee that ordinary utility riders should avoid. They do not win every encounter through impact, but should not feel restricted to chasing archers.

After contact, halt, disengage where feasible, and reform. Lost mounts can leave experienced riders unable to serve as cavalry. Lost riders leave horses without the formation knowledge needed to replace them. A successful battle should preserve the next campaign's mounted capacity, not merely produce a large kill count.

On a connected land route, they can escort a concentration, respond to a local threat, or exploit open ground beside a foot army. Across water, their speed advantage depends on transporting actual horses and support, landing safely, and restoring mounted order. ## 8. Hird Interface and One-Person Conservation

If the only validated Danish mounted source ultimately proves to be the royal Hird, revise this broad scope to a narrower assignment rather than fabricate magnate capacity.

The player chooses between shielded close-combat weight and mounted reach when the same people are involved. Separate available people may supply both. Equipment, leaders, horses, and support remain independently finite.

## 11. Rider Eligibility and Training

Military riding includes control under noise, formation pressure, weapon handling, turning, spacing, contact, disengagement, and recall. Riding for travel alone does not establish those abilities.

The intended formation draws on practiced riders and accepted mounted leaders. New entrants may be eligible yet lack familiar files or collective timing. Training should improve concrete behaviors: measured approach, stable spacing, threat judgment, short contact, reform, and horse care. It does not instantly grant campaign experience.

A veteran's advantage is better pacing and decisions, not immunity to fatigue. A skilled rider on an unfamiliar mount may remain less reliable than an established pair.

## 13. Equipment and Readable Silhouette

Proposed primary weapon: a serviceable lance or substantial mounted spear appropriate to the validated source and chronology. Proposed sidearms: sword or other compatible one-handed weapon where supported, with utility blades as backup. Shields should be visible where actually supplied and practiced. Helmets, substantial shields, textile protection, and visibly represented mail define the proposed better-supported household core. Mail is a meaningful part of the intended silhouette rather than an occasional cosmetic exception, but no exact distribution or universal full-mail issue is claimed. Swords or suitable sidearms must support actual mounted melee, not appear only as decorative kit.

No universal couched-lance doctrine, barding, late plate, exact shield shape, or fixed sword ownership is assumed. Mounted missiles are not baseline. A later missile expression needs its own evidence and tactical purpose rather than a decorative javelin that silently changes the unit.

The first read is better-equipped early noble-household cavalry with mounted reach, shields, a visible mail-bearing core, and coherent combat readiness. The card must look ready to maneuver and make controlled contact, not like tournament knights or unarmed couriers.

### 14A. Lance-to-Sidearm Contact and Equipment Loss

The proposed mounted spear or lance defines approach and initial contact. A broken or lost weapon reduces the next impact opportunity; a suitable sidearm permits continued mounted fighting only if the rider, mount, space, and formation remain viable. No infinite fresh lances appear between charges.

Shields and mail improve the intended protected-combat read but do not armor the horse or erase directional missile exposure. Sidearm transition needs a compatible carried weapon and animation; it is a design requirement, not a claimed implemented switch.

The field card must not become mostly lightly protected travel riders with a few mail-clad leaders. Conversely, source-valid equipment variation is welcome. Exact proportions and individual opening inventories remain unresolved.

## 16. Signature Battle Cycle

1. Deploy behind or beside the infantry with room to move and a known rally point.
2. Preserve horses during the opening exchange; use position to threaten rather than charge the first visible target.
3. Identify an exposed missile group, overextended attacker, threatened flank, or enemy fixed by friendly infantry.
4. Check the approach and return lane; avoid masking bowmen or crossing a spear frontage at contact.
5. Form, advance, and accelerate only when alignment and target justify it.
6. Strike for a local purpose: disrupt, relieve, intercept, or exploit.
7. Reassess immediately after contact; fresh enemy reserves can turn success into a trap.
8. Break off where feasible and reform behind support or on usable open ground.
9. Pursue only after a real break and only while recall, fatigue, and return remain acceptable.
10. Restore escort or reserve duty and carry actual losses into campaign recovery.

This is a proposed game behavior sequence, not a claimed historical Danish drill.


## 17. Charge, Contact, and Shock Ceiling

The card should have meaningful mounted impact against suitable targets. Avoid overcorrecting historical uncertainty into cavalry that can never charge. Its proposed shock is meaningful for an upper Early household formation and conditional on execution. Limited means a bounded commitment and lower developed-heavy-cavalry ceiling, not a token charge effect.

A coherent strike against exposed light infantry or an engaged flank can be decisive. A charge into fresh, formed spears can fail even with good riders. Approach distance, footing, alignment, target facing, mount condition, fatigue, and supporting action govern the result.

The card has no automatic penetration, knockdown, armor-piercing, or morale-collapse privilege. It should not routinely match developed heavy knightly cavalry in frontal impact. A controlled cavalry melee may be necessary and worthwhile after impact, especially against ordinary mounted opponents with local support. The player is not required to withdraw immediately on contact. Break off when the mission is achieved or support, fatigue, flanks, or mount condition make continued contact a poor bargain. Prolonged unsupported attrition still wastes the household core.


## 18. Counterattack and Cavalry Interception

Counterattack is strongest when an enemy has committed, turned, tired, or lost alignment. Against ordinary enemy cavalry, the proposed better mounts, protection, weapons, and practiced household files should provide real combat weight when present. Use local numbers, friendly spears, ground, and timing; household status alone never wins the collision.

The retainers can threaten an approach to bowmen or command and force a mounted opponent to reconsider. If heavier cavalry advances fresh and formed, seek spear support or disengage. Countercharge is a defining intended combat response, not an automatic anti-cavalry bonus.

Do not chase a withdrawing mounted opponent across unknown ground solely because the initial contact was favorable. The enemy may retain a reserve, and horse fatigue can reverse the advantage.


### 18A. Countercharge Window and Purposeful Melee

- **Too early:** the enemy can halt, turn, or draw the retainers into a prepared response.
- **Useful window:** the enemy has committed and the Danish formation can meet it with readable ground, angle, support, and rally space.
- **Too late:** the friendly flank has already broken or the lane is blocked.
- **False opportunity:** reserves, spears, crossfire, or poor footing punish the apparent opening.

After impact, continue a supported melee when it can secure the local objective or defeat ordinary cavalry at acceptable cost. Withdraw when the purpose is achieved, the opponent is reinforced, mounts tire, leaders lose control, or flanks become exposed. Rally restores facing and command only when actual survivors, space, and condition permit it.

The revised reward is not merely a stronger charge statistic. It is the ability to meet a serious mounted crisis, remain useful through necessary contact, and recover a coherent body afterward. A fresh superior heavy formation remains dangerous; noble-household does not mean universal cavalry superiority.

## 19. Flank Pressure and Anti-Skirmisher Work

A flank threat has value before contact: an opponent may turn, protect missiles, or hold back a reserve. Those are responses to actual positioning, not forced debuffs.

Exposed bowmen, javelinmen, and isolated light infantry are useful targets when the approach and exit are clear. Missiles behind spears, stakes, ditches, woods, or crossfire are not automatically vulnerable because they are missile troops.

Screening is a bounded secondary role. The card can watch a flank and deny easy approach, but does not gain perfect scouting, hidden enemy knowledge, or free messenger detachments. Routine reconnaissance should use the appropriate support capability where one exists.


## 20. Pursuit, Recall, and Extraction

Pursuit can turn a local break into a larger victory, but it consumes horse condition and disperses files. Set a rally point and stop when the enemy reforms, a reserve appears, the route narrows, fatigue rises, or the escort mission is exposed.

Recall travels through actual command and signals. Separated groups may return late or fail to receive it. Breaking contact from a pinned melee may require friendly pressure, spear support, or a costly withdrawal. No safe-disengage button is granted.

For commander or convoy extraction, protect a viable route and coordinate movement. The unit cannot teleport a protected person, make the escort invulnerable, or defend two distant objectives with the same riders.

## 22. Rider Fatigue, Horse Fatigue, and Morale

Track rider and horse condition separately. Long marches, repeated acceleration, turning, contact, pursuit, load, heat, poor footing, and inadequate feed or water affect the pair differently. Rested riders cannot erase exhausted mounts.

Cohesion depends on accepted leadership, familiar signals, clear purpose, compatible service, and formation practice. Leader loss can force simpler orders and rally around surviving subleaders. A protected person's danger can pull the unit back toward escort duty; it does not grant a supernatural morale surge.

Failure states include scattered pursuit, stalled contact, horse-depleted formation, lost command, and blocked withdrawal. High headcount does not prove the formation remains mission-ready.


## 23. Danish Combined-Arms Applications

**Spear-and-retainer field army:** Regional Muster Spears hold broad contact; Mounted Retainers threaten an outer flank and strike only when the enemy is fixed or exposed. Spears retain the main anti-mounted reception role.

**Bow protection and exploitation:** District Bowmen pressure the approach. Retainers protect a usable outer lane and punish exposed light troops or a disordered target. Stop friendly fire during passage; no hidden synergy bonus is created.

**Hird and mounted reserve:** Hirdmen provide shielded close-combat reliability and command protection on foot. Retainers supply movement, flank response, and pursuit. Shared contributors require an actual choice; independent sources may supply both.

**Crossing defense:** Foot troops secure the crossing and receiving frontage. Retainers wait on usable ground behind the line to respond after a penetration, not charge across a congested bridge.

**Relief and withdrawal:** Retainers escort movement, threaten pursuers, and intercept light threats. Local defenders and foot troops retain difficult-ground holding tasks. Saving the force can be a successful outcome without a decisive cavalry melee.


### 23A. Higher-Weight Danish Applications

**Mounted flank crisis:** keep the retainers behind a stable spear reference. When ordinary enemy cavalry commits toward the bowmen or outer flank, countercharge at a useful angle and fight the necessary supported melee. This is a defining use, not a last-resort utility assignment.

**Hird fixes, retainers strike:** the Hird stabilizes close contact while retainers attack a viable exposed sector. Actual separate people are required for simultaneous service. Foot quality and mounted reach cooperate without sharing a magic bonus.

**Infantry-created shock window:** spears hold an ordinary formation; bows force disorder or movement; retainers concentrate controlled impact on a suitable flank. They can contribute decisively before the entire army is in crisis.

**Important escort under threat:** retainers protect high-value movement because they can respond to a serious mounted attack. Routine message delivery and long low-value scouting should not consume the core when another validated capability can do the job. No lower cavalry card is created merely to supply that contrast.

## 25. Maritime Transport and Island Concentration

A horse-bearing crossing requires actual transport suitability, space, handlers, feed, water, tack custody, landing access, and a return plan. A ship that can carry people is not automatically adequate for a mounted formation.

Landing does not restore battle readiness instantly. Riders, mounts, equipment, and leaders must assemble on usable ground. A landing reserve may serve on foot only through a valid assignment; it does not create free Hirdmen or ship crews.

The proposed Danish strategic choice is whether mounted speed on the far side justifies the transport and recovery burden. Island origin provides neither cavalry immunity nor an automatic coastal combat advantage.


## 26. Siege, Settlement, and Dismount Boundaries

Useful mounted siege tasks include camp escort, relief interception, open-ground sally response, messenger protection, and pursuit after a genuine break. Walls, ladders, narrow breaches, and compressed streets do not suit the card's mounted role.

Prebattle foot assignment is conditional on equipment, practice, leadership, and horse custody. It reduces mounted capacity and does not automatically confer the Hird's royal membership or close-combat proficiency. Riders who lose horses remain actual survivors, not a newly generated elite infantry card.

Live dismount and remount remain unresolved implementation capabilities. Do not claim animation, AI, save, or replay support that has not been inspected and tested.


## 27. Availability, Cost, and Household Politics

Proposed availability is scarce and provider-specific. Costs should be substantial relative to ordinary foot troops because mounts, support, equipment, and replacement matter. Exact price, upkeep, pool capacity, recruitment duration, and unit size remain unresolved.

The royal household may prioritize personal protection; a magnate may retain riders for local security; a landed provider may face route, horse, or service-term limits. These are possible outcomes of actual relationships, not automatic stereotypes or fixed loyalty percentages.

The player should see the strongest displaced duty and the dominant blocker. Do not make every recruitment click a sequence of separate permissions. Household support, source condition, and prior losses should affect the available pool through the campaign model.

## 32. Campaign and Battle AI

Recruit or retain the card when a valid source exists and the army needs maneuver, escort, counterattack, or pursuit. Avoid it when the mission is mainly wall assault, dense-ground holding, or prolonged unsupported operations. Consider the cost to household security and horse recovery.

In battle, threaten before charging, seek supported targets, preserve a return lane, and reassess after contact. The AI must not hoard a fresh reserve while a decisive opportunity or crisis passes. Scarcity should improve timing, not cause paralysis.

Do not grant hidden horses, morale, knowledge, support, or replenishment on higher difficulty. The AI should recognize leader loss, scattered pursuit, exhausted mounts, and failed extraction instead of repeating an invalid charge.
