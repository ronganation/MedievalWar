**Decisive verdict:** Denmark presently retains four mounted families across Early and High development: Hestfolk, Mounted Retainers, Mounted Huskarle, and Danish Riddere. The two High Era cards are strong and distinct. No third High Era cavalry card is currently required.

Mounted Huskarle own guarded interception and bounded axe-and-shield mounted melee. Riddere own scarce formed heavy shock, heavy countercharge, and decisive reserve commitment. Earlier Hestfolk and Mounted Retainers persist because neither High Era card economically performs routine screening, pursuit, general household escort, or flexible cavalry work.

## 3. Why the Four-Family Portfolio Survives

Hestfolk are the economical answer to routine movement and pursuit. Mounted Retainers are the flexible noble-household cavalry answer. Mounted Huskarle concentrate household fighters, axes, shields, protection, and guard duty into a narrower mounted-melee formation. Riddere concentrate the strongest horses, equipment, alignment, patronage, and recovery into a decisive shock reserve.

Each card must be preferable in some missions and wasteful in others. If a later card becomes the universal answer, the portfolio has failed.

## 4. No Linear Upgrade Ladder

```text
Hestfolk -> Mounted Retainers -> Mounted Huskarle -> Danish Riddere
```

The above chain is prohibited. Individuals may transfer only through person-level release, horse reassessment or replacement, service change, equipment, training, ownership, command, integration, and recovery reconciliation. Parent formations persist.

## 5. Hestfolk Persistence

Hestfolk remain Denmark's practical regional light-to-medium cavalry. Their value is availability, movement, screen work, counter-skirmishing, pursuit, route response, and ordinary flank security.

They must not become junior knights, informal Mounted Huskarle, or a generic candidate pool. High Era development may improve tack, leaders, protection, horse care, and coordination without deleting the card.

## 6. Mounted Retainers Persistence

Mounted Retainers remain broad noble-household cavalry for escort, countercharge, controlled shock, and purposeful cavalry melee. They retain a lower institutional threshold and broader household connection than Riddere.

Mounted Huskarle do not replace them because guard proximity, axe-and-shield contact persistence, and competition with Foot Huskarle are narrower. Riddere do not replace them because decisive knightly shock is too expensive for ordinary household missions.

## 7. Mounted Huskarle Development Contract

Mounted Huskarle require actual qualified household fighters, release from incompatible foot or guard duty, suitable horses, one-handed axes, shields, protection, tack, leaders, mounted-melee training, guard assignments, supply, and recovery.

Their core rhythm is **Guard, Countercharge, Fight, Recover**. They should remain closer to protected objects, threatened flanks, command routes, missile withdrawals, baggage access, and extraction lanes than other Danish cavalry.

## 8. Danish Riddere Development Contract

Danish Riddere require recognized knightly or equivalent mounted service, selected qualified people, suitable higher-grade combat horses, lance or approved mounted spear, shields, strong protection, patronage, formation training, approach geometry, breakaway, reform, and costly recovery.

Their core rhythm is **Prepare, Align, Strike, Reform**. They are held for decisive shock, heavy countercharge, and elite reserve action rather than routine mobility.

## 9. Direct Mounted Choice Matrix

| Need | Preferred family | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Screening, counter-skirmishing, routine pursuit | Hestfolk | Economical practical mobility |
| General household escort and flexible cavalry combat | Mounted Retainers | Broader household mission and lower institutional threshold |
| Protect a critical command route or fight bounded mounted melee | Mounted Huskarle | Guard mission and axe-and-shield contact persistence |
| Deliver decisive formed shock or heavy countercharge | Danish Riddere | Strongest alignment, horse, equipment and institutional commitment |

Availability, source, horse condition, terrain, service, politics, and army composition can override the preferred family.

## 10. Why Norway Does Not Define Denmark

Norway's Svenner, Magnate Horsemen, Royal Lancers, and Riddarar form a four-card High Era family because Norway separately develops junior household support, politically controlled magnate response, transitional royal shock, and later knightly heavy cavalry.

Denmark already distributes related gameplay across persistent Hestfolk, Mounted Retainers, Mounted Huskarle, and Riddere. Creating Danish Svenner, Magnate Horsemen, or Royal Lancers would currently collide with existing cards rather than add a new decision.

## 15. Horse Suitability and Pairing

Horse suitability includes health, soundness, temperament, load, acceleration, balance, turning, contact tolerance, rider familiarity, tack, feed, shelter, handlers, terrain, mission, and recovery.

Travel horses, messengers, pack animals, breeding stock, and generic horse resources do not automatically qualify. Replacing a rider or horse weakens pairing; replacing both creates the weakest continuity case.

## 16. Horse and Equipment Competition

Hestfolk, Mounted Retainers, Mounted Huskarle, and Riddere compete for some combination of combat horses, tack, shields, sidearms, spears or lances, protection, handlers, farriers, leaders, routes, feed, and recovery.

High Era investment changes allocation and quality. It does not multiply the underlying horses or equipment by the number of cards.

## 17. Service and Political Ownership

Mounted people may remain under regional, household, magnate, Crown, landed, or other approved service relationships. Realm service does not silently transfer private people, horses, or property.

Every contribution must define owner, authority, mission, duration, command, recall, reward or maintenance, home-defense debit, casualty responsibility, return, and recovery.

## 18. Formation Transaction

Purpose selected
-> people and service verified
-> incompatible duties released
-> horses selected and paired
-> weapons, shields, protection and tack reserved
-> leaders and training assigned
-> formation and mission practice completed
-> feed, water, handlers, routes, transport, rally, return and recovery verified
-> company fielded.

Fatal failure restores the last valid state where possible and creates no duplicate reservation.

## 20. Battlefield Role Separation

Hestfolk seek mobility and low-cost opportunity. Mounted Retainers seek controlled flexible cavalry combat. Mounted Huskarle seek guard proximity, interception, and bounded mounted melee. Riddere seek alignment and a decisive shock window.

All four avoid prepared spears, severe missile exposure, blocked exits, bad ground, exhausted horses, and prolonged unsupported contact, but they encounter those dangers through different missions and acceptable risks.

## 21. Charge Countercharge and Contact

Charge success depends on approach, alignment, pace, horse condition, equipment, target facing and cohesion, ground, obstacles, missiles, support, and exit.

- Hestfolk use local pressure rather than heavy shock.
- Mounted Retainers use controlled shock and countercharge.
- Mounted Huskarle use interception and contact persistence after bounded impact.
- Riddere use the strongest formed shock and heavy countercharge.

No card guarantees rout, penetration, trampling, or safe disengagement.

## 22. Melee and Disengagement Ceilings

Hestfolk should leave sustained combat first. Mounted Retainers can conduct purposeful cavalry melee. Mounted Huskarle can persist longer in mounted axe-and-shield contact, especially near a guard objective. Riddere can fight after impact but lose their main value when trapped in prolonged melee.

Every family needs physical space, horse condition, formation control, and a viable route to break contact.

## 24. Combined Arms

Spears protect cavalry rally and deny enemy horse. Bowmen and Crossbowmen disrupt targets, cover approaches, and shape lanes. Landsmaend, Vaebnere, Axemen, and Huskarle fix or disorganize infantry. Hestfolk clear light threats. Mounted Retainers provide flexible mounted support. Mounted Huskarle guard and intercept. Riddere commit only when decisive value exceeds permanent risk.

These are physical relationships, not composition bonuses.

## 25. Terrain Weather and Routes

Firm open or rolling ground supports cavalry when lanes and exits exist. Woods, marsh, mud, steep slopes, walls, bridges, gates, dense streets, ditches, fences, snow, ice, fog, heat, and rain affect each formation through actual horse, route, visibility, and footing state.

Denmark receives no blanket plains, coastal, island, winter, or national cavalry modifier.

## 26. Maritime and Island Deployment

Denmark's maritime power does not remove cavalry transport burdens. Overseas deployment needs horse-capable vessels, loading, crews, handlers, feed, water, ventilation, safe landing, routes, shore assembly, recovery, and return.

Embarked riders are not battle-ready cavalry. A company becomes mounted-ready only after horses, riders, equipment, files, and command are restored ashore.

## 27. Dismounted Boundary

Dismounting does not create a second card or duplicate people. It requires compatible foot equipment, training, horse holding, space, time, command, and a disposition for the mounts.

Mounted Huskarle do not become fresh Foot Huskarle. Riddere do not become automatic Foot Knights. Emergency states remain degraded unless a separate recurring formation passes its own full card gate.

## 28. Campaign Opportunity Cost

Mounted development may reduce local defense, household security, Royal protection, foot Huskarle, line infantry equipment, messengers, escorts, breeding and remount reserves, treasury flexibility, court attendance, administration, and future cavalry candidates.

The UI should show the dominant cost without forcing animal-level micromanagement.

## 29. Supply and Recovery

All mounted families require rider support, horse feed and water, shelter, handlers, tack, weapons, protection, repair, routes, camps, medicine, rally, return, and recovery. Heavier or more specialized cards usually increase the burden.

Victory does not restore riders, horses, pairings, equipment, leaders, fatigue, political support, or household capacity.

## 30. Post-Battle Reconciliation

Track riders, leaders, horses, pairings, weapons, shields, armor, tack, captives, missing, wounded, killed, dispersed, equipment losses, mission outcome, source debit, service, and company history separately.

A cavalry company may win tactically while becoming strategically unavailable. Preserving a formation may be more valuable than maximizing pursuit casualties.

## 31. Recruitment Closure and Persistence

Recruitment may close when people, horses, service, politics, equipment, training, source institutions, routes, or recovery become nonviable. Surviving formations persist until destroyed, demobilized, dispersed, recalled, reassigned, or explicitly absorbed.

Calendar progression neither deletes old cavalry nor restores failed institutions.

## 32. High Era Development Direction

High Era development may improve horse selection, pairing, tack, equipment, mail and helmets, shields, lances, axes, cadre, formation practice, guard doctrine, shock discipline, repair, remounts, political service, and recovery.

These improvements ordinarily remain within the four families. Equipment or experience alone does not create Armored, Veteran, Royal, Magnate, or Elite variants.

## 33. Campaign AI

AI compares the exact mission against the cheapest adequate card. It prefers Hestfolk for routine mobility, Mounted Retainers for broad household cavalry, Mounted Huskarle for protected-object guard and contact persistence, and Riddere for decisive heavy shock.

AI does not recruit one of every card, receive hidden horses, ignore source duties, or use elite cavalry for low-value attrition.

## 35. UI and Player Information

Campaign UI shows source, owner, people, prior duties, horses, pairing, equipment, leaders, support, political and household debit, replacement, recovery, and dominant blocker.

Battle UI shows mission, alignment, horse and rider fatigue, weapon and shield state, target suitability, spear and missile threat, exit, reform, guard relationship, and recall.

## 36. Visual Separation

- Hestfolk read as practical regional horse with lighter, varied equipment.
- Mounted Retainers read as noble-household cavalry with flexible spear, shield, and sidearm presentation.
- Mounted Huskarle read as compact guarded axe-and-shield household cavalry.
- Riddere read as the most cohesive lance-or-mounted-spear heavy reserve with stronger protection.

Avoid palette swaps, universal mail, barding, great helms, identical heraldry, fantasy horses, or Norwegian visual copying.

## 37. Animation and Audio

Shared cavalry animation includes march, pace changes, wheels, alignment, contact, fatigue, breakaway, reform, rider separation, horse injury, rally, retreat, and recall.

Card-specific animation emphasizes Hestfolk response and pursuit, Mounted Retainer flexible charge and melee, Mounted Huskarle axe-and-shield guarding and interception, and Riddere formed lance or spear shock. Audio remains grounded and mission-readable.

## 38. Calibration Fixtures

Test every family on the same screening, escort, countercharge, exposed-missile, disordered-infantry, prepared-spear, heavy-cavalry, guard, withdrawal, pursuit, open-ground, broken-ground, settlement, maritime-arrival, fatigue, horse-loss, leader-loss, blocked-exit, and recovery scenarios.

Also test whether removing any one card loses a meaningful decision and whether any proposed third High Era card merely duplicates an existing family. No fixture has been executed.

## 39. Acceptance Criteria

Accept the authority when all four families remain understandable and useful, High Era development does not erase Early families, Mounted Huskarle and Riddere remain sharply separated, Norway informs depth without controlling Denmark's card count, people and horses are not duplicated, AI chooses the cheapest adequate mission tool, and no new card is admitted without the complete survival gate.

## 40. Stop Conditions

- Do not create a third High Era cavalry card for symmetry.
- Do not create Danish Svenner, Magnate Horsemen, or Royal Lancers by copying Norway.
- Do not turn ownership labels, equipment, quality, or experience into cards.
- Do not automatically convert Hestfolk, Mounted Retainers, Mounted Huskarle, or Riddere.
- Do not create people, horses, tack, weapons, titles, service, pools, or readiness from era, technology, stables, estates, or buildings.
- Do not double-count foot and mounted duties.
- Do not grant universal charge, armor-piercing, terrain, maritime, morale, scouting, or pursuit bonuses.
- Do not allocate exact values, Registry, Opening State, payload, importer, runtime, or Unreal state.
