## 2. Strong Unit Verdict

**Mounted Huskarle are a scarce Danish household cavalry card built for close escort, hard countercharge, and protected mounted melee after the initial impact.**

They are tougher in continued contact than Hestfolk, more guard-focused and axe-defined than Mounted Retainers, and less dependent on formed knightly shock than Riddere. Their principal weapon is a one-handed axe used with a mounted shield. The foot Huskarle card retains the two-handed axe assault identity.

The player bargain is direct: keep them near the protected flank or command route, meet dangerous light-to-medium cavalry, punish exposed missiles or disordered infantry, fight briefly with greater persistence than ordinary horse, then recover before spears, heavy knights, or exhaustion trap the company.

## 3. One-Screen Medieval II Player Card

- **Class:** scarce protected household melee cavalry.
- **Battle promise:** guard a critical flank or command route, countercharge a dangerous mounted threat, and remain useful during a bounded cavalry melee.
- **Best targets:** light cavalry, exposed missiles, disordered infantry, isolated riders, and pursuers threatening the command or withdrawal.
- **Strengths:** household cohesion, mail and shield protection where supported, axe-and-shield melee, countercharge, escort, and controlled recovery.
- **Weaknesses:** prepared spears, heavy knightly shock, crossbows, deep heavy infantry, poor ground, horse fatigue, and blocked disengagement.
- **Availability:** very scarce and household-specific.
- **Campaign bargain:** stronger contact persistence purchased with Huskarle manpower, suitable horses, equipment, household security, and slow recovery.
- **Opening:** not established for January 1062.

**Flavor:** Guard the household. Meet the charge. Finish the melee. Bring the horses home.

## 4. Player-Facing Description

Mounted Huskarle are household fighters whose eligible people, horses, tack, armor, shields, axes, leaders, and mounted-melee practice have been assembled into a real cavalry company. A Huscarl title, a horse, or mail armor alone is insufficient.

Keep the company near command, an exposed army flank, or the route by which valuable troops must withdraw. Commit against cavalry that Hestfolk should not fight alone, or against targets already opened by Danish spears, Crossbowmen, Axemen, or Riddere. The company can remain in a mounted melee longer than practical regional horse, but it is not built to grind against deep heavy infantry or absorb repeated knightly charges.

A successful action preserves the protected object and enough rider-horse pairs to reform. Maximum kills are secondary.

## 6. Why the Card Survives

The card survives only if five distinctions remain visible together:

1. household-elite rather than broad regional source;
2. one-handed axe and shield mounted melee;
3. command escort and protected-flank duty;
4. more contact persistence than Hestfolk or ordinary Mounted Retainers;
5. less formed shock, institutional weight, and heavy-cavalry dominance than Riddere.

If the result becomes Mounted Retainers with axes, Norwegian Household Axeriders under a Danish name, or Riddere with weaker charge statistics, it must merge or be rejected.

## 9. Relationship to Mounted Retainers

Mounted Retainers remain the broader noble-household cavalry answer for escort, countercharge, controlled shock, and purposeful cavalry melee. Mounted Huskarle are narrower and more expensive in household-elite people, but bring stronger axe-and-shield contact persistence and closer protected-object duty.

Mounted Retainers should usually be easier to justify for general cavalry needs. Mounted Huskarle are chosen when the army needs a compact guard cavalry company that can meet a dangerous mounted collision and keep fighting briefly after impact.

## 10. Relationship to Riddere

Riddere own the strongest formed charge, heavy countercharge, elite mounted reserve, and decisive exploitation. Mounted Huskarle own guard proximity, axe-and-shield contact, and bounded mounted melee.

Riddere should win the clean heavy-shock contest when properly aligned. Mounted Huskarle should be more comfortable in broken, compressed, or escort-centered fighting after the ideal charge geometry has failed. Mounted Huskarle must not become cheaper knights or gain free armor piercing.

## 11. Relationship to Hirdmen and Royal Spearguard

Royal Hird personnel and Royal Spearguard assignments remain finite. Mounted Huskarle may include valid household fighters only after explicit release from incompatible foot or protection duties. The card must not drain the ruler's closest guard invisibly.

Royal Spearguard own prepared spear-and-shield command protection on foot. Hirdmen own broader royal crisis response. Mounted Huskarle own mobile close escort and mounted interception where horses, routes, and space support it.

### 17A. Company Readiness Versus Household Status

A recognized Huscarl or household fighter may be mountless, wounded, politically withheld, equipment-incomplete, or untrained for mounted axe service. Household status is therefore not battlefield readiness.

A company may contain individually capable people and horses while remaining unready because pairing, leaders, signals, guard drills, contact control, or recovery support are incomplete.

## 18. Horse Architecture

The card needs sound combat-capable horses suited to protected mounted melee, not necessarily the heaviest knightly warhorses. Suitability includes health, temperament, load, contact tolerance, acceleration, balance, turning, rider familiarity, tack fit, feed, shelter, handlers, terrain, and recovery.

Travel horses, messenger mounts, pack animals, breeding stock, and generic horse resources do not automatically qualify. Horse armor and barding are not baseline.

## 21. Protection and Tack

Protection may include practical layered clothing, helmets, mail among supported riders, shields, saddles, bridles, and serviceable tack. The company should show stronger consistency than Hestfolk without matching later Riddere standardization.

Protection increases load, heat, cost, maintenance, horse demand, and recovery. Full plate, uniform great helms, universal mail, and full barding are not baseline.

## 22. Equipment States

- **Full serviceable issue:** Compatible axe, shield, protection, tack, and sidearm.
- **Mixed serviceable issue:** Functional but less consistent handling and presentation.
- **Shield-depleted:** Greater missile and melee exposure.
- **Axe-depleted:** Reduced card identity; sidearms preserve emergency combat only.
- **Protection-incomplete:** Contact persistence declines.
- **Tack worn or mismatched:** Control, acceleration, and breakaway worsen.
- **Rebuilt mixed issue:** Numbers return before full compatibility.

### 22A. Visible Equipment and Tack Degradation

The player should see meaningful degradation through silhouettes, shields, missing axes, riding posture, pace, and readiness indicators.

- shield loss reduces guard and missile-survival value;
- axe loss weakens the card's defining melee loop;
- tack damage reduces turning, acceleration, order, and breakaway;
- armor damage reduces contact persistence;
- mixed replacement equipment slows collective integration.

A roster name never preserves full capability after material loss.

## 23. Training Curriculum

Training includes military riding under load, axe and shield use from horseback, controlled countercharge, escort spacing, moving protection, cavalry-melee control, disengagement, recall, pursuit discipline, horse care, formations, signals, and cooperation with spears, Crossbowmen, Mounted Retainers, Hestfolk, and Riddere.

Foot axe experience does not substitute for mounted weapon control. Travel riding does not substitute for cavalry training.

## 24. Leaders and Cadre

A serviceable company requires a recognized mounted commander, file or subunit leaders, experienced rider-horse pairs, instructors or veterans, inspection responsibility, rally signals, and a clear relationship to household and army command.

Leader loss reduces escort precision, target selection, contact control, breakaway, pursuit recall, and reform before causing automatic collapse.

## 25. Formation Scale and Footprint

The formation should be smaller than principal infantry and likely compact relative to broader practical cavalry. It needs lateral space, depth, a rear route, and room to reform.

Understrength companies shorten frontage or reduce depth. They do not preserve full coverage through elite status. Escort detachments remove actual riders, horses, leaders, and equipment from the field card.

### 25A. Detachment and Escort Accounting

A small escort detached from the company removes actual riders, horses, leaders, and equipment. Detachments may reduce field frontage, guard coverage, command depth, and recovery.

The battle card should not spawn free bodyguards, messengers, scouts, or extraction teams. If the project later supports detachments, every detached element must reconcile with the parent company.

### 26A. Formation Geometry and Guard Position

Mounted Huskarle need enough lateral distance to intercept without blocking the protected formation and enough rear space to turn, reform, or cover withdrawal. Common guard positions include the threatened flank, rear-quarter route, and offset reserve.

A close escort has less acceleration room but faster response. An offset reserve has better countercharge alignment but may arrive late. A wide screen weakens density and command. There is no perfect guard posture.

## 27. Signature Battle Cycle

1. Guard command, the household flank, or a critical withdrawal route.
2. Screen the immediate threat with Hestfolk or terrain where available.
3. Identify a cavalry collision or exposed target worth committing household strength.
4. Confirm ground, horse condition, missile threat, support, and exit.
5. Countercharge or close under control.
6. Fight the bounded mounted melee with axe and shield.
7. Break contact before spears, heavy cavalry, or reserves fix the company.
8. Reform near the protected object or rally point.
9. Recover horses, files, shields, and command.
10. Pursue only after the primary duty is safe.

## 28. Guard and Escort Contract

The card's escort identity is physical, not an aura. Mounted Huskarle can maintain a moving screen, intercept a flanking rider group, occupy the threatened side of a command route, and cover extraction.

They cannot overlap the protected formation, see threats through fog, or block missiles and cavalry from every direction. Guarding reduces freedom to chase targets.

### 28A. Guard Success and Failure Outcomes

- **Threat deterred:** enemy refuses or redirects without contact.
- **Clean interception:** Mounted Huskarle meet the threat with usable alignment before it reaches the protected object.
- **Late interception:** contact occurs too close to the protected object, reducing maneuver and increasing spillover risk.
- **Mutual displacement:** both cavalry bodies lose cohesion and the guard mission remains uncertain.
- **Guard breach:** the enemy passes, pins, or draws away the company.
- **Successful extraction:** the protected formation escapes despite Mounted Huskarle losses.

Guard success is measured by mission outcome as well as casualties inflicted.

## 29. Countercharge Contract

Countercharge depends on warning, facing, space, horse condition, alignment, support, and enemy quality. The company is strongest meeting light-to-medium mounted threats or disrupting cavalry already committed elsewhere.

Against organized Riddere-class or comparable heavy shock cavalry, Mounted Huskarle need favorable timing, terrain, flank position, or support. They should not win a clean heavy-cavalry collision by name alone.

## 30. Mounted Melee Contract

Mounted Huskarle may remain in contact longer than Hestfolk because shields, household cohesion, equipment, and axe practice support close fighting. That advantage is bounded.

Prolonged melee compresses horse movement, exposes flanks, breaks pairings, tires riders, and invites spears or reserves. The company should disengage once the protected objective is safe or the tactical advantage disappears.

### 30A. Mounted-Melee Phases

1. **Contact entry:** preserve shield, horse control, and pair spacing.
2. **Axe engagement:** strike exposed riders and horses while avoiding uncontrolled compression.
3. **Local dominance:** push lighter or disordered cavalry away from the protected lane.
4. **Threat reassessment:** identify spears, heavy reserves, fatigue, and exit collapse.
5. **Breakaway or bounded finish:** disengage, cover extraction, or complete the local fight.
6. **Reform:** restore pairs, leaders, axes, shields, and guard orientation.

Remaining in melee after local dominance is achieved should normally be a mistake.

## 31. Charge and Impact

The card delivers credible local impact but is not a pure shock unit. Clean approach, pace, alignment, target disorder, and flank access matter. Impact can be clean, partial, glancing, stalled, or aborted.

A successful charge creates an opportunity for axe-and-shield melee. It does not guarantee rout, armor penetration, trampling, or shield destruction.

## 33. Foot Infantry Relationship

Friendly spears create cavalry-safe rally space and stop enemy charges. Crossbowmen and bowmen disrupt targets. Foot Huskarle and Herremaend Axemen can create infantry disorder. Vaebnere and Landsmaend secure ground after mounted action.

Mounted Huskarle cannot charge through friendly files or substitute for spear frontage. Combined arms are physical relationships, not composition bonuses.

## 34. Dismounted Boundary

Mounted Huskarle may dismount because horses are lost, terrain forbids riding, or a specific guard assignment requires foot action. This does not create a second Foot Huskarle card or preserve full mounted statistics.

Emergency dismounted fighters should use source-valid one-handed axes, shields, and sidearms. The established Foot Huskarle two-handed assault card remains a separate assignment and equipment identity.

### 34A. Emergency Dismount and Remount Integrity

Live dismount and remount remain deferred. If emergency dismount is represented, riders require safe space, time, horse control, and an assignment for the mounts. Dismounted soldiers use one-handed axes and shields rather than transforming into two-handed Foot Huskarle.

Remounting requires surviving suitable horses, rider-horse reconciliation, tack, leaders, and space. It is not an instant stance switch.

## 35. Fatigue and Horse Condition

Rider and horse fatigue remain distinct. Armor, repeated acceleration, close mounted melee, heat, mud, hills, transport, wounds, and poor fodder reduce charge, weapon control, turning, and breakaway.

Veterans pace the company better, but repeated contact cannot be cycled without cost.

## 36. Morale and Cohesion

Confidence may benefit from household association, recognized leaders, familiar pairs, equipment, and a clear guard mission. It can collapse after leader loss, horse casualties, isolation, failed protection, blocked retreat, political conflict, or belief that the protected household is lost.

Household identity grants neither fanaticism nor immunity to surrender.

## 37. Terrain and Weather

Firm open ground supports movement but exposes the company to missiles and heavy cavalry. Rolling ground can mask a countercharge if alignment survives. Sparse woodland limits lanes; dense woodland defeats coherent mounted melee. Marsh, mud, steep slopes, bridges, gates, walls, and narrow streets restrict formation and exit.

Rain, snow, ice, heat, fog, and seasonal fodder affect horses, tack, visibility, fatigue, and recovery through actual conditions, not national bonuses.

### 37A. Terrain Decision Matrix

| Context | Mounted Huskarle decision |
|---|---|
| Firm open ground | Good interception and reform, but exposed to missiles and heavier cavalry |
| Rolling terrain | Can mask guard reserve if visibility and alignment survive |
| Farmland and lanes | Useful bounded routes; fences and ditches may trap breakaway |
| Sparse woodland | Limited interception lanes and divided command |
| Dense woodland | Mounted card usually loses its core value |
| Mud or marsh | Horse fatigue, compression, and failed disengagement risk rise |
| Bridge or ford | Guard receiving side or route; avoid frontal mounted commitment in the crossing |
| Broad street | Conditional interception and extraction if exits remain open |
| Narrow street or gate | Emergency mounted use at best; dismount or reserve may be preferable |
| Shore landing | Restore pairing and formation before combat readiness |

## 38. Siege and Settlement Use

Strong uses include outer escort, relief-battle reserve, sortie cover, broad-street interception, gate extraction, and pursuit after a besieging force breaks.

Poor uses include ladders, wall assault, narrow breaches, deep streets, bridge frontal attacks, and prolonged investment without horse support. Dismounted site duty is a separate state.

## 39. Maritime and Island Deployment

Denmark's maritime geography does not make cavalry transport frictionless. Overseas service requires horse-capable vessels, loading, handlers, feed, water, tack, secure landing, shore assembly, routes, recovery, and return.

Embarked riders are not a cavalry card. The company becomes mounted-ready only after rider-horse pairing, equipment, files, and command are restored ashore.

## 40. Campaign Opportunity Cost

Fielding Mounted Huskarle may reduce foot Huskarle availability, Royal or magnate household security, escorts, mounted-retainer depth, horses, armor, officers, court or estate functions, and future Riddere candidates.

The company is strategically expensive even without exact currency values because it concentrates scarce household people and horses in one battlefield expression.

## 42. Supply and Support

The company requires rider food and shelter, horse fodder and water, tack, armor and shield repair, axes and sidearms, handlers, farriers or equivalent care, medical support, routes, camps, and household administration.

Grooms, servants, handlers, and carts remain campaign support rather than free battlefield combatants.

### 42A. Household Support and Remount Burden

Remounts are not a hidden second unit. Reserve horses require ownership, suitability, transport, feed, handlers, protection, and pairing. A baggage or horse-line attack can reduce future readiness even if the visible battle card escapes.

Losing grooms, handlers, tools, spare tack, or feed does not instantly kill the company, but it can block normal recovery and future movement.

## 43. Casualties, Capture, and Ransom

Track riders and horses separately as fit, wounded, killed, missing, captured, dispersed, returned, serviceable, injured, lost, or recovered. High-status or household-connected prisoners may enter ransom and exchange systems.

Captured axes, shields, armor, tack, and horses require custody and disposition. Victory does not automatically restore them.

Replacing both rider and horse produces the weakest recovery case.

### 44A. Post-Battle Receipt

The campaign receipt should return separate states for riders, leaders, horses, pairings, axes, shields, armor, tack, captives, ransom candidates, protected-object outcome, guard success, pursuit discipline, household debit, handlers, routes, and formation memory.

A tactically victorious company may still fail its guard mission. A costly extraction may be a strategic success despite poor casualty exchange.

## 45. Persistence and Recruitment Closure

Recruitment may close after household decline, political rupture, horse shortage, equipment loss, or institutional change while surviving formations remain. Veterans may recover, merge around surviving cadres where lawful, return to foot service, or demobilize.

Closing recruitment does not delete survivors. Reopening access does not instantly restore pairings, leaders, trust, horses, or equipment.

## 47. Native and Mercenary Boundary

Mercenary axe cavalry may overlap tactically but uses an external company, contract, replacement path, and departure risk. Mounted Huskarle depend on Denmark's own household people, horses, equipment, politics, and recovery.

Hiring a foreign company does not create the native institution. Native development does not remove the urgent utility of hired cavalry.

### 47A. Development Without Card Proliferation

Improved helmets, mail, shields, tack, horses, or household display should normally remain equipment and company states. Create another cavalry card only if the source, service, weapon loop, horse requirement, battlefield role, and recovery bargain all change materially.

Mounted Huskarle should not split into Early, Armored, Veteran, Royal, or Magnate cards merely because equipment or ownership varies.

## 48. Campaign AI

AI recruits when household people, horses, equipment, leaders, patronage, support, and recovery exist and when guard cavalry or contact persistence fills a real army need.

AI avoids the card when Hestfolk can solve routine mobility, Mounted Retainers fill the broader household role, Riddere are needed for decisive shock, foot Huskarle are more valuable, or household security and horse sources are depleted.

### 48A. Campaign AI Composition Test

AI should compare Mounted Huskarle against:

- additional Foot Huskarle for assault;
- Hestfolk for routine mobility;
- Mounted Retainers for broad household cavalry;
- Riddere for decisive shock;
- Hirdmen or Royal Spearguard for foot command protection;
- household security and horse recovery.

Unlock status alone is never a recruitment reason.

### 49A. Battlefield AI Guard Logic

AI should remain near the protected object until a real threat appears, prefer interception over pursuit, and reassess after every contact. It must abandon a tempting exposed target when leaving would open the command route.

AI can abort an approach if spears appear, heavier cavalry aligns, horse condition falls, or the exit closes. Higher difficulty improves evaluation without hidden bonuses.

## 50. Autoresolve and Continuity

Autoresolve considers mission, protected object, rider and horse quality, pairing, equipment, leaders, formation, fatigue, terrain, missiles, enemy spears, cavalry weight, support, and exit space.

Elite or Huscarl labels cannot erase numbers, encirclement, heavy shock, crossbows, or poor ground. Results return actual rider, horse, equipment, capture, cohesion, fatigue, provider, and recovery consequences.

## 51. UI and Accessibility

Recruitment UI shows owner, eligible people, competing foot and mounted assignments, horses, pairing, axes, shields, protection, tack, leaders, support, household debit, and dominant blocker.

Battle UI shows guard relationship, horse fatigue, alignment, contact state, shield and axe condition, exit risk, reform, and recall. Silhouette, icons, text, animation, and audio supplement color.

### 51A. Player-Facing Status Language

Suggested compact battle statuses:

- Guarding
- Threat on protected flank
- Aligning countercharge
- Mounted melee
- Exit compromised
- Horses fatigued
- Shields depleted
- Reforming
- Guard assignment failed

These states should remain concise and should not expose internal simulation ledgers during battle.

## 52. Visual Direction

Distance read: compact protected household cavalry with one-handed axes, mounted shields, practical helmets, and a guard-reserve posture.

Close read: repaired mail and textile protection, varied but compatible shields, fitted tack, household marks, sidearms, campaign wear, and visible rider-horse variation.

Avoid two-handed axes swung unrealistically from horseback, universal mail or barding, fantasy oversized horses, identical royal livery, horned helmets, and a palette swap of Norway's Household Axeriders or England's Saxon Axe Horsemen.

## 53. Animation and Audio

Animations include mounted column, escort arc, countercharge alignment, axe-ready approach, shield reaction, controlled impact, mounted axe melee, wheel, breakaway, reform, pursuit recall, horse fatigue, rider separation, mount injury, surrender, and emergency dismount.

Audio communicates tack, pace, breathing, commands, shield and axe contact, horse distress, rally, recall, and household identity without fantasy metal noise.

### 53A. Denmark-Specific Visual Separation

Mounted Huskarle should visually sit between Mounted Retainers and Riddere while remaining connected to Foot Huskarle:

- more axe-forward and guard-oriented than Mounted Retainers;
- less lance-centered, horse-heavy, heraldic, and standardized than Riddere;
- mounted shield and one-handed axe rather than the Foot Huskarle two-handed assault silhouette;
- stronger household marks and protection consistency than Hestfolk;
- practical Danish campaign wear rather than pristine fantasy armor.
