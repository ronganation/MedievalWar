Norse Mercenary Swordsmen are the Theater's dependable paid melee infantry. They close with ordinary enemy foot, exploit disorder, support a threatened flank, and reform before the fight spreads beyond their control. They cost more than comparable domestic swordsmen because they can be hired immediately from the mercenary pool and arrive as an existing company.

They sit between Viking Raiders and heavier axemen. Raiders are faster and more offensive against soft targets. Axemen apply greater shield and armor pressure. The swordsmen provide the balanced middle: stronger sustained melee, better defense and reform, and fewer extreme matchups.

## 2. Unit Identity Strip

- **Class:** sword-and-shield mercenary infantry
- **Era:** Early origin, persistent across later eras
- **Quality:** seasoned common hired infantry
- **Battle role:** flexible close combat and counterattack
- **Best use:** ordinary infantry melee, line reinforcement, flank fighting, streets, gates, camps, and exploiting enemies fixed by spears
- **Weakness:** cavalry, strong missiles, long spears, heavy axes, elite infantry, and high upkeep
- **Recruitment:** regional Norse mercenary pool
- **Economic bargain:** immediate melee capability at premium cost
- **Free upkeep:** none by default
- **Exact values:** tuning pending

## 3. One-Screen Player Contract

### Battlefield

- Engage ordinary enemy infantry with a balanced sword-and-shield formation.
- Exploit enemies fixed by spearmen or disrupted by missiles and raiders.
- Turn and reform more effectively than close spear formations.
- Hold longer than light raiders, but avoid heavy cavalry and specialized shock infantry.
- Use a short controlled push rather than an endless charge.

### Campaign

- Hire immediately when an appropriate company is in the regional pool.
- Pay more than for a comparable domestic sword unit.
- Replenish through compatible mercenary pools.
- Retain veterans when their experience justifies the expense.
- Dismiss surplus companies when peace or domestic recruitment makes them inefficient.

## 4. Player-Facing Card

### Short description

Seasoned northern sword-and-shield hirelings who fight effectively in a sustained melee, exploit disorder, and reform for the next engagement.

### Encyclopedia description

Norse Mercenary Swordsmen are paid companies built around practical close combat. Their shields help them survive the approach and stand in a crowded fight, while swords and occasional sidearm variation give them the flexibility to attack, defend, and change facing more readily than a spear line. Some have served several employers; others are newly gathered around a captain with enough reputation to attract experienced men.

They are best used after missiles, spearmen, or raiders create a favorable engagement. They can reinforce a wavering line, press an ordinary infantry fight, clear a street, or counterattack an enemy that has lost order. They are not armored champions. Heavy cavalry, long spears, elite infantry, powerful axes, and concentrated missiles remain serious threats.

### Flavor line

**The sword is theirs. The hour is yours, provided the silver is good.**

### Strengths

- balanced attack and defense;
- good sustained melee for an early mercenary;
- shield protection;
- useful turning, counterattack, and reform;
- immediate regional hire;
- effective in streets and constrained infantry fights.

### Weaknesses

- high cost and upkeep;
- limited anti-cavalry value;
- only moderate armor;
- vulnerable to long spears and heavy axes;
- less burst damage than shock infantry;
- pool-dependent replacement.

## 15. Formation Tradeoffs

- **Open melee line:** turns and advances better, but has weaker mutual shield support.
- **Close shielded line:** resists ordinary frontal pressure and missiles better, but becomes slower and easier to flank.
- **Counterattack files:** concentrate fresh fighters through a local gap, but risk overextension.
- **Guarded reserve:** preserves cohesion and fatigue, but contributes no immediate frontage.
- **Street front:** powerful where walls protect flanks, dangerous where missiles or a stronger force controls the approach.
- **Emergency ring:** may delay collapse under multiple threats, but cannot maneuver or break out easily.

## 25. Preferred Tactical Tasks

- Engage ordinary infantry that lacks a specialist advantage.
- Counterattack enemies fixed on Norse Mercenary Spearmen.
- Protect or recover Northern Woodland Bowmen after displacement.
- Support Viking Raiders once the first assault loses momentum.
- Fight through wide streets, gates, camps, and captured approaches.
- Hold a reserve behind the line and close a sudden gap.
- Exploit a wavering flank without pursuing beyond support.

## 26. Poor Tactical Tasks

- Receive cavalry without spear support.
- Attack an intact long-spear front.
- Trade missiles in the open.
- Break heavily armored infantry through sword power alone.
- Chase mounted or foot skirmishers across open terrain.
- Lead repeated ladder or breach assaults.
- Continue pushing after flanks and files lose contact.

## 30. Terrain Behavior

Open ground gives room to reform but exposes the unit to cavalry and missiles. Broken ground limits enemy cavalry but can fragment company command. Sparse woods suit close infantry better than spear formations, while dense woods still damage signals and mutual support. Farmland, streets, gates, camps, and settlement edges give the unit useful bounded melee spaces. Marsh, deep mud, steep slopes, crossings, and landing disorder reduce the controlled engagement that defines the card.

## 32. Crossings and Chokepoints

Swordsmen are better than spearmen after a crossing collapses into close combat, but worse at denying cavalry or holding reach. On defense they should normally stand behind or beside spears, then counterattack enemies compressed at the exit. On offense they require missiles, shields, or another formation to absorb the initial exposure.

## 47. Visual Identity

Practical sword-and-shield companies with mixed but maintained equipment. Round shields dominate early silhouettes. Swords must be clearly visible without making every blade identical. Helmets are common but not universal. Mail is concentrated among captain, veterans, and wealthier members. Company colors and shield rims provide internal identity without faction uniformity.

Avoid horned helmets, fantasy fur armor, universal mail, oversized swords, berserker imagery, and presenting the unit as impoverished rabble.

## 101. Detailed Player Decision Examples

### Emergency reinforcement

A faction army has missiles, cavalry, and damaged spearmen but lacks dependable close infantry. The swordsmen are worth hiring because they can enter service immediately, guard the missile line, counterattack behind surviving spears, and keep the campaign moving. Their premium cost buys operational time.

### Strong domestic roster

A faction already possesses rested, experienced domestic sword infantry and has access to cheap replenishment. The mercenary company may not be efficient unless the army needs another formation immediately, operates far from home, or wants to preserve domestic troops for another front.

### Veteran retention

A veteran company with a known captain, high experience, and maintained equipment may remain worth its high upkeep because replacing its cohesion and battlefield judgment would take longer than replacing raw headcount. Retention is a strategic choice, not an automatic upgrade into a faction unit.

### Peacetime burden

Several active companies can turn a previously secure treasury into a deficit. The player must choose between dismissal, continued readiness, transfer to an active front, or investment in later mechanics that reduce contract burden without granting ordinary free upkeep.
