Viking Raiders are source-bound hired or released fighting bands used for brief offensive work. Their promise is Strike, Disrupt, Withdraw: approach through a screened route, attack an exposed troop body or weak point, create confusion or displacement, and leave before disciplined resistance fixes them. They are not weak Drengja, melee Skogmenn, generic swordsmen with a coastal bonus, or free Viking-age manpower. Their value comes from tempo, target selection, flexible light protection, practiced internal leadership, and a real ability to stop the attack and rally.

The unit should feel dangerous when used correctly but expendable only to a careless commander. It must remain capable of winning a favorable local fight while failing badly against prepared spears, heavier infantry, cavalry, concentrated missiles, a closed exit, or a fresh reserve.

## 4. Player-Facing Card and Guidance

### Short description

Fast hired melee raiders who punish exposed troops and weakened flanks, but lose their advantage when cavalry, missiles, or disciplined infantry pin them in prolonged combat.

### Encyclopedia description

Viking Raiders are contracted or otherwise validly released Norse fighting bands assembled from exact sources rather than a universal Scandinavian pool. They carry practical swords, axes, knives, spears, and shields according to their source and fight through speed, aggression, small-unit familiarity, and brief local concentration rather than heavy armor or formal line discipline.

On the battlefield they move behind a screen or along a viable flank, select a soft or already occupied target, strike hard enough to disrupt it, and withdraw before reserves arrive. They are valuable against exposed archers, camp defenders, disordered light infantry, and vulnerable flanks. They are poor against fresh spears, heavy infantry, cavalry, sustained missile fire, or any position whose exit can be sealed.

### Strengths

- rapid offensive repositioning by light infantry standards;
- strong pressure against exposed missiles and support troops;
- useful flank attack against an enemy already fixed;
- effective bounded pursuit and camp-edge disruption;
- lower equipment burden than heavy mercenary infantry;
- strong classic mercenary-raider readability.

### Weaknesses

- light and variable protection;
- poor prolonged frontage;
- vulnerable to cavalry and concentrated missiles;
- unreliable against fresh heavy or spear infantry;
- pursuit can break cohesion and close the return lane;
- replacement remains external and source-bound;
- contract, route, and source obligations limit availability.

### Player guidance

Keep the band screened until a valuable target is exposed. Confirm its support and exit, attack briefly, then rally behind steadier infantry. Do not let a successful first charge become an uncontrolled chase or a grinding line fight.

## 18. Target Selection, Counterplay, and Failure Modes

**Preferred targets:** exposed archers and crossbowmen; isolated support personnel; disordered or tired light infantry; an occupied flank; weak camp or baggage defenders where contract and law permit; broken troops within a controlled pursuit limit.

**Conditional targets:** medium infantry fixed by friendly frontage; settlement outskirts with clear exits; landing-edge defenders after an actual landing; an enemy reserve still forming; a rear area objective that can be abandoned or handed off.

**Poor targets:** fresh spear or polearm frontage; formed heavy infantry; protected missiles; cavalry with maneuver room; deep reserves; defended walls, gates, bridges, or narrow streets; targets whose defeat leaves no safe exit.
