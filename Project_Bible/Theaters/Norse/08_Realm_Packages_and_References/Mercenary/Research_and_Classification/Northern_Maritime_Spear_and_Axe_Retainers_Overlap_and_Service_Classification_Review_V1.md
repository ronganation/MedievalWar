## 1. Review Verdict

Do not admit Northern Maritime Spear and Axe Retainers as a distinct unit under its current name and definition.

Do not reject the broader Norse hired-infantry role. Preserve that role as an open Theater content need that may later be fulfilled by:

- a historically supported mercenary infantry source;
- a broad mercenary family with Norse regional presentation and bounded access;
- a source-owned regional formation available through contract or external service;
- one canonical unit family referenced through several service relationships;
- an existing Realm or legacy concept after proper reconciliation;
- a later replacement candidate with clearer chronology and identity.

The present concept is too broad and overlaps too heavily with existing military families. Its failure does not mean the Norse Theater should lack recognizable hired infantry.

## 2. Source Concept Preserved

The draft proposes a compact dismounted hired following for expeditions, landings, escort work, rearguard duty, and difficult defensive situations. Its intended identity is seasoned close infantry rather than raiding spectacle. Proposed equipment includes spears, hand axes, mixed shields, and varied protection. The source already identifies itself as a creative draft, leaves source and service classification unresolved, and establishes a merge-or-reject condition if defensive hired service cannot be evidenced.

Useful design elements retained for future comparison:

- compact hired infantry;
- dependable close combat rather than shock spectacle;
- escort and rearguard utility;
- landing-perimeter and chokepoint usefulness;
- visible logistics and transport burden;
- finite personnel and replacement;
- conditional rather than universal access;
- practical, varied presentation rather than uniform Viking styling.

Unsupported elements remain quarantined:

- the working name;
- a coherent northern maritime source population;
- a captain-led company;
- a permanent spear-and-axe formation;
- opening availability;
- precise equipment, armor, cost, pool, or geographic access.

## 4. Comparison Set

### 4.1 Norway Ship Crews and Maritime Fighters

The Norway concept covers recruits from seafaring communities, vessel and crew owners, coastal households, and regional contribution systems. Its functions include transport, boarding, landing support, raiding, and infantry service ashore. It is not a production unit and creates no fixed crew or force state.

Overlap with the candidate:

- maritime source imagery;
- landing and infantry service;
- escort or expeditionary context;
- ships, crews, supply, repair, and home-defense opportunity costs.

Boundary:

A hired infantry role must not duplicate ordinary Norway crew or contribution capability. Maritime employment alone cannot separate the candidate.

### 4.2 Denmark Regional Muster Spears

The Denmark concept covers defensive field line, coastal defense, and reinforcement through regional contributions. It explicitly separates contribution, delivery, command, and readiness.

Overlap with the candidate:

- spear infantry;
- coastal or crossing defense;
- regional concentration;
- line-stiffening and reinforcement.

Boundary:

Mercenary status, external service, or a distinct source relationship must provide more than a hired label. Otherwise the candidate duplicates regional muster infantry.

### 4.3 Sweden Assembly Muster Contribution

The Sweden concept covers regional and assembly-mediated defensive contribution without creating a production unit.

Overlap with the candidate:

- regionally sourced foot;
- defensive line support;
- conditional mobilization;
- local political and logistical dependencies.

Boundary:

A general Norse foot concept must not flatten Sweden's separate regional and assembly institutions into one pan-Norse hired pool.

### 4.4 Legacy Norse Raiders

The legacy file presents fast light mercenary infantry for raids, harassment, and ambushes, with fixed statistics, costs, recruitment conditions, and Norse-territory access.

Overlap with the candidate:

- generic Norse mercenary identity;
- coastal military use;
- light or medium infantry space;
- swords, axes, shields, and mobile employment.

Potential distinction:

- Northern Maritime Retainers emphasize compact defense, escort, formation, and line stability rather than speed, harassment, and ambush.

Boundary:

The legacy unit's exact statistics, costs, events, infrastructure, and geographic access are not current authority. Its raiding role remains a comparison archetype, not an adopted unit.

### 4.5 Viking Axemen

The attached file contains only its filename. It confirms that an older concept under that name exists, but it supplies no role, equipment, chronology, service form, or authority status for substantive comparison.

### 4.6 Jutland Wagon Pioneers

The Jutland review redirects that concept to campaign engineering and protected-work capability. Its escort component should come from existing forces rather than create another hired close-infantry card.

Boundary:

Northern Maritime Retainers should not absorb engineering or work-party protection as a defining identity merely to manufacture distinction.

## 5. External Game and Mod Benchmarks

### 5.1 Viking Mercenaries

The Stainless Steel reference presents Scandinavian mercenary axe infantry recruited across northern regions. It demonstrates a recognizable broad hired-infantry archetype with Norse flavor.

Design use:

- supports retaining a comprehensible Norse mercenary infantry role in the design space;
- provides no historical authority for this candidate's name, exact source, equipment, access, or chronology;
- shows the danger of defining the unit almost entirely through inherited Viking imagery.

### 5.2 Hunters

The Stainless Steel reference presents low-quality bow-using local mercenaries available across northern and eastern Europe.

Design use:

- demonstrates that a mercenary ecosystem may include ordinary local capability rather than only elite cultural units;
- suggests that Norse mercenary flavor need not be limited to axe infantry;
- provides no direct support for Northern Maritime Retainers.

### 5.3 Mercenary Crossbowmen

The Stainless Steel reference presents a broadly available professional missile-company archetype, including Scandinavian availability in its game design.

Design use:

- demonstrates role-first mercenary coverage with regional availability;
- supports considering later or development-gated Norse access to a shared crossbow family if separately justified;
- does not establish opening 1062 access or a Norse-specific crossbow company.

### 5.4 Sámi Axemen

The Medieval II Kingdoms reference presents Sámi heavy axe infantry recruited by Norwegian generals.

Design use:

- identifies a separate regional identity question that must remain independently owned and researched;
- cannot be folded into a generic northern maritime unit;
- the reference's descriptive framing is not adopted as historical authority or as a basis for behavioral traits.

### 5.5 Berserkers

The Attila Age of Charlemagne reference presents Danish melee infantry built around a berserk shock role.

Design use:

- demonstrates a distinct shock-infantry archetype rather than a mercenary service proof;
- remains separate from compact defensive hired infantry;
- does not establish a January 1062 mercenary pool or justify permanent Viking-age styling.

## 10. Household, Allied, and External Service Test

Household retainers already provide experienced close infantry and personal service. Allied contingents and external service can bring source-owned forces into another employer's campaign. The candidate must therefore demonstrate why a temporary hired formation is distinct from these routes.

```yaml
service_route_result:
  household_following: HighOverlap
  allied_contingent: PossibleAlternative
  external_service: PossibleAlternative
  mercenary_contract: PreferredResearchHypothesis
  regional_recruitment: UnsupportedAtPresent
  ordinary_realm_recruitment: HighDuplicationRisk
```

If research finds only household or allied service, the candidate should be redirected to a relationship authority rather than retained as a mercenary unit.

## 11. Meaningful Difference Requirements

A future replacement or successor candidate must establish several of the following:

- an identifiable source that historically or plausibly supplied temporary external military service;
- a service relationship materially different from household obligation, levy contribution, alliance, or ordinary crew duty;
- a battlefield role centered on disciplined close defense, escort, rearguard, or landing security rather than generic raiding;
- equipment and protection appropriate to the source and period;
- command, cohesion, duration, and withdrawal characteristics tied to the service form;
- finite replacement and collision with local defense, shipping, household, and regional obligations;
- bounded regional access and cross-Theater circulation;
- a player-facing identity that communicates source and role without permanent Viking stereotype.

Spear and axe equipment alone is insufficient.

## 12. Recommended Future Content Strategy

### 12.1 Preserve familiar mercenary coverage

The Norse ecosystem may eventually include familiar roles comparable to other Theaters:

- affordable local missile hire;
- general hired infantry;
- heavier or more experienced contract infantry;
- professional missile service where chronology and contact support it;
- maritime or cross-Theater hired forces;
- displaced, returned, or demobilized service generated by campaign events.

These are design categories, not approved units.

### 12.2 Apply Norse flavor through source and conditions

Regional flavor should arise through:

- supported source identity;
- appropriate equipment and protection;
- maritime or regional service routes;
- finite replacement and seasonal movement;
- cross-Theater opportunities;
- interactions with ships, ports, households, assemblies, and regional obligations;
- visual and linguistic presentation after evidence review.

Flavor should not be created solely by adding "Norse," "Viking," "northern," axes, fur, or a coastal bonus to a generic unit.

### 12.3 Prefer shared families where appropriate

A familiar mercenary role may use one shared canonical family with Norse-specific relationship, availability, presentation, and development references if the underlying military content is substantially the same. A separate Norse definition is justified only when multiple meaningful differences survive review.
