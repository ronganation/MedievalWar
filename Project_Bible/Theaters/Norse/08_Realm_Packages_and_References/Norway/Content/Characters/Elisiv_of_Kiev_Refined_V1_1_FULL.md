## Core Character Thesis

Elisiv of Kiev is the international dynastic and diplomatic pillar of Harald Hardrada's royal household. Her value comes not from battlefield command or a universal influence score, but from royal experience, Rurik lineage, Christian court formation, marriage to Harald, motherhood of Ingegerd and Maria, and access to political worlds beyond Norway.

Her central strategic tension is whether she can convert international access and queenly position into durable support for House Hardrada without overstating foreign commitments, displacing domestic noble interests, or allowing court access to be mistaken for government authority. Elisiv may become a diplomatic queen, regency participant, court matriarch, cultural patron, dynastic strategist, royal widow, political exile, or bridge between courts according to campaign state.

## Historical Identity

Elisiv of Kiev, also known as Elisaveta Yaroslavna and Ellisif, is a daughter of Yaroslav the Wise and Ingegerd Olofsdotter. She belongs durably to the Rurik dynasty and is married to Harald Hardrada. At the campaign opening she is alive, Catholic, Queen Consort of Norway under the controlling Opening State and Title authorities, and resident within the Norwegian royal court.

Her durable family identity includes:

- birth into the ruling family of Kievan Rus';
- maternal descent connected to Swedish royalty;
- marriage to Harald before his established sole kingship in Norway;
- motherhood of Ingegerd Haraldsdotter and Maria Haraldsdotter;
- participation in the royal household alongside Harald, Tora Torbergsdatter, Magnus II, and Olaf III;
- a cross-realm identity that links Norway to Kievan Rus', Scandinavia, and wider Christian court culture.

Elisiv remains one canonical person. Her Rurik identity, marriage, queenly title, household role, Norwegian court culture, and participation in House Hardrada's continuity are related but distinct.

- Preserve House Hardrada's continuity.
- Protect Ingegerd and Maria.
- Maintain useful Rurik and international access.
- Support stable succession.
- Promote foreign action only when dynastic risk is controlled.
- Build workable cooperation with Tora, Magnus, Olaf, clergy, nobles, and officials.
- Use marriage diplomacy strategically rather than automatically.
- Seek clear authority before accepting government responsibility.
- Avoid destructive household factionalism.
- Preserve routes for negotiation, sanctuary, and recovery.

## Restraints

Elisiv's AI should not:

- create alliances from kinship;
- command armies or fleets without extraordinary valid development and authority;
- appoint herself regent;
- treat foreign birth as hostility to Norway;
- force rivalry with Tora;
- treat Magnus or Olaf as her dependents automatically;
- dispose of her daughters as diplomatic assets without separate agency and authority;
- assume complete information from correspondence or court access;
- support or oppose every war on principle;
- schedule the English expedition, widowhood, Maria's death, or return east;
- directly mutate authoritative state.

## Information boundary

Elisiv may plausibly receive information from Harald, her daughters, household members, envoys, clergy, merchants, scribes, foreign kin, Tora, Magnus, Olaf, and authorized councils. Information may be delayed, filtered, partisan, translated, intercepted, or false. She must not know private foreign intentions, hidden court alignments, battlefield facts, or future outcomes without valid evidence and access.

## Prohibited Opening Assumptions

- Elisiv is not regent at opening.
- Queen Consort is not identical to ruler, government, household owner, or dynasty head.
- Rurik kinship does not create automatic alliance, funds, troops, sanctuary, or knowledge.
- Marriage to Harald does not erase Elisiv's birth-dynasty identity.
- Elisiv and Tora do not begin in an automatic feud.
- Elisiv is not the biological mother of Magnus or Olaf under the primary interpretation.
- Court access does not create command, appointment power, or omniscience.
- Diplomatic inclination does not make every peace proposal wise or every war unacceptable.
- Ingegerd and Maria are separate people, not marriage resources owned by Elisiv.
- Elisiv is not guaranteed to join Harald's future English expedition.
- Maria's historical death is not scheduled.
- Elisiv is not guaranteed to become queen dowager, regent, matriarch, or return east.
- Later epithets and reputations are not opening Traits.
- Opening State defaults must not overwrite advanced campaign state.

## Migration and Cross-Realm Notes

- Use the canonical identity `Character.Norway.ElisivOfKiev` where an authorized registry reference is required.
- Elisiv of Kiev, Elisaveta Yaroslavna, Ellisif, and supported spelling variants refer to one person when source context resolves correctly.
- Display names and filenames are not primary identity.
- Do not create separate Norway, Kievan Rus', Byzantine, Swedish, or expeditionary versions of Elisiv.
- Birth dynasty, marriage, queenly title, household participation, and court culture must remain separate concepts.
- Legacy `DynasticReach`, `CourtInfluence`, universal Authority, universal Legitimacy, or flat relationship values must not remain authoritative character fields.
- Future regency, queen-dowager status, return, exile, alliance, patronage, or epithet fields must migrate to conditional and specialist-owned state.
- Relationship, marriage, household, regency, title, dynasty, patronage, and reputation concepts embedded in legacy prose must route to their proper owners.
- Legacy numeric attributes and decorative future-title lists are superseded as authoritative profile content. Their useful intent is preserved through qualitative readiness, conditional paths, access boundaries, and audience-specific reputation references.
- Ambiguous records using only `Elisiv`, `Elisaveta`, or `Elizabeth` require contextual resolution rather than blind aliasing.

## Player-Facing Summary

Elisiv of Kiev gives Norway access to a political world beyond the North Sea. She can support diplomacy, marriage strategy, court continuity, and a broader regency, but her family connections open negotiations rather than guarantee alliances. Her challenge is to turn queenly position and Rurik access into durable advantage while maintaining domestic cooperation and protecting her daughters from the risks created by Harald's ambitions.

## Final Design Summary

Elisiv of Kiev begins as Queen Consort of Norway, a member of the Rurik dynasty, wife of Harald Hardrada, mother of Ingegerd and Maria, and the royal household's principal international dynastic connector. Her power lies in access, experience, ceremony, correspondence, and strategic relationships, not battlefield command or universal influence. Elisiv may become a diplomat, regency participant, court matriarch, cultural patron, dynastic strategist, queen dowager, returnee, exile, or politically isolated figure according to campaign conditions. The profile preserves her source-grounded depth while routing title, dynasty, marriage, household, succession, governance, relationships, memory, patronage, and reputation to their proper authorities.
