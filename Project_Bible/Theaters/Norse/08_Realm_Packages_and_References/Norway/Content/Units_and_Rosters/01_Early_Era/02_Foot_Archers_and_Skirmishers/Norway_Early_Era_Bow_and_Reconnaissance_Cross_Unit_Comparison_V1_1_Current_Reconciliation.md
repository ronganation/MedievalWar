## 2. Bow-family battlefield comparison, not a quality ladder

### Leidang Bowmen
Leidang Bowmen own routine district missile coverage. Their advantage is broader potential availability and a lower institutional threshold, not free people or unlimited arrows.

### Bogsveinar
Bogsveinar own the trained intermediate archer decision. Their supported advantages are better fire discipline, target selection, ammunition conservation, controlled movement, formation cohesion, and withdrawal. They are scarcer and slower to replace. Their name grants no automatic range, damage, accuracy, firing speed, armor penetration, forest concealment, or melee advantage.

### Regional Bowmen
A regional card survives only when a distinct people source, service, equipment or training pattern, campaign cost, and battlefield decision remain visible. Otherwise preserve regional access, presentation, and logistics as a profile of an existing card.

### Royal Hird bow capability
Personal bow skill or a small contextual assignment may exist among actual Hird personnel and must debit Royal Hird protection and competing duties. The separate Hird Bowmen card is retired.

### Shared formation contract
Separate equipment, ammunition, proficiency, formation eligibility, readiness, officers, current execution, friendly obstruction, and withdrawal. The AI receives no perfect lane, ammunition exemption, or hidden knowledge.

## 3. Reconnaissance is an information service, not a fourth archer

A district archer who sees an enemy during battle does not automatically send a usable campaign report. A scout's value comes from a defined question, an available observer, actual access and observation, a messenger/recipient chain, uncertainty and return. The Norway route authority distinguishes route from access, knowledge from permission, messenger from message, dispatch from delivery, and delivery from receipt. A scout's warning may be delayed or wrong; neither the player nor AI should gain a global reveal because the support family exists.

**Distinct campaign scenarios for testing:**

1. **Coastal warning:** A real guide observes a validated landing approach and attempts to report to a named commander. Test observation quality, dispatch, receipt, decision timing and local warning lost during absence. Do not create a port, ship, enemy marker or army-capable route.
2. **Northern bow expedition:** A validated northern community may retain its qualified bow people for local defense or supply a distant host. The guide who knows a coastal leg, if any, must be separately assigned; the bow card does not confer universal navigation. Check ships, crew, landing, arrows, return and service terms independently.
3. **Royal position under threat:** The player weighs district bow coverage against bow-capable hird personnel. Reassigning hird reduces close guard; using district archers reduces district capacity. A guide's report can improve the decision only if it arrives and is understood. No extra royal bow pool.
4. **Battle-card temptation:** If an actual armed scout party is proposed, test screening, limited observation on the battle map, safe withdrawal, weapons, command and distinct counterplay against a normal district bow unit. If the only useful result is prebattle information, keep it as campaign support. The legacy Svenner light-horse template cannot be used to bypass a separate mounted-source and battle gate.

**Operational handoff, not implemented procedure:** observer/source owner establishes the person and service; route and information owners establish known endpoints, access and observation; communication owner handles dispatch and receipt; military owner uses the report subject to uncertainty; battle owner decides any tactical card. This file does not assert that these systems have been implemented.

## 4. Fieldability, resource accounting and AI

**Design stress case: source-rich, supported host.** Several validated districts should be able to provide useful bow support *alongside* spear frontage and a modest assault allocation if real qualified people, equipment, obligations and arrows exist. Do not make every ordinary bowman require an elite institution, a separate invented northern corps or a royal guard transfer. Finite pools should support a varied playable army rather than reduce Norway to a handful of elites. Exact source breadth and quantities remain owner-produced unknowns.

**Design stress case: depleted or distant host.** An exhausted district may lack bow-qualified replacements or arrows even while spear personnel remain; a bow stockpile does not create trained people. A northern unit may be locally available but unable to reach a distant host without validated route and lift. Royal bow duty may be blocked by urgent guard needs. Scouts may have no safe report path. Each blocker should be intelligible rather than a stack of invisible gates. The AI should choose district bows for routine coverage where available, protect hird for more consequential duties unless a real command-adjacent bow need exists, and request scouts only for a specific information gap; no AI-only free people or hidden knowledge.

**One reservation ledger concept for later owner review, not an existing implementation claim:** identify each actual source, available and committed qualified people, equipment issued, ammunition reserved/remaining, service term, home-defense allocation, current location, recovery and reassignment. Bowmen, spear/axe foot, crew, watch, scouts and hird profiles debit their own originating sources. A returned survivor does not become a fresh person; replacing lost arrows does not replenish casualties; a research unlock grants eligibility, not recruits. Population constrains finite recruitment pools without direct Rome-style population-as-army-manpower spending.

**Trained-middle stress case.** Bogsveinar become available only when selection, officers, instructors, collective practice, serviceable equipment, ammunition, provider support, and household or labor debits pass together. If those layers fail, use Leidang Bowmen where valid rather than giving Bogsveinar hidden statistics or a free elite-building pool.

## 5. Current disposition gates and tests

- **Leidang Bowmen:** preserve as the broad district missile card.
- **Bogsveinar:** preserve as the distinct trained intermediate archer. Separation must survive equal-equipment testing through reliability, organization, service, replacement, AI use, and player choice.
- **Haalogaland or other regional Bowmen:** remain deferred. Use a regional profile when source or battle behavior does not justify another card.
- **Royal Hird bow capability:** preserve individual equipment and small contextual assignment only.
- **Viking Scouts / Route Scouts and Guides:** preserve campaign support with named source, question, knowledge scope, access, observation, report chain, uncertainty, return, and opportunity cost.

### Paired tests not yet performed
- Leidang Bowmen versus Bogsveinar with comparable bows, arrows, terrain, protection, and targets.
- Fire discipline, target selection, ammunition use, movement, withdrawal, officer loss, and replacement dilution.
- Regional candidate versus admitted bow families with source and route costs visible.
- Royal Hird contextual bow use versus lost protection and competing duties.
- Armed scout party versus ordinary infantry and bow formations in a genuine observation or screening scenario.
- Friendly obstruction, counterfire, cavalry pressure, arrow depletion, failed withdrawal, and AI preservation behavior.

**Decision criterion:** a separate card survives when source and assignment are accountable, battlefield behavior is materially distinct, campaign cost and replacement are visible, and the card adds an understandable army-building choice.
