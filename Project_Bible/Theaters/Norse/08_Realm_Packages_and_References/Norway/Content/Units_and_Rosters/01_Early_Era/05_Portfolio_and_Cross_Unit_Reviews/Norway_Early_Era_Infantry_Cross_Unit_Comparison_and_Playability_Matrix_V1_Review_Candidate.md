## 1. Playable army promise

A player should be able to assemble **more than a few elite squads**: a dependable district spear center, qualified bow support from the same constrained district ecosystem, an assault choice that takes men away from that center, optional inland and local protection where sources exist, and a narrow but effective royal or negotiated noble reserve. The force can grow through multiple districts, assemblies, political agreements, equipment and shipping capacity. The game should show useful availability and recovery without making the player administer each individual spear. No single new card is mandatory merely to hit a roster-size target.

Three independent questions govern every card: (1) Is there a valid source and currently unreserved people? (2) Does this weapon/formation/assignment change a battlefield or campaign decision? (3) Is its availability sufficient for its intended player role after equipment, officers, obligations, local defense, crew needs and transport are counted? The first two prevent duplication; the third prevents overrestriction. If the third fails, improve the *supported* source's usability or reconsider an unjustified gate rather than fabricate a pool.

## 3. Formation execution comparison, not a universal upgrade ladder

The shared formation standard distinguishes access from proficiency and current state. Evaluate **entry time**, **interruption**, **maintenance**, **turning**, **casualty-gap response**, **disorder recovery**, **command response**, **fatigue cost**, and **weapon compatibility** separately. The proposed comparative bands below are hypotheses for a later battle-owner matrix, not current Norway values.

| Candidate or expression | Prepared spear/brace | Shielded close order | Form/hold/reform expectation to test | Assault and reserve tradeoff |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Local Watch | Only with compatible local spear kit | Not default | Can hold a known short approach, but weak on extended field maneuvers; no free wall | Remain to protect site versus reassign to army |
| Upland Spearmen | Conditional prepared frontage | Conditional if actual shields and practice | Compare against Leidang on *same* terrain/kit; distinctness should come from source and regional mission, not magic hill rating | Preserve home approach versus field response |
| Leidang Spearmen | Candidate basic prepared spear brace | Conditional basic shielded order; no universal advanced proficiency | More credible than emergency foot at holding a district line; turning and reform vary with officers and veteran continuity | Broad frontage versus district men allocated to bow, axe, crew or home duty |
| Experienced/reorganized Leidang **state, not ninth opening unit** | Same spear family, potentially improved execution | More reliable only through surviving cadre, kit and practice | Candidate faster reform and better gap handling than inexperienced members of *same source*; no new people | Keep veterans in old formation or reorganize with actual personnel debit |
| Leidang Axemen | No spear brace | Compatible axe-and-shield order only | Entry and recovery judged for timed close assault; not simply stronger spear wall | Assault punch versus less district spear frontage |
| Landsmenn, if independent | Depends on verified actual weapons | Conditional | Must outperform an ordinary district line at a *specific* transition or support task, without duplicating veteran status; otherwise profile | Flexible relief versus higher source and equipment burden |
| Noble Drengja | Only if actual spear subgroup | Conditional shielded assault | Cohesion may be strong within one following but coordination with royal command can fail; do not award universal superiority | Magnate cooperation versus royal-guard deployment |
| Royal Hirdmen spear/shield expression | Candidate strong if kit, training and order support it | Strong **candidate**, not blanket access | Test dependable crisis entry, controlled relief and reform; still exposed to flanks, fatigue, missiles and command loss | Hold household security versus field reserve |
| Hird Axeguard | No spear brace in axe state | One-handed shield variant conditional; **none while active two-handed axe** | Can excel at limited assault without being best at holding a shielded spear line | Commit scarce hird and lose protective coverage |

**Battle test:** same equipment, terrain, fatigue, command, enemy and formation size where comparison is meaningful; then vary training and source. Separately test with actual kit and scenario to expose strategic distinctions. A shield-wall icon should display eligibility, entry risk and why an order failed; it should not conceal an unexplained defense bonus.

## 4. Directional tactical calibration and legacy statistics

The England seven-unit calibration preserves legacy attack/morale numbers as evidence only and explicitly flags straight stat replacement. Norway's own legacy evidence records Norse Spearman spear attack 4 and morale 5, Landsmenn sword attack 6 and morale 6, Drengja axe attack 8 and morale 8, and Norse Huskarl heavy-axe attack 9 and morale 9. **Those numbers are NOT proposed Norway balance values**, nor is a higher number proof of a historical regiment. They cannot be compared as if all use identical weapon states, protection, roles or personnel. No new attack, defense, morale, size, upkeep or pool count is specified by this matrix.

Directional tests for future tuning:

- **Frontage and availability:** district spears should provide more sustainable field frontage than a royal household, but their losses and competing ship/local duties must matter. Do not solve balance by making all low-source troops worthless.
- **Close assault:** axemen should offer a reason to spend a district allocation on offense, while a hird axe detachment can be more dependable in a narrow decisive commitment but far more costly to lose. Neither needs an unexplained universal anti-armor bonus.
- **Defensive reliability:** actual kit, officers, preparation, experience and current state should influence how a unit forms and holds; avoid both automatic weak levies and automatic elite invulnerability.
- **Morale and cohesion:** a prepared district line can be solid; household troops may be steadier under recognized command, but isolation, loss of leader, fatigue, hunger and casualties still matter. Do not copy England's legacy morale ladder.
- **Size and recruitment:** the ordinary line should be recruitable from more valid district sources than household elite. Pool breadth is source coverage and replenishment potential, **not** one fabricated headcount or free units at every settlement. Recruitment and replenishment are different states.
- **Costs:** household losses have political and protection costs; district losses reduce labor, local defense and crew options. A cost curve should reflect both material and opportunity costs, not make diverse armies unaffordable.
- **Counterplay:** prepared spears can resist frontal mounted action but lose protection when turned/disordered; assault foot is exposed to fresh spears and cavalry; site defenders can be bypassed; all suffer if transport/supply and command fail.

## 5. Finite-pool ecology and quantity check

| Source family | Candidate outputs and competition | What can legitimately increase army variety/quantity | What cannot create a new pool |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coastal districts | Spear, bow and provisional axe assignments; crew, local defense and labor compete | More **validated** contributing districts; available unreserved people; bows/axes/shields, officers, summons and actual recovery | Three card labels, a barracks, an era tick, a free second copy of the same contributors |
| Inland communities | Upland regional spear or other owner-validated contribution | Independently identified community, service basis and usable route | Merely placing a mountain label on the same coastal district |
| Local sites | Watch if separate duty and people exist | Multiple real sites with actual protection sources and equipment | Settlement icon, unbuilt wall, empty garrison slot |
| Noble households | Noble Drengja or a different weapon profile within one household | Several genuinely distinct cooperating households with their own followers and terms | Counting one magnate's followers as both royal and noble |
| Royal household | Royal Hirdmen with optional axe assignment | Actual available retainers and improved readiness; fieldable detachments only after security allocation | Separate axe, bow, shipguard and parent pools from one body |
| Veterans and reorganized cadres | Experience and better formation handling within original source | Survivors, trained officers, practice, repairs, real replacement and reorganization | Veteran purchase from a building or duplicate experienced soldiers |

**Playability check, not a numeric quota:** Can a Norway player in a source-rich but historically supportable campaign assemble several district-based spear formations, missile support, at least one credible non-elite offensive expression where equipment permits, and a limited elite reserve without exhausting every home defense? Can a smaller or damaged realm still defend locally and recover gradually? Can the AI do the same without hidden people or blocking itself with unnecessary permission gates? If a valid source can field only an implausibly tiny army because of design friction rather than evidenced scarcity, simplify or tune the constraints. If one source creates many simultaneous formations from the same people, fix the reservation accounting.

**Illustrative composition, not an opening stack or unit-count claim:** coastal field army = district spear frontage + district bow support + an axe allocation if separately admitted + a small hird reserve if protection permits + real scouts, crews, ships and supply as needed. Inland response = validated inland or district spear source + locally assigned defenders + any available household support. Noble-supported expedition = district line plus a *delivered* magnate contingent and a royal core if political and household obligations allow. Each package has different risks and a reason to exist.

## 6. Pairwise survival tests and decisions

1. **Local Watch vs Leidang Spearmen:** protect a site with a different duty and source? If yes, local card can coexist. If not, district personnel receive a site assignment. England's civic militia is a method comparator, not proof Norway has identical towns.
2. **Upland vs Leidang:** independently identifiable inland community, obligation and campaign access? If yes, regional source can coexist even if basic spear animations overlap. If only art or terrain label differs, retain a regional eligibility/profile.
3. **Leidang Spearmen vs Axemen:** different weapon allocation, spear brace versus supported close assault, clear AI use and debit of same district people? If yes, two **playable cards** can share one constrained source family. Do not collapse a fun offensive choice merely because source is shared; do not grant an extra pool.
4. **Leidang vs Veteran Leidang:** experience is a persistent property of surviving people. Reorganization may improve field reliability but cannot manufacture a fresh recruit queue.
5. **Landsmenn vs veteran district, Axemen and Noble Drengja:** identify source first, then compare flexible-line relief and shielded combat under equal kit/experience. If it is a true independent organized field source with a differentiated role, consider a card; if it is the same veterans or household, preserve the role as a profile. Do not force a third source solely to fill a middle-stat slot.
6. **Noble Drengja vs Royal Hirdmen:** separate owners and political access matter even if some close-combat animations overlap. Test source, command, duration, loss and tactical assignment. Never merge a magnate's people into the Crown just to reduce card count.
7. **Royal Hirdmen vs Axeguard:** strong defensive/reserve parent versus concentrated axe assault; the player may choose both **expressions** only by dividing existing personnel and reducing protection. If no coherent separate battle behavior, keep the axe equipment/deployment profile without deleting its design value.
