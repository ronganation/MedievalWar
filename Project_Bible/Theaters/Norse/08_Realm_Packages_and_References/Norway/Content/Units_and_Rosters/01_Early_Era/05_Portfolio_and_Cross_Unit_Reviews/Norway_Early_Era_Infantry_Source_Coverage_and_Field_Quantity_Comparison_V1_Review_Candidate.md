# Norway Early Era Infantry: Source Coverage and Field Quantity Comparison V1 — Review Candidate

## Scope and verdict

This is the first substantive follow-through on the Early infantry cross-unit matrix, concentrating on Local Watch, Upland Spearmen and Leidang Spearmen. It is a **game-design comparison**, not a replacement definition, new source authority, numerical pool specification or January 1062 Opening State. Proposed location is `Norway/Content/Units_and_Rosters/01_Early_Era/`, alongside the portfolio and cross-unit matrix. The owner's physical folder is not accessible for direct writing in this session.

**Verdict:** Protect **Leidang Spearmen as the ordinary field-frontage candidate**, preserve **Upland Spearmen as a possible additional inland *source and access* choice**, and preserve **Local Watch as a potentially useful site-protection choice**. Neither conditional candidate is rejected merely because its weapon silhouette overlaps with Leidang Spearmen. Neither earns extra soldiers from its name. A fun Norway army should have substantial eligible district frontage and meaningful regional and local options; it should not be reduced to a few hirdmen by a stack of abstract permission gates. Conversely, local watch personnel should not become the main cheap overseas infantry simply because they can hold spears.

## 1. Three sources, three distinct choices

| Candidate | What the player recruits or assigns | Where useful strength should come from | Why the card might deserve to survive | What would make it redundant |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Leidang Spearmen | Spear-and-shield fighting contribution from an eligible coastal district, subject to a real muster | A *network* of eligible districts, their actual people and equipment, repeatable but finite muster and recovery; not an elite building | Dependable field frontage, bow protection, conditional spear brace and embarkation when separate lift exists; most plausible breadth among these three | Only if another card already owns exactly the same field-source/role and the player gains no distinct spear decision; current packet does **not** show that collapse |
| Upland Spearmen | Eligible noncoastal or distinct inland community contribution for regional field or approach defense | Multiple **independently established** communities where a real duty, equipment, assembly and route exist; not an extra levy automatically attached to every hill | An inland fieldable source with home-defense and route tradeoffs, not a coastal unit with +hill stats; a different map and strategic response | If the proposed community is already counted in a ship district, or evidence supports only site duty; then regional eligibility for a shared spear card or a local-defense assignment |
| Local Watch | Accountable defenders of an actual settlement, store, landing or crossing, if their protection duty is distinct | Real site-linked service and local people, where established; several validated sites may each have their own bounded capacity | Defend home nodes while field forces leave; useful battle defense at a *real* site; local protection has a player-visible price | If these are the same district spear personnel on temporary guard, use a site assignment of that parent card; do not make every place generate a garrison |

**Crucial correction to an overly restrictive reading:** one source can produce *several* units over time or across real districts, and one card can be recruited in several validated regions. Finite does not mean singular. The no-double-count rule applies to the *same person in simultaneous commitments*, not to a national cap of one district unit. This is a proposed design interpretation for pool tuning, not a claim that an exact 1062 number is known.

## 2. Availability should be readable and generous where supported

The three cards should not require identical permission chains. These are proposed **player-experience tests**, not existing implemented rules:

- **District field line:** when the player controls a validated eligible source and there are available contributors, equipment and a lawful call, offer Leidang Spearmen in the normal recruitment flow. Show the principal limiting reason if depleted or committed. Do not demand a bespoke diplomatic negotiation for each routine recruitment action; owner-produced state can resolve the underlying obligation. Mobilization still needs muster and readiness before a force can march.
- **Inland field source:** when a distinct community and service basis are evidenced, make its own regional contribution visible, with the local-defense cost and route to the intended army. Do not force a ship district on every inland community. Equally, do not presume inland means exempt from coastal obligations: some district relationships may extend inland.
- **Site security:** when a real local watch source exists, make it available at its site for defense. Its *restricted expeditionary use* should be legible, not a hidden punishment. If site defenders are instead district people assigned to guard, show their assignment and the district field pool's corresponding reduction.
- **Recovery:** returning survivors, repair, renewed eligibility and training can restore contribution over time. A low-status card should not become worthless because its pool recovers so slowly that the player can never sustain a field line; neither should a destroyed unit be instantly replaced without personnel and kit.
- **Unknown state:** if source strength, exact opening site duty or a route remains unverified, the static document cannot assert zero or an invented figure. The eventual opening-state and population owners must settle a playable baseline; this comparison only states the desired design behavior.

## 3. Field-quantity stress cases without fabricated counts

| Case | Desired playable result | Anti-frustration check | Anti-duplication check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home defense during a coastal expedition | Districts can contribute enough spear frontage to field a serious combined-arms force while at least some home protection remains where supported | Do not reserve every potential contributor for every conceivable future threat; assess *current* local defense need and risk | People assigned to crews, bow, axe or local defense cannot also stand in a spear formation |
| Inland threat while ships are elsewhere | A validated inland community can assemble a useful regional spear response even if coastal transport is unavailable | A messenger route alone is insufficient for army movement, but do not impose a ship requirement on valid land service | A community that already supplies the coastal obligation does not become a second independent pool |
| Settlement under attack | Site defenders can make a genuine difference to defense without the Crown parking a hird unit at every location | No blanket watch at every town; validated site and duty can produce a legible local defensive choice | If district soldiers are the watch, one personnel reservation is shown in both views, never counted twice |
| Campaign losses and demobilization | Surviving men return to their source; replacement draws on people, equipment and organization rather than a universal reset | Recovery should permit renewed play over a campaign, not trap a faction permanently after one loss unless its sources really are exhausted | Wounded, dead, absent, crew-assigned and currently fielded people cannot simultaneously refill a second unit |
| Expanding or consolidating control | More genuinely cooperating sources can broaden field choice; development can improve assembly and equipment | A new region is not forced to wait for a special unit unlock when a valid ordinary service basis already exists | Mere map ownership or a barracks does not transfer local allegiance or create population |

**What a good army feels like:** a stable spear center assembled from multiple actual sources, bowmen and a smaller assault allocation from the same district ecosystem, with local security where relevant and a scarce household reserve. This is a *composition target*, not a preloaded army, an exact count or an entitlement to ignore regional depletion. District men need not be portrayed as disposable peasants to give Norway enough field units. The coastal organization supports a strong strategic identity; the actual personnel and ship commitments still require separate state.

## 4. Practical source-and-assignment model for review

For each validated community or district, the future recruitment owner should expose an intelligible account of: available eligible people; people committed to spear, bow, axe, crew, watch and other duties; unavailable, recovering or absent people; usable weapons/shields; local command and service basis; and return/recovery. **This is a proposed interface, not an assertion that the game already has these fields implemented.** Population constrains a finite pool but is not directly consumed as Rome-style army manpower. Ships, crews and embarked fighters are separate even if one individual can change assignment over time. An officer or veteran may improve organization without becoming a fresh population source.

A recruitment decision can remain as simple as “Recruit Leidang Spearmen” with a visible pool and short explanation. Deep state belongs underneath the choice. Only consequential decisions, such as stripping a vulnerable site or redirecting a household, need prominent tradeoff messaging. The AI should use the same eligible people and state, without hidden free garrisons or an artificial ban on building a full army.

### Collision examples to resolve explicitly

1. A coastal person already serving as ship crew: may the person fight ashore after arrival? Potentially, if the vessel's continuing operation and return are accounted for; **not** simultaneously counted as a full crew member and an independently available field replacement.
2. A district spearman assigned to a landing watch: this is one person's change of duty, not proof of an independent Local Watch pool.
3. A separate site-service person defending the same landing: may be a genuine local source if duty and personnel are established; the site does not itself create that person.
4. A valley contributor inside an actual coastal service catchment: assign to the documented duty and prevent a second free “upland” reservation; preserve regional presentation if useful.
5. A distinct inland contributor reaching a coastal army: may join when service, assembly, route and transport permit; inland origin does not prohibit field service or grant free ships.

## 5. Battlefield distinction without a false tier ladder

- **Leidang Spearmen:** strongest case here for a baseline **field** spear line. Compare entry into a basic close spear order, prepared frontal brace, maintenance under casualties, turn and reform. Their equipment and officers can vary; do not force a weak shield-wall stereotype because the quality band says levy.
- **Upland Spearmen:** compare under *the same equipment and terrain* with Leidang. A distinct card can still be useful if it has a genuinely different service source and campaign access, but its battle proficiency must come from training and readiness, not the word upland. If the tactical job is identical, a shared spear card with distinct regional recruitment is a valid playable outcome.
- **Local Watch:** measure ability to hold a short defended approach with actual warning, stores and cover. Its weakness is long-distance field maneuver and endurance, not an arbitrary negative attack stat. Do not grant a wall defense when there is no wall.

**Suggested test cases, not executed battle results:** prepared field line under frontal pressure; a forced turn after flank threat; casualties breaking frontage; local site with and without warning; an inland approach with and without an army-capable route; and recovery after withdrawal. Formation access, speed to form, ability to hold and ability to reform are separate. None is a flat quality upgrade that makes all lower service obsolete.

## 6. Disposition and next content move

**Keep the three existing definitions unchanged.** The comparison finds no basis to delete Local Watch or Upland Spearmen now; both can expand the *variety of valid sources and missions* if their evidence gates are met. Leidang Spearmen remains the primary field line. The most important missing information is actual district and inland source coverage, local site duty, and the number of fieldable people after concurrent obligations. The available authorities deliberately leave exact opening totals unknown. Do not claim numerical pool adequacy until the population, region, law, equipment, site, military and Opening State owners bind real records.

**Next substantive pass:** compare Leidang Spearmen and Leidang Axemen as two competing outputs of district service. Set their formation handling, assault utility, AI use, and opportunity cost side by side. This directly advances battlefield variety rather than making another process-only audit. After that, revisit Landsmenn's proposed middle-field role against experienced district troops and noble followers.
