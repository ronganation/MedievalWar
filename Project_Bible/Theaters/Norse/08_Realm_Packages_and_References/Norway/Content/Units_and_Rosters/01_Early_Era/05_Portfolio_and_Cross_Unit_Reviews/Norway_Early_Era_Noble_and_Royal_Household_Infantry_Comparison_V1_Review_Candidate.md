# Norway Early Era Noble and Royal Household Infantry Comparison V1 - Review Candidate

## 0. Purpose, authority and placement

This is the next focused Early Era infantry comparison after the district spear/axe and middle-field reviews. It tests three existing candidates together: **Noble Drengja**, **Royal Hirdmen**, and **Hird Axeguard**. ## 1. The playable household choice

**Royal Hirdmen** are the parent royal following and the strongest candidate for a restricted, cohesive elite reserve: protect royal command, steady a threatened point, or make a limited supported commitment. Their opportunity cost is fewer qualified people protecting the ruler, heirs and government, and slower replacement of losses. They must feel valuable in battle, not merely expensive on paper.

**Noble Drengja** test a different way to strengthen an army: obtain selected foot from a *specific cooperating magnate household*. The people and equipment remain that household's; service may have bounded command, duration, area, reward and return terms. The candidate's preferred battlefield emphasis is a supported offensive commitment, not royal guard duty. This source can broaden the available elite *contributors* across real cooperating households without making any one household infinite. No particular opening magnate or delivered contingent is assigned here.

**Hird Axeguard** is an optional axe-equipped assignment of **the same royal hird personnel**. Its proposed job is a short, well-timed assault, sacrificing some guard and flexible-reserve coverage. Its default disposition is an equipment/deployment profile of Royal Hirdmen. A distinct tactical card is possible if actual kit, a coherent detachment, AI use and different battle counterplay earn it. It must not become a third household population.

**Verdict for the candidate pass:** protect Royal Hirdmen as a strong parent card candidate and Noble Drengja as a conditional *separate-owner* card candidate. Keep Axeguard visible as a potentially fun tactical variant, profile by default. This is a recommendation for further calibration, not admission to the 1062 opening or an order to delete any file.

## 2. Source, role and ability comparison

| Question | Noble Drengja | Royal Hirdmen | Hird Axeguard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who supplies the people? | One identified magnate household if it actually agrees or owes valid service | The king's existing household | The **same** royal hird, reassigned from other duties |
| What does the player gain? | A politically contingent select foot reserve that can expand the host without stripping the king's guard | A reliable restricted royal guard/reserve for command protection, line relief and a timed commitment | A sharper, narrower assault expression at the expense of royal protection and parent reserve flexibility |
| What blocks availability? | Household people, consent or valid obligation, equipment, command terms, route and delivery | Existing people, kit, royal mandate, government and heir protection | All parent constraints **plus** suitable axes, practice, coherent group and guard reallocation |
| Battlefield focus to test | Supported close assault after a district line fixes the target; coordination with field command may be less certain | Hold, stabilize or counterattack under a recognized royal command; not a universal spear brace or shock winner | Short assault against a fixed or disordered enemy; must not be the best general defender |
| Shielded order | Conditional on actual shield, weapon and practice | Conditional on actual shield-compatible subgroup and practice | One-handed axe/shield may qualify; active two-handed axe cannot simultaneously receive shield protection |
| Formation proficiency | Test household cohesion separately from integration with a royal field commander | Test entry, maintenance, controlled relief and reform if trained and equipped | Test assault entry, withdrawal and recovery rather than copying the parent's full defensive reliability |
| Battlefield counter | Formed spear front, exposed flanks, missiles, mounted pressure, dual-command failure | Isolation, fatigue, missile pressure, flanks and forced multiple commitments | Fresh spears, missiles, cavalry flanks, blocked withdrawal and the loss of command protection elsewhere |
| Loss and return | Losses reduce the source magnate's household; return and reward follow actual terms | Losses reduce royal guard and government protection | Losses reduce the **same** royal guard; no independent refill |
| Default candidate outcome | Conditional distinct noble-source card | Strong parent card candidate | Parent profile, separate tactical card only after direct proof |

The quality differences above are **test propositions**, not assigned stats. The formation standard requires separate assessment of eligibility, entry, holding, turning, casualty-gap response, recovery, fatigue and command. Neither noble status nor the word hird grants a shield-wall button or automatic morale immunity. A unit may be powerful in its intended job without being stronger in every matchup.

## 3. Three strategic configurations that should all be enjoyable

**A. Royal-centered host (illustrative, not an opening stack).** District spear and bow contributions form the main body. The royal hird stays with command or is committed at a crisis. This army remains viable without any magnate agreeing to join. Its strength is dependable royal control; its cost is exposing protection at home if too much hird service is deployed.

**B. Magnate-supported host.** One or more *separately validated* cooperating households contribute actual selected foot. The Crown can keep more royal hird in protective reserve while a noble contingent takes a supported offensive task. Household owners retain personnel and service terms. A request is not delivery, and the same noble follower cannot also be a royal hirdman.

**C. Axe-commitment host.** A real royal following has suitable axe-equipped people, and the player chooses a limited Axeguard expression for a particular battle. This should create an exciting assault option **only if** the reduced guard and reserve coverage is visible and the tactical result differs from simply ordering ordinary hirdmen to attack. If not, retain axe equipment and art variation within the parent card rather than adding a redundant tactical card.

**Quantity/playability test:** Norway's normal field army must not depend on three rare household cards to exist. Multiple valid districts provide ordinary frontage and missiles; real noble households can supplement it when politically available; the royal following remains small enough to be meaningful but strong enough to reward careful use. Do not translate finite recruitment into one elite formation for the entire game or force repeated micromanagement of each retainer. The player and AI should see available contributions, principal blockers, home-defense exposure and likely recovery without a stack of opaque permission gates. No counts or pool rates are invented.

## 4. Direct overlap tests and battle scenarios

1. **Equal-kit comparison.** On the same terrain, with comparable shields, armor condition, experience, fatigue and command where meaningful, compare a noble following and royal hird in line relief, a short counterattack and withdrawal. If their *combat* behavior overlaps, their distinct source and political availability may still justify different cards; do not fake a large stat gap. If their sources are also indistinguishable in the actual records, revisit the classification rather than force two labels.
2. **Different command chain.** Repeat a timed assault with a noble contingent under agreed field orders and with royal hird under royal command. Test order receipt, cohesion, withdrawal and household leader coordination. Do not impose an automatic noble-command penalty: only actual command state and agreement can affect execution.
3. **Parent versus axe assignment.** Hold royal personnel and total kit constant. Compare a parent hird formation assigned to protect command against a debited axe-equipped detachment assigned to assault. If the axe detachment cannot show different formation behavior, equipment tradeoff or AI choice, retain a parent profile. A separate battle card can share the parent personnel ledger if its tactical choice is real.
4. **Weapon compatibility.** Compare spear/shield, one-handed axe/shield and a separately dated two-handed axe hypothesis. Only an eligible spear subgroup may test spear brace. Shielded close order does not mean identical anti-mounted reach. Two-handed axe use must not retain simultaneous active shield defense. No universal armor-piercing or berserker ability.
5. **Competing protection crises.** A field victory with heavy noble casualties weakens the source household; a victory with heavy hird casualties weakens royal protection. A command threat while Axeguard is committed tests whether the player has preserved a reserve. These are design consequences for owner validation, not automatic scripted penalties.
6. **Campaign availability.** Compare source-rich, divided and depleted campaigns. Can a player assemble a mixed army when a magnate refuses? Can a cooperating household deliver a useful contingent without creating a free clone? Can AI preserve royal protection when a less politically costly district or noble option suffices? Test transport and return separately for an overseas expedition.

**Record in each future test:** actual people and equipment reserved; formation access versus execution; casualty and fatigue outcomes; order/command clarity; ability to withdraw and reform; protection left behind; AI reason for recruitment and deployment; and whether a player can explain the choice. No test is claimed to have run.

## 5. Card-or-profile gates

- **Royal Hirdmen:** preserve as the core royal-household candidate. An eventual card needs actual royal personnel, service and kit; tested reserve/protection behavior; readable counters; source-bound replacement; historical art and owner approval. Exact opening availability is separate.
- **Noble Drengja:** retain as a conditional distinct card because its **source ownership and political access differ**. Require a specific household, people, service terms, delivery and a worthwhile selected-foot role. The working name is a game label, not proof of a formal Norwegian 1062 corps. If the household exists but its battlefield behavior does not earn a separate card, retain a noble-household foot profile. Do not merge the magnate's people into the king's pool for roster neatness.
- **Hird Axeguard:** keep as a royal hird equipment/assignment profile by default. A separate tactical card can still be fun if a coherent detachment, kit, guard debit, distinct assault use and counterplay pass review. A separate *recruitment pool* is not required and must not be fabricated. If only a few hirdmen carry axes, keep visual variation within the parent.
- **Landsmenn / selected district middle foot:** remains a different middle-field test; neither noble nor royal people should be borrowed to fill it silently. District Axemen remain a less scarce non-elite assault choice, not a lower-stat copy of Noble Drengja.
- **Chronology:** a candidate file, medieval hird institution, or later hird law does not approve a January 1062 troop instance, a fixed unit size, a royal shipguard, or a knightly upgrade.
