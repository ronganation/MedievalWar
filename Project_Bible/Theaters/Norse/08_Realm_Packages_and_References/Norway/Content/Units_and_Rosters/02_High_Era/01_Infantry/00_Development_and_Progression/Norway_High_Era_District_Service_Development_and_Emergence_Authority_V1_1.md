## 2. District-service development thesis

Norway's district military development should be represented as a causal portfolio of choices inside finite regional sources.

```text
Actual district and community
+ eligible people
+ lawful or customary service
+ current compliance
+ real weapons and protection
+ assembly and command
+ training or experience
+ stores routes supply and return
= supportable contribution
```

Development may improve one or more of the following without creating another population:

- summons and notice;
- inspection and equipment readiness;
- command and order communication;
- repeated assembly and formation practice;
- selective weapon assignments;
- protection and repair;
- supply and transport preparation;
- survivor experience;
- coordination with bows, household troops, sites, and fleets.

The district system must remain regionally uneven. A ruler may develop selected regions without converting every district at once. Ordinary service remains useful wherever later organization, equipment, or specialized assignments are absent or unaffordable.

## 3. Candidate comparison and disposition

### 3.1 Leidang Spearmen

```yaml

source: OwnerValidatedNorwegianCoastalDistrictsAndCommunities
historical_name_classification: DesignInterpretationFromAttestedInstitution
current_disposition: PrincipalPersistentDistrictDefensiveInfantryCandidate
opening_status: CandidateNotOpeningStateFact
separate_pool_created_here: false
```

Leidang Spearmen remain the readable defensive foundation. Their job is to establish supported frontage, protect missile troops, resist limited mounted pressure when formation and facing hold, secure an actual landing perimeter, and contribute to local defense.

The parent definition already permits variable equipment and limited mail. It therefore absorbs ordinary variation in shields, helmets, personal sidearms, and protection. Minor improvement does not justify Royal Leidang Spearmen or Armored Leidang as additional cards.

The parent remains vulnerable to sustained missiles, flanking, command failure, fatigue, disrupted formation, mobile enemies, and stronger assault infantry. It has no cavalry immunity, universal shield wall, terrain bonus, or instant maritime capability.

### 3.4 Leidang Axemen or District Axemen


District Axemen have the strongest separate-card case inside the district family because a supported close-assault role can differ materially from defensive spear frontage.

The intended choice is:

```text
Spearmen establish and protect frontage.
Axemen exploit a fixed or disordered enemy.
Bowmen apply missile pressure.
Household foot remain the scarce elite reserve.
```

The axemen card survives only if all of these are demonstrated:

- selected, non-duplicated district personnel;
- available axe and shield equipment;
- a real assignment or practice distinct from spear-line allocation;
- supported close-assault behavior and counterplay;
- an AI choice that is not merely higher attack;
- visible opportunity cost to spear frontage, local defense, crew, or labor;
- no intrinsic raid, ambush, berserker, armor-piercing, forest, or maritime bonus.

If the assault distinction fails, merge the concept into the mixed-weapon parent family. The legacy raider fantasy survives only as inspiration for operations and tactical use, not as proof of a permanent raider class.

```yaml
royal_coordination_may:
  - IssueOrSupportAValidSummons
  - ImproveNoticeInspectionAssemblyAndCommandWhereAccepted
  - SponsorEquipmentRepairOrStoresWhereResourcesExist
```

A reform is therefore a capability and eligibility change, not a completed muster. District politics, local defense, labor, equipment, routes, season, and current obligations remain active constraints.

## 10. Maritime, regional, site, and expedition interfaces

### 10.1 Maritime expedition

Embarkation requires real ships, crews, lift, stores, route, access, weather, landing, command, and return. More armor may increase lift and supply burden. District infantry are passengers or embarked fighters unless separately assigned and qualified as crew.

### 10.2 Regional variation

Western fjords, Trondelag, southern coasts, inland valleys, uplands, and northern communities may differ in source, obligation, equipment, political relationship, route, and military use. A place-name, palette, or terrain stereotype is not enough for a separate unit.

### 10.3 Site defense

A district contribution retained at a site remains its source's people unless a distinct local-watch or site service is proven. Castle or fortress service belongs in the site-defense comparison, not as an automatic late form of all district infantry.

### 10.4 Expedition versus home readiness

Every field commitment may reduce:

- local defense;
- labor and production;
- ship crew options;
- household support;
- route and landing security;
- equipment available for later musters;
- political cooperation after casualties or prolonged service.

## 15. Historical, art, and battle review questions

### Historical and institutional questions

- Which district obligations, inspection practices, command relationships, and equipment expectations apply to which periods and regions?
- Which details can be used for 1062, and which belong only to later High Medieval contexts?
- Does `Royal Leidang Spearmen` misstate ownership strongly enough that `Organized Leidang Spearmen` should replace it?
- Can a selected axe-equipped contribution be supported as a useful game interpretation without inventing a standardized corps?
- Are there distinct local-watch institutions or only local assignments of broader community service?
- What protection types and ownership patterns justify later better-equipped district profiles?

### Battle-owner tests

- ordinary versus organized spear formations with equal people and kit;
- ordinary versus better-equipped spear formations with equal training and command;
- organized and better-equipped combined without double-counting benefits;
- spear frontage versus selected axe assault against fixed and fresh enemies;
- district axemen under missile and cavalry pressure;
- defensive line under fatigue, flank attack, broken command, and incomplete muster;
- movement and embarkation burden with greater protection;
- local watch versus district spearmen at an actual site;
- veteran survivors with inexperienced replacements;
- AI choice among broad frontage, organization, protection, assault, bows, crew, and home defense.

No attack, defense, armor, morale, unit size, speed, cost, upkeep, fatigue, formation, or recruitment quantity is created here.

## 16. Player-experience acceptance test

The district family succeeds only if the player can understand:

- why ordinary Leidang Spearmen remain useful;
- why organization is not a new population;
- why additional armor is not a national upgrade;
- why veterans cannot be freshly recruited;
- why axemen may justify a different card while organized or armored spearmen often do not;
- why bow, spear, axe, crew, watch, and labor assignments compete;
- why royal coordination still requires district compliance and actual people;
- why equipment, repair, routes, ships, supply, and return matter;
- why losses weaken the original community;
- why AI follows the same finite constraints.

If the result is a simple ladder of `Leidang Spearmen -> Royal Leidang Spearmen -> Armored Leidang` with automatic stat increases and forced replacement, this comparison fails.
