## 2. Norway's missile-development thesis

Norway's missile identity should develop through continuity plus selective added capability.

The inherited bow family remains useful because it is comparatively accessible, socially connected to district service, tactically suited to screening and sustained missile support, and compatible with Norway's infantry-centered army. High Era development can improve inspection, organization, arrow provision, command, and field reliability without converting every district bowman into a new unit.

Crossbow service creates a different investment problem. It requires an appropriate historical window, qualified people, real weapons, bolts, strings, repair, training, command, a valid service source, protection during reload, replacement, and a supply route. The value is not that crossbows become universally superior. The value is a lower-tempo, equipment-intensive missile option that may provide selective pressure from protected positions and create a meaningful campaign-development decision.

The resulting player choice is not:

```text
Early Archer -> Better Crossbowman -> Elite Fortress Crossbowman -> Royal Crossbow Guard
```

The intended choice is:

```text
Persistent district bow service
+ conditional specialized crossbow service
+ accountable site and royal assignments
+ finite people equipment ammunition training and protection
```

### Coexistence principle

Bows and crossbows may coexist because they can draw on different equipment, training, institutions, regions, and tactical uses. Coexistence is not automatic. It must be supported by actual capacity. A realm that invests heavily in crossbows may still rely on district bows where crossbow equipment, repair, or service is absent. A realm that neglects crossbow development may retain a viable bow-centered roster rather than becoming artificially obsolete.

## 8. Field, site, siege, maritime, and royal assignments

### 8.1 Field service

Norwegian Crossbowmen may support a spear line only when actual protection, fire lanes, bolts, command, and withdrawal exist. The card does not include a free protective screen. Leidang Bowmen remain the more accessible sustained missile option where district capacity exists.

### 8.2 Site service

Posting shooters to a fortress or defended site changes their assignment, location, supply, and local-defense value. It does not create a new population. Site cover and elevation belong to the actual battle map, not a permanent unit bonus.

### 8.3 Siege service

Crossbow personnel may defend approaches or support an attack where actual siege conditions permit. This does not make them engineers, miners, sappers, artillery crews, wall-damage specialists, or automatic assault infantry. Siege endurance depends on real stores, water, food, bolts, sanitation, routes, relief, command, and morale.

### 8.4 Maritime service

Bow and crossbow infantry may embark only when ships, crews, lift, access, stores, weather, landing, and return exist. Neither unit becomes a ship crew or fleet. Crossbows do not create naval combat capability, and maritime use does not create a separate card.

### 8.5 Royal service

Royal assignment is a campaign duty. It matters because retaining shooters for the ruler can reduce field or site coverage, while deploying them can weaken royal protection. The same household or contracted people retain their real source and replacement path.
