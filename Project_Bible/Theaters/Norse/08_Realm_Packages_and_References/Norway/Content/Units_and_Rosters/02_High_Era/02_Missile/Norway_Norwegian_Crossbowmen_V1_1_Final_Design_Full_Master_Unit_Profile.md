## 4. Player-facing identity strip

- **Unit:** Norwegian Crossbowmen
- **Era:** High Era I emergence candidate with possible High Era II persistence
- **Class:** equipment-intensive missile infantry
- **Source:** one validated royal, town, household, site, or contract service at a time
- **Primary use:** careful volleys from protected positions against selected targets
- **Secondary use:** approach defense and spear-supported field missile pressure
- **Major weaknesses:** reload exposure, flank attack, cavalry closure, bolt shortage, damaged equipment, and weak unsupported melee
- **Relationship to bows:** additional capability, not replacement
- **Opening State:** unavailable in January 1062

## 5. One-screen Total War design card

### Battlefield
- Deliver deliberate, lower-tempo crossbow fire from behind Spears, obstacles, elevation, or real fortification cover.
- Reward target selection and protected firing lanes rather than volume alone.
- Reposition slowly enough that bad deployment matters.
- Become vulnerable when the protective frontage collapses or enemies close during reload.
- Retain only basic or source-dependent melee capability.

### Campaign
- Requires qualified people, crossbows, strings, bolts, maintenance, training, command, and replacement.
- May emerge from royal sponsorship, a developed town or craft network, a real defended site, a household program, or contract service.
- Does not require every region to follow the same adoption path.
- Coexists with Leidang Bowmen, whose availability and sustained bow role remain valuable.

### Access
- A technology or institutional choice may authorize development, but it creates none of the people, weapons, ammunition, workshops, training, or formations.
- A fortress or royal title changes assignment only when real shooters and equipment exist.

## 24. Signature battle cycle

```text
Take position behind Spears obstacles elevation or real fortification cover
-> inspect weapons strings bolts and firing lanes
-> identify a worthwhile target
-> receive fire command
-> deliver deliberate shot or bounded volley
-> reload under protection
-> shift lane or target as needed
-> conserve bolts when target value falls
-> withdraw immediately if frontage or flank protection fails
-> rally near command ammunition repair and resupply
```

## 26. Counterplay

- close during reload or force repeated repositioning;
- attack flanks or the protective Spear line;
- use cover, shields, terrain, and formation appropriately;
- threaten ammunition, repair, supply, and withdrawal routes;
- induce waste against dispersed or low-value targets;
- contest real firing lanes and protected positions;
- force prolonged close combat.

## 2. What the player actually gains

Norway's inherited bowmen harass exposed targets and screen its spear line with comparatively accessible district bow service. **Crossbowmen would instead reward deliberate equipment investment:** a limited missile formation suited to defended firing positions and selective pressure on better protected targets, paid for with slower follow-up shots, costly bolts, fragile reloading opportunities and a much narrower equipment/replacement pipeline. This is a **design proposition** for later battle testing, not a claim that every historical crossbow pierced every armor type. An army can take bows for sustained, flexible screening; crossbows for selective protected volleys; or both if the campaign can support both sources. The optimal answer should vary by opponent, ground, supply and budget.

**Medieval II readability:** the eventual card, if admitted, should show a crossbow and bolt silhouette, its actual service source, a low-tempo/high-commitment missile niche, dependence on protection, and limited replenishment. No hidden anti-armor multiplier, auto-win against knights or universal range superiority. A smaller number of well-placed crossbowmen can be interesting without being a prestige super-unit. The design must be enjoyable in siege defense and in a protected field line, while neither role implies a second population pool.

**Norway specificity:** access follows the actual royal, town, household, craft or validated site network rather than "all Norse towns." The monarchy may favor protected coastal staging and strongpoints where they truly exist; that is a *possible* campaign development, not an automatic 1062 castle or port. Denmark and Sweden may develop analogous technology through their own sources; Norway does not borrow their people, facilities or dates by Theater membership.
