# PART I. HUMAN-READABLE UNIT PROFILE

## 3. Unit identity strip

- **Unit:** Fjordmen
- **Canonical owner:** Norse Theater Regional portfolio
- **Opening relationship candidate:** Norway, pending separate relationship and Opening State review
- **Class:** Western-fjord regional spear infantry
- **Quality:** Militia to basic trained
- **Source:** Finite, validated western fjord communities or coastal district service
- **Availability:** Regional, source-bound, settlement-supported, equipment-dependent, seasonal, and limited by home defense, labor, crew, transport, and other assignments
- **Primary role:** Hold regional coastal frontage and defend approaches, settlements, roads, and real landing perimeters
- **Best use:** Prepared coastal or settlement defense, short frontage beside terrain or structures, protection of Bowmen, regional response, and bounded field service
- **Major weakness:** Ordinary equipment, limited offensive pressure, missile and flank exposure, uneven endurance, and loss of home defense when committed elsewhere
- **Development:** May gain better organization, equipment consistency, command, and recovery without automatically becoming elite or ceasing to be Fjordmen
- **Opening State:** Candidate not initialized
- **Exact values:** None promoted

## 4. One-screen player card

- **Class:** Regional coastal spear-and-shield infantry
- **Source:** Eligible unreserved people from validated western fjord communities
- **Availability:** Regional but useful; depends on people, service, equipment, leaders, assembly, protected home-defense minimums, and settlement or route support
- **Battle promise:** Affordable regional frontage with a memorable coastal identity and practical garrison value, not a stronger copy of Leidang Spearmen
- **Best use:** Coastal approaches, settlement edges, landing perimeters after actual deployment, roads, narrow frontage, Bowmen protection, and local defensive reinforcement
- **Worst use:** Unsupported assault, deep pursuit, prolonged distant campaigning without rotation, open flanks, concentrated missiles, or use as ship crew or elite raiders
- **Strategic tradeoff:** Recruiting or detaching the unit reduces the people and equipment available to defend its home approaches, support fishing and transport, crew vessels where qualified, and recover the source community
- **Counterplay:** Missiles, flanking, stronger infantry, fatigue, route disruption, bypassed defensive positions, cavalry after disorder, and threats against the source region
- **Special boundary:** Coastal identity affects recruitment, garrison, source, preparedness, and recovery, not universal combat bonuses

## 5. Player-facing description

Fjordmen are spear-and-shield infantry raised from validated western coastal communities. They provide regional frontage for settlements, approaches, and real landing perimeters, or join a wider Norwegian army when their home defense, service, equipment, supply, and return can support the commitment.

Fjordmen are related to the broader Leidang infantry ecosystem but retain their regional formation identity. They are neither elite coastal warriors nor weak novelty troops. Familiar organization, nearby rally points, defended flanks, local supply, and a clear objective can make them reliable in their intended setting, while open flanks, missiles, fatigue, and stronger assault troops remain serious threats.

A surviving Fjordmen formation may replenish at compatible friendly military settlements without losing its identity. Returning to a compatible western source may offer the strongest access to original replacements, equipment, and community recovery, but exact home return is not required for every casualty.

## 18. Battlefield rhythm

1. Occupy a clear regional objective, settlement approach, road, camp, or landing perimeter.
2. Use terrain, structures, stronger troops, or supporting frontage to protect flanks.
3. Preserve spear facing against mounted pressure.
4. Protect Bowmen and allow Axemen or selected infantry to counterattack.
5. Avoid unsupported pursuit or offensive overextension.
6. Withdraw toward a known rally point, garrison, or supporting formation.
7. Preserve survivors because losses damage the home source and future coastal defense.
