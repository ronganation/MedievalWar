This authority establishes the Norse Theater method for regional military content, mercenary service, conditional external military access, canonical Theater ownership, finite source capacity, portfolio development, and Realm relationship routing. It does not declare a completed Norse mercenary roster, create Opening State forces, admit brainstorm units as historical facts, or authorize implementation.

## 1. Authority Verdict

Develop Norse regional and mercenary military content as a connected but nonuniform Theater ecosystem shaped by coasts, fjords, straits, islands, inland waterways, regional political authority, household and community military capacity, ports and settlements, maritime movement, commercial contact, war, exile, displacement, and bounded external service.

The system must provide meaningful military opportunities beyond ordinary Realm recruitment without replacing Denmark, Norway, or Sweden's Realm rosters. Every formation must retain a supported source identity, service basis, finite capacity, chronology, replacement path, and owner route. A familiar battlefield role does not justify a generic hired-unit duplicate, and a regional name does not establish historical existence.

## 2. Purpose

This authority provides the Norse Theater foundation needed to:

- organize canonical regional and mercenary unit profiles at Theater level;
- connect those profiles to one or more Realms through relationship authorities rather than duplicate files;
- distinguish ordinary Realm recruitment, regional recruitment, allied or external contingents, household service, institutional attachment, maritime service, mercenary contract, emergency service, and event-generated forces;
- preserve finite recruitment and replacement pressure across all competing consumers;
- define Norse-specific ecosystem portfolios and research gates;
- integrate existing Norwegian, Danish, Swedish, island, Baltic-facing, North Atlantic, Britania-facing, and continental-facing work without flattening their identities;
- support future content additions when campaign, historical, or design work reveals a justified source;
- provide a stable interface for a later Norse union, hegemony, and consolidation system without implementing that system here.

## 3. Scope

This authority governs:

- Theater-owned regional and mercenary content organization;
- source-area and service-form classification;
- canonical unit ownership and relationship-based Realm access;
- Norse regional military ecosystem portfolios;
- content admission, overlap review, research, and rejection rules;
- finite capacity and replacement requirements;
- chronology and development-band treatment;
- maritime, island, frontier, and cross-Theater circulation review;
- AI and player-facing explanation requirements;
- future union-system interface requirements;
- static validation and handoff boundaries.

It does not itself determine current force availability, political access, route safety, contracts, payment, exact pool values, unit statistics, Opening State, or runtime state.

## 4. Controlling Design Thesis

The Norse Theater is a connected but divided maritime world. Denmark, Norway, and Sweden remain independent Realm projects. Regional military capability may travel through service, opportunity, political relationship, exile, trade contact, maritime connection, dynastic connection, or war, but none of those conditions automatically creates recruitment access.

Norse regional and mercenary military content should feel especially sensitive to:

- maritime distances that may connect places strategically while still constraining transport and supply;
- seasonal movement and recovery;
- local and regional authority;
- island, coast, fjord, forest, lake, river, strait, and Baltic operating environments;
- overlapping household, regional, naval, local-defense, expeditionary, and economic obligations;
- the difference between a person capable of fighting, a formed unit, an available service source, and an assembled campaign force;
- changing military institutions and equipment across Early, High, and Late development without freezing all Norse content into a Viking-age image.

## 8. Service-Form Classification

Every candidate must be classified before roster admission. Permitted review classes include:

1. ordinary Realm recruitment;
2. regional recruitment tied to a bounded source;
3. controller access to a distinct regional source;
4. household or personal following;
5. allied contingent;
6. affinity, kinship, or dynastic contingent where separately supported;
7. external military service;
8. mercenary contract service;
9. urban, guild, port, or institutional service;
10. ecclesiastical or religiously sponsored service;
11. maritime fighting or transport attachment;
12. emergency, rebellion, exile, refugee, survivor, or demobilized service;
13. campaign-specific or Historical Emergence force;
14. support personnel or mission attachment that does not justify a battlefield unit.

A candidate may have more than one possible service route, but uncertainty must be preserved. Mercenary classification is not the default destination for any force outside an ordinary Realm roster.

## 9. Finite Capacity and Competing Commitments

Regional and mercenary capability is finite. Every admitted source must identify its capacity owner and collision rules across:

- ordinary and regional recruitment;
- household and noble followings;
- ship crews and maritime fighting contingents;
- local and coastal defense;
- allied or external service;
- mercenary contracting;
- garrisons, escorts, messengers, scouts, engineers, craftsmen, transport labor, and supply support;
- recovery, replacement, training, and return obligations.

Population informs source capacity but is not converted directly into army manpower. Technology, decisions, buildings, wealth, and political union may improve organization, reach, training, equipment, contracting, or recovery only through their owners. They do not create people, ships, horses, equipment, officers, companies, or recruitment pools.

No exact pool size, recovery interval, company strength, price, or availability rate is authorized by this document.

## 10. Availability and Circulation Inputs

Future availability review may consume owner-produced state concerning:

- local-defense requirements and competing expeditions;
- cross-Theater opportunities and restrictions;
- actor-visible knowledge.

These inputs affect discoverability, eligibility, willingness, reach, or service feasibility only when the controlling owners provide the necessary receipts.

## 11. Norse Theater Ecosystem Portfolios

The following portfolios organize research and future content. They are not fixed rosters and do not guarantee that every concept becomes a unit.

### 11.1 Danish Straits and Southern Scandinavian Portfolio

Primary review surfaces include Jutland, the Danish islands, Scania-facing contexts where opening authority supports them, the Danish straits, southern Baltic contacts, and continental corridors.

Research priorities:

- forces or specialists shaped by strait defense, island movement, coastal service, transport, or port security;
- urban, ecclesiastical, commercial, or continental-facing service relationships;
- bounded engineering, wagon, bridge, siege, convoy, or logistical protection roles;
- cross-border and cross-Theater military labor where evidence supports actual service;
- differences between royal, regional, urban, institutional, and contractual service.

The existing Jutland Wagon Pioneers brainstorm remains quarantined. Its useful core is a narrow engineering-guard and wagon-defense problem. Its title, historicity, exact service form, chronology, and unit status remain unverified. It may be redirected to attached specialist service, regional labor protection, institutional support, or rejection rather than forced into the mercenary roster.

### 11.2 Norwegian Coasts, Fjords, Uplands, and North Atlantic Portfolio

This portfolio interfaces with Norway's existing army, fleet, maritime, roster, and unit authorities without duplicating them.

Research priorities:

- regional forces whose identity and availability arise from a bounded Norwegian source rather than the general Norway roster;
- coastal, fjord, upland, route-guide, scout, missile, local-defense, maritime-fighting, escort, or specialist roles where evidence supports distinction;
- Orkney, Shetland, Faroe, Icelandic, and other North Atlantic relationships through their correct owners;
- service abroad, exile, demobilization, royal campaigning, and cross-Theater military opportunity;
- collision between ship crews, local defense, household service, expeditionary concentration, regional recruitment, and contract service.

Norway's developed Realm units remain Norway-owned. They do not become Theater mercenaries merely because they can fight abroad or embark on ships.

### 11.3 Swedish Lakes, Forests, Regional Centers, and Baltic Coasts Portfolio

Primary review surfaces include Götaland, the Mälaren sphere, inland waterways, forests, regional assemblies, Gotland and Öland interfaces, Baltic coasts and archipelagos, and eastern-facing contacts where separately supported.

Research priorities:

- regionally bounded infantry, missile, scouting, guide, maritime, island, or garrison service;
- differences among inland, lake, forest, coastal, island, and long-distance service environments;
- political and assembly recognition affecting access without becoming a universal Swedish pool;
- Gotlandic, Finnic, Baltic, Sámi, Åland, Finland-facing, or Rus-facing contacts through their independent owners;
- urban, commercial, ecclesiastical, and maritime service networks where evidence supports them.

External communities retain their identities. They are not generic Swedish auxiliaries or empty sources waiting for a roster label.

### 11.4 North Atlantic Island and Sea-Route Portfolio

Possible source areas include Orkney, Shetland, Faroe, Iceland, and other owner-validated island or maritime communities.

This portfolio must distinguish:

- community defense from external service;
- local seafaring knowledge from an available military company;
- regional recruitment from Norway-facing political relationships;
- maritime transport or guidance from battlefield-unit status;
- island origin from automatic loyalty to a mainland crown;
- map representation from historical or relationship relevance.

An island does not need to be a normal major campaign province to contribute through character origin, route knowledge, service relationships, trade, exile, or event-driven participation. Such contribution still requires its own owner and access basis.

### 11.5 Baltic Island, Frontier, and Eastern Interface Portfolio

This portfolio covers possible contacts involving Gotland, Öland, Bornholm, Åland, Finland-facing regions, Finnic and Sámi communities, southern Baltic actors, Baltic communities, Rus-facing routes, and other eastern interfaces.

Each candidate must distinguish:

- native regional recruitment;
- allied or affinity contingent;
- contractual service;
- settled or displaced military community;
- route guide, scout, sailor, trader, or support role;
- imported equipment or tactical influence;
- independent political actor.

No candidate may be reduced to a generic "eastern" unit or culturally deterministic bonus.

### 11.6 Maritime, Port, and Commercial Service Portfolio

This portfolio examines bounded military service associated with ports, ships, convoys, merchants, urban institutions, crossings, and maritime routes.

Potential role questions include:

- boarding or shipboard fighting;
- port and landing defense;
- convoy escort;
- marines or maritime fighting contingents where terminology and chronology are appropriate;
- crossbow, missile, guard, or specialist service tied to actual institutions or contacts;
- ship, bridge, wagon, siege, repair, navigation, or transport protection.

Trade contact may improve discoverability but never creates recruitment access by itself. A seaman is not automatically a soldier, a crew is not automatically a unit, and a port is not automatically a mercenary market.

### 11.7 Exile, Adventurer, Demobilization, and Event-Service Portfolio

The Theater's wars, succession contests, overseas expeditions, failed claims, displaced households, returned veterans, demobilized forces, and political exclusions may create temporary service possibilities.

Every such case requires:

- a triggering historical or campaign state;
- a living or institutionally credible source;
- a service basis and employer relationship;
- finite survivors and replacement boundaries;
- a current route and assembly path;
- closure, return, reintegration, dispersal, or transformation outcomes;
- no presumption that a temporary host becomes a permanent mercenary pool.

### 11.8 Continental and Cross-Theater Contract Portfolio

The Norse Theater may connect to Britania, Western Francia, southern Baltic, Imperial or Central European spaces, and eastern routes when geography, politics, service history, and campaign state support the connection.

Foreign specialists, armored contingents, missile troops, siege personnel, port guards, or other professional service must remain source-owned. Imported military capability should complement Norse and Realm identities, not erase them or become a universal late-era upgrade.

## 12. Holy Orders and Religious Military Service Boundary

Holy Orders are not established as a foundational Norse Theater roster pillar by this authority.

Religious military participation may be evaluated through:

- crusading or expeditionary contingents;
- returned or redirected participants;
- church-sponsored defense or travel;
- external orders operating through a validated adjacent Theater or Baltic interface;
- individual character affiliation;
- institutional patronage, hosting, passage, or contractual relationships.

No symmetrical Norse Holy Order portfolio should be invented merely because other Theaters possess stronger order systems. A distinct Norse or Norse-operating order presence requires separate historical evidence, institutional ownership, chronology, and service review.

## 13. Chronology and Development

Every candidate must record:

```yaml
chronology_review_requires:
  - earliest_supported_window
  - strongest_supported_window
  - persistence_or_decline_basis
  - opening_1062_disposition
  - early_high_late_development_relationship
  - equipment_and_institutional_change
  - service_form_change
  - naming_change
  - merge_split_or_retirement_conditions
  - prohibited_backward_projection
```

The Theater may support Early, High, and Late developments, but later forms are not back-projected into 1062. Viking-age language, equipment, and identity cannot be used as a permanent decorative wrapper for all later Norse service. Development may produce professionalization, urban or institutional specialization, foreign contact, changed equipment, changed contract forms, or the disappearance of an earlier source.

## 14. Unit and Company Admission Gate

A distinct canonical unit profile may proceed only when the review establishes:

1. an evidenced or honestly bounded source identity;
2. a supported military role;
3. a supported service form or a clearly quarantined unresolved classification;
4. a meaningful difference from nearby Realm, regional, and mercenary profiles;
5. finite-capacity and competing-commitment treatment;
6. chronology and opening disposition;
7. equipment, formation, training, and replacement directions without fabricated precision;
8. source and service geography;
9. Realm relationship and cross-Theater interfaces;
10. player decision value and AI use;
11. name and translation policy;
12. owner review and provenance;
13. a nonduplication disposition;
14. explicit nonauthorization of Opening State and runtime promotion unless separately approved.

A company requires separate evidence and ownership. A unit family does not create a company, and a company name does not create an underlying unit family.

## 15. Meaningful-Difference and Nonduplication Standard

A separate profile should normally require multiple substantive differences among:

- source and service basis;
- chronology;
- equipment and protection;
- formation and command;
- training or experience;
- campaign mobility and terrain use;
- maritime, siege, scouting, escort, or support function;
- eligibility and employer restrictions;
- transport and supply burden;
- finite replacement source;
- withdrawal, return, or closure behavior;
- political or institutional meaning;
- AI use and player decision value.

Reject a separate unit where only the name, color, banner, icon, accent, model decoration, generic regional bonus, or minor statistic changes. Use one canonical definition with multiple owner-approved relationship references when the underlying military content is the same.

## 16. Player-Facing System

The player must be able to understand:

- what the force is and where its identity comes from;
- whether it is regional, household, allied, external, institutional, maritime, contractual, emergency, or event-generated service;
- why the player can currently seek or use it;
- what capacity, route, political, seasonal, or institutional conditions limit access;
- what role it adds beyond the ordinary Realm roster;
- what competing commitment the player is consuming;
- how it replenishes, withdraws, returns, disperses, or closes;
- why a seemingly adjacent source is currently unavailable.

The system should communicate decisive conditions without turning every service relationship into contract-administration busywork.

## 17. AI Employment

AI must:

- use regional and mercenary forces to solve specific strategic or battlefield problems;
- avoid replacing the Realm military identity with hired content;
- respect finite capacity and all competing commitments;
- consider route, transport, supply, season, mission, employer relationship, political cost, and recovery;
- abstain when a normal roster unit solves the problem more coherently;
- avoid access to hidden sources or conditions;
- value scarce specialists according to mission rather than raw auto-resolve power;
- account for local-defense and maritime-capacity opportunity costs;
- evaluate dismissal, retention, return, and recovery after the original need ends;
- never receive free access or replacement solely from difficulty settings.

## 18. Interface to Norse Union, Hegemony, and Consolidation Work

A later authority may define conditional personal unions, negotiated confederation, dynastic union, hegemony, conquest-based consolidation, union crisis, dissolution, or restoration. This authority supplies only the military-content interface.

A future union state may request owner-controlled effects such as:

- wider discoverability of constituent regional sources;
- negotiated military-access relationships;
- shared or coordinated contracting channels;
- expanded maritime coordination;
- union-specific service or reward candidates;
- improved communication, assembly, escort, or recovery arrangements;
- political conditions affecting willingness and replacement.

It must not automatically:

- merge canonical unit profiles;
- erase regional identities;
- transfer source ownership;
- create unlimited pools;
- make every constituent unit recruitable everywhere;
- create ships, crews, companies, officers, equipment, or active forces;
- bypass local autonomy, council, assembly, diplomatic, route, capacity, or supply owners.

How the union is achieved may influence which military benefits and liabilities become eligible. Exact rewards, naval units, achievements, prestige, legitimacy, access rules, and emergence conditions belong to the later authority and their substantive owners.

## 20. Research Priorities

The first bounded research and content pass should inventory rather than fabricate:

1. existing Norse, Denmark, Norway, Sweden, island, Baltic, and cross-Theater brainstorm files;
2. existing Norwegian and other Norse unit profiles that must remain Realm-owned;

The first implementation-helpful outcome is a candidate inventory and disposition map, followed by one justified profile at a time.
