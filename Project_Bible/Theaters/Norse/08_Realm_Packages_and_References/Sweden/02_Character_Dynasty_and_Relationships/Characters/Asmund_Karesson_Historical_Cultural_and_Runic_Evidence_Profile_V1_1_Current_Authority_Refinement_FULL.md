## 1. Concise Verdict

Åsmund Kåresson should be developed as Sweden's first dedicated historical cultural and material-evidence character. His value lies not in court office or royal succession but in the visible objects through which families, communities, patrons, journeys, religious formulae, social claims, and artistic practice can survive into historical memory.

The profile must keep the person, signature, inscription, monument, commissioner, commemorated subject, site, style attribution, text reading, later movement, and modern catalog record separate.

## 2. Core Character Thesis

**A specialist maker whose historical presence is reconstructed through signed and attributed monuments, and whose strategic importance lies in transforming private commemoration and patronage into durable public material evidence without making every inscription true, complete, original, or permanently fixed in place.**

His profile makes cultural production operational: a monument requires commission, design, text, stone, tools, labor, route, site, payment, audience, custody, and survival.

## 15. Text, Translation, and Truth Boundary

The inscription's words are evidence of what was carved or can be reconstructed, not automatic proof of every statement. A text may be damaged, formulaic, commissioned, selective, commemorative, self-interested, later recut, or modernly misread.

Transliteration, normalization, translation, historical interpretation, and game-facing summary are separate layers. The maker does not automatically endorse every claim in a commissioned text.

## 16. Family, Kinship, and Memorial Claims

Runic inscriptions may name relatives and relationships, but each claim belongs to the inscription's evidence record. It does not automatically activate a genealogy or establish biological truth.

A monument can publicize kinship, commemoration, inheritance, reputation, travel, or social standing. Whether those statements are accurate, contested, recognized, or legally effective belongs to genealogy, law, property, and evidence owners.

## 17. Christian Formulae and Religious Boundary

Christian prayers, crosses, or commemorative formulae may be present in the relevant corpus. Such features can support material evidence for Christian expression by commissioners or audiences, but they do not prove Åsmund's exact personal belief, church office, doctrinal formation, missionary role, or identity with Bishop Osmund.

Religious imagery is not jurisdiction. Sacred wording is not ordination. A runestone is not a church institution.

## 18. Art Style and Attribution Boundary

The external discovery source associates Åsmund's work with early Urnes style and credits him with an important Uppland runestone style. This supports a strong art-historical research surface, but stylistic leadership must remain attributed to specialist scholarship rather than converted into a personal innovation Trait.

Style is useful for comparison, chronology, workshop relationships, and attribution. It does not reveal private intention or exact training.

## 19. Workshop and Collaboration

The corpus may involve collaboration, helpers, apprentices, commissioners, text providers, stone suppliers, transporters, painters, and monument raisers. No workshop roster is invented.

A co-signed or similarly styled object may support collaboration review. It does not establish permanent employment, teacher-student hierarchy, household membership, or shared ownership.

## 20. Tools, Materials, and Labor

A commission may require stone selection, extraction or acquisition, transport, planning, cutting tools, sharpening, labor, scaffolding or handling, paint where evidenced, and monument raising.

The profile creates no quarry, tool kit, workshop, wage, or production rate. Material difficulty and route cost should make monumental work consequential rather than instantaneous.

## 21. Site, Route, and Placement

Monument placement requires land or customary permission, route access, transport capacity, local labor, and an intended audience. A road, assembly route, burial context, churchyard, estate, bridge, shore, or settlement may shape meaning only when site evidence supports it.

Current location must not be confused with original location. The external page for U 871 explicitly distinguishes its original Ölsta context from its later display at Skansen.

## 22. Audience and Public Visibility

A raised monument may address family, neighbors, travelers, assembly participants, religious communities, patrons, rivals, or future generations. Audience is inferred through site, text, access, and historical context, not assumed universal.

Visibility can change through vegetation, route changes, relocation, reuse, damage, burial, museum display, restoration, or loss.

## 23. Knowledge and Observer Boundary

If Åsmund is Historical Legacy only, he has no current observer state. Living characters know an inscription only if they encounter, hear, copy, or receive reports about it.

The player should distinguish object existence, object discovery, readable portions, language ability, interpretation, and historical conclusion. A visible stone does not provide perfect translation or universal knowledge.

## 34. Decision Portfolio

### Authenticate a signed work

Review signature, reading, object, and provenance. Benefit: stronger maker attribution. Risk: damage or ambiguity.

### Commission a monument

Only if Åsmund is living and available. Benefit: durable memory. Risk: cost, delay, contested wording, or insecure site.

### Attribute by style

Submit an unsigned work to specialist review. Benefit: corpus understanding. Risk: false certainty.

### Preserve in place

Protect object and context. Benefit: stronger provenance. Risk: weather, conflict, and limited access.

### Move or reuse

Respond to danger or new needs. Benefit: survival or utility. Risk: lost context and changed meaning.

### Quarantine the Osmund merger

Preserve two independent identities. Benefit: historical integrity. Risk: retained uncertainty only.
