## 1. Concise Verdict

Emund is the prior ruler whose absence defines the opening as much as Stenkil's presence. His reign supplies transition uncertainty, prior-line memory, church conflict, possible family connection, regional precedent, and legitimacy arguments, but he never appears as a living or command-capable participant on 1 January 1062.

His gameplay value lies in contested inheritance of consequences. Later people and institutions may remember, invoke, reject, distort, or investigate his reign. They do not receive Emund's authority, knowledge, relationships, property, policy, or intentions.

## 2. Core Character Thesis

**A dead predecessor whose politically active legacy consists of unresolved transition, disputed ecclesiastical precedent, prior-line memory, and competing interpretations rather than continuing personal agency.**

Emund is not a ghost ruler or a bundle of inherited modifiers. He is a provenance-bearing historical subject through whom the campaign asks what survived a reign, who remembers it, whose account controls public understanding, and how Stenkil chooses continuity or departure.

## 34. Decision Portfolio

### Invoke continuity

Use a validated memory or prior arrangement to support current policy. Benefit: familiarity and legitimacy. Risk: counter-memory or false equivalence.

### Mark a break

Reject an Emund-era policy or institutional relationship. Benefit: clarity and new alignment. Risk: alienating those invested in continuity.

### Reconcile church memory

Address Osmund and Bremen-facing legacy through negotiation or institutional review. Benefit: reduced dispute. Risk: reopening authority conflicts.

Leave an uncertain legacy unresolved. Benefit: avoids fabrication. Risk: surrendering the narrative to others.
