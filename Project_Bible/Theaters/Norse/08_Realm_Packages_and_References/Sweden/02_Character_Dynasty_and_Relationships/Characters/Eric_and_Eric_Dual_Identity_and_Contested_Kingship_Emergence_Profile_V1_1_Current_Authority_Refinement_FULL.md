## 1. Concise Verdict

The two Erics should be preserved as two separate later historical candidates whose only safe shared baseline is that a near-contemporary source reports two contenders of the same name in a destructive post-Stenkil kingship conflict. Their personal identities are otherwise unresolved.

The design must make two independent people possible without manufacturing “Eric Stenkilsson” and “Eric the Heathen,” and must make contested kingship possible without scheduling war, mutual death, elite destruction, religious collapse, or Halsten's accession.

## 2. Core Design Thesis

**Two incompletely identified contenders whose political significance can emerge only through separate identity, claim, recognition, coalition, and capacity records, with conflict arising from incompatible authority rather than from a prewritten duel.**

Their distinctiveness comes from the mystery itself: later actors and the player may know that two Erics were reported, yet not know who they were, where they originated, what they believed, or why either was followed.

## 22. Settlement Before War

Before armed conflict, actors may attempt precedence settlement, regional partition, co-rule, withdrawal, marriage or affinity arrangement, hostage or surety terms, assembly arbitration, church mediation, compensation, exile, or delayed review.

The profile does not guarantee that these options are legally or politically acceptable. It requires the AI to consider non-war outcomes when valid.

## 23. Co-rule and Partition Boundary

Co-rule or regionally divided recognition may be possible only through title, office, assembly, law, region, and governance owners. The shared title “king” in a source does not prove equal co-rule or simultaneous universal kingship.

Partition must not become permanent territorial ownership without validated borders, recognition, institutions, and enforcement.

## 24. War and Force Boundary

If war emerges, each side requires independently owned commanders, forces, contributions, routes, supply, intelligence, mandate, objectives, and recovery. The two Erics do not personally encompass their factions.

No battle site, army size, campaign route, command skill, duration, or result is assigned. Severe reported consequences cannot be translated into a fixed casualty multiplier.

## 25. Magnate Loss and Institutional Damage

The source report of extensive elite losses is preserved as a bounded historical claim. A comparable runtime war may harm office continuity, assemblies, households, military leadership, church protection, communication, and regional governance.

Those effects require actual participants and casualties. The term “all magnates” cannot delete every elite character or institution.

## 26. Christianity Disruption Boundary

The report connects conflict with severe disruption to Christianity. Runtime religious consequences may arise through destroyed protection, absent bishops, unsafe routes, lost clergy, damaged property, factional association, or failed communication.

War does not automatically reduce a universal religion score. Effects must be local, institutional, material, and observer-specific.

## 27. Death and Incapacity Boundary

Neither Eric is scheduled to die. If either is killed, incapacitated, captured, missing, or exiled, owners review claim persistence, office state, household, supporters, forces, property, negotiations, regional recognition, and memory.

One death does not automatically cause the other's. Both deaths do not automatically make Halsten king.

## 28. Succession After Conflict

After withdrawal, defeat, or death, the succession system reassesses all eligible candidates. Halsten may become relevant through his own lifecycle, dynasty, claim, recognition, relationships, and capacity. Inge, Anund, Håkan, another candidate, or no stable ruler may also be considered if valid.

The source-reported sequence remains history, not a binding transition table.

## 29. Governance During Rival Recognition

When two candidates hold overlapping recognition, governance may fragment across messages, contributions, justice, property, church protection, routes, and military command.

Neither side controls everything by title label. Institutions may cooperate with one, both, neither, or switch through valid state. Local continuity may persist despite royal conflict.

## 30. Information and Propaganda

Each camp may produce claims about identity, religion, genealogy, legitimacy, atrocities, foreign influence, or divine favor. These are evidence and narrative records, not truth.

The invented later names are useful examples of why propaganda and retrospective simplification need source labeling. Runtime factions may similarly create labels that affect reputation without altering identity.

## 32. Characterization Without Invented Personality

Because personal evidence is absent, neither Eric receives a personality template. Character differentiation must emerge through validated origin, household, claim, sponsors, decisions, treatment of rivals, governance, and crisis conduct.

The design should not create a pious prince versus brutal traditionalist pairing. That would merely convert later speculation into fiction labeled as history.

## 33. AI Candidate Evaluation

Realm and succession AI should evaluate each Eric independently against all other candidates. It should consider identity confidence, eligibility, recognition, relationships, household, governing readiness, church implications, military support, routes, resources, and settlement options.

The AI must not select both simply because history reports two, or oppose them because their names match.

## 34. AI Conflict Evaluation

Conflict AI must explain the incompatible objective, failed settlement, mobilized resources, likely institutional damage, and alternatives. It should model abstention, neutrality, mediation, and limited recognition.

Historical fatality cannot increase willingness to fight. The AI has no knowledge that both were reported to die.

## 35. Player-Facing Strategic Meaning

### Short summary

**Two later contenders known only by the same reported name. They may emerge independently after a genuine succession crisis, but their identities, religions, claims, coalitions, war, deaths, and successor remain unresolved.**

### Opportunities

- discover authentic competing candidates;
- negotiate before mobilization;
- maintain regional continuity under disputed kingship;
- expose fabricated genealogy or religious labels;
- allow one candidate to develop independently of the reported conflict.

### Pressures

- severe identity uncertainty;
- factional propaganda;
- overlapping recognition;
- elite and institutional loss;
- military escalation;
- temptation to script mutual destruction.

## 36. Decision Portfolio

### Investigate each Eric

Build separate identity and claim dossiers. Benefit: informed succession. Risk: delay and contradiction.

### Recognize one candidate

Commit a regional or institutional audience. Benefit: clarity. Risk: polarization and rival reaction.

### Convene settlement

Seek precedence, withdrawal, co-rule, partition, or arbitration. Benefit: reduced destruction. Risk: unstable compromise.

### Remain neutral

Protect local continuity. Benefit: reduced immediate exposure. Risk: retaliation or loss of influence.

### Mobilize

Support an independently validated claimant through proper force owners. Benefit: possible resolution. Risk: war and institutional damage.

### Expose invented identities

Reject unsupported religious or dynastic labels. Benefit: evidence integrity. Risk: weakening a useful political narrative.
