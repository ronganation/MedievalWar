# PART I: VERDICT, PURPOSE, AND OWNERSHIP

## 1. Concise Verdict

Sweden's presentation should communicate a negotiated and regionally varied kingship rather than a uniform modern nation-state. Royal identity, House Stenkil continuity, Christian institutional growth, older religious practice, assemblies, political centers, waterways, island interfaces, regional recognition, and uncertainty should be visible as distinct but related presentation layers.

The presentation must not collapse Sweden into one fixed capital, one heraldic standard, one national church, one national visual culture, one uniform settlement set, one universal military appearance, or one permanent religious conflict. Sigtuna, Uppsala, Skara, Levene, Husaby, Vreta, Västergötland, Östergötland, the Mälaren sphere, Gotland, Öland, and other regional surfaces require differentiated, evidence-bounded treatment.

This authority defines presentation intent and content boundaries only. It creates no canonical banner, coat of arms, portrait, costume, building, unit model, icon, map asset, interface implementation, audio asset, text localization, accessibility certification, or visual-source approval.

# PART III: VISUAL THESIS AND INFORMATION HIERARCHY

## 10. Core Visual Thesis

Sweden should read as a connected but uneven royal project shaped by regional landscapes, inland waterways, Baltic interfaces, assemblies, households, Christian institutions, older religious practice, and emerging political centers.

Presentation should favor layered regional and institutional identity over a single nationalized aesthetic.

## 11. Information Hierarchy

Presentation should distinguish:

- Realm identity;
- royal faction identity;
- Crown, title, office, ruler, dynasty, and household;
- regions and communities;
- political centers and their differing roles;
- institutions and religious practices;
- settlements, routes, property, economy, military, memory, pressure, and uncertainty;
- known, disputed, conditional, historical, external, Unknown, and IntentionalEmpty state.

No single emblem, color, number, or icon should replace these distinctions.

## 12. Visual Restraint

Where evidence is incomplete, visual treatment should remain bounded, generic only at the correct abstraction level, or explicitly uncertain. Unsupported specificity is prohibited.

The absence of a validated asset does not authorize a decorative historical claim.

# PART IV: ROYAL, DYNASTIC, AND HOUSEHOLD PRESENTATION

## 13. Royal Identity

Swedish royal presentation should communicate recognized but negotiated kingship. It must not imply uninterrupted centralized rule, modern sovereignty, universal regional obedience, or direct ownership of every institution, settlement, property, route, or force.

## 14. House Stenkil

House Stenkil may receive a distinct dynastic presentation layer after source and asset validation. Dynasty presentation must remain separate from Realm, Crown, faction, household, title, and office presentation.

Later heraldry cannot be projected backward automatically.

## 15. Royal Household

The Royal Household may receive presentation cues for mobility, residence, protection, communication, ceremony, and continuity through proper owners.

Household presentation cannot imply a permanent palace, centralized court, civil service, standing army, treasury, or fixed capital.

## 16. Character Presentation

Stenkil, Emund the Old, Halsten, and Inge require Character-owner identity, lifecycle, chronology, visual-source, asset, and knowledge validation.

No unsupported facial likeness, exact age, costume, equipment, heraldry, residence, or office scene is canonized here. Emund remains historical-only. Halsten and Inge remain conditional and cannot be visually presented as fixed heirs or adult commanders without owner validation.

# PART V: REGIONAL AND POLITICAL-CENTER DIFFERENTIATION

## 17. Regional Variation

Västergötland, Östergötland, Uppland, the Mälaren sphere, Södermanland, Västmanland, Närke, Värmland, Småland landscape groups, Gotland, Öland, northern interiors, and eastern interfaces may differ in landscape, settlement, material, route, religious, assembly, and institutional cues after source validation.

Regional differentiation must not harden uncertain boundaries into modern province branding.

## 18. Sigtuna

Sigtuna may be presented as a bounded royal, settlement, exchange, craft, literacy, minting-evidence, ecclesiastical-development, landing, and Mälaren-facing surface.

Presentation must not show an automatically operating mint, market, port, cathedral, palace, treasury, storehouse, garrison, or permanent capital.

## 19. Uppsala

Uppsala may use bounded religious, assembly, elite, ceremonial, and memory cues. Presentation must avoid exact unsupported ritual reconstruction, one national older-religion institution, universal opposition, fixed election imagery, or inevitable destruction.

## 20. Skara

Skara may receive bounded western ecclesiastical and settlement cues. Presentation must not imply an owner-validated bishop, cathedral, complete diocesan jurisdiction, school, archive, market, fortification, or garrison unless those owners provide active state.

## 21. Levene, Husaby, and Vreta

These places may receive bounded residence-tradition, church, elite, burial, dynastic, route, settlement, and memory cues. Later importance cannot be presented as opening operation or ownership.

# PART VI: RELIGIOUS PRESENTATION

## 22. Christian Institutions

Christian presentation should show uneven institutional growth and regional variation. It must not present one national church, universal conversion, automatic bishop obedience, complete Hamburg-Bremen control, or church property as Crown property.

## 23. Older Religious Practice

Older religious practice may be shown as regional, household, community, seasonal, site-specific, mixed, contested, or Unknown through proper owners.

It must not become one faction color, one national opposition bloc, one priesthood, one ritual calendar, or one permanent hostility state.

## 24. Mixed Conditions

The interface should allow Christian, older-practice, mixed, uncertain, and contested conditions without forcing binary conversion presentation.

Religious presentation must not disclose hidden community or character state.

# PART VII: MAP, INTERFACE, AND UNCERTAINTY CUES

## 25. Map Presentation

The map may distinguish regions, waterways, islands, coasts, crossings, routes, settlements, political-center roles, institutions, access, hazards, and knowledge confidence through owner-approved cues.

Map cues do not create borders, control, access, operation, or universal knowledge.

## 26. Political-Center Cues

Royal-presence, household-residence, assembly, ecclesiastical, exchange, route, ceremonial, and memory roles should remain independently representable.

The interface must not display one false fixed-capital icon when no fixed capital is owner-validated.

## 27. State and Confidence Cues

The presentation system should distinguish known, bounded, conditional, disputed, historical-only, ReviewOnly, external, Unknown, IntentionalEmpty, and excluded-later-outcome states where player knowledge permits.

Color alone should not carry essential state.

## 28. Tooltips and Explanations

Tooltips should identify subject, known role, owner-controlled state, confidence, dependencies, blockers, and whether information describes a definition, active state, proposal, history, or uncertainty.

Tooltips cannot reveal hidden state or later-history outcomes.

# PART VIII: MILITARY, ECONOMIC, AND ROUTE PRESENTATION

## 29. Military Presentation

Military visuals must distinguish force definitions, unit instances, command, readiness, equipment, ships, crews, escorts, garrisons, routes, supply, and operations.

The presentation must not imply a standing national army or fleet, automatic levies, invented commanders, or unsupported exact troop strength.

## 30. Economy and Property Presentation

Property, possession, use, production, markets, minting evidence, goods, obligations, trade, storage, stock, delivery, receipt, consumption, loss, revenue, and supply require separate presentation states.

Minting evidence must not use the same cue as an operating mint. Exchange importance must not use the same cue as an operating market.

## 31. Route and Access Presentation

Route definitions, known routes, open access, capacity, condition, hazard, transport suitability, and local-season effects must remain distinguishable.

Adjacency must not be presented as access. Waterway presence must not be presented as navigability. Winter must not be presented as universal frozen-water access.

# PART IX: LOCALIZATION AND ACCESSIBILITY

## 32. Localization

Names, titles, offices, institutions, regions, places, labels, tooltips, descriptions, and uncertainty terms require stable localization keys, owner-approved source text, context, plural behavior, grammar notes where required, fallback behavior, and validation.

A localization key is not a translated string. No final localized text is approved here.

## 33. Accessibility

User-facing information should not depend on color alone. Presentation briefs should consider contrast, scalable text, readable icon silhouettes, alternate text, input independence, motion reduction, audio alternatives, subtitle and caption support, and uncertainty clarity where applicable.

These are design requirements, not accessibility validation claims.

## 34. Audio and Motion

Audio, animation, particles, camera motion, and environmental effects require separate briefs, source ownership, accessibility review, implementation, and validation.

No soundscape, music, voice, motion, or effect asset is approved or created here.
