### 0.1 Refined Verdict

Sweden opens with a real kingship, a living adult ruler, an emerging royal dynasty, uneven regional recognition, and a government problem rooted in distance, custom, assembly consent, religious variation, and limited executable capacity. Stenkil is king, but kingship does not make every Swedish region directly controlled, every assembly compliant, every church royal, every route open, or every military contribution available.

The player-facing promise is:

**Make the kingship durable without pretending the realm is already centralized.**

### 6. Opening Strategic Summary

#### Strengths

- living adult king with a supported opening title and office;
- new royal dynasty with two conditional continuity paths;
- strong bounded western association through Västergötland;
- access to assemblies, households, communities, and institutions as negotiated political tools;
- Christian royal patronage and a strong Skara-facing ecclesiastical surface;
- Sigtuna and Mälaren-facing royal, exchange, communication, and institutional potential;
- geographically diverse land, lake, island, coastal, and Baltic interfaces;
- ability to seek regional contributions without maintaining a standing national army.

#### Pressures

- regional recognition and practical control are uneven;
- exact royal itinerary, household composition, property, treasury, and military following remain unresolved;
- House Stenkil continuity exists without a fixed heir or confirmed adult successor;
- Christian institutions coexist with older and mixed religious practice;
- no uniform national church, administration, law, or military system exists;
- winter conditions constrain movement without one uniform weather state;
- Norway, Denmark, Hamburg-Bremen, Gotlandic, Baltic, Finnic, Sámi, Slavic, and Rus-facing interfaces require external owners;
- later conflict traditions must not be preloaded as January 1062 war.

#### Immediate player concerns

- secure Stenkil's practical recognition beyond his strongest western base;
- protect House Stenkil without prematurely fixing succession;
- establish reliable communication between western and Mälaren-facing political surfaces;
- develop Christian institutions without provoking unsustainable confrontation;
- build contribution capacity before demanding military service;
- distinguish royal title from property, revenue, force, and route control;
- preserve options toward Norway, Denmark, Gotland, and Baltic contacts;
- convert negotiated authority into durable institutions without fabricating centralized administration.
