## 2. Strong Authority Verdict

Sweden's health system is the human-continuity layer of a negotiated, regionally uneven kingship. It models whether named people, households, regional communities, institutional groups, travelers, crews, and military contributors can continue meaningful activity when illness, injury, exposure, inadequate support, loss, or prolonged recovery places pressure on finite people and material capacity.

Sweden does not receive one national health score, one universal care network, one royal hospital service, one homogeneous disease risk, one standardized medical profession, or one automatic church-care system. Care emerges from separately validated households, communities, institutions, settlements, providers, routes, supplies, shelter, knowledge, permission, and available time. Royal authority may coordinate, patronize, request, negotiate, or prioritize. Royal authority cannot manufacture people, facilities, competence, supplies, access, consent, recovery, or favorable outcomes.

The playable question is not whether the Realm can buy a health bonus. The playable question is which obligations can be sustained without abandoning the people, households, communities, and institutions that make those obligations possible.

## 3. Player Promise and Total War Scale

The player manages consequences and commitments, not clinical procedures.

The player may:

- investigate a credible health concern;
- protect household or regional recovery capacity;
- request institutional hospitality or assistance;
- reserve transport, shelter, food, water, fuel, and caregivers;
- reduce, delay, redirect, or cancel a conflicting commitment;
- coordinate wounded-person transfer and receiving capacity;
- support a disrupted community or settlement;
- preserve identification, mortality, burial, and family notification chains;
- decide whether partial recovery permits a different useful role;
- accept uncertainty rather than force a false answer.

The player does not prescribe remedies, perform procedures, diagnose from a title or trait, or run a modern hospital administration. Ordinary low-impact conditions remain summarized. Named or regional episodes become prominent only when they materially affect leadership, household continuity, labor, travel, service, recruitment, settlement capacity, institutional cooperation, public order, or a consequential decision.

## 4. Sweden Health Identity

Sweden's Health authority is shaped by:

- negotiated royal authority and uneven regional recognition;
- separately owned households, communities, assemblies, and institutions;
- western, eastern, Mälaren, island, coastal, inland, forest, and external-facing surfaces;
- waterway, crossing, landing, road, portage, and seasonal-access dependence;
- a Winter 1062 opening without one uniform weather condition;
- uneven Christian institutional development and persistent regional variation in practice;
- finite labor, caregiver, transport, military, maritime, and household capacity;
- no automatic Crown ownership of settlements, institutions, routes, goods, people, or care;
- no universal Swedish population, recruitment, levy, crew, or recovery pool;
- a political center that may move among people, institutions, assemblies, routes, and residences rather than operate from one fixed capital.

Health differences emerge from actual ownership, access, capacity, evidence, and local conditions. They do not emerge from stereotypes about region, religion, culture, age, status, or identity.

## 9. Subject and Scope Model

Health supports two principal scopes.

### Named-person scope

A named episode requires an admitted person reference. Health may hold observations, care attempts, uncertainty, and recovery interpretation. Character owners retain private health, availability, incapacity, and life-state mutation.

### Aggregate scope

A cohort episode requires an admitted population or community scope, membership or sampling basis, observation window, uncertainty, and deduplication basis. A cohort pressure never diagnoses every member. Aggregate records must preserve uneven impact, unknown cases, recovery variation, migration, missing information, and overlapping-source risks.

The same person cannot be counted simultaneously as an anonymous cohort death, military casualty death, and named-person death without reconciliation.

## 12. Assessment and Knowledge Limits

Assessment compares observed condition, incident history, time, source quality, reporter access, available learning, local inputs, and competing explanations. It may use `Unknown`, `Suspected`, `SupportedWithinModel`, `Contested`, or `Superseded`.

The authority does not define remedy recipes, dosages, surgical instructions, or clinical protocols. Historical practice may be represented as an evidence-qualified support attempt only after appropriate source and education review. Improvement after an attempt does not prove causation.

Knowledge is observer-specific. A report reaching Stenkil's household does not instantly inform Skara, Sigtuna, Uppsala, a regional assembly, a military commander, an island community, or every institution.

## 13. Disease and Outbreak Boundary

An outbreak candidate requires:

- multiple independently grounded observations or a valid aggregate pattern;
- bounded place and time;
- defined case-pattern scope;
- reporting-coverage assessment;
- population or community scope;
- competing explanations;
- source provenance;
- owner-approved condition model.

One traveler, one rumor, one duplicated report, one settlement condition, one religious group, or one campaign-season label cannot create an outbreak. No transmission rate, prevalence, susceptibility, or mortality modifier is assigned here.

## 14. Injury and Functional Consequences

Injury may follow battle, accident, labor, travel, violence, exposure, or another admitted incident. Health interprets care and recovery. Character or Population writes task-specific availability. Military writes readiness and return to service.

A person may be unable to ride yet able to advise, receive envoys, keep records, or remain within a household. Functional limits must be task-specific. Injury does not erase political identity, office, relationships, household membership, knowledge, or legal rights.

## 20. Regional Health Surfaces

Regional surfaces are decision envelopes, not opening health assignments.

- **Västergötland and Skara-facing surface:** regional households, routes, western ecclesiastical contacts, and institutional hospitality may support bounded care and record interfaces. Skara does not automatically contain an operating hospital, provider network, or superior health state.
- **Östergötland surface:** households, communities, routes, church interfaces, and elite or settlement capacities remain separately owned. No western institutional pattern is copied automatically.
- **Uppland and Mälaren surface:** waterways, Sigtuna, Uppsala, islands, assemblies, movement, landing, and religious variation create complex access and information conditions. Strategic importance does not create care capacity.
- **Södermanland, Västmanland, Närke, and Värmland:** local movement, forest, lake, route, household, and seasonal conditions may differ. Adjacency does not equal shared services.
- **Småland landscapes:** no single health, settlement, route, or community condition applies across the grouping.
- **Gotland and Öland:** island identity does not create Crown access, transport, providers, stock, or evacuation. Receiving help requires the appropriate political, community, route, maritime, and supply gates.
- **Dalarna and northern interiors:** research and interface surfaces only. Sparse evidence cannot be converted into emptiness, immunity, isolation, or universal hardship.
- **Åland-facing, Finland-facing, Baltic, and eastern interfaces:** contact plausibility does not create disease, aid, migration, route access, or Swedish authority.

## 27. Decision Package: Care Competes With Service

**Eligibility:** an admitted care need overlaps agricultural, fishing, craft, transport, institutional, messenger, household, or military reservations.

**Player options:** reduce or delay the competing commitment; find an accepted substitute; negotiate shorter service; redirect supplies; seek external or institutional help; accept partial performance with disclosed consequences.

**Hard rule:** the same caregiver, provider, transport asset, bed space, food stock, or person cannot be assigned twice.

## 28. Decision Package: Institutional Partnership

**Eligibility:** a validated need and an admitted institutional counterpart exist.

**Review:** jurisdiction, permission, existing obligations, people, competence, physical space, goods, routes, security, duration, and handback.

**Options:** request hospitality; supply goods or transport; negotiate a bounded partnership; support household care; use another setting; defer expansion.

A partnership does not transfer property, create obedience, appoint clergy, open a hospital, or guarantee results.

## 29. Decision Package: Route Failure During Care

**Eligibility:** an accepted transfer or delivery cannot proceed as planned.

**Options:** wait with support; divert through an admitted route; request local aid; divide compatible goods; return where feasible; cancel onward travel.

The result must retain consumed supplies, lost time, changed commitments, stranded recipients, and unresolved risk. Selecting diversion does not create a route or carrier.

## 30. Decision Package: Community Health Pressure

**Eligibility:** independently grounded observations indicate a bounded community pressure.

**Review:** observation quality, reporting gaps, population scope, local conditions, competing explanations, labor, food, water, shelter, routes, institutions, and public knowledge.

**Options:** investigate; support households; protect essential services; reduce gathering or travel through lawful owners; request institutional help; prioritize deliveries; preserve uncertainty.

No option creates an outbreak merely to produce drama.

## 31. Decision Package: Leader Limitation and Delegation

**Eligibility:** a Character-owner availability receipt affects Stenkil or another admitted officeholder.

**Review:** private/public knowledge, office responsibilities, travel, command, household, assemblies, diplomacy, deadlines, and alternatives.

**Options:** pause; narrow activity; delegate a bounded task; use local support; change route; reduce a commitment.

No automatic regency, succession, recovery, or historical outcome is created.

## 32. Decision Package: Mortality and Disputed Burial

**Eligibility:** a mortality report exists but identity, confirmation, jurisdiction, custody, access, or permission remains incomplete.

**Options:** verify; preserve an unresolved record; seek an accepted temporary arrangement; negotiate access; route to another permitted site.

Missing never becomes dead. Administrative closure never manufactures certainty.

## 32C. Crisis Escalation, Backlog, and Recovery Pressure

A health concern becomes a Realm-level crisis only when accepted evidence and owner state show material pressure across several connected systems, such as settlement capacity, household continuity, military recovery, route access, institutional support, food or water delivery, political continuity, or public reception.

Escalation requires:

- a bounded affected scope;
- current evidence rather than repeated copies of one report;
- identified capacity or continuity failures;
- unresolved dependencies and opportunity costs;
- an owner-approved review threshold;
- a communication and response surface.

De-escalation requires actual improvement, restored capacity, accepted handoff, reduced scope, or a justified reclassification. Passing time or suppressing a prompt does not end the crisis. Backlogs preserve pending identity, care, transport, burial, support, and review obligations.

## 32D. Interruption, Substitution, Handback, and Closure

A care plan may be interrupted by provider loss, household pressure, military demand, route failure, supply loss, withdrawal of permission, settlement damage, communication failure, or changed recipient state. The record must preserve:

- work already performed;
- goods and time already consumed;
- unresolved need;
- newly exposed dependents or commitments;
- temporary substitutions;
- the next responsible owner;
- review and closure conditions.

Substitution requires an independently valid person, cohort, setting, route, good, or authority. It cannot duplicate an indivisible source or silently reduce another obligation. Handback requires receiving-owner acceptance. Closure requires a resolved outcome, accepted transfer, continuing-support record, mortality handoff, or explicit unresolved disposition.
