## 2. Sweden Realm Thesis

Sweden opens on 1 January 1062 as a negotiated and uneven royal sphere under Stenkil, connecting western and eastern regional networks through recognition, assemblies, households, local law and custom, Christian institutions, inherited royal memory, inland waterways, Baltic routes, contribution, hospitality, communication, and selective enforcement.

Sweden is not modeled as:

- a mature centralized territorial state;
- a fixed-capital monarchy with direct control over every region;
- one national assembly, legal code, church, culture, faith state, army, fleet, economy, or route network;
- a predetermined Stenkil succession sequence;
- a backward projection of later Swedish institutions, borders, rulers, conflicts, or religious outcomes.

The central strategic question is whether Stenkil can convert recognized but uneven kingship into durable royal and dynastic capacity without erasing the regional communities, assemblies, households, institutions, routes, obligations, religious variation, and external interfaces that make authority executable.
