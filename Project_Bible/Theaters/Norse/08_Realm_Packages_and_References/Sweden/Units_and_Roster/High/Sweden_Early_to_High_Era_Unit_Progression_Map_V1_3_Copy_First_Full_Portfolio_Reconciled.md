## 1. Decisive Progression Verdict

Sweden does not receive a universal High Era replacement roster. All twelve Early families remain valid unless their real source, service, equipment, battlefield purpose, or recruitment basis fails.

High Era development proceeds through independent channels:

- continuing the same family with better organization or equipment;
- creating a narrower branch from shared finite capacity;
- establishing a genuinely new institution and card;
- changing an assignment without creating another pool;
- ending fresh recruitment while surviving formations persist;
- failing to develop a proposed High Era family at all.

## 1A. Twelve-Family Double-Check Verdict

The overall architecture survives. All twelve Early families remain valid High Era parents or persistent parallel families. None should be deleted, globally upgraded, or renamed simply because the campaign enters the High Era.

The review now locks four controlling refinements:

1. **Byalag and Byavärn remain separate persistent families beside admitted Svenska Borgarmän.** Borgarmän are a distinct town-resident urban card, not the mandatory replacement of either Early family.
2. **Hundare and Härad persist, while Pansrade Häradsspjutsmän are admitted as the protected Härad-facing branch.** No symmetry-only protected Hundare card is required.
3. **Spjutmän, Sköldmän, Hirdmen, and Huskarla persist beside admitted Svenska Väpnare and Svenska Yxmän.** The new cards own maintained shielded field service and controlled non-household axe pressure rather than replacing their parents.
4. **District Bowmen and Bågskyttar persist beside admitted Bågsvener and Crossbowmen, while Hastkarlar and Hirdryttare persist beside admitted Frälsemän, Svenner av Vapen, and Riddare.** All nine High Era cards are settled design-portfolio decisions; campaign availability remains physical and source-bound.

## 15. Norway and Denmark Benchmark Boundaries

Norway demonstrates persistent district parents beside narrower protected cards, bow and crossbow coexistence, assignment rather than card proliferation, and several mounted pathways without a mandatory upgrade chain.

Denmark demonstrates an ordinary regional spear parent beside an armored branch, persistent bows beside institutional crossbows, retained foot that does not replace the Hird, and knightly cavalry that leaves practical and household mounted cards useful.

Sweden does not automatically receive Norway's Armored Leidang, Bymenn, Svenner, Royal Lancers, or Riddarar, nor Denmark's Armored Jutland Spearmen, Vaebnere, Herremaend Axemen, Crossbowmen, Mounted Vaebnere, or Riddere. Swedish cards require Swedish sources, roles, institutions, chronology, and names.

## 17. Future Additional-Card Admission Gates

Any future additional High Era card is considered only if it answers all material questions:

- Who are the actual nonduplicated people?
- What service, institution, provider, or assignment supports the card?
- What equipment, horses, ammunition, leaders, practice, and support are required?
- What earlier card or duty loses access to those inputs?
- What battlefield role, rhythm, counters, and AI use differ from existing cards?
- Why is this a new card rather than equipment, readiness, veteran state, assignment, or visual expression?
- Why does the parent card remain useful?
- Can recruitment close while survivors persist?
