## 1. Assessment Purpose

This assessment determines the strongest next eastern Realm candidate for the North Africa theater after the developed Almoravid western movement-state and the completed Hammadid central-Maghrib authority portfolio. It evaluates the Zirids of Ifriqiya as the primary eastern-Maghreb candidate, distinguishes a possible Zirid Realm package from Hammadid, Fatimid, Banu Hilal, Banu Sulaym, Sicilian, Kalbid, Norman, and maritime external-owner state, and defines the smallest safe development sequence.

The assessment is an intake and sequencing authority only. It does not create a Zirid Realm package, choose canonical IDs, decide every theater association, populate a January 1062 Opening State, create character profiles, invent territorial control, define a final roster, create Banu Hilal or Banu Sulaym as a universal faction, or activate any implementation work.

## 5. Historical Research Findings and Evidence Discipline

The external source set identifies a Zirid dynasty that ruled Ifriqiya, distinguishes the Zirids from the Hammadid branch, and records a later coastal phase centered at Mahdia. ### 5.2 Fatimid relationship

The Cambridge article on Fatimid-Zirid diplomacy describes embassies, chancery letters, presents, investiture, legitimacy, and the importance of the connection between Cairo and Zirid Ifriqiya, including rupture and intervention in the eleventh century. This makes the Fatimids a required external diplomatic, religious-legitimacy, information, and historical-pressure interface for Zirid development.

The relationship must not be reduced to a simple Catholic-versus-Protestant analogy. That analogy can be a useful reminder that political authority, legitimacy, doctrine, institutions, and international alignment interact, but the Zirid-Fatimid relationship requires its own Islamic, dynastic, regional, and chronological evidence. The assessment therefore requires distinct records for religious affiliation, public recognition, caliphal allegiance, dynastic legitimacy, chancery practice, diplomacy, coinage or sermon evidence where supported, and practical political behavior.

### 5.3 Banu Hilal and Banu Sulaym

The user-provided History Atlas page states that Banu Hilal and Banu Sulaym had been moved into Ifriqiya by the Fatimids and were present in the Mahdia-related context by 1060. Britannica describes Banu Hilal and Banu Sulaym movement into the Maghreb at Fatimid instigation and identifies long-term political instability in the eastern Maghreb. These sources support treating both as consequential actor families.

They do not justify a single universal Banu Hilal Realm with one leader, one roster, one territory, one loyalty value, or one unified private state.

### 5.4 Sicily and the central Mediterranean

Research on the Zirids and Normans of Sicily describes economic, political, cultural, and military connections across the medieval Mediterranean and rejects a single overarching ideological explanation for every phase of their relationship. This supports a flexible interface capable of collaboration, commerce, rivalry, conflict, opportunism, and changing policy rather than a permanently scripted religious war.

The Zirid assessment should move the North Africa workstream toward Sicily without claiming Sicily as Zirid private state. Sicily, its local Muslim polities, Kalbid legacies, emerging Norman power, ports, trade, diplomacy, military intervention, and information routes require their own owners and theater associations.
