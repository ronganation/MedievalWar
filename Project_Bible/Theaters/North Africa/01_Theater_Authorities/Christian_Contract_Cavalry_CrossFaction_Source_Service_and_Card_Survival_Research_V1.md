The reviewed sources support a real broader phenomenon of Christian soldiers from Iberian kingdoms serving Muslim rulers in al-Andalus and the Maghreb. The reviewed Farfan summary describes later medieval soldiers, mostly from Christian Iberian kingdoms, serving Western Mediterranean Muslim dynasties as mercenaries and fighting in dense heavy cavalry or infantry formations under Christian officers. The same summary says the origins of Farfan units are uncertain and presents several possible routes, including allied contingents, exiles, captivity and later service, and deported communities.

The reviewed Reverter summary describes a Catalan noble and military leader captured in the 1120s who later led Christian mercenaries for the Almoravid state in North Africa against Almohad pressure. That is later than the 1062 opening and supports a campaign-emergent possibility, not an opening pool.

The reviewed academic article abstract concerns Christian mercenaries in the Almohad Caliphate during the later twelfth and thirteenth centuries and explicitly separates mercenary service from slave-soldier status. Its chronology strengthens later cross-faction research but does not establish an opening Almoravid card.

The service family survives because it can create a distinct campaign and battlefield decision: a ruler gains temporary or renewable access to formed Christian cavalry through diplomacy, exile, contract, negotiated protection, captivity followed by legally distinct service, or another source-valid route. The family is neither faction-owned population nor a universal tavern-style mercenary pool.

No direct unit profile is authorized yet. The phenomenon spans different chronologies, legal statuses, political arrangements, equipment sources, command relationships, and controllers. The next work must close the **service model and first consumer**, not merely select a famous name.

## 2. Semantic and Ethical Boundary

```text
Christian identity
!= one culture
!= one kingdom
!= one political loyalty
!= mercenary status

Mercenary
!= enslaved person
!= prisoner of war
!= exile
!= allied contingent
!= permanent household retainer

Contract
!= ownership of people
!= unlimited recruitment
!= guaranteed loyalty
!= service against every target
```

The project must distinguish voluntary paid service, sovereign-lent contingents, household or retainer service, exile bands, captives, freed people, coerced service, deported communities, converted individuals, and descendants of earlier service communities. These categories can intersect historically but cannot be merged into one low-cost recruitment mechanic.

Coercion, captivity, enslavement, hostage status, forced settlement, and conversion require separate historical, legal, population, and character ownership. They cannot be represented as an ordinary contract toggle.

## 9. Target and Mission Restrictions

A contract may restrict service against the source ruler's co-religionists, home kingdom, kin, allies, or specific places. Another contract may permit broader service. The project does not assume one universal religious restriction.

```yaml
mission_terms_may_define:
```

A ruler may seek the formation precisely because it is outside local tribal, urban, or dynastic networks. That potential political value is a research hypothesis and must not be assumed for every contract.

## 11. Equipment, Horses, and Replacement

A mounted contract formation requires real riders, horses, remounts, grooms, tack, armor, weapons, officers, and maintenance. Equipment may arrive with the formation, be supplied by the receiving ruler, be purchased locally, or become mixed over time. Each route changes appearance, dependency, and replacement.

```text
Contract signed
!= horses created
!= armor created
!= remount system created
!= local replacement pool created
```

Heavy equipment or European fighting style cannot be inferred for every Christian formation. The consumer must establish the actual class and source.

## 14. AI and Player Contract

AI may seek Christian contract cavalry when the service offers a real military capability and the diplomatic, financial, political, and replacement costs are acceptable. AI must compare local household, regional, allied, and other alternatives.

AI cannot:

- buy a universal unit from a menu;
- ignore target restrictions;
- replenish without a source;
- treat captives as volunteers;
- use the same people for multiple contracts;
- retain the formation after breach or expired service without a valid outcome.

The player-facing briefing should explain the source, legal and diplomatic route, mission limits, cost, command, current strength, replacement route, reliability evidence, known restrictions, and consequences of breach or dismissal.

## 15. Acceptance Fixtures

1. **Taifa alliance contingent:** cavalry arrives under an allied agreement but remains owned by the contributing polity and leaves when terms end.
2. **Almoravid later emergence:** a Reverter-like leader and contingent arise only from actual campaign events and owner-valid status changes; no historical script guarantees them.
3. **Contract heavy cavalry:** the unit provides formed shock distinct from Amir's Horse Guard and Andalusi Lancers; fail if it is only a reskin.
4. **Mission restriction:** the formation refuses or renegotiates a prohibited target according to its contract; fail if every contract permits every war.
5. **Source closure:** war, diplomacy, route loss, or source-political change closes replacements while veterans remain.
6. **Settlement:** survivors settle under explicit privileges and obligations; fail if settlement instantly becomes local population ownership.
7. **Defection or breach:** command, pay, treatment, or political change can end service; fail if contract loyalty is absolute.
8. **Captive distinction:** captivity alone does not create a mercenary unit.
9. **Cross-faction expression:** two Realms may field similarly named contract cavalry but retain separate internal identities, terms, visuals, and battle roles.
10. **Remote campaign:** AI developments can produce or prevent the service family without an arbitrary preset.
