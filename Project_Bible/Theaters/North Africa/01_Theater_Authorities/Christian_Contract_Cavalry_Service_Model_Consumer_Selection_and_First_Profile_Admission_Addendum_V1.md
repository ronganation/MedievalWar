## 2. Why the Almoravids Are the First Consumer

The reviewed <External>Reverter</External> summary describes a Catalan military leader captured during Iberian fighting in the 1120s and later commanding Christian mercenaries for the Almoravids in North Africa against Almohad pressure. The source supports a later Almoravid service possibility, not a January 1062 formation or guaranteed historical event. citeturn26search94

The first-consumer selection is a **design decision** based on that later historical prompt and the current campaign-path architecture. It does not claim that Reverter's exact route, date, contingent, leadership, or outcome must occur in any campaign.

The Almoravid consumer also provides the clearest anti-bloat test:

- Amir's Horse Guard owns internal household protection and countercharge.
- Andalusi Lancers own regionally sourced Andalusi heavy or shock cavalry development.
- Saharan Horsemen own their own allied or regional mounted-service role.
- Christian Contract Cavalry must therefore own an external, bounded, agreement-specific formed cavalry service with a different replacement and departure cycle.

## 11. Political, Religious, and Social Effects

A Christian contingent in Almoravid service can affect audiences differently. Potential support, opposition, suspicion, pragmatic acceptance, or prestige changes must come from Realm, religious, juristic, diplomacy, population, and public-order owners. This file does not assign a universal reaction.

The formation may provide a ruler with military capability less embedded in local confederational or urban networks. That is a proposed political value, not an automatic loyalty bonus. External troops can also create cost, resentment, jurisdiction disputes, dependence, or reputational risk.

## 12. Pay, Privilege, Land, and Supply

A contract may use money, gifts, booty shares, privileges, safe conduct, land revenue, settlement permission, protection, or another owner-valid consideration. The file does not assert one standard payment.

```text
Promised payment
!= treasury funds delivered

Land grant
!= empty or ownerless land

Privilege
!= unlimited immunity

Paid formation
!= replaceable formation
```

Failure to meet terms can lower readiness, block renewal, produce negotiation, desertion, breach, or defection according to owner-backed procedures.

## 13. AI and Player Use

AI may pursue the family when formed shock capability, political distance from local networks, or immediate strategic need outweighs financial, diplomatic, religious, command, and replacement risks.

AI must compare:

- Amir's Horse Guard;
- Andalusi Lancers;
- Saharan or other allied horse;
- infantry and missile alternatives;
- diplomacy and contract cost;
- whether the mission fits restrictions;
- whether source and transport are reliable;
- whether nonrenewal would create a future capability gap.

The player sees the source, leader, route, term, mission, restrictions, pay or privilege, current strength, replacement source, known confidence, strongest blocker, and consequences of breach. Hidden information remains hidden.

## 16. Acceptance Fixtures

1. **No opening source:** January 1062 has no generic Christian contract cavalry formation or pool.
2. **Ally contingent:** a Christian ruler commits a heavy formation for one named war; replacements remain source-owned.
3. **Exile band:** a displaced leader and followers negotiate service; the band can leave or settle only through valid terms.
4. **Captive distinction:** captured riders remain captives until a separate status and service transition passes.
5. **Heavy-shock separation:** the formation charges and holds an agreed objective, while Amir's Horse Guard remains the household protection and extraction card.
6. **Andalusi separation:** a regional Andalusi lancer source does not convert into contract cavalry merely because riders are Christian.
7. **Target restriction:** a prohibited target blocks an order or triggers renegotiation rather than being ignored.
8. **Breach:** unpaid or mistreated service can suspend, end, or redirect the contract according to owner-backed results.
9. **Replacement closure:** loss of source contact or horses prevents replacement while veterans persist.
10. **Alternative history:** the family may never arise, may serve another Realm, or may appear under different leadership without breaking the campaign.
