The evidence currently supports three different propositions that must not be collapsed:

1. Oghuz or Ghuzz were a broad western Turkic people whose warriors served widely in Islamic armies. The reviewed summary states that Oghuz warriors served in Islamic armies from the 1000s and reached Spain and Morocco, but it does not identify a January 1062 western formation, employer, contract, or unit establishment. citeturn28search107
2. Nomadic Ghuzz or Seljuk military traditions included effective mounted archery. The reviewed military-technology extract describes true nomadic horse archers as important in early Seljuk armies and emphasizes that shooting style varied by tactical circumstance. That extract concerns the wider Islamic military world, not a direct North African roster attestation. citeturn28search112
3. A secondary military summary states that Turcomans served with the Almohads in Spain and North Africa and were still called Ghuzz, relying heavily on light horse archers. This supports a later Almohad or western Mediterranean research direction, but the summary does not close exact dates, formation size, migration route, employer, or Almoravid access. citeturn28search114

The <External>Medieval Kingdoms Total War: The Almohad Caliphate</External> comparator presents Ghuzz Horse Archers as Turkish mercenaries associated with Iberian Islamic cavalry armies and estates in Ifriqiya. That description remains a mod-design prompt until independently sourced. citeturn23view149

## 13. Campaign Effects

A Ghuzz or validated Oghuz mounted-archery source can alter the Theater by:

- providing a rare mobile missile capability to one controller;
- creating diplomatic competition for a finite external formation or community;
- increasing the value of routes, patronage, safe conduct, settlement, and horse support;
- changing how armies protect marches, flanks, camps, water, and supply;
- creating military dependence if a Realm builds tactics around a source that later leaves;
- generating different faction expressions without universal roster symmetry.

These are design consequences of an admitted source. They are not claims that such a source exists in the opening scenario.

## 14. AI and Player Contract

AI must seek the source only through reachable information, contact, migration, diplomacy, settlement, or service networks. AI must compare the capability against cost, route, horses, ammunition, political reception, command integration, and the risk that the source departs.

In battle, AI must:

- preserve range and escape paths;
- avoid stationary missile duels when mobility is the advantage;
- avoid prepared heavy cavalry interception;
- manage ammunition and horse fatigue;
- coordinate with pinning infantry or cavalry rather than operate as an isolated trick unit;
- withdraw or switch to limited support when ammunition or cohesion fails.

The player-facing panel must show source, route, legal status, employer relationship, availability, replacement path, ammunition and remount constraints, mission limits, and strongest blocker.

## 18. Acceptance Fixtures

1. **No opening source:** no faction has Ghuzz horse archers in January 1062 from this research alone.
2. **Almoravid survival without source:** High Era remains complete without the family.
3. **Later source arrival:** a formation or community can enter the Theater only through valid migration, contract, patronage, or settlement.
4. **Almohad conditional access:** later historical direction does not create an automatic Almohad pool.
5. **Ifriqiyan settlement claim:** no estate or settled service is created until evidence and property owners pass.
6. **Mounted-javelin separation:** Lamtuna Cavalry and Andalusi Jinetes cannot be converted by research or equipment issue alone.
7. **Saharan separation:** medium regional cavalry does not become horse archers without an actual source and skill system.
8. **Finite depth:** one arriving formation cannot produce unlimited copies.
9. **Ammunition and fatigue:** prolonged harassment eventually loses effectiveness.
10. **Interception:** fast or well-positioned enemy cavalry can punish failed withdrawal.
11. **Controller change:** a new ruler must renegotiate service or settlement; the source does not transfer automatically.
12. **Remote campaign variability:** the source may serve a different Realm, remain outside the region, fragment, settle, or never appear.
