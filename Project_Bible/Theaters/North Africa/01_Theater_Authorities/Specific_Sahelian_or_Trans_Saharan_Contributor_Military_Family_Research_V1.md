## 13. Campaign Effects

A valid contributor may:

- deepen a southern commercial, diplomatic, military, or settlement relationship;
- make route security, water, market access, safe conduct, and information strategically important;
- provide a finite capability while displacing the contributor's local defense or other obligations;
- create dependence on external replacement or a vulnerable corridor;
- generate political competition among North African Realms;
- preserve a contributor after controller transition through renegotiated service;
- close, return, migrate, fragment, or change employer as the campaign changes.

No flat faction bonus is granted. Outcomes return to diplomacy, population, economy, geography, military, property, and information owners.

## 16. Acceptance Fixtures

- **Trade contact only:** no formation appears.
- **Route control only:** control of a corridor does not transfer neighboring people into a pool.
- **Generic label:** “Sahelian infantry” without a source fails admission.
- **Existing escort:** eligible spearmen assigned to a caravan do not create a new family.
- **Specific allied contingent:** a named source may provide a bounded formation if people, equipment, command, route, and terms pass.
- **Contributor refusal:** good relations do not override local defense or another owner obligation.
- **Blocked water or passage:** an accepted agreement cannot deliver a formation through an unusable route.
- **Finite loss:** casualties and equipment losses constrain replacement.
- **Controller change:** service must be renegotiated and is not inherited automatically.
- **No racial attributes:** combat statistics never derive from skin color or a broad continental label.
- **Coercion firewall:** captives or enslaved people do not become an ordinary recruitable family.
- **Alternative history:** the contributor may serve another Realm, remain local, migrate, fragment, or never enter North African service.
