## 1. Concise Verdict

Active Almoravid faction at opening: recommended. The Almoravids should exist as an active faction at the campaign opening, but the opening faction must not be authored as the later trans-Maghreb-and-al-Andalus empire already completed.

The strongest working interpretation is:

```text
January 1062:
  Active Almoravid movement-polity
  Active military expansion
  Active religious and confederational legitimacy
  Active Saharan and southern Maghrebi power base
  Northern state formation still incomplete
  Marrakesh foundation phase uncertain
  Abu Bakr ibn Umar and Yusuf ibn Tashfin authority relationship unresolved in exact form
  No control of southern Iberia
  No inevitable invitation from the taifas
  No inevitable Battle of Sagrajas
  No inevitable annexation of the taifas
```

The opening design should therefore use a **Movement-to-Empire Faction Model**, not a conventional finished kingdom template.

The key problem is not whether the Almoravids exist. The movement had already emerged, conquered important Saharan and southern Moroccan centers, lost its founding religious leader, and continued expansion under new leadership before January 1062. The key problem is **which authority configuration is active on the exact opening date**, what is directly controlled, what is only militarily occupied or confederationally bound, and whether a new capital is already founded, under construction, or still only an emerging military-administrative base.

## 2. Why January 1062 Is Historically and Mechanically Valuable

The project opening falls at a rare transition point:

```text
Founding reform movement:
  already exists

First conquests:
  already occurred

Abdallah ibn Yasin:
  already dead

Abu Bakr ibn Umar:
  active senior military-confederational leader

Yusuf ibn Tashfin:
  active northern lieutenant and future imperial ruler

Marrakesh:
  disputed foundation window, commonly 1062 or about 1070

Fes:
  not yet securely an opening possession under the safer chronology

Tlemcen, Tangier, Ceuta:
  later northern expansion targets

Al-Andalus intervention:
  decades later and contingent
```

This creates a faction with immediate strategic action but incomplete institutional settlement. The Almoravid campaign begins with momentum rather than with a static crowned realm.

## 3. Historical Baseline

### 3.1 Movement origin

The Almoravid movement emerged among western Saharan Sanhaja groupings, especially the Lamtuna, Gudala, and Massufa. Religious reform, tribal confederation, military discipline, and control of trans-Saharan routes were intertwined from the movement's beginning.

The package must not flatten this into:

```text
One tribe
One dynasty
One army
One religious office
One hereditary king
One fixed capital
```

### 3.2 Early leadership sequence

The safe high-level sequence is:

```text
Yahya ibn Ibrahim:
  early reform patron and tribal leader

Abdallah ibn Yasin:
  founding religious and ideological leader

Yahya ibn Umar:
  early military leader

Abu Bakr ibn Umar:
  succeeding military and confederational leader

Yusuf ibn Tashfin:
  northern lieutenant who later consolidates dominant imperial authority
```

Exact dates, titles, and transfers require source-level reconciliation. Leadership must be modeled as multiple authority channels rather than one universal ruler score.

### 3.3 Pre-opening conquests and activity

By the mid-1050s the movement had captured or contested strategically important centers connected to Saharan trade, including Sijilmasa and Awdaghust. Aghmat was taken before the opening date. Abdallah ibn Yasin died in 1059 during conflict with the Barghawata. Expansion therefore remains active in the early 1060s.

This supports an opening military situation.

It does not prove one exact army stack, exact troop count, exact commander location, or exact campaign objective on 1 January 1062.

### 3.4 Marrakesh foundation dispute

Sources commonly give either 1062 or approximately 1070 for the foundation of Marrakesh. Attribution also varies between Abu Bakr ibn Umar, Yusuf ibn Tashfin, or a phased process initiated by one and consolidated by the other.

Therefore Marrakesh must enter review as a **Foundation Process**, not as an unquestioned completed capital.

Possible reconciled stages:

```text
Stage 0:
  no founded city; candidate base only

Stage 1:
  surveyed or selected encampment

Stage 2:
  military camp and administrative concentration

Stage 3:
  settlement foundation underway

Stage 4:
  functioning capital without later monumental form

Stage 5:
  consolidated imperial capital
```

For January 1062, Stage 1, 2, or 3 is plausible enough for review. Stage 4 requires stronger source approval. Stage 5 is later history and must be rejected at opening.

## 5. Authority Decomposition

The faction requires separate authority channels.

### 5.1 Religious-reform authority

Owns:
- doctrinal legitimacy;
- reform obligations;
- juristic endorsement interfaces;
- discipline expectations;
- religious reputation;
- accusations of deviation or laxity;
- relationships with Maliki scholars.

Does not automatically own:
- every military command;
- treasury;
- tribal allegiance;
- every mosque;
- succession;
- direct character loyalty.

### 5.2 Confederational authority

Owns:
- participating tribal and clan commitments;
- mobilization obligations;
- recognition of senior leadership;
- dispute resolution;
- desert-route cooperation;
- confederation fracture risk.

Does not imply identical loyalty or equal integration among Lamtuna, Gudala, Massufa, or other associated groups.

### 5.3 Military authority

Owns:
- named campaigns;
- army command;
- recruitment and contingent requests;
- garrisons and field forces;
- operational objectives;
- discipline and supply;
- surrender and occupation requests.

An active opening campaign may exist only when the exact target, commander, army identity, and location are reconciled.

### 5.4 Territorial authority

Owns:
- directly controlled settlements;
- occupied settlements;
- tributary settlements;
- allied territories;
- contested corridors;
- routes and crossings;
- local administrative reach.

Conquest does not instantly create uniform administration.

### 5.5 Trade-route authority

Owns:
- caravan-route access;
- transit security;
- toll and customs claims;
- gold and salt route interfaces;
- oasis dependencies;
- market access;
- route disruption.

Trade-route control is strategically important, but unknown revenue is not zero and must not become invented opening treasury.

### 5.6 Dynastic and household authority

Owns:
- lineage;
- household membership;
- marriage;
- parentage;
- succession eligibility;
- family continuity;
- household resources where evidenced.

The dynasty does not own the movement, confederation, religious program, or every army simply because later rule becomes hereditary.

### 5.7 Urban and capital authority

Owns:
- Aghmat's current administrative role;
- Marrakesh foundation process;
- court location;
- treasury location;
- military camp status;
- building and water requirements;
- capital transfer.

The city foundation should be a strategic project, not a decorative date trigger.

## 6. Opening Leadership Problem

### 6.1 Abu Bakr ibn Umar

The opening package should treat Abu Bakr ibn Umar as a major senior authority candidate with strong confederational and military standing.

Questions requiring reconciliation:
- exact title on 1 January 1062;
- exact location;
- active campaign;
- direct territorial control;
- authority over northern forces;
- authority over Yusuf ibn Tashfin;
- role in Marrakesh's founding;
- whether southern and northern responsibilities are already separating.

### 6.2 Yusuf ibn Tashfin

The opening package should treat Yusuf ibn Tashfin as a major northern military and administrative authority, but must not back-project the fully consolidated later ruler into January 1062 without evidence.

Questions requiring reconciliation:
- exact title;
- exact delegation from Abu Bakr;
- command scope;
- residence or headquarters;
- role at Aghmat or Marrakesh;
- recognized succession or merely practical authority;
- whether independent policy-making has begun;
- whether the later north-south division is already active.

### 6.3 Dual-leadership design

The highest-value design is a **dual but asymmetric authority structure**:

```text
Abu Bakr ibn Umar:
  senior confederational legitimacy
  major field authority
  southern and movement continuity

Yusuf ibn Tashfin:
  northern operational authority
  settlement and administrative consolidation
  emerging long-horizon state-builder
```

This relationship must be directed and mutable. It is not automatically rivalry, loyalty, friendship, or eventual peaceful transfer.

## 9. Movement-to-Empire Mechanics

The Almoravid package introduces mechanics that should be reusable but not overgeneralized.

### 9.1 Faction form transition

```text
Religious Reform Movement
  -> Military Confederation
  -> Territorial Movement-State
  -> Consolidated Emirate
  -> Trans-Maghreb Empire
  -> Maghreb-and-al-Andalus Empire
```

Transitions are pressure pathways, not mandatory level-ups.

### 9.2 Confederation cohesion

Separate:
- doctrinal agreement;
- campaign success;
- desert-route security;
- tribal obligations;
- leader recognition;
- distribution of spoils;
- delegation confidence;
- regional balance;
- fatigue and overextension.

Do not collapse these into one cohesion score.

### 9.3 Reform legitimacy

Reform legitimacy may influence:
- recruitment willingness;
- juristic endorsement;
- discipline;
- taxation acceptance;
- relations with urban elites;
- justification for removing rulers;
- accusations of hypocrisy;
- internal opposition.

It must not function as universal religious truth or automatic popular loyalty.

### 9.4 Capital foundation project

Marrakesh can become a campaign-map strategic project with:
- site security;
- water access;
- labor and material needs;
- market relocation;
- military encampment;
- court relocation;
- mosque and administrative construction;
- relations with Aghmat;
- tribal neutrality or rivalry;
- route connectivity.

The project must avoid routine construction clicks. Major phases should create strategic consequences.

### 9.5 Delegated regional command

Long distances require delegated authority:
- southern Sahara;
- trade-route zone;
- Aghmat and Haouz;
- northern Morocco;
- coastal campaigns;
- later strait and al-Andalus commands.

Delegation creates capability and risk. Delegation is not ownership transfer.

### 9.6 Expeditionary intervention

The later Iberian entry should use an expeditionary contract:

```text
Invitation or request
Threat assessment
Juristic endorsement
Cross-strait access
Landing port
Supply commitment
Taifa cooperation
Command hierarchy
War objective
Duration
Payment or subsidy
Right to retain garrisons
Right to negotiate separately
Return condition
Renewal
Breach
```

The 1086 intervention must arise from dated facts and owner decisions, not from a scripted countdown.

### 9.7 Protector-to-overlord risk

A taifa may invite military aid without consenting to annexation.

Separate:
- military assistance;
- protection;
- alliance;
- tribute;
- military access;
- garrison rights;
- arbitration;
- deposition claim;
- annexation;
- direct rule.

This is essential to model the transition from invited defense to later Almoravid conquest without treating that conquest as inevitable.

## 11. Later al-Andalus Emergence

### 11.1 Required dated pressures

Potential dated or emergent facts include:
- Christian conquest or siege of a major taifa center;
- escalating paria burden;
- taifa military defeat;
- juristic petition;
- formal invitation;
- loss of Toledo in 1085 if reached by simulation;
- strait access becoming available;
- Almoravid consolidation in the Maghreb;
- internal Almoravid leadership settlement;
- taifa breach of cooperation;
- request for renewed intervention.

### 11.2 1086 is not guaranteed

The Battle of Sagrajas or al-Zallaqa is a historical outcome, not an Opening State script.

It may occur only if:
- the relevant powers exist;
- armies can assemble;
- an invitation or intervention decision occurs;
- access and supply are available;
- the strategic situation produces the battle.

The simulation may produce a different battlefield, date, commander combination, or no intervention.

### 11.3 annexation is not guaranteed

Later Almoravid deposition and annexation of taifa rulers must require:
- authority and capability;
- political or juristic justification;
- local and military conditions;
- owner decisions;
- successful operations;
- settlement control;
- administrative capacity.
