# 1. Concise Verdict

The available evidence supports an active Almoravid faction on 1 January 1062, but it does not yet support a finished empire, a single uncontested imperial ruler, a completed capital at Marrakesh, a fixed January army position, or any authority in al-Andalus.

The safest provisional opening interpretation is:

```text
FactionForm:
  Religious reform movement
  Military confederation
  Emerging territorial state

SeniorAuthorityCandidate:
  Abu Bakr ibn Umar

NorthernOperationalAuthorityCandidate:
  Yusuf ibn Tashfin

EstablishedUrbanBase:
  Aghmat

Marrakesh:
  Foundation process under review

ActiveMilitaryPressure:
  Strongly supported

ExactOpeningCampaign:
  ReviewOnly

OpeningControlOfAlAndalus:
  false

OpeningEmpireComplete:
  false
```

The Source Inventory supports the completed Historical Reconciliation and remains the provenance and uncertainty authority. Registry V1.1 design authority and a Static-Checked Opening State content package now exist, but this inventory does not mutate either surface or authorize Theater association activation.

# 2. Source-Investigation Questions

The inventory is designed to answer, or preserve uncertainty around, twelve opening questions:

1. Who holds senior movement authority on 1 January 1062?
2. What authority has already been delegated to Yusuf ibn Tashfin?
3. Where are Abu Bakr ibn Umar and Yusuf ibn Tashfin physically located?
4. Is one field campaign underway, recently concluded, or being prepared?
5. Which settlements are directly controlled, occupied, tributary, allied, or only claimed?
6. What is Aghmat's political, military, commercial, and household status?
7. Has the Marrakesh site been selected, encamped, founded, or not yet begun?
8. Which Sanhaja groups are active participants, and under what obligations?
9. What survives from Abdallah ibn Yasin's religious authority after death?
10. Which trade-route claims are exercised rather than merely desired?
11. Which accounts derive from later chronicles, dynastic legitimation, or modern synthesis?
12. What minimum scenario design is needed to make the faction playable without fabricating opening facts?

# 4. Source Tiers

## 4.1 Tier A: Medieval narrative and documentary sources

Priority source families for specialist retrieval:

- al-Bakri, particularly western Saharan geography, peoples, routes, and settlements;
- Ibn Idhari, especially the Almoravid conquest sequence and Aghmat chronology;
- Ibn Abi Zar and the Rawd al-Qirtas tradition, with explicit late-composition caution;
- Ibn Khaldun, especially Sanhaja, Lamtuna, Gudala, Barghawata, and movement succession;
- al-Baydhaq and Almohad-era material where later retrospective claims concern Almoravid institutions;
- numismatic evidence for ruler names, titles, mints, and chronology;
- archaeological evidence from Aghmat and Marrakesh;
- legal, biographical, and Maliki scholarly traditions where religious office and juristic authority are concerned.

Tier A is not automatically infallible. Medieval sources may be late, partisan, geographically distant, or shaped by later dynastic narratives.

## 4.2 Tier B: Specialist modern scholarship

Priority modern research families:

- Amira K. Bennison, *The Almoravid and Almohad Empires*;
- Ronald A. Messier, *The Almoravids and the Meanings of Jihad*;
- H. T. Norris on Saharan and Sanhaja history;
- N. Levtzion and J. F. P. Hopkins on West African and Saharan Arabic sources;
- Pierre Guichard and Maghrebi/Andalusi political scholarship;
- archaeological publications from the Mission Archéologique d'Aghmat;
- scholarship on Marrakesh's foundation, Almoravid urbanism, water systems, and early state formation;
- specialist numismatic catalogues and mint studies.

These sources should control chronology and institutional interpretation where accessible.

## 4.3 Tier C: Reliable reference synthesis

Useful for orientation and cross-checking, not sufficient alone for contested opening facts:

- Encyclopaedia Britannica;
- Encyclopaedia of Islam entries;
- Cambridge histories and reference companions;
- EBSCO research summaries;
- museum and university summaries;
- carefully sourced archaeological-site publications.

## 4.4 Tier D: Discovery aids

Use only to identify claims and source leads:

- Wikipedia and Wikiwand;
- broad educational encyclopedias;
- public history articles;
- library discovery pages;
- general timelines.

Tier D claims must not become canonical solely because several derivative pages repeat the same date.

## 4.5 Tier E: Visual and popular material

Maps, videos, generated illustrations, game maps, social-media posts, unsourced infographics, and tourism pages are visual or discovery references only. They cannot establish opening authority, army location, settlement control, or precise chronology.

# 5. Core Chronology Inventory, c. 1035–1073

## 5.1 Reform movement before territorial expansion

```text
Claim:
  A religious reform initiative develops among western Saharan Sanhaja groups.

Associated figures:
  Yahya ibn Ibrahim
  Abdallah ibn Yasin

Opening relevance:
  Explains why religious authority cannot be reduced to dynastic kingship.

Disposition:
  StronglySupportedContext
```

The movement's early history is commonly narrated through the pilgrimage of Yahya ibn Ibrahim, contact with reformist scholarship, and the preaching of Abdallah ibn Yasin. Exact dates, group participation, and the meaning of a ribat require specialist source reconciliation.

## 5.2 Conquest and loss of Sijilmasa

```text
Approximate period:
  early to mid-1050s

Strategic meaning:
  access to a major trans-Saharan commercial gateway

Opening assumption prohibited:
  permanent uninterrupted control without review

Disposition:
  StronglySupportedContext
```

Sijilmasa should eventually be separated into city, market, mint, route node, garrison, jurisdiction, and revenue surfaces. Conquest cannot be translated directly into uniform government or fixed income.

## 5.3 Awdaghust and Saharan trade-route expansion

```text
Approximate period:
  mid-1050s

Strategic meaning:
  pressure on trans-Saharan trade and route networks

Opening status:
  ownership, occupation, tribute, and route control require separate review

Disposition:
  ReviewOnly for exact opening state
```

Repeated summary claims that Awdaghust was taken in 1054 are useful leads but require direct source verification and correct distinction between settlement possession and wider route control.

## 5.4 Yahya ibn Umar's death and military succession

```text
Claim:
  Yahya ibn Umar dies during the early expansion phase.

Consequence:
  Abu Bakr ibn Umar becomes a major military authority.

Opening relevance:
  Senior authority does not begin with Yusuf alone.

Disposition:
  OpeningCanonicalCandidate after exact chronology check
```

The succession from Yahya ibn Umar to Abu Bakr ibn Umar must remain separate from Abdallah ibn Yasin's religious authority.

## 5.5 Aghmat conquest

```text
Commonly cited date:
  27 June 1058

Authority involved:
  Almoravid movement under Abdallah ibn Yasin and Abu Bakr ibn Umar

Opening relevance:
  Aghmat is a strong candidate for established Almoravid urban base on 1 January 1062.

Disposition:
  OpeningCanonicalCandidate
```

General references report that the Almoravids entered Aghmat in 1058 and used it as an early political and commercial base before Marrakesh. The exact officeholders, garrison, treasury, household location, local elites, and city-government arrangements remain unresolved. [Aghmat chronology reference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aghmat)

## 5.6 Death of Abdallah ibn Yasin

```text
Approximate date:
  1058 or 1059 in common chronologies

Context:
  campaign against the Barghawata

Opening relevance:
  founder-reformer is dead before January 1062
  religious authority must persist through institutions, jurists, doctrine, memory, and legitimacy rather than a living founder

Disposition:
  OpeningCanonicalCandidate with exact-date review
```

Britannica identifies an Almoravid invasion in 1059 and notes that the Barghawata killed Abdallah ibn Yasin in battle, while other summaries use 1058. The contradiction matters for exact chronology but not for the January 1062 fact that Abdallah ibn Yasin is no longer living. [Britannica Barghawata overview](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Barghawatah)

## 5.7 Abu Bakr ibn Umar after Ibn Yasin

```text
Claim:
  Abu Bakr ibn Umar holds major senior military and confederational authority after Ibn Yasin's death.

Opening relevance:
  strongest initial candidate for senior movement authority

Unresolved:
  exact title
  exact jurisdiction
  exact location
  exact relationship to Yusuf

Disposition:
  OpeningCanonicalCandidate with bounded uncertainty
```

## 5.8 Yusuf ibn Tashfin's rise

```text
Approximate period:
  early 1060s

Common later interpretation:
  Yusuf becomes northern ruler and state-builder.

Opening problem:
  whether this process is already complete by 1 January 1062

Disposition:
  ReviewOnly
```

Reference summaries often give Yusuf a reign beginning in 1061, but those summaries may compress delegation, practical authority, later consolidation, and dynastic retrospective into a single accession date. The Source Inventory does not accept sole opening sovereignty from that date alone. [Yusuf overview](https://www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Yusuf_ibn_Tashfin)

## 5.9 Marrakesh foundation

```text
Competing dates:
  1062
  1068/1069 decision phase
  c. 1070 foundation

Competing attributions:
  Abu Bakr ibn Umar
  Yusuf ibn Tashfin
  phased contribution by both

Opening disposition:
  ReviewOnly foundation process
```

Modern references vary significantly. Some use 1062, while others place founding around 1070 and identify Abu Bakr ibn Umar as founder, with Yusuf completing or consolidating the city. A single completed-city fact is therefore unsafe for January 1062. [Marrakesh reference chronology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marrakesh)

## 5.10 Abu Bakr's southern return or departure

```text
Common narrative:
  Abu Bakr returns south to address confederational or Saharan problems and delegates northern authority to Yusuf.

Competing dates:
  early 1060s
  late 1060s
  1071 in some Aghmat narratives

Opening importance:
  controls leader location, active army, authority split, and campaign start

Disposition:
  Quarantined pending specialist chronology
```

This is the single most important opening-date problem. The scenario cannot assume that Abu Bakr has permanently transferred northern power and departed unless the date and authority terms are reconciled.

## 5.11 Fez, Meknes, Tlemcen, and later northern expansion

```text
Period:
  mainly later 1060s and 1070s in broad narratives

Opening use:
  campaign and strategic pressure candidates

Opening control:
  not assumed

  LaterHistoricalOutcome or ReviewOnly by site
```

The Source Inventory rejects a January 1062 map based on the empire's later maximum extent.
