## 1. Concise Verdict

The Almoravid campaign should begin with momentum, legitimacy, military potential, and strategic reach, but without a finished state. Player progression is the conversion of a religious reform movement and military confederation into a durable political order through maintained commitments, bounded leadership, defensible routes, informed choices, finite military capacity, working institutions, and negotiated regional control.

The campaign must not reduce progress to territorial conquest, a universal authority meter, scripted succession, inevitable Marrakesh, or a countdown to Iberian intervention. Expansion is one strategic tool among consolidation, negotiation, recovery, institution building, capital choice, confederational renewal, information gathering, and selective withdrawal.

## 3. Player-Facing Campaign Promise

The player inherits an expanding movement whose proven capacity exceeds its institutional completion. The campaign fantasy is not to begin as the later empire. The campaign fantasy is to decide whether and how that empire-like capacity emerges.

The player must balance:

- senior movement authority against northern operational autonomy;
- reform discipline against confederational plurality;
- military initiative against finite people, equipment, animals, officers, water, pasture, and supply;
- Aghmat continuity against Marrakesh investment;
- Saharan depth against northern consolidation;
- juristic legitimacy against political expediency;
- local negotiation against coercive occupation;
- incomplete information against strategic urgency;
- movement continuity against dynastic consolidation;
- historically powerful opportunities against player-chosen divergence.

A successful campaign may produce a durable confederational movement-state, a northern territorial monarchy, a negotiated dual-authority arrangement, a commercially anchored Saharan power, a capital-centered state, a looser reform coalition, or another coherent outcome supported by actual world state. No one result is mandatory.

## 7. Opening Pressure Portfolio

### 7.1 Authority clarification pressure

Abu Bakr and Yusuf represent distinct authority surfaces. The player needs usable decision rights without converting one leader into a decorative subordinate or scripting a succession conflict.

Potential responses include confirmation of bounded delegation, periodic review, geographic division of responsibility, jointly issued priorities, reserve rights, emergency initiative, or negotiated handback conditions.

### 7.2 Aghmat consolidation pressure

Aghmat is the safest northern base candidate, but settlement importance does not prove universal loyalty, complete administration, secure approaches, adequate stores, or uncontested authority. The player must build working relationships with urban, market, juristic, household, labor, and regional systems.

### 7.3 Confederational maintenance pressure

Lamtuna, Gudala, Massufa, and broader Sanhaja classifications cannot become one loyalty pool. Participation, recognition, service, route access, mediation, compensation, refusal, fatigue, and fracture must remain separately represented.

### 7.4 Saharan and southern pressure

Southern commitments and trans-Saharan routes may sustain legitimacy, trade access, transport knowledge, and military depth. They may also disperse leadership, increase communication delay, consume animals and supplies, and expose northern consolidation.

### 7.5 Tamesna and Barghawata aftermath pressure

The founder's death and prior conflict create strategic pressure, not a revenge script or proof of complete pacification. The player must distinguish reconnaissance, defense, negotiation, specific threat suppression, access, occupation, and long-term settlement.

### 7.6 Marrakesh choice pressure

Marrakesh is a conditional capital-foundation pathway. The strategic issue is whether a new center can be justified and sustained without imagining land rights, water systems, labor, transport, markets, institutions, defenses, or administrative transfer.

### 7.7 Military recovery and readiness pressure

Military potential is not an army. Repeated mobilization can damage households, labor, pasture, transport, trade, equipment, animal stocks, political commitments, and future readiness. Recovery is a strategic action, not an idle delay.

### 7.8 Religious and juristic legitimacy pressure

Reform identity may strengthen discipline and purpose, but juristic consultation, criticism, procedure, enforcement, and community reception remain separate. Expediency may generate short-term capacity while weakening later legitimacy or institutional cooperation.

### 7.9 Information pressure

Leaders operate through observations, reports, messengers, merchants, guides, jurists, communities, commanders, and external contacts. Delayed, disputed, stale, or missing information should shape objective confidence and alternative selection.

## 20. Milestones and Evidence

Milestones are evidence-backed strategic achievements, not arbitrary experience thresholds.

Potential milestone classes:

- authority settlement recognized and functioning;
- confederational commitments renewed with deliverable terms;
- Aghmat administration and access made more dependable;
- a route protected through actual agreements and capacity;
- a military force recovered and made ready for a bounded objective;
- a juristic or educational institution validly established;
- a capital-project phase validly entered;
- a regional settlement reached after military or diplomatic action;
- information reliability improved for a strategic corridor;
- a later Historical Emergence pathway made eligible.

Every milestone requires owner evidence, effective date, provenance, compatibility version, invalidation conditions, and player-facing explanation.

## 21. Setbacks, Failure, and Recovery

Failure must create strategic causality rather than a reload-only punishment.

Possible setbacks include:

- commitment refusal or expiration;
- authority dispute;
- message loss or delay;
- route denial;
- water or pasture failure;
- supply exhaustion;
- force losses and prolonged recovery;
- Aghmat disruption;
- Marrakesh project suspension;
- juristic criticism or institutional resistance;
- local revolt or failed settlement;
- loss of access without loss of all regional influence;
- overextension and home-defense exposure.

A failed objective may transform into defense, negotiation, recovery, route diversion, limited withdrawal, institutional repair, or a revised political settlement. Failure never forces the later historical result.

## 22. Anti-Snowball and Strategic Cost Rules

- Conquest creates occupation, administration, information, legitimacy, property, supply, and defense burdens.
- Repeated mobilization reduces future availability until Population, Recruitment, Movement, Equipment, Horse, Officer, Economy, and Recovery owners validate restoration.
- A new capital competes with existing urban systems and military supply.
- Extended routes increase communication, transport, water, pasture, security, and recovery demands.
- Concentrating authority may improve speed while increasing resistance, exclusion, or succession risk.
- Delegation may improve initiative while creating review, coordination, and handback problems.
- Wealth and trade access remain vulnerable to route disruption and external control.
- Victories create memory and momentum pressure but never free commands or guaranteed loyalty.

## 23. Player Decision Cadence

The player should usually decide major priorities, high-consequence delegation, major commitment requests, military objectives, recovery versus continued operations, Aghmat versus Marrakesh investment, institutional direction, external agreements, and occupation or settlement choices.

Routine route comparisons, ordinary report sorting, low-impact supply matching, minor scouting, and repetitive recovery allocation should be automated by policy where owner systems support it. Automation must expose decisive reasons, uncertainty, and exceptions.
