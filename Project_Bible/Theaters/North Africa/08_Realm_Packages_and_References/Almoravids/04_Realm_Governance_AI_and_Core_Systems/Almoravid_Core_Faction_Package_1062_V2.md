The Almoravids enter the January 1062 campaign as an active religious reform movement, military confederation, and emerging territorial state. The faction has real leadership, movement institutions, military potential, route experience, commercial interfaces, religious and juristic authority, and an important northern urban base. ## 2. Core Campaign Promise

The player inherits a movement with momentum but without a finished state. The campaign asks whether reform, confederational service, northern administration, trans-Saharan access, regional negotiation, urban support, military pressure, and dynastic continuity can be converted into durable rule without destroying the coalitions and communities that made expansion possible.

The intended experience is neither a scripted march to empire nor a generic conquest faction. The realm must continually reconcile:

- movement discipline with plural confederational participation;
- senior authority with delegated northern command;
- military mobility with finite people, animals, officers, equipment, water, pasture, and supply;
- religious legitimacy with juristic procedure and community reception;
- Aghmat continuity with conditional Marrakesh development;
- Saharan and Maghrebi strategic depth with local and external ownership;
- historical momentum with player-driven causality.

## 7. Government and Confederational Constitution

The realm is governed through layered mandates, recognized authority, delegation, consultation, movement participation, service commitments, local institutions, and negotiated compliance. Governance does not convert confederational participants into ordinary subjects or erase community, regional, religious, military, and external owners.

Core governance problems include:

- mandate between senior and delegated leadership;
- recognition and communication across distance;
- contribution requests and responses;
- appointment, office, claim, title, and jurisdiction boundaries;
- enforcement capacity distinct from legal authority;
- consultation and representation;
- breach, renewal, appeal, refusal, withdrawal, and fracture;
- succession without a prebuilt later imperial dynasty.
