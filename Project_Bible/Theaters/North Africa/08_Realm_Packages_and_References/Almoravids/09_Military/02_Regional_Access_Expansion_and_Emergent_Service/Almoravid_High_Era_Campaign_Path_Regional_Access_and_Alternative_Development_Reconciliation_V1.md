## 1. Controlling verdict

The Almoravid High Era is **a set of possible developments of a surviving Almoravid movement-state**, not the historical Almohad roster, a compulsory Iberian conquest, or a linear upgrade of every Early family. An Almoravid Realm can remain competitive in North Africa, gain limited Iberian allies, establish deep cross-Strait integration, lose its Iberian presence, fracture, or coexist with a separately emerging Almohad movement. These states can overlap and reverse.

```text
Faction identity + surviving sources + valid relationships + real capacity
-> possible military and political development

Conquest alone != access
Access != owned population
Era label != equipped unit
Religious appeal != volunteer formation
Historical Almohad victory != required outcome
```

**Design rule:** every available card must be explainable through an actual source, controller relationship, personnel, equipment, command, supply, and service route. Every inaccessible card must show its strongest meaningful blocker. A player who never crosses into Iberia must still have a full, enjoyable Almoravid campaign rather than a deliberately incomplete roster.

## 2. Owner and precedence map

- The Theater authority owns cross-Realm path vocabulary and regional-access causality; this file owns the Almoravid application and its roster-facing decisions.
- The Almoravid movement authority owns confederational participation, commitment, routes, and fracture; a shared Sanhaja label is not one pool or one loyalty value.
- Diplomacy owns contact, reciprocal agreement, invitation, alliance, vassalage, contract, performance, breach, and external locks. A proposal does not grant access.
- Population and regional owners supply actual communities, location, movement, and competing claims. This file does not set population counts or assume compliance.
- Military, roster, and recruitment owners control unit profiles, eligibility, finite pools, officers, horses, weapons, command, readiness, battle behavior, and recovery.
- Religious and juristic owners supply Almoravid and external reception and disagreement, not military command or automatic Ghazi recruitment.
- Geography, maritime, route, supply, economy, and property owners control ports, crossings, transport, water, stock, finance, and the material means of an expedition.
- Historical Memory, Momentum, and Emergence owners evaluate possible movements and successors. This file does not instantiate the Almohads.
- Character, household, governance, and succession owners determine leadership continuity and competing claims; no personality or future decision is predetermined here.

## 3. Starting identity versus future possibility

The opening Almoravids are a Sanhaja-led reform and military movement-state with distinct Lamtuna, Gudala, Massufa, urban, route, and political relationships, not a completed empire or one generic Berber manpower pool. The current opening authority keeps Aghmat as the principal base, treats the Marrakesh foundation as a reversible project rather than a completed capital, and does not preload an Iberian expedition. This file **does not amend those opening bindings**.

Opening priorities may emphasize security of movement sources, water and routes, urban relations, rival North African powers, and potential cross-Strait opportunities. They are *strategic tendencies for later owner review*, not new January 1062 active objectives. An AI or player can redirect effort when defeats, alliances, opportunities, and capacity change.

## 6. Complete Africa-only development path

### 6.1 What the player can develop

Without meaningful Iberian access, the Almoravid Realm can still pursue:

- Lamtuna-associated field spear and mounted-javelin continuity, with actual officers, equipment, horses, and veteran development;
- Massufa-associated screening and route-facing service where owners and communities remain valid;
- Gudala-associated light foot only where its distinct relationship and conditional service pass;
- Aghmat and other valid Maghrebi civic, missile, garrison, craft, and fortified capabilities, each with its own regional source;
- a persistent basic spear levy where local people and obligations survive, with source-specific localization rather than a universal provincial upgrade;
- selected veterans, household service, and bounded campaign mobilization where their separate personnel and institutional gates pass;
- Atlas, pass, Saharan, coastal, and other regional relationships only when actually established;
- siege and logistical capacity through actual specialists, stores, animals, transport, construction, and maintenance rather than a generic engineer-infantry button.

This is a **capability-complete design obligation**, not a claim that every named profile is automatically recruitable or already accepted. Army effectiveness must be tested against credible later opponents using Africa-sourced infantry, missiles, mobility, defense, assault support, command, logistics, and recovery. If a genuine missing battlefield role remains, the later roster comparator pass can propose a specific Africa-sourced family; it must not fill the gap with a copied Almohad unit.

### 6.2 Development levers

Confederational cohesion, protection of routes and water, urban relationships, veteran cadres, equipment and repair, regional defense, household investment, siege practice, and stable diplomacy can deepen this path. The player can instead prioritize mobile campaigning, urban consolidation, fortified defense, trade security, or other supported emphases without a mandatory cultural fork.

### 6.3 Failure and recovery

Loss of a route, local revolt, exhausted animals, broken promises, leader loss, or community displacement may narrow a specific source. Surviving formations persist where valid, but replacements and new recruitment may close. Recovery requires the relevant relationship, people, equipment, and owner approval, not a timer.

## 7. Limited Iberian intervention

The expedition needs a reachable counterpart, maritime and port access, ships or transport, finance, water, supplies, commanders, return planning, and knowledge. A successful crossing does not grant Andalusi recruitment.

## 9. Deep cross-Strait integration

A powerful Almoravid state might maintain durable authority in African and Iberian regions, but success must remain expensive and reversible. The later army can become broader through surviving Andalusi institutions and relationships, not through automatic absorption of every regional source. Sustained cooperation can produce mature access, training, and combined-arms coordination; overextension can simultaneously damage route security, local defense, legitimacy, transport, and recruitment.

An integrated empire can have more **types** of troops without possessing unlimited **numbers**. One rider, horse, workshop, officer, or veteran cannot fill several pools simultaneously. Separate regional obligations, local defense minimums, and recovery compete with expeditionary demands.

## 10. Failed Iberian expansion, Christian dominance, and withdrawal

An Almoravid intervention can lose a battle, fail to hold a port, lose a Taifa partner, or withdraw before annexation. Iberian Christian powers may dominate more territory than in one historical trajectory. These outcomes can reduce or close Andalusi access, change regional community locations, produce refugees or contracts, alter calls for aid, and shift the Almoravid strategic center back toward Africa.

```yaml
failure_does_not_automatically:
  - DeleteAfricanHighEraDevelopment
  - DeleteSurvivingIberianVeterans
  - CreateRefugeeMilitaryPools
  - MakeEveryTaifaHostile
  - ForceAlmohadVictory
  - GrantTheConquerorEveryFormerRegionalUnit
```

A unit serving abroad may remain active while its original source closes to replacement. Any refugee, migrant, negotiated, or converted service must pass actual population, diplomatic, legal, military, and material gates.

## 11. Fragmentation, rival dominance, and Almohad challenge

The Almoravid movement may face disagreement among distinct confederational participants, urban authorities, household or succession interests, regional commanders, and religious or juristic audiences. These tensions are owner-backed and not a single universal loyalty meter. A Hammadid or Zirid power may dominate parts of North Africa instead; neither result automatically destroys the Almoravid identity or transfers its entire roster.

The Almohad movement is a separate conditional historical-emergence consumer. Its potential Masmuda and High Atlas support, leadership, reform claims, organization, and military challenge must be independently established. It can fail, remain local, coexist, negotiate, defeat, or be defeated by the Almoravids or another power. It does not replace the Almoravids by date and does not automatically inherit their units. Likewise, surviving Almoravids do not automatically recruit Almohad-specific formations.

A collapse may leave exiles, veteran formations, regional institutions, local loyalties, or negotiated service under another controller. Each transition needs a validated owner and may close new recruitment while preserving existing people and lineages.

## 13. High Era I and II under changing world states

The public eras remain Early and High. High Era I and II are internal development guides, not fixed calendar centuries or automatic tier promotions. High Era I can emphasize first urban, regional, household, frontier, and fortified capability; High Era II can support more mature professional, campaign, siege, contract, and regional expressions if people and institutions actually develop.

One campaign can reach mature High Era military practice without controlling Iberia. Another may have Andalusi troops but weak African confederational cohesion. Another may remain regionally mixed and politically fragile. Era labels explain development rather than prescribe one unit list.

## 14. Religious and campaign-call integration

The shared Islamic framework and Almoravid religious authority may provide audience-specific reception, juristic consultation, leadership credibility, community communication, disagreement, and campaign-purpose context. Military owners still require actual contributors, officers, weapons, shields, provisions, service terms, return and recovery.

The Almoravid Ghazi Infantry profile remains an organized, mission-bound **Assault and Seize** expression. Its name is not a universal Islamic unit. Mutatawwi'a remains a comparative mobilization hypothesis and is not admitted as a second Almoravid card. A mosque, religious center, conquest, conversion, or declaration cannot summon a formation or grant flat faction-wide religious combat strength.

## 15. AI priorities and causal choice

The AI should evaluate reachable objectives through known information, expected military benefit, costs, diplomatic opportunities, source preservation, cross-Strait transport, rival pressure, and potential recovery. It can prefer Africa-first security, limited intervention, deep Iberian commitment, negotiation, or withdrawal as conditions change. These are proposals and preferences, not a scripted choice of historical outcome.

An AI tactical defeat can cause casualties, lost horses, damaged reputation, changed allied expectations, and altered route control. Another AI can exploit that opening if it knows of it and has the capacity. The same logic applies when the player fights or watches an AI-only battle. No AI-only free pools, guaranteed conquests, perfect knowledge, or immunity from recruitment closure.

## 16. Player-facing historical briefing

Each strategic review should explain, where known:

- what the Almoravid core currently controls or can credibly influence;
- which regional communities, institutions, and service routes are available;
- which allied or external formations can serve without being locally recruitable;
- why an Andalusi or African family is open, constrained, suspended, or closed;
- whether the strongest blocker is politics, people, command, horses, equipment, transport, or supply;
- how a battle, siege, betrayal, relief, marriage or succession outcome, or policy changed the prospects;
- whether an Almohad current is only rumored, organized, challenging, or not known to this observer;
- what may recover access without pretending to know hidden future outcomes.

The player sees decisions and consequences, not a compulsory administrative checklist.

## 17. Acceptance fixtures

### 17.1 No Iberian access, strong African campaign

The Almoravids develop a capable later army through valid African sources and institutions while every Andalusi-specific source remains unavailable. AI and player both have meaningful choices. **Fail** if High Era becomes incomplete solely because Iberia was not taken.

### 17.2 Intervention without annexation

A Taifa requests aid and contributes a bounded contingent. The Almoravids cross with valid transport and supply, but do not gain permanent local recruitment. **Fail** if the agreement is treated as city ownership or a universal Iberian pool.

### 17.3 Partnership deepens unevenly

One Andalusi community supports crossbow service, another withholds cavalry, and a frontier network agrees only to local defense. **Fail** if a single relationship unlocks all three.

### 17.4 Iberian defeat and African recovery

A failed expedition costs veterans, ships, and confidence; the Almoravid African core survives and can recover through actual sources. **Fail** if the defeat deletes the faction's High Era future or magically restores losses.

### 17.5 Christian-dominant Iberia

A Christian Realm gains major territory. Andalusi populations and institutions follow their controlling owner-specific outcomes, not universal disappearance or automatic transfer. **Fail** if the Almoravids receive a refugee pool merely because a city fell.

### 17.6 Taifa or rival North African dominance

A Taifa gains African service through valid relationships, or a Hammadid or Zirid Realm becomes dominant. Almoravid-associated sources remain separately owned and may negotiate, resist, or persist. **Fail** if all sources convert with a map-color change.

### 17.7 Almohad divergence

The movement never forms, remains local, or emerges against a different dominant power. A separate fixture permits a powerful Almohad state without compulsory historical borders. **Fail** if date or Almoravid weakness alone creates an Almohad army.

### 17.8 Distant-player observation

A player elsewhere, such as Norway, can encounter distinct North African and Iberian worlds across runs. Trace each difference to decisions, battles, relationships, population, institutions, information, and any owner-authorized seeded uncertainty. **Fail** if a hidden arbitrary preset determines the winner.
