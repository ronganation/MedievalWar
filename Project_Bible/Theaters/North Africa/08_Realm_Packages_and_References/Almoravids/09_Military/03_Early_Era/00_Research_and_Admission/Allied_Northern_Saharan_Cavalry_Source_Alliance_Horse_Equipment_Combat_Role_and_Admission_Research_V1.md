## 0.1 Research Verdict

A distinct allied northern Saharan cavalry family is conditionally admissible for master-profile planning as a stronger conventional cavalry contingent whose availability depends on an actual external relationship. The family survives because it can contribute greater charge, melee, and pursuit weight than Lamtuna Cavalry while retaining external ownership, separate commanders, negotiated service, and withdrawal rights.

The strongest comparative source located states that the Almoravids won allies among richer northern Saharan cavalry tribes and describes the allied horsemen's role as breaking and pursuing a weakened enemy. That source is explicitly framed as 1085 to 1250 and cannot establish an opening January 1062 ally, named tribe, force, pool, equipment set, or deployed contingent. The current internal diplomacy and roster authorities provide the correct design owners for a relationship-dependent unit, but the retrieved snippets do not specify a current alliance or contingent.

The candidate is therefore admitted as a development family, not an opening formation. No invented allied polity, tribe, leader, treaty, or horse pool is created. When a qualifying relationship exists, the unit should appear as a direct battlefield card controlled under agreed command terms while remaining owned by the contributor.
