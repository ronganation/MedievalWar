## 2. One-Screen Design Card

### Battlefield

- Forms the ordinary defensive spear frontage around which the Early Era Almoravid army can operate.
- Protects archers, javelinmen, baggage, commanders, camps, water access, and mounted-force maneuver space.
- Deters mounted attack only when properly faced, cohesive, sufficiently deep, supported, and not exhausted.
- Performs more reliably than improvised local emergency muster because it has a defined source, accepted service relationship, basic formation practice, and internal leadership.
- Remains less consistent and less resilient than selected movement foot or later professional formations.
- Works through combined arms rather than by winning every frontal melee.

### Campaign

- Draws from eligible Lamtuna-associated communities and contribution relationships rather than a national manpower reserve.
- Competes directly with mounted service, guides, escorts, caravan duties, camp security, pastoral work, household survival, transport, and future recruitment from the same source.
- Mobilization consumes people, leaders, spears, shields, sidearms, protection, food, water, pack capacity, routes, assembly time, and political cooperation.
- Excessive service can weaken herd protection, caravan traffic, source-community stability, mobility, local security, later recruitment, and confidence in leadership.
- Demobilization returns survivors and surviving property through owner-valid reconciliation. It does not erase casualties, fatigue, equipment loss, or political burden.

### Access

- Requires a specific eligible source, current participation state, valid authority or accepted contribution, available unreserved people, compatible equipment, officers, supply, route access, and return terms.
- Local defensive eligibility does not prove distant campaigning, siege assault, occupation, Iberian service, or permanent retention.
- A building, research decision, population total, faction label, or movement membership cannot create the unit.
- Exact January 1062 access remains owned by Opening State and supporting population, community, obligation, territory, equipment, economy, law, and military authorities.

### Development

- Persists because source identity, opportunity cost, equipment burden, recovery, and battlefield function remain useful even when stronger infantry develops.
- May improve through repeated service, veteran instruction, clearer records, more consistent shields and helmets, stronger officers, equipment stores, improved assembly, and combined-arms experience.
- Later professional, urban, Andalusi, or Almohad-linked families do not automatically rename, upgrade, delete, or refill this unit.
- Existing veterans may survive after new recruitment closes.

## 3. Player-Facing Description

Lamtuna Spearmen are confederational field infantry drawn from eligible Lamtuna-associated sources within the Almoravid movement. Equipped principally with spears and shields, they provide the ordinary defensive frontage around which scouts, skirmishers, cavalry, selected foot, baggage, and commanders can operate.

They are neither an improvised peasant mob nor a permanent professional corps. Every formation depends on a real contribution, available people, serviceable equipment, internal leaders, assembly, water, supply, routes, and a valid purpose. Recruiting too deeply from the same source can weaken mounted service, guides, caravan work, pastoral security, household stability, or the next muster.

On the battlefield, Lamtuna Spearmen are strongest in supported lines, around camps and crossings, while protecting missiles, or while denying cavalry easy access to vulnerable troops. Disorder, flanking, missiles, fatigue, poor terrain, broken command, and loss of water or retreat routes can turn a credible spear line into a vulnerable formation.
