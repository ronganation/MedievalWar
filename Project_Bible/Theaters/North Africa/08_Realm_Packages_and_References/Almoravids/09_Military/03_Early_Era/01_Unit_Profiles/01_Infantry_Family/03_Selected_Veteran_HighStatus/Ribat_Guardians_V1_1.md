## 1. Player-facing unit identity

- **Working unit:** Ribat Guardians. The name is a player-readable working label, not an attested historical corps or proof of a specific fortified site.
- **Class:** scarce, trained/selected, shield-bearing **committed objective-defense infantry**. The intended second Early Era high-value foot choice beside Selected Almoravid Foot.
- **Battle promise:** advance to a defined critical position, establish a defensive hold, protect its immediate approaches, and endure a bounded attack long enough for supporting troops to act. **Commit and hold**, rather than chase every crisis.
- **Source promise:** owner-valid confederational and movement-associated contributors with demonstrated defensive service, potentially including locally grounded frontier or route defenders where their own owner, duty and field-release conditions pass. This is a proposed source architecture, **not** a documented 1062 ribat garrison.
- **Principal equipment concept:** shield and close-combat weapon, **sword-and-shield as the desired player silhouette only where actual supply and evidence pass**. No free swords, universal mail, special religious armor, or exclusive ethnic roster.
- **Best use:** defend a camp approach, crossing, fortified position, constrained passage, rally point, or strategically selected ground with spear, missile and mounted support.
- **Weaknesses:** expensive to concentrate, finite personnel and equipment, poorer than a mobile reserve at responding to several distant emergencies, vulnerable to isolation, missile pressure, bypass, water/supply failure and attrition in prolonged unsupported combat.
- **Campaign promise:** recruit locally from a verified source, wait for assembly and training, then move to the army; a building, site label, or doctrine never spawns troops.
- **Opening status:** definition candidate only; no January 1062 unit, recruitment pool or site is created.

### 1.1 Short player-card copy

**Ribat Guardians** are a scarce Almoravid defensive infantry candidate assembled from proven, owner-released fighters for the positions an army cannot afford to lose. With shields and suitable close-combat weapons, they hold a chosen approach, crossing or camp line while ordinary spears and missile troops work around them. Their strength lies in committing to one objective and sustaining its defense; unlike Selected Almoravid Foot, they are not a fresh reserve for every emerging crisis. Their working name describes the gameplay idea, not an established historical regiment.

**Strengths:** chosen-ground defense; shielded close contact; controlled local counter-move; protection of a defined objective; meaningful partnership with spears and missiles. **Weaknesses:** scarcity; slow assembly and replacement; opportunity cost to contributing communities; limited reaction to distant threats; prolonged defense consumes supplies and cohesion. **Avoid:** unsupported charges, open-ground pursuit, simultaneous defense of several sites, or assuming a religious label guarantees morale.

### 1.2 One-screen designer card and player decision

> **RIBAT GUARDIANS** | Selected defensive sword-and-shield infantry, **if equipped and admitted**  
> **Role:** Commit to a critical position; absorb a local assault and preserve the objective.  
> **Recruitment:** scarce, source-owned defensive-service contributors; a separate formation is not yet admitted.  
> **Best use:** shielded defense of a camp entrance, narrow road, ford bank, rally corridor or genuinely fortified approach with spear frontage and missiles covering the flanks.  
> **Avoid:** treating them as an army-wide reserve, a cavalry stopper in the open, a mobile frontier watch, or an expendable breach-assault troop.  
> **Decisive weakness:** once committed, they cannot protect another threatened point without disengaging, moving and reforming; if the position is bypassed or its flanks collapse, the investment may be stranded.  
> **Opportunity cost:** qualified defenders, officers, shields and weapons leave their source duties; the same veterans cannot simultaneously be Selected Almoravid Foot.  
> **Player command:** *Choose the ground, protect the flanks, hold until relief or withdrawal is worth the cost.*  
> **Evidence status:** working gameplay composite, not an attested corps or approved 1062 recruit.

**Readable short description:** Selected Almoravid shield-bearers assigned to defend a position the army cannot easily surrender. They hold a narrow approach under pressure and make short, controlled counter-moves against attackers already checked by the line. Unlike the mobile Selected Almoravid Foot reserve, their value depends on committing early to the right ground and keeping relief, water and flanks secure.

**Compact card tags (proposals, not implemented traits):** `Objective Defender` | `Shielded Close Infantry` | `Scarce` | `Limited Pursuit` | `Requires Flank Support`. A tooltip must explain *why* the current position is useful; no invisible objective-defense bonus is implied. In a battle without a defensible critical position, this card still fights as real shielded infantry but its strategic specialization has little value.

### 1.3 Identity that can be seen and felt

The unit's fantasy is **a deliberate commitment that visibly costs flexibility**, not a religious super-guard. At long camera distance the compact, shield-forward frontage, a recognizable command group and a held-ground icon should read as a defensive concentration. At medium distance show mixed but maintained shields, working close weapons and disciplined spacing rather than identical expensive armor. In contact, files absorb pressure, close gaps, rotate a tired section if room and officers allow, and counter-move only far enough to restore the immediate approach. Under fatigue, damaged shields, leader loss or flank threat, that neat frontage degrades visibly. The card's identity survives a name change; it does not depend on a mosque, a stone fort, a specific banner or a fixed color.

The contrast at selection is simple: **Selected Foot is held back until the crisis is known; Guardians are placed before the crisis to make one position difficult to take.** Neither is a straight upgrade of the other. If both are sent to the same frontline without differentiated commands, source terms or equipment, the separate card has failed its own test.

### 1.5 Player-facing strengths, weaknesses and matchups

- **Strong:** a bounded, reachable position with a narrow approach, coherent shield frontage, covered flanks and a relief plan; resisting ordinary infantry already compressed or disrupted by spears and missiles; local recapture of the *same* objective after a shallow penetration.
- **Conditional:** holding against better-protected foot for a limited period if terrain, support, equipment and fatigue favor the defense; resisting mounted pressure only with a spear screen and constrained approach; defending an actual gate or breach only if the map contains one and the unit is assigned there.
- **Weak:** exposed advance through sustained archery, open flanks against cavalry, deep pursuit, multiple separated objectives, attacking fresh spears, an unsupported bridge assault, prolonged contact after the line loses officers or water.
- **Enemy counterplay:** bypass the defended point; fix the front and attack a flank; draw the unit out with a false threat; concentrate missiles; cut relief or water; force a second crisis elsewhere. These are contingent tactical possibilities, not automatic debuffs or guarantees.

### 1.6 Presentation boundary

The card's attractive sword-and-shield promise is a **conditional principal configuration**, not a statement that swords are common or uniformly issued. A validated shield plus another suitable close weapon may be a source-specific expression only if the battle owner still sees *committed close defense*, not a spear frontier clone or axe exploitation clone. If the sword-and-shield proposition is essential to a distinct player decision but cannot be supplied, the card remains held; do not quietly ship an unrelated weapon profile under the same title.

## 3. Recruitment and source ownership

### 3.1 Source architecture

This is a **single candidate family with multiple possible owner-valid source expressions**, not a newly allocated source ID or a claim that all expressions coexist. A confederational contribution must identify the actual contributing owner, eligible people, service obligations or negotiated permission, and continuing community duties. A route, camp or frontier service source requires an actual duty and owner, not just a map label. A site source requires a real site and release terms; a settlement can be a recruitment interface without owning all personnel in its catchment. Religious adherence or the word *ribat* does not qualify anyone automatically.

Possible contributors include proven shield-bearing foot with sustained defense experience, trained defenders released from a valid community or movement source, and leaders competent in watch, controlled hold, rotation and withdrawal. These are **eligibility categories**, not claims that such a named corps exists. A source may support both Selected Foot and Ribat Guardians only with distinct unreserved contributors or an explicit choice to reassign the same contributors, never with duplicate bodies. Personnel from the Amir's household are not a default source; an owner may explicitly release a contributor only if the household's protection obligation remains valid.

### 3.2 Conservation and strongest blocker

Eligibility chain: population and community presence → qualified service category → owner permission and obligation → source capacity → currently unreserved personnel → compatible leaders and equipment → assembly and training → mission-ready formation → actual travel and arrival. Every transition can fail. Protect local defense, pastoral labor, routes, caravans, messengers, existing formations, recovery cadres and household obligations. Reserve people, leaders, swords/other weapons, shields, protection, food, water, transport and mission duration together; failed commitment releases only the candidate reservations. Show the strongest blocker to player and AI. A sword shortage must either block the sword-and-shield expression or trigger an **owner-approved** alternative equipment expression that still meets the distinct battle-role test. It cannot silently grant swords or produce a free second card.

### 3.3 Settlement choice and production timing

Where both options genuinely qualify, the settlement interface may show **Selected Almoravid Foot: keep elite foot fresh for uncertain crisis** and **Ribat Guardians: commit elite foot to defend the critical objective**. They need not share a fixed exclusive faction-wide slot, but competing personnel, officers, equipment, local assembly space and queue capacity can make one practical choice at a given moment. The player should see the local queue, real inputs, estimated readiness/arrival only after a future balance owner defines them, source depletion, and whether the army can wait. Recruitment at one settlement does not teleport the formation to a departing army. No invented fixed queue time, pool count, settlement building or opening site is specified here.

## 4. Battlefield design: a second elite choice that earns its slot

### 4.1 Core battle cycle

1. **Select a defensible objective:** a camp gate, crossing, constrained approach, defended line segment, rally corridor or similarly meaningful position. A map marker alone does not make terrain defensible.
2. **Commit and establish:** move under orders; arrive with the actual fatigue, cohesion and equipment state; take a compact shield-bearing posture supported by ordinary spear frontage and missiles where available.
3. **Hold and absorb:** protect the immediate position, maintain order and shield coverage, rotate local frontage if actual leadership and space permit. Morale follows command, service and current conditions, not an automatic faith bonus.
4. **Local counter-move:** strike a disordered attacker threatening the objective, then reform. This is not a free shock assault or open pursuit.
5. **Withdraw or fail:** if the position is bypassed, supplies fail, cohesion breaks or withdrawal is ordered, preserve survivors and return consequences to their source. A held objective does not erase casualties.

**Formation access, subject to battle owner:** compact objective defense (core); shield-forward hold (conditional on equipment, training and cohesion); supported gate/crossing defense (core when geometry permits); short local counter-move (conditional); orderly relief/withdrawal (conditional); open-ground chase (poor); universal shield wall, phalanx, heavy assault or invulnerable last stand (not granted). A spear may be used by an owner-approved equipment expression, but if the principal battle cycle becomes *watch, delay and withdraw*, it collides with the frontier spear family and requires reclassification rather than a second card.

### 4.2 Direct collision matrix

- **Selected Almoravid Foot:** flexible high-reliability crisis reserve, command/archer protection and controlled counterattack. Ribat Guardians trade that flexibility for sustained defense of a preselected position. A selected veteran may change assignment under valid owner release but cannot serve both at once.
- **Lamtuna Spearmen:** ordinary trained confederational spear frontage and mounted-threat deterrence. Ribat Guardians are scarcer shielded close-contact objective holders, not a replacement for a long spear line or guaranteed anti-cavalry wall.
- **Aghmat Militia and urban garrison:** locally owned civic defense or maintained urban defense; Ribat Guardians must have their own owner-valid movement/defense service rather than rebranding the same Aghmat people as a second elite card.
- **Aghmat Axemen:** aggressive axe-and-shield close support, counter-skirmisher melee and exploitation. Ribat Guardians hold a position and resist pressure; do not inherit axe-specific offensive role.
- **Lamtuna/Andalusi frontier infantry:** frontier spear system emphasizes watch, prepared delay, route/crossing defense and fighting withdrawal, with separate source and chronology. This candidate emphasizes *commit and hold* under close attack; a frontier aesthetic or name alone cannot duplicate frontier cards.
- **Amir Protection Detail and Amir's Horse Guard:** protect a person or mounted household command. Ribat Guardians protect a position, not the Amir as a default personal bodyguard; no silent household transfer.
- **Later Dismounted Almoravid Retainers / Ghazi Infantry:** later sword-and-shield durable close-and-hold service and motivated assault respectively require distinct High Era sources and development. This Early Era candidate must not become the later retainer institution by renaming or erase those later player choices.

### 4.3 Counterplay and environment

Strongest when defending a prepared, supplied, reachable objective with intact leadership, supported flanks and a withdrawal route. Vulnerable to enemy missiles, flanking, bypass, multiple separated objectives, prolonged siege-like attrition, supply/water interruption, disordered arrival, loss of officers and unsupported mounted contact. A shield is not immunity; a chosen position is not an automatic defensive modifier. Siege defense is an assignment, not proof of an engine crew, fortification ownership or an opening garrison.

### 4.4 Formation portfolio and commitment states (candidate behavior, not new abilities)

| State | Player-facing behavior | Prerequisite and failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Approach | Move toward the selected objective under an ordinary movement order; avoid an exposed unsupported march. | Real route, command and arrival condition; missiles or disorder can spoil the approach. |
| Establish | Orient a shielded frontage and leave room for rear files and relief. | Suitable ground, shields, leaders and cohesion; no free fortification or automatic shield wall. |
| Hold | Keep the immediate approach closed while friendly spears and missiles protect its wider edges. | Contact, flank security, fatigue, command and water remain consequential. |
| Local countermove | Step into a shallow break or punish a checked attacker, then reform on the objective. | Clear lane and intact command; no long charge, deep pursuit or automatic armor penetration. |
| Relieve or rotate | Exchange tired files or hand the position to another real formation when space and orders allow. | Actual replacement, passage and communication; no duplicated unit or instant fatigue reset. |
| Disengage | Leave the position under orders, potentially conceding the objective. | Open exit and support; enemy fixation, loss of command or blocked route may make withdrawal fail. |

These are **shared-system behavior intentions**, not six bespoke buttons. The tactical distinction is strongest when the player has to decide whether to hold, spend a reserve on relief, or abandon the objective. Holding to the last person is not the default AI or morale rule.

### 4.5 Equipment, training and command as battle causes

**Equipment envelope:** a shield suitable for coherent close defense and an actually issued one-handed close weapon are required for the principal design. Sword and shield are the preferred card read *only if the source can furnish them*; variable helmets, padded protection, limited mail, sidearms, footwear and campaign kit follow actual source and period evidence. No fixed sword pattern, shield geometry, armor percentage or numerical defense bonus is specified. A damaged or missing shield changes the front rank's credible defense; a weapon change can change the role enough to trigger re-review. Spears remain the ordinary family's principal anti-mounted frontage, not this card's hidden secondary ability.

**Training curriculum candidate:** shield facing and spacing; holding a short frontage without overextending; recognition of the protected objective and its flanks; passing orders through file leaders; controlled relief and short counter-move; opening a lane for friendly missiles or reinforcements; casualty extraction without dissolving the line; ordered withdrawal and rally. A veteran from another family does not automatically know these tasks. Experienced personnel can improve judgment and cohesion through actual service, but do not generate shields, officers, extra morale or a second pool.

**Command group:** a formation leader, available file leaders and a real order-relay method must keep the held position legible. A standard or visual marker is optional and requires historical-art approval; it is not an independent morale source. A leader casualty should affect response, local reformation and relief reliability through shared command rules, never cause a guaranteed rout. The source's community or movement leadership remains distinct from the army's operational commander; field assignment must not erase contribution terms.

### 4.6 Readiness, frontage and bounded endurance

**Ready:** valid mission and actual formation with coherent equipment, trained files, command, supplies and a reachable objective. **Understrength:** surviving skilled files may hold a shorter frontage but cannot cover the original width or rotate as freely. **Disordered:** shield spacing, command and relief degrade even if headcount remains. **Isolated:** support, water, exit or relief is threatened; prolonged hold becomes increasingly risky. **Broken/withdrawn:** people and equipment still require accounting; losing the objective does not erase surviving lineage. These are qualitative authoring bands, not approved stat thresholds.

A full headcount is not full readiness. Freshness, shield condition, officer depth, formation memory, current fatigue and water determine how long a defense remains credible. Do not convert this into a flat “guardian” aura or permanent bonus when standing on any objective marker. A shorter understrength unit may be excellent in a doorway but unable to protect a long camp perimeter; the map, frontage and support decide.

### 4.7 Terrain, siege and combined-arms play

**Good when real geography supports it:** narrow camp approach, short road cut, defensible bank, actual gate passage, breach interior, covered rally corridor or compact defensive line with protected sides. **Mixed:** urban streets and courts can shorten frontage but create bypasses; a hill helps only when facing, footing, access and supply permit; an oasis is a contested water objective, not a combat bonus. **Poor:** broad open plain under cavalry threat, several parallel approaches, exposed bridge or ford assault, deep marsh, unstable slope, beach landing under missiles, or a position without relief or retreat. Weather and season change footing, visibility, access, water and sustainment through their owners, not by a fixed “desert-trained” modifier.

In **siege defense**, the candidate may protect an existing gate, breach interior, stores or rally position. In **siege offense**, it may consolidate an already secured entry or guard the assault force's return corridor, but is not an engineer, miner, ladder-first-wave specialist or guaranteed wall fighter. In **camp defense**, it protects one critical approach rather than becoming a universal camp-and-route guard. **Combined arms:** Lamtuna spears receive mounted pressure and extend the line; archers or slingers shape an enemy approach; mobile troops scout and threaten flanks; Selected Foot stays available to answer a new crisis; Guardians make the designated point costly to force. These supports must be present, not silently granted by the card.

### 4.8 Three battle vignettes for comparison testing

1. **Camp approach, favorable:** the player commits Guardians behind a short entrance with Lamtuna spears screening the open flank. An enemy infantry assault presses the entrance; the Guardians hold, make a brief local countermove, then need relief. Selected Foot remains available for a second crisis. If the spears are removed, mounted flanking becomes dangerous. This is a proposed test, not a historical event or implemented outcome.
2. **Crossing, contested:** Guardians defend a reachable bank after friendly missiles slow the advance. Enemy missiles and a second crossing threaten to bypass them. The player must spend a reserve, shift with loss of position, or withdraw. A blanket “defend crossing” bonus would fail this test.
3. **Wrong ground, failure:** the same unit is sent into an open field against fresh cavalry and long-range missiles. Its shielded contact quality does not compensate for the lack of spear frontage and flank security. If it wins reliably because of a hidden elite or religious modifier, the profile has been misimplemented.

## 1. Decision in brief

**Conditionally retain Ribat Guardians for a distinct Early Era elite-infantry admission test, but hold approval of the unit card and opening availability.** The desired player choice is position-defense sword-and-shield infantry that is deliberately committed to a critical objective, beside rather than instead of Selected Almoravid Foot, whose principal decision is to keep a flexible veteran reserve fresh for a crisis. The design distinction is clear; the independent source and historical name are not yet proven. Therefore the current result is **role passes, source and name remain held, master profile not authorized**.

The candidate is neither a generic lower-tier garrison nor a permanent personal Amir Guard. A ribat association is a research question, not proof of a standing corps, a fortified monastery on the 1062 map, a building that generates soldiers, or universal religiously conferred combat quality. If a distinct service source cannot be demonstrated, retain the defensive assignment capability without a new card. [Internal: Selected_Almoravid_Foot_V1_2.md; Amir_Guard_Research_V1_1.md; Almoravid_Religious_and_Juristic_Authority_V1.md. External: Humphrey J. Fisher, "What's in a Name? ## 2. Evidence and naming firewall

Fisher identifies competing interpretations of the Almoravid name and explicitly presents the ribat-as-network interpretation as a hypothesis. The realm religious authority likewise forbids turning *al-Murabitun* into a particular building or universal military order without institution, place, founding, residents, ownership, and functional evidence. Consequently **Ribat Guardians** is a short working gameplay label, not an attested title of a January 1062 regiment. Do not use an asserted endonym, named ribat, uniform religious order, or fixed recruitment site without source-specific review.

Evidence still needed: (a) a dated or chronologically defensible source connecting a specific Almoravid community or institution to continuing armed position-defense service; (b) source owner and valid contribution terms; (c) whether swords and shields were sufficiently available and central to justify the proposed silhouette; (d) whether the role was field-deployable rather than exclusively site-bound; and (e) whether the name implies an institution not actually evidenced. Separate the historical claim from the gameplay composite. [Internal: Almoravid_Source_Inventory_V1.md; Almoravid_Religious_and_Juristic_Authority_V1.md. External: Fisher, JSTOR 1581237.]

## 3. Candidate source models, not invented facts

1. **Movement-associated committed defenders:** potentially qualified foot from named confederational contributors under explicit release and continuing defense service. This is the most plausible *design route*, not a proven exclusive ribat source. It must not absorb every Lamtuna, Gudala, or Massufa contributor by faction label.
2. **Specific site-associated defenders:** viable only if a particular site and its military service, owner, population, and release to field deployment are independently established. Aghmat's principal-base status alone does not establish a ribat there, a garrison, or a transferable guard pool.
3. **Cross-source selected personnel:** possible only with strict reservation and a genuinely different continuing defense institution. If this is merely the same veterans reassigned from Selected Almoravid Foot, use an assignment or mutually exclusive employment state rather than duplicate recruitable personnel.
4. **Amir household personnel:** reject as the default source. Personal and command protection belong to the Amir Protection Detail assignment or the separate mounted household guard; neither proves a ribat infantry institution.

Every candidate needs a named owner, eligible people, service and political permission, protected local obligations, officers, training, swords, shields, other protection, food, water, transport, duration, return, and recovery. A settlement may display two recruitment choices without creating two independent people pools. [Internal: Almoravid_Recruitment_Matrix_V1_2.md; Amir_Guard_Research_V1_1.md; Selected_Almoravid_Foot_V1_2.md.]

## 4. Battlefield survival test

| Family | Principal decision | Battlefield identity | Opportunity cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Selected Almoravid Foot | Preserve or commit the flexible veteran reserve | Stabilize threatened frontage; protect archers and command; controlled counterattack | Depletes selected veterans, leaders, equipment, and source readiness |
| Ribat Guardians, proposed | Commit scarce elite foot to defend a critical position | Sword-and-shield defense of a defined objective, holding under pressure and protecting its immediate approaches | Ties scarce qualified personnel and equipment to that objective and denies their use elsewhere |
| Aghmat Militia or later garrison | Provide local civic or maintained settlement defense | Local holding, gates, streets, relief, and site security | Draws on civic or maintained urban service, not automatically on a movement-wide elite source |
| Amir Protection Detail | Protect a person and command function | Mission-bound attachment, foot/mounted/mixed as sourced | Competes for contributors but is not a recruitable infantry card |

**Positive battlefield gate:** position-defense must change a real player decision: where the unit is deployed, what objective is worth committing it to, what happens when the objective is lost or orders change, and why the selected reserve cannot do that job equally well. The proposed sword-and-shield configuration must have defensible equipment access; a sword label alone cannot establish an elite institution. The unit must be vulnerable to isolation, exhausted defense, lost supply, and attacks outside its assigned position, rather than gaining a universal morale or armor aura.

**Failure gate:** if the same people, equipment, command, and battlefield cycle can be represented as a defensive assignment of Selected Foot, a site guard, or a developed garrison, reject the separate card while retaining the useful position-defense behavior. No symmetry-only second elite slot. [Internal: Selected_Almoravid_Foot_V1_2.md; Early_Infantry_Progression_V1_2.md; Amir_Guard_Research_V1_1.md.]

## 5. Two-elite settlement recruitment experience

The player should be able to see **both eligible choices**, where both actually pass their sources and availability gates: Selected Foot as a flexible reserve and Ribat Guardians as committed objective defenders. If only one can be raised before an army departs, that must follow from actual queue and arrival constraints, not an arbitrary faction-wide either/or rule.

Recruitment, readiness, and arrival are distinct. A valid order does not teleport the formation to the field army. Location of the people, assembly, training, route access, travel, water, service duration, and return must be accounted for. A decision or building may coordinate capacity but cannot create qualified people or equipment. The AI faces the same eligibility and delay. No numeric queue time or pool size is set here.
