**Verdict: conditional yes to a separate *design* card; no to a full unit profile yet.** The proposed High Era II pair is useful only if the player gets two different tools: **Maghrebi Foot Guards anchor and deny a field approach with organized spear-and-shield depth; Dismounted Almoravid Retainers close and hold through sustained sword-and-shield melee in confined ground.** A new heavy spear unit cannot be admitted merely because it has heavier equipment, a guard name, or a later-era date. It must have a distinct source institution, actual finite people and kit, a different battle cycle from mature Lamtuna Spearmen, and a credible opportunity cost when its protected duties are released. The admission design is strong enough to research as a genuine card, but neither a historical named corps nor an exact source owner has been verified. Keep the full profile gated on those source and collision questions, as item 4 in the user's sequence explicitly requests.

## 1. Sources and evidentiary limits

**Current Almoravid comparators:** `Dismounted_Almoravid_Retainers_V1_2_Benchmark_Parity_FULL.md` establishes the High Era II sword-and-shield retainer's owner-bound sustained melee, defensive counterstroke and strongpoint retention; `Early_Infantry_Progression_V1_2.md` keeps Lamtuna Spearmen as persistent field spear frontage, Selected Foot as crisis reserve, Aghmat Garrison Infantry as city-specific maintained defense, and Ghazi Infantry as objective-bound assault. `Almoravid_High_Era_Roster_Status_and_Gap_Audit_V1.md` warns against another generic spear card after the retainer gap and prefers maturation of existing families where roles do not change. `Almoravid_Recruitment_Matrix_V1_2.md` is the current finite-pool reference for the Early roster, not an admission of this later card. Earlier whole-roster progression and later High Era infantry work have different dates and counts; use the newer infantry progression for role boundaries, without treating either count as a freshly reconciled High Era matrix.

**Historical basis and boundary:** Camilo Gómez-Rivas, *The Almoravid Maghrib* (Arc Humanities Press, 2023), discusses the Almoravid movement's transformation and growing urban/commercial contacts; that contextualizes later Maghrebi institutional development but does **not** document a named Maghrebi Foot Guards corps or its precise equipment. Humphrey J. Fisher, “What's in a Name? The Almoravids of the Eleventh Century in the Western Sahara,” *Journal of Religion in Africa* 22.4 (1992), warns about contested interpretation of Almoravid/ribat terminology; no religious label should be turned into a uniform guard regiment. The specific heavy-spear field guard below is a **historically plausible gameplay hypothesis**, not a claim of attested historical organization. The working English name needs history, language, and visual review. No later imperial state, Almohad institution, Iberian kit, or universal Maghrebi ethnicity is projected backward into January 1062.

## 2. What a separate card would actually do

**Player promise:** in a developed army, spend scarce maintained infantry capacity on a field guard that can take a *limited, deliberate frontage* and deny a cavalry approach or protect the space into which a retainer counterstroke can return. This is neither a cheap full-line spear wall nor an unbreakable defensive elite. It gives the player a tactical choice: invest in a reliable anchor at a critical crossing, reserve wing, siege-camp exit, or protected approach, while ordinary Lamtuna Spearmen cover the wider line. Retainers can then enter close fighting when the opponent is fixed or the ground is confined. Without the guard, Lamtuna Spearmen and terrain may still accomplish the job; the unit is optional, not mandatory for a viable Almoravid army.

**Candidate battle cycle:** move in controlled order behind screening; establish a defensible, short-to-medium frontage; face the actual mounted or infantry approach; keep shield coverage and spear reach; absorb and contest a limited attack while officers and reserves maintain cohesion; hand off a disrupted or stalled opponent to Retainers, Selected Foot, Ghazi Infantry or cavalry as the mission warrants; withdraw or rotate before flank pressure, missiles, exhaustion or lost leaders destroy the formation. It should not charge through an infantry line, fight an extended sword melee as well as Retainers, cover the entire map, or win automatically because the target is cavalry. A spear and shield give conditional mounted-threat deterrence only when the achieved spear length, facing, formation depth, ground and cohesion support it. A shield cannot stop every missile; heavier protection adds maintenance and fatigue burdens.

**Distinctiveness stress test:** place mature Lamtuna Spearmen and the proposed guards at the same threatened crossing. The Lamtuna card remains the more natural broad confederational field frontage and ordinary spear spine. The new guard should be chosen only where a separately organized, more consistently equipped and trained **maintained field-guard service** offers a tighter, more resilient local anchor with an officer-led relief/reform decision, and its source cost makes that choice meaningful. If the player and battle AI make the same decisions with both cards and the only difference is armor or stats, **reject the new card** and express the improvement through Lamtuna equipment/training development. The “guard” title is not a bonus.

## 3. Pair test against the Retainers

| Question | Maghrebi Foot Guards, conditional design | Dismounted Almoravid Retainers, existing profile |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Player's immediate need | Deny a narrow field approach and protect the line before penetration | Enter, sustain and resolve close sword-and-shield combat; retain or retake a confined point |
| Weapon and formation | Spear-and-shield depth with disciplined facing, bounded frontage and conditional bracing | Sword-and-shield medium-depth melee, relief and short defensive counterstroke; spear wall not standard |
| Best setting | Open or semi-open approach, crossing, camp exit, reserve wing, protected field frontage | Street, breach, gate, camp interior, strongpoint and compressed melee |
| Failure | Flanked, outmaneuvered, worn down by missiles, pulled into sustained sidearm melee | Exposed to open cavalry, long spear reach, concentrated missiles, prolonged attrition |
| Source hypothesis | Distinct later maintained *field-guard* contributor with owner-controlled spear/shield issue and service | Developed ruler/amir household, high-trust retainer and veteran networks already specified in V1.2 |
| Political cost | Removes real field-guard personnel and cadres from another maintained defense or field commitment | Removes owner-backed retainers, household security, selected veterans or command capacity |

The pair should create **complementary decisions**, not a rock-paper-scissors guarantee: guard fixes or denies a local approach; retainers exploit close-ground conditions or hold after contact. Both can fight ordinary infantry when properly supported. Neither is a universal upgrade of the other. The Retainers' V1.2 file already permits a *secondary spear* under owner review; that does not turn their core sword-and-shield card into this proposed spear-frontage unit. Conversely, Maghrebi Foot Guards may carry sidearms but do not inherit the Retainers' sustained melee identity. Source overlap is possible only with explicit owner release and shared finite-capacity accounting, never two simultaneous copies of the same veterans, officers, shields or pay.

## 4. Full roster collision, including item 5 and item 6

- **Mature Lamtuna Spearmen:** retain confederational field spine, broad frontage, ordinary mounted-threat deterrence and veteran persistence. A guard card needs an independently maintained source and narrow anchor/relief use; better mail alone fails.
- **Almoravid Spear Levy / Sus Levy Spearmen:** remain affordable, source-bound mass and secondary defense; a high-quality guard is neither a renamed regional levy nor an automatic levy upgrade.
- **Aghmat Garrison Infantry and other valid city expressions:** own maintained local gates, walls, streets, stores and relief. A field guard must have a real *field-release* mandate, travel, return and different mission; a city garrison standing outside a gate does not create a new roster family.
- **Selected Almoravid Foot:** owns mobile crisis stabilization, command/missile protection and short counterattack. Guard's strength is deliberate prepared denial, not a second selected reserve.
- **Ghazi Infantry:** owns mission-bound assault and seizure, not automatic religious morale, permanent guard service or a generic heavy spear upgrade.
- **Lamtuna Frontier Spearmen and Andalusi Frontier Infantry:** own watch, warning, delay, counter-raid and fighting withdrawal. The guard is a formed local anchor, not a better frontier scout or a foreign pool borrowed by name.
- **Murabitun Spear Brotherhood, item 5:** held for a later High Era committed-spear admission pass. It must not be preempted by giving the guards religious zeal, brotherhood membership, unique shock, or automatic faith bonuses. If both proposals converge on the same source, mission and spear behavior, retain only the one distinct card; do not create three heavy-spear tiers by symmetry.
- **Sudanese Guard Infantry, item 6:** separately held for regional/source-specific review. “Sudanese” must not be treated as a uniform racial, ethnic or equipment label, and neither its recruitment nor its battlefield role is created by this file. If its actual source yields the same guard function, consider a source-specific expression or bounded contingent rather than a duplicated card. No opening source or transfer is asserted.

**Decision if collisions fail:** retain the Dismounted Retainers as the existing distinct High Era II card; improve mature Lamtuna Spearmen and any appropriate garrison institution within their own authority. The guard *concept* can still survive as an assignment, localized appearance, or formation-development state without a separate recruit button. This is a real fallback, not a presumption that the proposal must pass.

## 5. Source and institution admission test

**Hypothesis A, developed internal field-guard service:** an Almoravid-aligned owner has an actual maintained infantry body, separately qualified for field movement and protected-position duty, with continuing leadership, spear/shield equipment and finite pay, obligation or provisioning. This is the preferred source *type* for a realm-distinct field card, not a statement that such a body already exists in 1062 or every High Era campaign. Specify the owner and jurisdiction; named source people; protected city, household and ordinary army duties; command and release; regional geography; and replenishment/return. A generic “Maghrebi population” is not a source.

**Hypothesis B, urban or regional contributor:** a particular Maghrebi city, district, or regional institution may release qualified trained defenders for bounded field duty. Compare directly to its garrison, militia and ordinary regional infantry. The owner must preserve the home-defense minimum, show field competence beyond gate duty, and permit travel. If it is simply the same garrison with heavier shields, localize/develop the garrison instead of duplicating it.

**Hypothesis C, household/retainer overlap:** a ruler or amir may furnish spear-armed followers, but if their personnel, duties and formation are just the Dismounted Retainers with a spear, no new source or card exists. A household contribution must pass independent personnel, command and battlefield tests. Do not raid Amir's Horse Guard, Selected Foot or Retainers for free cadres.

**Hypothesis D, external contributor:** a verified regional provider may offer a bounded heavy spear contingent under agreement. Preserve external ownership, arrival, recall and source recovery. A regional contingent should not be called a realm-wide Maghrebi guard by default. Diplomacy, payment, conquest or a building opens no automatic source.

**Gate checklist:** source owner and date-compatible service; enough unreserved trained people and leaders; maintained spear/shield/sidearm and variable armor actually issued; training for coherent limited frontage and relief; valid field mission and geography; food, water, route, quarters, medicine and return; compensation or obligation; protected household/civic/army minimums; distinctive battle decision versus Lamtuna and Retainers; readable visual identity; and owner approval. A High Era II label and research can expose a question but cannot manufacture the answers. No S-number, candidate reference, exact pool, cost, armor grade or stat is allocated in this decision.

## 6. Prospective card and two-unit encounter fixtures

> **MAGHREBI FOOT GUARDS** (working name, design preview only)  
> **Class:** maintained heavy spear-and-shield field infantry.  
> **Role:** anchor and deny a limited approach while other infantry counter or relieve.  
> **Best use:** a threatened crossing, field reserve wing or defended camp exit with supported flanks.  
> **Strengths:** organized local frontage, spear reach, disciplined facing, controlled relief when qualified officers and support exist.  
> **Weaknesses:** missile attrition, flanking, fatigue, long unsupported frontage, sustained close melee, expensive replacement and owner-release limits.  
> **Access:** only from a verified source with field-guard service and finite equipment, not a general Maghrebi settlement unlock.  
> **Status:** conditional design preview; no live recruitment or opening unit.

**Fixture A, open approach:** mature Lamtuna Spearmen hold a wider army line; a small guard anchors a threatened gap. Retainers wait behind. If the guard can cover all the frontage more cheaply, the design fails. If the guard only has a higher defense stat, the design fails. A valid test shows a clear narrow-anchor benefit and a real source/availability cost.

**Fixture B, street breach:** Retainers enter and hold a confined breach; guard spears struggle to deploy fully and instead control the outer approach or protect a withdrawal. If the guard is also best at close sword fighting, the pair fails.

**Fixture C, mounted threat:** a formed mounted attack approaches the guard front while light missiles harass its flank. The guard may deter or delay a frontal contact if braced, supplied and facing correctly; the attacker can outflank, soften or force a withdrawal. There is no guaranteed anti-cavalry hard counter.

**Fixture D, recruitment collision:** a developed city or household has one eligible cadre already protecting a gate or command area. Recruiting the guard removes that real capacity; recruiting Retainers or reinforcing the garrison may compete for some people or equipment. If a separate field-guard source cannot be identified, the proposed card remains blocked rather than drawing from an abstract faction elite pool.

**Fixture E, political change:** a High Era Almoravid owner loses a city, breaks a service arrangement, or faces later Almohad pressure. New guard recruitment can close while surviving formations persist, withdraw, realign or dissolve only through owner-valid transactions. No date-driven deletion or automatic successor conversion.
