### 2. Profile Verdict

**Maghrebi Foot Guards remain the user-approved conditional High Era spear-and-shield field-guard profile.** They establish a short-to-medium organized frontage, deny a mounted or infantry approach, protect a reserve wing, crossing, camp exit, command avenue, or threatened gap, and preserve enough order to relieve, reform, hand off, or withdraw.

The card is not a heavier repaint or universal upgrade of Lamtuna Spearmen. Lamtuna Spearmen remain the broad confederational field spine. Maghrebi Foot Guards trade breadth, availability, and source flexibility for tighter maintained equipment, stronger officer control, disciplined facing, local relief, and resilience at one important point. Dismounted Almoravid Retainers continue to own sustained sidearm melee and strongpoint retention after contact compresses.

The design direction is settled, but the exact historical name, specific source owner, recruitment binding, values, and implementation remain pending. The force cannot arise from a generic Maghrebi identity, generic population, date, research, building, treasury, armor package, or guard title. If later battle and source review reduce the profile to armor and defense statistics, the separate-card identity fails without deleting personnel or validated field-guard behavior.

## 5. Player-Facing Description

Maghrebi Foot Guards are maintained spear-and-shield infantry used to secure the part of the battlefield that cannot be allowed to fail. In disciplined files they occupy a crossing, reserve wing, camp exit, command approach, or narrow field gap, keep their front aligned, and deny an enemy an easy breakthrough. Their value comes from controlled facing, maintained equipment, officers, local reserve depth, and the ability to relieve or reform without turning the entire line into a static wall.

They are not designed to win every infantry fight. Lamtuna Spearmen hold broader frontage at lower institutional cost. Dismounted Almoravid Retainers enter the sustained sword-and-shield melee once contact closes or terrain compresses. Selected Almoravid Foot answer a mobile crisis. Maghrebi Foot Guards instead make one limited approach difficult to force, buying the army time and shape for the next decision.

### 6.1 Definitive player choice and fluid battle loop

Choose the guard when one approach must remain denied while the rest of the army maneuvers: a threatened reserve wing, exposed camp exit, prepared crossing, or gap between ordinary spear formations. Choose mature Lamtuna Spearmen when the problem is a long main line, distributed spear protection, or a broader confederational muster. Choose Aghmat Garrison Infantry when the site itself, watch, relief routes, and civic defense are the continuing mission. Choose Selected Foot when the crisis is not yet known. Choose Retainers when the fight has already become compressed sidearm contact.

### 8.1 Survival must pass four independent tests

1. **Source:** identify a maintained field-service relationship with its own authorized people, cadre, equipment, protected duty and return, or a separately allocated share of a real source. A better-equipped Lamtuna formation by itself fails.
2. **Player decision:** at equal terrain and threat, the guard should be preferable only for a shorter, more controlled and more expensive anchor; Lamtuna should still be preferable for broad frontage and ordinary recruitment.
3. **AI decision:** the AI must sometimes choose Lamtuna, sometimes the guard, and sometimes neither, for explainable mission and source reasons. A fixed “elite first” priority fails.
4. **Campaign consequence:** release of guards must measurably weaken a real prior obligation; if nothing loses personnel, officers, kit or coverage, the source model fails.

If any of these fail, preserve the V1 and V1.1 research and express the accepted defensive capability through a qualified Lamtuna development, garrison field assignment, or localized formation without adding a duplicate recruitment card. A fallback is not a deletion of people or history.

## 21. Signature Battle Cycle

1. Move behind screening troops, terrain, or the main line.
2. Select one critical approach rather than attempting to cover the whole battlefield.
3. Dress spear-and-shield files into a coherent bounded frontage with protected flanks.
4. Face the real threat and deny an easy mounted or infantry penetration.
5. Absorb limited pressure while preserving depth, officers, shields, and spear alignment.
6. Relieve tired files, reform localized disorder, or hand the fixed enemy to retainers, selected foot, axemen, missiles, or cavalry.
7. Withdraw before missiles, fatigue, broken flanks, or sidearm compression destroy the formation.
8. Rally, inspect losses and equipment, and reconstitute only from surviving real capacity.
