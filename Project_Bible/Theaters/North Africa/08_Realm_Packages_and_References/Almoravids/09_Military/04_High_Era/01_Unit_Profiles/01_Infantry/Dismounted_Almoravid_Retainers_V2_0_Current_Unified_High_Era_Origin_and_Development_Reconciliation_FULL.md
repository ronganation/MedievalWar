## 2. Strong Unit Verdict

**Dismounted Almoravid Retainers remain the user-approved conditional High Era continuing foot-retainer profile with a sustained sword-and-shield mission.** The card is not Selected Almoravid Foot with higher values, Amir's Horse Guard with horses hidden, Ghazi Infantry with stronger armor, a generic garrison expression, or a recolored foreign dismounted household unit.

The player spends scarce household trust, veteran experience, officers, equipment, service continuity, and political security to form or maintain a body that can enter a prepared close fight, remain coherent after first contact, consolidate a breach or strongpoint, deliver a bounded defensive counterstroke, and withdraw with its lineage intact. Selected Almoravid Foot retain flexible crisis response. Dismounted Retainers own continued prepared close combat and strongpoint retention.

Exact historical naming, source bindings, personnel relationships, values, and implementation remain pending. A household label, date, research, treasury, building, armor package, mounted status, or prior eligibility never creates a foot formation or duplicates assigned people, equipment, officers, horses, or protected duties.

## 3. Designer One-Screen Card

- **Class:** Veteran High Era Almoravid household sword-and-shield infantry.
- **Source:** Qualified retainers, veterans, officers, file leaders, standards, equipment, support labor, and owner-authorized service relationships.
- **Battle promise:** Enter a shaped close fight, sustain pressure, consolidate the local objective, and hold or withdraw in order.
- **Best use:** Breaches, gates, courtyards, wider streets, camp interiors, command-area crises, strongpoint retention, and local defensive counterstrokes.
- **Worst use:** Cheap frontage, unsupported open-ground advance, missile exposure, clean cavalry contact, deep pursuit, repeated expendable assault, or missions better handled by spears, Ghazi, Selected Foot, garrisons, or mounted retainers.
- **Strategic cost:** Draws from household security, command protection, mounted service, officers, veteran cadres, equipment owners, and political relationships.
- **Counterplay:** Reach, missiles, cavalry, fatigue, flank attack, officer loss, blocked withdrawal, heat, thirst, fire, smoke, and source exhaustion.

## 4. Player-Facing Description

Dismounted Almoravid Retainers are veteran military followers organized for continuing foot service with swords, shields, and owner-supported protection. They are committed where a flexible reserve is no longer enough and where a temporary assault body cannot hold the result. In a breach, gate, courtyard, street, camp, or threatened command area, the Retainers close under protection, preserve their files, push through a bounded local fight, and remain long enough to secure the ground.

Their quality is expensive. The same households and military relationships may also be protecting an amir, maintaining mounted followers, training replacements, guarding records and stores, or sustaining other formations. Heavy losses therefore damage more than one unit card. The Retainers are strongest when prepared, supported, and given a clear stopping point. They remain vulnerable to missiles, reach weapons, cavalry on open ground, exhaustion, encirclement, and disorder.

Avoid unnecessary missile exposure and clean cavalry contact. Enter when the opening and objective justify the cost. Preserve shield, file, officer, and standard relationships. Sustain contact only while the formation can control its frontage. Consolidate the point or deliver the bounded counterstroke. Refuse deep pursuit. Break contact before encirclement, exhaustion, or command collapse. Recover wounded, standards, equipment, records, and any related mounts.

## 36. Offensive Doctrine

The unit should not normally be the first expendable body into an unknown breach or fresh spear frontage. Strong attacks enter after missiles, spears, Ghazi, terrain, engineers, or another formation has shaped access and fixed the enemy. The Retainers then close, clear the local resistance, preserve cohesion, and hold the gain against a counterattack.

## 37. Defensive Doctrine

Strong defensive uses include sealing a breach, retaking a gate, protecting a command or rally area, holding a courtyard or camp lane, reinforcing a strongpoint, and covering a controlled withdrawal. Holding never means mandatory destruction. The formation should surrender, withdraw, or fragment when the military and household cost of continued resistance exceeds the mission.

## 38. Anti-Cavalry Boundary

Shields, secondary spears, obstacles, narrow ground, and friendly spear support can improve survival. The formation is not a dedicated spear or pike line. A coherent heavy charge into open, disordered, unsupported, or exhausted frontage remains dangerous. Retainers should not chase mounted skirmishers or expose flanks through pursuit.

## 39. Missile, Fire, and Reach Counterplay

Shields and protection reduce but do not erase missiles. Crossbows, repeated arrows, elevated or flank fire, damaged shields, smoke, fire, collapsing structures, and confined routes can force movement or separation. Fresh long-reach formations can deny sword working space. Support, rapid passage, suppression, terrain, or mantlet-type protection may be required for exposed approaches.
