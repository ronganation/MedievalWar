### 2. Profile Verdict

**Sus Provincial Infantry remain the user-approved conditional High Era North African regional profile for organized provincial field frontage, reinforcement, relief, reserve-line service, road and crossing defense, camp protection, settlement-approach defense, and supported provincial campaigning.** The card represents the development of valid Sus regional military-service relationships without inventing one universal provincial corps or population pool.

The formation sits between local militia and more selected or specialist infantry. Aghmat Militia and other local defenders retain local emergency and civic defense. Lamtuna Spearmen retain the confederational spear spine. Aghmat Garrison Infantry retain maintained site custody. Selected Almoravid Foot retain the scarce crisis reserve. Sus Provincial Infantry own dependable ordinary regional field service when real source owners, people, officers, equipment, stores, routes, service terms, and support pass.

The design direction is settled. Exact localization, source owners, service bindings, values, and implementation remain pending. Territorial control, a provincial building, political labels, research, treasury, or the High Era date cannot create the people, equipment, officers, stores, training, readiness, or service relationship required by the profile.

### 5. Player-Facing Description

Sus Provincial Infantry are regionally organized spear-and-shield troops drawn from valid communities and service relationships in the Sus. They provide a dependable field layer for armies that can no longer rely only on confederational contingents, civic militia, and scarce selected veterans. The formation can reinforce a battle line, relieve a threatened position, defend a road or crossing, secure a camp approach, and support a regional campaign without pretending to be elite infantry.

Their usefulness comes from availability, organization, and regional support rather than exceptional individual quality. Recruitment removes people, leaders, weapons, stores, transport, and labor from towns, districts, farms, workshops, markets, routes, and local defense. A provincial levy ordered too far from its support base or retained beyond its service terms may lose readiness, cooperation, and replacement depth even before battle losses are counted.
