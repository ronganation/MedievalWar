## 1. Identity Strip

- **Unit:** Farfanes
- **Class:** conditional external heavy or heavy-medium contract cavalry
- **Era:** High Era conditional emergence, with later maturity preferred and exceptional earlier named-source scenarios held separately
- **Quality:** source-dependent, potentially experienced and cohesive, never automatically elite
- **Core action:** charge, secure, and serve within the contract
- **Primary role:** deliver a formed charge, seize or protect an agreed objective, and remain for a bounded mission period
- **Secondary role:** controlled countercharge, contracted reserve, withdrawal protection, escort, and reinforcement where authorized
- **Best use:** commit against an exposed or engaged target whose defeat opens a contract-valid objective, then secure the objective until relief, completion, or withdrawal
- **Major weaknesses:** prepared spears, missiles, constricted terrain, attritional melee, remount loss, source replacement limits, unclear orders, invalid missions, payment or jurisdiction breach, recall, and nonrenewal
- **Source:** one validated Christian mounted formation, exile band, allied contingent, settled service community, or other lawful service source under a current contract
- **Opening State:** rejected
- **Exact values:** unresolved and not promoted

## 3. Player-Facing Description

Farfanes are Christian mounted formations serving an Almoravid or successor authority through a specific political and military relationship. Their officers, riders, horses, weapons, privileges, discipline, families, and replacement routes remain tied to the source formation and its agreement. They are not purchased from a global market and do not replenish from ordinary Almoravid populations.

On the battlefield, Farfanes combine formed charge capability with a mission-bound willingness to secure an agreed objective. They may break an exposed flank, countercharge a mounted threat, escort a critical person or convoy, take a crossing, or hold a breach long enough for allied troops to exploit the opening. They are not guaranteed to obey an order outside the contract, and even a tactically successful battle can damage renewal if the receiving authority violates payment, jurisdiction, return, captive, religious-practice, or privilege terms.
