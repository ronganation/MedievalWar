## 2. Player-Facing Description

Granadine Jinetes are agile Andalusi regional horsemen trained to harass with javelins, challenge enemy light cavalry, protect exposed flanks, and control pursuit. Their battlefield value comes from deciding when to remain mobile and when to commit. A well-timed javelin pass can disrupt a screen or force an enemy cavalry formation to react, while a careless melee commitment can leave the formation trapped by heavier horsemen, missiles, or prepared spears.

Unlike Lamtuna Cavalry, Granadine Jinetes are a High Era regional family shaped by Iberian frontier warfare, counter-cavalry demands, disciplined pursuit, and more specialized transitions between harassment and contact. The distinction is not a simple statistical increase. Lamtuna Cavalry remain the persistent confederational mounted-javelin family. Granadine Jinetes earn a separate card through regional source, counter-light-cavalry work, pursuit denial, conditional feigned withdrawal, and support for Granadine Lancers.

## 2. One-Screen Design Card

### Battlefield

- Screens the army and denies enemy scouts or raiders easy access.
- Harasses with finite javelins rather than remaining indefinitely at range.
- Challenges enemy light cavalry when local numbers, cohesion, horse condition, and support are favorable.
- Threatens exposed archers, skirmishers, messengers, foragers, and withdrawing troops.
- Controls pursuit by pressuring enemy horsemen who overextend against friendly withdrawals.
- May use a disciplined feigned withdrawal only when leadership, signals, terrain, route, and enemy behavior permit.
- Performs poorly against prepared spears, concentrated missiles, formed heavy cavalry, and constricted terrain.

### Campaign

- Draws from actual Andalusi regional mounted sources rather than a generic Iberian cavalry pool.
- Requires qualified riders, agile suitable horses, remounts, tack, shields, javelins, sidearms, officers, handlers, fodder, water, routes, and recovery.
- Competes with frontier response, scouts, messengers, escorts, local defense, breeding, remounts, and economic horse use.
- Can serve through integration, alliance, vassal contribution, contract, or successor continuity while regional ownership remains visible.
- Recruitment may close while veteran formations persist.

### Access

- Requires a valid regional owner and service route.
- Requires mounted-javelin training, formation control, ammunition discipline, withdrawal practice, and rider-horse pairing.
- Requires horses selected for agility, endurance, acceleration, responsiveness, and terrain suitability.
- A stable, decision, city-control flag, cultural label, or stock of horses does not create the formation.

### Development

- Improves through veteran leadership, stronger signals, mounted-javelin training, horse conditioning, remount management, ammunition support, counter-cavalry experience, and pursuit discipline.
- Does not automatically become Andalusi Lancers.
- Does not gain mounted bows through progression.
- May localize as Granadine Jinetes only after an appropriate later southern-Iberian context passes.

## 3. Player-Facing Description

Andalusi Jinetes are agile regional horsemen trained to harass with javelins, challenge enemy light cavalry, protect exposed flanks, and control pursuit. Their battlefield value comes from choosing when to remain mobile and when to commit. A well-timed javelin pass can disrupt a screen or force an enemy cavalry formation to react, but a careless melee commitment can leave the formation trapped by heavier horsemen, missiles, or prepared spears.

Unlike Lamtuna Cavalry, Andalusi Jinetes are a later regional family shaped by Iberian frontier warfare, counter-cavalry demands, disciplined pursuit, and more specialized transitions between harassment and contact. The distinction is not a simple increase in statistics. Lamtuna Cavalry remain an effective early and persistent mounted-javelin family. Jinetes earn their separate card through regional source, stronger counter-light-cavalry work, pursuit denial, conditional feigned withdrawal, and support for Andalusi heavy cavalry.

Each formation depends on real riders, horses, remounts, javelins, tack, officers, stores, and regional service relationships. Losses are not restored by a stable building. A campaign that exhausts horses, ammunition, or local mounted households can close recruitment while surviving veterans continue to serve.
