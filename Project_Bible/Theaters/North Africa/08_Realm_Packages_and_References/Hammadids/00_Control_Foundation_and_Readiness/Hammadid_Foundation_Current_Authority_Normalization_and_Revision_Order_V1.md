## 1. Purpose

This normalization pass audits the first four Hammadid foundation files as one authority family. It identifies what is strong, what is duplicated, what is missing, what is malformed, and what must be carried forward through copy-first additive successors before further Realm expansion.

The pass does not replace the four source authorities. It creates a controlled revision order so the Hammadid package reaches the same standard expected of developed Realm packages without stripping the distinctive opening already established.

The normalization pass has six objectives:

1. preserve the full substantive content already created;
2. align document control, authority language, statuses, paths, temporal treatment, validation, and owner gates;
3. eliminate formatting defects visible in the stored files;
4. convert broad research leads into explicit claim, evidence, confidence, owner, and disposition records;
5. reduce contradiction and repetition without compressing useful detail;
6. establish a safe handoff into character, dynasty, geography, governance, diplomacy, economy, military, and eventual Opening State work.

The Hammadid foundation is substantively good. The Realm already has a compelling identity: an established central-Maghrebi dynastic power, a mountain capital, a violent accession, incomplete recognition, predecessor networks, a western campaign memory, Zirid kinship and rivalry, Banu Hilal pressure, and the possibility of a later coastal center.

Several files also contain escaped Markdown characters and malformed transitions between prose and structured records.

## 5. What Is Already Strong

### 5.1 Source Inventory

The source inventory correctly:

- identifies the Hammadids as the first new North Africa Realm research stream;
- distinguishes orientation sources from stronger published and archaeological sources;
- receives the uploaded circa-1065 map without treating the map as literal 1062 Opening State authority;
- exposes the January versus July 1062 chronology problem;
- selects An-Nasir ibn Alnas as the preferred ruler-facing candidate while preserving Buluggin ibn Muhammad as a major recent memory;
- identifies Qal'a Bani Hammad as the preferred capital candidate;
- holds exact borders, armies, governors, vassals, Banu Hilal arrangements, and opening offices;
- defines phased package development.

### 5.2 Leadership and Assassination Authority

The leadership authority correctly:

- rejects a guaranteed scripted assassination after campaign start;
- proposes a bounded post-assassination Hammadid opening rather than silently falsifying chronology;
- treats legitimacy as typed recognition rather than one universal score;
- preserves several possible interpretations of the killing;
- gives Namirt bint Alnas substantive political and dynastic importance;
- prevents the historical assassination from creating a free reusable assassin agent;
- separates holder-specific memory, knowledge, interpretation, and action.

### 5.3 Realm Identity and Political Order

The Realm identity authority correctly:

- separates the Hammadids from the Almoravids and Zirids;
- defines an established dynastic Realm rather than an emerging movement-state;
- treats Qal'a Bani Hammad as an interior fortified dynastic center;
- preserves the distinction between control, recognition, tribute, vassalage, alliance, contribution, contract, and influence;
- defines Banu Hilal actors as independent political-military networks rather than generic rebels or owned manpower;
- establishes a clear Qal'a-to-Béjaïa development tension;
- avoids scripting later territorial and dynastic outcomes.

### 5.4 Capital Authority

The capital authority correctly:

- treats Qal'a Bani Hammad as a city, fortress, court, mosque center, market, craft center, route interface, and dynastic monument;
- separates capital status from complete Realm control;
- separates urban population, garrison, household guards, militias, and field forces;
- prevents mosques, palaces, workshops, markets, stores, and walls from creating loyalty, officials, money, equipment, people, or security by label alone;
- makes water, food, sanitation, routes, and repair part of capital viability;
- avoids silently completing Béjaïa at the opening.

## 6. Foundation-Wide Normalization Defects

The stored files visibly contain escaped underscores, escaped list markers, escaped brackets, and backslash-prefixed values inside what should be normal Markdown and YAML.

Examples include forms such as:

```text
Hammadid\_Realm\_Identity
\- RealmIdentityAuthorityCandidate
\[\]
```
