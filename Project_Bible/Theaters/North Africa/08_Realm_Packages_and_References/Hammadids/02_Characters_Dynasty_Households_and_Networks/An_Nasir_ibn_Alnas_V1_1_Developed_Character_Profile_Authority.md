## 8. Core character thesis

The profile should present <Person>An-Nasir ibn Alnas</Person> as a political actor already capable of ruling but not yet insulated from the consequences of succession violence. The character inherits a functioning capital, dynasty, court, offices, military and regional relationships, and recent western-campaign consequences. The central drama is not whether government exists. The central drama is whether the ruler can make inherited authority practical, recognized, informed, supplied, and durable without destroying the networks that make government possible.

## 69. Court, petition, justice, and public explanation loop

An-Nasir's ruler identity must include government-facing human interaction, not only strategic direction.

- receive petitions and complaints through controlled access;
- request evidence, witnesses, records, and local interpretation;
- distinguish a legal or administrative dispute from political opposition;
- consult officers, jurists, commanders, governors, notables, and affected households according to subject matter;
- decide whether to confirm, mediate, compensate, investigate, dismiss, prosecute, defer, or refer;
- communicate the result and its authority;
- verify whether the result was understood and performed;
- review grievance, appeal, evasion, incapacity, and restoration.

Public explanation may strengthen legitimacy or create new scrutiny. Silence may preserve flexibility or deepen suspicion. Neither is universally correct.
