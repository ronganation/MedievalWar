## 1. Purpose

This authority defines how the Hammadid Realm candidate governs from Qal'a Bani Hammad after a violent accession. It establishes a political constitution based on ruler authority, dynasty, court, offices, cities, fortresses, regional delegates, local authorities, juristic institutions, negotiated contributors, routes, and compliance rather than treating every colored region as uniformly administered.

The authority is substantial but definition-only. It does not create named officeholders, regional governors, vassals, tax receipts, armies, settlement controllers, or Opening State bindings. It defines what later evidence and owners must establish before any such record can exist.

```text
ruler installed != ruler obeyed
title != practical capacity
office != person
officeholder != owner of office resources
governor != province ownership
appointment != compliance
dynastic kinship != loyalty
tribute != direct administration
recognition != tax payment
fortress command != regional government
religious recognition != civil control
map color != government
```

## 3. Strong Governance Verdict

The Hammadid Realm should begin as an established dynastic emirate with a functioning capital and government, but not as a fully centralized territorial bureaucracy. An-Nasir ibn Alnas is the preferred ruler candidate. The ruler's authority must be made practical through the court, trusted officers, military commanders, city and fortress authorities, regional delegates, jurists, local notables, routes, information, resources, and negotiated compliance.

  - Negotiate external and regional relationships
  - Allocate patronage gifts honors and resources through proper owners

  - Designate or clarify succession where constitutionally permitted

  - Every tax collection
  - Every judgment
  - Every garrison duty

An-Nasir's opening authority should be strong enough to act but dependent enough to make governance meaningful. The ruler can order a governor to provide troops, but the actual response depends on the governor's mandate, local capacity, route access, political relationship, military sources, and information.
