## 1. Purpose and Authority

This authority defines how education, teaching, learning, literacy, numeracy, scribal practice, juristic study, administrative knowledge, translation, manuscripts, archives, record custody, scholarship, patronage, historical memory, poetry, arts, and technical-cultural production may operate in the Hammadid Realm.

It supplies the missing knowledge owner required by governance, law, religion, diplomacy, economy, information, construction, military administration, population, constituency, faction AI, and presentation. It does not invent a university, madrasa, library, archive building, universal literacy, named scholars, books, collections, teachers, scribes, poets, or technology effects in January 1062.

## 5. External Evidence Boundary

UNESCO explicitly identifies Qal'at Bani Hammad as the first Hammadid capital and a major fortified Muslim city with a monumental mosque. That supports the existence of substantial urban and institutional contexts, but UNESCO does not state that the site contained a university, madrasa, library, archive, named school, or opening scholarly staff.

A secondary orientation summarizes later historical claims that Qal'a attracted poets, sages, and theologians and offered resources for sciences, commerce, and arts. This supports focused research into cultural and scholarly activity, but it does not establish January 1062 named individuals, permanent institutions, curricula, collections, offices, or quantities.
