## 1. Purpose and Authority

This authority defines the Hammadid Realm's conditional maritime development from coastal access and harbor functions through shipping, port labor, ship acquisition, construction, maintenance, merchant transport, naval movement, coastal security, patrol, escort, interdiction, raiding, diplomacy, trade protection, and recovery.

It does not back-project later Bejaia-oriented power into January 1062. It creates no opening port, fleet, arsenal, shipyard, quay, harbor master, shipwright, mariner, sailor, merchant vessel, warship, naval unit, coastal garrison, or capital transfer.

## 5. Historical Evidence Boundary

The internal <File>Hammadid_Economy_Material_Flow_Markets_Trade_Caravans_and_Maritime_Exchange_V1.md</File> explicitly owns maritime exchange within the Realm's economic system, while <File>Hammadid_Property_Construction_Urban_Development_and_Infrastructure_Authority_V1.md</File> owns physical development and infrastructure. This authority adds the operational maritime capability between exchange and infrastructure rather than replacing either file. citeturn29search67turn29search65

<External>History of the Port</External> states that al-Bakri mentioned a small port of Bejaia frequented by Andalusian sailors and describes later Bejaia as a commercial Mediterranean port with an arsenal supporting merchant and war fleets. This supports research into pre-existing coastal access and later port, shipping, arsenal, merchant-fleet, and war-fleet capabilities. The source does not establish January 1062 Hammadid ownership, opening infrastructure, workforce, fleet composition, or readiness. citeturn29search59

<External>Hammadid dynasty</External> states that Qal'at Bani Hammad was the capital until roughly 1090 and that Bejaia later became the capital. <External>Nasir ibn Alnas</External> associates the establishment of Bejaia as a second capital with greater importance for maritime trade. These statements support conditional later development, not a fixed scripted transfer or opening maritime payload. citeturn29search60turn29search62
