## 1. Purpose and Authority

This authority defines Hammadid siege warfare as an extended contest of fortifications, people, command, information, water, food, health, engineering, materials, labor, routes, field works, assault, relief, negotiation, and recovery.

It establishes the operational layer between a fortress definition and an actual siege. It creates no opening siege, engineer, engine, ladder, mine, camp, garrison, stockpile, breach, damage state, relief army, capitulation, or occupied settlement.

## 40. Rationing

Rationing requires an authorized actor, inventory knowledge, population and force scope, allocation rules, distribution, monitoring, exemptions or priority, and health consequences. A ration order does not create accurate stock or fair delivery.

## 41. Besieger Supply

Besiegers require continued food, water, fodder, replacement equipment, engineering materials, medical support, pay or obligation support, and route security. Time pressures both sides.

## 42. Water Pressure

Water may be defended, contested, diverted, damaged, rationed, contaminated as a claim, carried, or lost. Environmental and health owners determine condition and safety. Siege actors do not gain automatic control over a source because the target is invested.

## 43. Food Pressure

Food pressure depends on real stocks, population, rationing, spoilage, access, substitution, smuggling, relief, and duration. Encirclement does not create a starvation timer detached from inventory.

## 44. Fuel and Fodder Pressure

Fuel affects cooking, heating, repair, production, and care. Fodder affects animals and transport. Both can constrain defenders and besiegers before human food is exhausted.

## 45. Civilian Population

Population authority owns people, households, dependents, migration, and displacement. Siege authority owns their exposure to confinement, rationing, bombardment, fire, labor demands, damaged shelter, and negotiations.

## 46. Civilian Protection

Potential protections include shelter, controlled movement, water and food distribution, fire response, health care, records, evacuation where possible, and protection from abuse. Protection consumes capacity and is never automatic.

## 47. Labor Mobilization Inside Defenses

Civilian or household labor use requires authority, capacity, tools, support, care burdens, safety, compensation or obligation treatment, and legal review. Population presence does not create free repair labor.

## 48. Morale Boundary

Morale is not a single hidden cultural score. It may depend on leadership, legitimacy, supplies, health, casualties, confidence, family safety, relief prospects, treatment, information, and prior events. This authority does not infer emotions of individuals.

## 55. Siege Withdrawal

## 56. Capture and Occupation

## 60. Immediate Aftermath

Immediate needs may include fire control, water access, care, burial, missing-person records, prisoner security, shelter, food, sanitation, route clearance, structural assessment, and prevention of abuse.

## 61. Repair Versus Reconstruction

Repair restores bounded function. Reconstruction may redesign, replace, expand, or relocate systems and requires political, fiscal, property, labor, production, population, and historical review.

## 62. Fortification Reconstruction

Fortification recovery requires damage assessment, strategic need, design, materials, labor, security, funding, supply, construction stages, inspection, and maintenance. Capture does not automatically restore defensive value.

## 64. Field Fortifications Outside Sieges

The same engineering model may support temporary camps, roadblocks, river crossings, defensive lines, protected supply points, and battle preparations. No universal field-work package appears from army stance alone.

## 65. Road and Crossing Engineering

Road, bridge, causeway, ramp, drainage, or crossing work requires terrain, design, people, tools, materials, protection, and maintenance. Geography and property owners control stable infrastructure; military engineering controls temporary operational work.

## 66. Demolition and Denial

Any deliberate destruction of bridges, gates, waterworks, stores, roads, or fortifications requires authority, means, time, safety, and aftermath review. Strategic denial may harm civilians and future recovery.

## 67. Scorched-Earth Boundary

Destruction of food, forage, water access, shelter, or infrastructure is never a free abstract command. It requires located assets, actors, authority, capability, legal-political consequences, and persistent damage.

## 68. Fiscal Interface

Fiscal authority owns funding, treasury commitment, emergency expenditure, debt, compensation, audit, and recovery finance. Siege authority owns operational need and verified consumption. A siege budget creates no engine, camp, ration, or repair.

## 69. Production Interface

Production owns materials, tools, equipment, animals, workshops, storage, transport, quality, repair inputs, and delivery. Siege authority assembles those capabilities for engineering and operations.

## 70. Property Interface

Property owns fortification structures, gates, roads, waterworks, storage, housing, damage, maintenance, repair, and construction. Siege authority owns operational readiness, use, attack, defense, and siege-specific works.

## 71. Geography Interface

Geography owns terrain, approaches, water sources, routes, settlements, distances, and stable fortification locations. Siege authority owns investment, camps, coverage, works, access, and operational movement.

## 72. Environment Interface

Environment owns weather, heat, cold, rain, wind, fire hazard, water stress, soil, slope interaction, erosion, and exposure. Siege authority owns effects on camps, works, routes, supply, assault, health, and recovery.

## 73. Health Interface

Health owns illness, injury, mortality, food and water safety, care, disaster, burial, and recovery. Siege authority owns operational causes, exposure, evacuation, casualty flow, and access constraints.

## 74. Population Interface

Population owns people, households, labor, migration, displacement, dependents, and settlement capacity. Siege authority consumes actual people and creates no defenders, workers, engineers, or civilians.

## 75. Military Interface

Military owners control strategy, command, forces, recruitment, combat, readiness, relief, and occupation priorities. Siege authority owns the specialized siege-operation and engineering framework.

## 76. Law and Governance Interfaces

Law owns rights, custody, property claims, prisoners, capitulation disputes, remedies, enforcement, and appeal. Governance owns mandates, appointments, emergency authority, settlement administration, and restoration of public authority.

## 77. Diplomacy Interface

Diplomacy owns safe conduct, external relief, surrender guarantees, prisoner negotiations, foreign specialists, and treaty consequences. Siege pressure does not create foreign consent.

## 78. Information Interface

Information owns scouts, reports, spies, messengers, secrecy, deception, provenance, and confidence. Siege authority consumes bounded intelligence without omniscience.

## 79. Event Interface

Events may surface warnings, engineering setbacks, rationing dilemmas, civilian petitions, negotiations, relief opportunities, betrayal claims, fires, disease reports, breaches, or capitulation proposals. Event definitions cannot manufacture siege state.
