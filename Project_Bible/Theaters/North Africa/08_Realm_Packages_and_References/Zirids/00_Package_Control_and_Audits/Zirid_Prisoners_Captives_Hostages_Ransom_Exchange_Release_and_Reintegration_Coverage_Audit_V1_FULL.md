## 1. Executive Verdict

The focused audit finds strong distributed Zirid coverage for legal authority, military capture context, diplomatic negotiation, health, property, public-order consequences, information, communication, built space, and release-related interfaces. That distributed coverage is not enough to own the complete person-specific custody chain. No verified current Zirid authority owns the continuous operating record from capture or detention through identity, classification, custodian, transfer, holding capability, support, health access, property custody, incidents, ransom, exchange, physical release, reception, and reintegration.

The developed-realm comparators demonstrate that this is a distinct operational lifecycle rather than a justice or diplomacy wrapper. The audit therefore confirms a substantive gap and recommends one dedicated Zirid custody and captivity authority. It rejects a generic prison-building authority, a generic penal-administration authority, and any opening prisoner roster or ransom tariff.
