## 1. Strong Historical-Systems Verdict

The Zirid package requires strong historical pressure without scripted historical imprisonment. January 1, 1062 begins with al-Mu'izz ibn Badis as the living ruler and Tamim ibn al-Mu'izz as a principal successor candidate whose January office, location, authority, and knowledge remain limited to owner-supported facts. The recorded succession later in 1062 is a major historical anchor, but it must occur only through a valid life-state transition and succession process.

Every later event is treated as historical memory, dated evidence, momentum, or a conditional emergence portfolio. No event is guaranteed merely because it occurred historically. The campaign may reproduce, delay, redirect, transform, or avoid recorded outcomes when world state, actors, resources, relationships, and choices differ. Nonoccurrence is an authored and valid result rather than a system failure.

## 41. Validation Catalogue

**Fatal:** future outcome becomes opening knowledge; historical date automatically kills, crowns, creates, spawns, defects, attacks, or intervenes; memory is universal; emergence creates missing actors or resources; urban or external private state is absorbed; nonoccurrence is overwritten; advanced save is forced back toward recorded history.

**Blocking:** evidence lacks assertion, source, date or window, confidence, owner, and opening disposition; momentum lacks drivers and counterforces; emergence lacks actors, world state, knowledge, resources, blockers, owners, alternatives, and nonoccurrence; succession skips life state, eligibility, recognition, and office handover.

**Advisory:** history is only a scripted event list; all later events are inevitable; divergence has no consequences; nonoccurrence is silent; AI uses historical hindsight; exact dates are fabricated for technical convenience.
