## 1. Strong Realm Verdict

The January 1062 Zirid public-order problem is the survival and renewal of a contracted Mahdia-centered government across plural urban, rural, coastal, inland, institutional, military, displaced, mercantile, maritime, and mobile constituencies. Order depends on protection, provisioning, lawful and intelligible demands, petition access, juristic and institutional mediation, credible records, restrained and accountable security conduct, directed relationships with urban and regional actors, and the ability to repair material and political damage.

Public order is never one happiness value. Mahdia's security does not establish the compliance of Kairouan, Tunis, Sfax, Gabes, Gafsa, Tripoli, countryside communities, maritime actors, or distributed Banu Hilal and Banu Sulaym constituencies. Likewise, a garrison may deter violence without producing legitimacy, a ritual may communicate recognition without producing consent, and fear may reduce visible resistance while preserving grievance and future relapse risk.
