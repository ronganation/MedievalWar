## 1. Strong Economic Verdict

The January 1062 Zirid economy is a contracted, network-dependent economy centered on Mahdia and its land and maritime provisioning needs. It retains meaningful urban, coastal, Mediterranean, inland, and Saharan-facing opportunities, but no generalized prosperity, fiscal abundance, secure trade flow, or uniform control is assumed. Every economic effect must be traced through property rights, people, production, stocks, markets, contracts, transport, custody, delivery, security, season, and receipt.

The player-facing economic problem is not simply to increase income. The problem is to preserve Mahdia, keep government and defense supplied, maintain credible custody and payment, reconnect or negotiate with urban markets, protect finite flows, and decide which economic relationships can be made durable without pretending that historical claims equal practical access.

## 44. Validation Catalogue

**Fatal:** historical prosperity creates opening wealth; property, output, stock, money, labor, transport, ships, trade, tax collection, or treasury balance is invented; the ruler owns public assets by profile; ports create fleets or customs; urban or external private state is absorbed; later outcomes are backdated; Opening State overwrites an advanced save.

**Blocking:** economic record lacks owner, custody, location, quantity or typed unknown, rights, state, evidence, or receipts; production lacks inputs and labor; transfer lacks source debit and destination credit; shipment lacks goods, carrier, route, security, payment, and acceptance; fiscal flow skips assessment, collection, remittance, or receipt.

**Advisory:** economy becomes one income number; prices are omniscient; scarcity ignores substitution and transport; military requisition has no civilian cost; project approval creates progress; failure has no recovery.
