## 1. Strong Population and Society Verdict

The January 1062 Zirid social world must be represented as a network of people, households, neighborhoods, urban institutions, rural communities, merchants, craftspeople, laborers, scholars, jurists, military households, mariners, migrants, displaced people, and mobile constituencies. The Realm population is not a passive tax total, a generic culture label, or a direct manpower pool.

Mahdia is the concentrated capital and refuge interface, but its exact population and composition remain unknown. Kairouan, Tunis, Sfax, Gabes, Gafsa, Tripoli, the countryside, coastal settlements, and distributed Banu Hilal and Banu Sulaym constituencies each retain separate demographic and social state. Political contraction and migration create pressure, opportunity, displacement, renegotiation, and recovery pathways. They do not prescribe universal collapse, depopulation, hostility, or cultural replacement.

**Blocking:** population record lacks identity or typed collective, place, date, count state, owner, and confidence; labor lacks people, availability, skill, time, tools, and competing demand; migration lacks origin, destination, route, access, dependants, property, and capacity; public response lacks affected constituency, cause, evidence, and channel.

**Advisory:** society becomes fixed cultural percentages; communities have one opinion; labor ignores care and subsistence; displacement ignores origin property and host-community cost; recruitment has no civilian consequence; recovery becomes an instant reset.
