## 1. Strong Geographic Verdict

The Zirid Realm at the January 1062 scenario anchor is geographically organized around a concentrated Mahdia capital and coastal survival system rather than a continuous, uniformly administered territorial block. Mahdia provides a capital, port, court, defense, communication, and provisioning interface. Kairouan remains a major former-capital, juristic, urban, and memory center whose exact opening relationship to the Zirids is unresolved. Tunis, Sfax, Gabes, Gafsa, Tripoli, and other centers must be represented as separately owned geographic and political interfaces, not anonymous provinces.

The defining strategic problem is connectivity. Coastal and maritime access may preserve communication, supply, diplomacy, and intervention when inland authority and routes are unreliable, but maritime access is itself dependent on ports, vessels, crews, weather, security, information, and external consent. Inland claims or historical rule do not guarantee secure movement, taxation, recruitment, or supply.
