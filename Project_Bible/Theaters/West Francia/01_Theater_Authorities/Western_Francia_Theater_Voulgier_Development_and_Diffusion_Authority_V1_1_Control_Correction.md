Voulgier development is authorized as a later West Francia theater capability family. France may become a primary native developer, but the capability is not an exclusive national possession and does not spread as an identical copied roster entry.

The capability must arise from real combinations of people, service institutions, workshops, weapons, officers, repeated military experience, political support, supply, and recovery. A ruler may inherit access to some of those assets through conquest or succession, but political control does not instantly create loyalty, organization, training, equipment, or field-ready formations.

## 3. Historical Evidence Boundary

### 3.1 Supported claims

Available external reference material supports the following bounded claims:

- the voulge or vouge is described as a medieval polearm associated especially with fifteenth-century France;
- infantry using the weapon are described as Voulgiers or Vougiers;
- the weapon is associated in reference material and period imagery with francs-archers;
- period-linked discussion distinguishes the voulge from a later erroneous nineteenth-century classification that treated an early halberd form as a voulge;
- a cited Burgundian expenditure record dated 13 November 1474 refers to the manufacture and delivery of 200 vouges and demand from English and Lombard buyers.

### 3.2 Claims not established by the current evidence

The current evidence does not by itself establish:

- one standardized French Voulgier corps;
- one continuous institutional line from every Guisarmier formation;
- universal use by all francs-archers;
- one exact weapon geometry across all regions;
- one exclusive French royal ownership model;
- one uniform relationship between France, Burgundy, Normandy, England, and other realms;
- exact recruitment numbers, costs, training periods, or battle performance;
- exact first appearance in every campaign timeline.

### 3.3 Status of the user's forthcoming webpage

The user has identified a separate webpage for later review. That page is not yet incorporated. No claim in this V1 authority depends upon content that has not been supplied and inspected.

```text
Capability emerges from people + source obligation + workshops + weapons + leaders
```

Voulgier capability is developmental rather than date-triggered. The player or AI must create and preserve the conditions that make the formation sustainable.

## 14. Political Inheritance and Conquest

A conqueror or successor may inherit control over territory containing:

- surviving population sources;
- existing Voulgier formations;
- workshops and weapon stocks;
- officers and veteran cadres;
- local service obligations;
- muster sites and routes;
- political resistance, divided loyalty, or cooperation;
- damaged, intact, or collapsed institutions.

The conqueror does not automatically inherit:

- willing service;
- legal clarity;
- trusted command;
- intact records;
- replenishment;
- replacement equipment;
- institutional continuity;
- a native version of the conquering realm's roster card.

## 50. Source and Claim Ledger

### 50.1 External references reviewed

- John Waldman, *Hafted Weapons in Medieval and Renaissance Europe: The Evolution of European Staff Weapons between 1200 and 1650*. Used through reference summaries for broad voulge morphology, later medieval French association, and classification cautions.
- French-language “Vouge (arme)” reference page. Used for the working claims that the weapon is associated primarily with fifteenth-century France, its infantry users are called Vougiers or Voulgiers, it is associated with francs-archers, and a Burgundian 1474 expenditure record concerns vouges.
- “Franc-archer” reference summaries. Used only to establish that francs-archers were a French royal militia institution created in 1448 and were not a timeless universal communal infantry category.
- Ian Heath, “Franc-Archer,” *Armies of the Middle Ages, Volume 1*, as reproduced in an online reference excerpt. Used for a bounded equipment and institutional comparator, not as proof that every franc-archer was a Voulgier.
