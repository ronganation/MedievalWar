## 1. Concise Verdict

Aquitaine in January 1062 is not one people, one language, one customary community, one population pool, or one body of subjects directly available to the duke. The principality connects Poitou, Aquitaine, and Gascony through one ruler while preserving different lordships, urban and rural communities, ecclesiastical jurisdictions, coastal and river populations, mountain approaches, household networks, labor systems, military obligations, and local identities.

The central community game is **cooperation across a composite principality**. William VIII can possess recognized titles without possessing direct demographic, fiscal, military, or institutional access to every community associated with those titles. Practical capacity depends on local authority, accepted burdens, household resilience, routes, property, markets, institutions, security, and the willingness or obligation of people to perform specific work.

The authority therefore supports readable Total War decisions about taxation, service, recruitment, transport, construction, billeting, relief, occupation, and regional integration without turning population into one manpower currency or culture into a passive percentage map.

## 8. Aquitaine Community Thesis

Aquitaine's population must be understood through several overlapping but non-identical worlds: the Poitevin ducal center, northern and eastern magnate lordships, Atlantic and Charente communities, Bordeaux and the Garonne system, varied Gascon lordships, interior Limousin and Périgord communities, ecclesiastical institutions, and Pyrenean approaches.

The practical opening problem is not whether William VIII possesses enough abstract population. The problem is whether the duke can obtain specific cooperation, labor, expertise, transport, service, provisions, information, recruits, and institutional support without exhausting the households and local systems that generate them.

## 18. Rural Communities

Rural communities may include cultivators, tenants, dependents, free or customary households, pastoral households, woodland workers, mill and processing dependents, cart and draft-animal households, riverbank communities, local guides, and route-maintenance labor.

Possible contributions include food, dues, labor, carts, animals, lodging, guidance, intelligence, local defense, and legally or customarily bounded service. Each contribution requires a valid basis and must compete with household subsistence and recovery.

Potential vulnerabilities include loss of seed, draft animals, fodder, tools, transport, shelter, access, and labor through requisition, billeting, recruitment, route damage, siege, raid, weather, disease, or private violence.

## 21. Merchant, Marine, and Transport Communities

Merchants, brokers, boatmen, pilots, ferrymen, handlers, carters, pack-animal owners, lodging providers, and market intermediaries may make Aquitaine's Atlantic and river geography usable. Their interests do not equal ducal loyalty.

Forced loans, arbitrary seizure, unsafe roads, denied passage, dishonored payment, piracy, private violence, and repeated requisition can reduce future capacity. Maritime identity does not create a fleet, and merchant access does not create ducal ownership of goods, vessels, ports, or crews.

## 23. Ecclesiastical Communities and Institutions

Aquitaine's ecclesiastical communities may provide worship, records, literacy, education, hospitality, relief, care, sanctuary, mediation, burial, memory, estate administration, storage, and local representation. Exact people, property, stores, jurisdictions, and officeholders remain with their owners.

Poitiers, Bordeaux, Auch, Bazas, and other reviewed institutions may possess different local communities and capacities. Institutional property is not ducal property. Patronage is not control. A bishop, abbot, chapter, monastery, religious community, building, estate, and jurisdiction remain separate records.

Routine clergy remain officeholders or agents unless a named person's repeated military or governing agency justifies a full Grand Campaign profile.

## 27. Labor, Burden, and Opportunity Cost

The same households, animals, carts, boats, tools, stores, and working days cannot support maximum taxation, maximum recruitment, maximum construction, maximum transport, maximum billeting, maximum route repair, and maximum relief simultaneously.

Aquitaine's composite structure makes this competition regional. Taking labor from Poitou cannot be assumed to satisfy a Gascon obligation. Requisitioning maritime transport near Bordeaux does not create cart capacity near Poitiers. Recruiting from one lordship can weaken its local defense, harvest, household continuity, and future replenishment.

## 29. Community Response Portfolio

Communities may respond through compliance, negotiation, petition, request for compensation, delayed contribution, partial service, refusal of one demand, concealment of assets, appeal to a lord or institution, relocation of activity, temporary flight, passive obstruction, organized resistance, or support for another political actor.

Strain does not automatically create rebellion. Resistance requires organizers, communication, leadership, capacity, opportunity, and a political container. Cooperation with one demand does not create general loyalty.

## 31. Military Service and Local Defense

Military service may arise from title, lordship, household, contract, custom, negotiation, emergency, community defense, or mercenary agreement. This authority supplies only population and community capacity inputs.

A service obligation does not establish receipt of a summons, acceptance, muster, arrival, equipment, cohesion, command, supply, or campaign endurance. Pulling people from local defense creates a visible tradeoff for castles, roads, markets, institutions, households, and harvests.

## 38. Culture and Language Change

Culture and language change must arise through movement, household formation, settlement, institutional continuity, trade, service, education, conquest, displacement, intermarriage, patronage, repression, accommodation, and generational transmission through their proper owners.

No turn tick converts a region automatically. No occupation instantly changes identity. No language difference creates hostility. Cultural continuity can survive political conquest, while political loyalty can change without cultural replacement.

## 39. Institutional Capacity and Social Continuity

Institutions can preserve records, training, relief, memory, hospitality, legitimacy, local mediation, and specialized labor after political disruption. Institutional continuity still depends on people, property, leadership, routes, security, and supplies.

An institution can survive while a building is damaged. A building can survive while its community disperses. A new officeholder does not automatically recover the former institution's knowledge, relationships, stores, or local trust.
