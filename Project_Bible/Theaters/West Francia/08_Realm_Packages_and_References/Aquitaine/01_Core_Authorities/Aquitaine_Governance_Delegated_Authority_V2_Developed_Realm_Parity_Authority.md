# 1. Verdict

Aquitaine should use a negotiated territorial-principality model with a strong Poitevin center, distinct title jurisdictions, regionally mediated authority, and explicit delegated powers.

The governance challenge is not to maximize one authority meter. The challenge is to convert recognized rank into usable action across several title channels and regions without losing control of the court, succession, military assembly, routes, or local cooperation.

Core statement:

> William VIII can claim attention across a vast principality, but governing it requires presence, recognized intermediaries, bounded powers, and repeated negotiation.

# 23. Validation Contract

## 23.1 Ownership failures

Hard fail if:

- governance owns a person, title, relationship, army, or church office;
- Realm Personality mutates governance;
- a realm-specific subsystem duplicates shared authority;
- vassalage and delegation become one record.

## 23.2 Delegation failures

Hard fail if a delegation lacks:

- canonical delegator;
- canonical delegate;
- jurisdiction;
- powers;
- prohibited powers;
- duration or review condition;
- reporting;
- revocation;
- vacancy behavior;
- succession behavior.

Hard fail if delegation creates loyalty, competence, knowledge, title, or hereditary authority automatically.

## 23.3 Chronology and identity failures

Hard fail if:

- a review person holds an active delegation;
- William IX or a later spouse enters opening governance;
- a deceased brother is restored as delegate or successor;
- an unresolved office holder becomes canonical;
- an external lock receives Aquitaine authority.

## 23.4 Territorial failures

Hard fail if:

- title extent creates direct administration;
- one generic Gascon governor replaces several jurisdictions;
- Bordeaux becomes capital automatically;
- a review site receives active jurisdiction;
- regional custom collapses into one integration score.

## 23.5 Network failures

Hard fail if:

- access creates loyalty;
- a relationship creates delegation;
- reverse edges are automatic;
- review relationships enter runtime;
- governance changes the network directly.

## 23.6 Barbastro failures

Hard fail if:

- expedition participation creates a permanent office;
- military command and home delegation become one power;
- ducal absence guarantees rebellion;
- papal endorsement creates Aquitaine law;
- occupation rights are predetermined.

## 23.7 Warnings

Warn if:

- no named household officer exists at runtime-authoring stage;
- no Gascon governing actor is resolved;
- Poitiers or Bordeaux site IDs remain proposed;
- delegation powers are too broad;
- player workload requires routine petition clicks;
- governance depends on one unexplained score;
- save and migration behavior is absent.

## 23.8 Determinism

Process governance records by:

```text

PowerType

```

Equal state, content, versions, input, and seed must produce equal validation, evaluation, command proposals, traces, and hashes.

- Saves store canonical IDs and active delegation records.
- Opening defaults apply once.
- Reloading does not restore revoked powers or old officers.
- Review IDs cannot become active delegates.

- Delegation schema changes require explicit power disposition.
- A title succession does not silently preserve personal delegations.
- Vacancy, death, removal, and incapacity trigger defined review rules.
- Later source corrections do not overwrite progressed government state.
