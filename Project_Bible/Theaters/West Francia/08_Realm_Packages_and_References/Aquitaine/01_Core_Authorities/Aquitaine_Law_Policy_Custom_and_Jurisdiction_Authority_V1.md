## 1. Concise Verdict

Implement Aquitaine law as a plural, negotiated, and territorially uneven order in which ducal authority, title-specific jurisdiction, local custom, lordship, property, settlement practice, household authority, and ecclesiastical competence overlap without becoming one centralized legal system.

Aquitaine may summon, hear, mediate, confirm, negotiate, protect, and judge where valid authority and practical access exist. A valid judgment still requires notice, evidence, accepted venue, communication, officers, records, local cooperation, property or military access, and a credible enforcement path.

The governing campaign problem is:

> Convert recognized ducal and title-specific authority into credible process, workable settlement, and enforceable performance across Poitou, Aquitaine, and Gascony without erasing regional custom or turning every dispute into a ducal court case.

### 6.1 Ducal and title-specific jurisdiction

William VIII may possess recognized authority connected to Aquitaine, Gascony, and Poitiers. The three channels remain separate for jurisdiction, service, custom, property, revenue, recognition, and succession.

```text
One holder != one jurisdiction
Ducal hearing != direct administration
Royal recognition != local enforcement
```

### 6.2 Direct property and officer jurisdiction

Where ducal property, officers, custodians, accounts, stores, or household resources are validated, the appropriate owner may support review of possession, collection, custody, performance, negligence, loss, and reporting.

The title itself does not prove the property.

### 6.3 Magnate and lordship jurisdiction

Aimery IV of Thouars, Hugh VI of Lusignan, Bernard II Tumapaler, and other validated lords may hold separate lordship, household, service, defense, property, and local-court interfaces. Their profiles do not by themselves activate a jurisdiction or grant the duke direct administrative control.

### 6.4 Settlement and market jurisdiction

Settlements may possess practices involving:

- local peace and order;
- markets and exchange;
- tolls and fees;
- weights and measures;
- property boundaries;
- debt and performance;
- lodging and transport;
- craft and merchant disputes;
- records and witnesses.

A named settlement does not automatically possess a commune, municipal sovereignty, or later institutional form.

### 6.5 Household and dynastic jurisdiction

Household and dynasty matters may involve:

- marriage;
- separation or dissolution;
- child residence;
- custody;
- guardianship;
- education;
- support;
- dower or marriage property;
- succession evidence;
- household access;
- representation during absence.

```text
Spouse != Regent
Parent != Exclusive Custodian
Protection != Guardianship
Guardianship != Government
Dynasty Membership != Title Eligibility
```

### 6.6 Ecclesiastical jurisdiction

Ecclesiastical owners retain competence over their institutions, offices, internal discipline, marriage processes, sanctuary, spiritual sanctions, and other validated subjects.

```text
Patronage != Ownership
Protection != Appointment
Ducal Interest != Canonical Authority
Papal Standing != Diplomatic Reliability
Excommunication != Automatic Deposition
```

## 7. Regional Legal Portfolios

### 7.1 Poitiers and central Poitou

Poitiers is the strongest candidate center for ducal hearings, records, household business, officer review, petitions, settlement, ecclesiastical consultation, and title-specific government.

Poitiers does not create universal competence over every Poitevin lordship or Gascon matter.

### 7.2 Thouars and northern Poitou

The Thouars portfolio may include local lordship, service, castle, route, market, property, and military-contribution questions. Aimery's service to William requires a specific basis, objective, duration, command arrangement, reward, reporting duty, and termination.

Refusal or negotiation does not automatically constitute rebellion.

### 7.3 Lusignan and eastern Poitou

The Lusignan portfolio may include castle access, local defense, property, roads, institutional relationships, service, and mediation. Later La Marche possession and later ecclesiastical conflict are excluded from the opening unless their owners create valid campaign records.

### 7.4 Saintonge, Charente, and Angoumois

These regions may involve river transfer, tolls, ferries, markets, storage, cultivation, route labor, local lordship, and ecclesiastical interests. The corridor is not one legal district merely because it is strategically connected.

### 7.5 Limousin and Perigord

These regions retain local lordship, castle, property, institutional, route, and customary variation. Aquitaine's title geography does not erase regional competence or convert every conflict into ducal litigation.

### 7.6 Bordeaux, Gironde, and Garonne

Bordeaux may support metropolitan, commercial, river, estuary, property, market, toll, merchant, transport, and Gascon-facing legal interfaces.

```text
Port importance != ducal ownership
Market access != tax right
Metropolitan jurisdiction != secular control
River access != unrestricted passage
```

### 7.7 Gascony, Armagnac, Auch, Bazas, and Agen

Gascony remains legally and politically plural. William's ducal title does not create one Gascon court, one custom, one treasury, or one service law.

Bernard II Tumapaler's prior position may matter to memory, recognition, claims, relationships, and settlement. It does not create an opening claim, rebellion, army, or universal Gascon support.

### 7.8 Bayonne and the Pyrenean approaches

The southern portfolio may involve passage, guides, transport, tolls, market exchange, local security, ecclesiastical jurisdictions, and external agreements. One route agreement does not grant universal Iberian access.

### 7.9 Toulouse-facing southeast

Toulouse and other validated Regional Powers may possess recognized standing without requiring full playable-Realm depth.

```text
Regional Power != Rebel Faction
Nonplayable != Lawless
Recognized local authority != Full playable package
```
