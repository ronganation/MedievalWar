## 1. Verdict

Reserve a portfolio of bounded early campaign opportunities for Aquitaine without fabricating a January 1062 war, rebellion, claimant host, Papal request, fort, army, commander assignment, route, settlement, or title transfer.

Aquitaine should open with immediate strategic direction, but not a mandatory tutorial war. The player begins as William VIII, a ruler whose three title channels and broad recognized sphere exceed routinely usable authority. Early play should convert that scale into chosen capacity through relationships, title-specific recognition, lordship agreements, routes, finite recruitment, supply, institutions, and military performance.

The reservation protects early battle, consolidation, commander-development, diplomacy, political settlement, Atlantic development, Gascon reconciliation, and optional southern intervention as meaningful possibilities while leaving activation to current world state and owner commands.

## 4. Opening Strategic Proposition

Aquitaine's opening proposition is asymmetric strength:

- William VIII personally connects Aquitaine, Gascony, and Poitiers.
- Poitiers is the strongest political and practical heartland.
- Gascony has separate title history, recognition, lordship, and former-rival interfaces.
- direct ducal domain is narrower than broad map color suggests.
- Capetian hierarchy is recognized but operationally limited.
- dynastic continuity is insecure.
- north, southeast, Atlantic, and Pyrenean orientations compete for attention.
- recruitment and supply are finite and local.
- William's personal command can improve concentration while exposing government and household continuity.

The player should feel able to act immediately but unable to solve every problem at once.

## 8. Composite Principality Consolidation

Aquitaine, Gascony, and Poitiers share William VIII but retain distinct title histories, recognition communities, laws, lordships, properties, military obligations, and succession channels.

Possible approaches include:

- confirm existing local arrangements;
- negotiate more reliable service;
- strengthen Poitiers-centered coordination;
- establish bounded officers or itineraries;
- recognize Gascon autonomy in return for cooperation;
- mediate lordship disputes;
- seek hostages, guarantees, oaths, or records where valid;
- leave some questions unresolved;
- use force only after current resistance and capacity justify it.

This opportunity can generate major political play without a battle. It may also create a field engagement, siege, or occupation if current actors escalate.

## 30. Counter-Expansion and Coalition Response

A successful Aquitaine opening can change the regional balance. Rapid conquest, force concentration, broken agreements, claimant support, or military presence near borders may create coalition pressure.

Coalitions still require communication, leadership, compatible objectives, forces, routes, finance, supply, and consent. Shared fear creates an opportunity for cooperation, not guaranteed unity.

A coalition may include playable Realms, Major Regional Powers, minor factions, local lordships, claimant networks, or ecclesiastical interests through their owners.

## 31. Battle, Siege, Captivity, and Casualty Handoff

Battle and siege systems receive only validated forces, commanders, objectives, terrain, weather, reinforcement rules, service terms, retreat options, and supply state.

They return:

- objective result;
- casualties;
- wounded, missing, captured, deserted, released, or ransomed state;
- equipment and mount loss;
- cohesion and morale consequences;
- commander-performance evidence;
- occupation or withdrawal opportunity;
- unresolved pursuit or relief.

The opportunity must never equate participation with development, missing with dead, victory with title, or capture with permanent removal.

- create runtime opportunities from reservation definitions;

- replay opening defaults;
- backload later historical outcomes;
- merge opportunity, Momentum, and Emergence state;
