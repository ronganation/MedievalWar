## 3. Concise Verdict

Aimery IV should enter Aquitaine as a major Poitevin magnate candidate whose local lordship, regional access, independent military relevance, household interests, and capacity for service make Aimery useful to William VIII without making Aimery a routine subordinate. Aimery's opening value lies in the choice between cooperation and autonomy. Aimery can help connect the ducal center to northern and western Poitou, contribute or organize force, mediate among local interests, or later carry delegated command. Every service should involve scope, cost, recognition, risk, and return.

## 4. Core Character Thesis

Aimery's central tension is:

> **profitable service versus preserved independence**

Aimery is most compelling when neither loyal retainer nor automatic rebel. The viscount can support William VIII when service protects Thouars, rewards the household, expands reputation, settles disputes, or creates access to wider campaigns. Aimery can withhold, limit, redirect, or renegotiate when service threatens local defense, household continuity, resources, or political standing.

The profile must remain valid if Aimery supports William, opposes one ducal request, serves in Iberia, avoids Iberia, participates in a Norman expedition, changes alignment, focuses on Thouars, loses a campaign, or becomes more influential after success.

## 13. Personality and Decision Tendencies

### 13.1 Supported design values

Aimery is best interpreted as valuing:

- preservation and advancement of the Thouars lordship;
- recognition appropriate to rank and service;
- military opportunity with meaningful reward;
- freedom to negotiate scope and duration;
- household continuity and local defense;
- access to influential rulers and coalitions;
- clear compensation, booty, rights, or political return;
- flexibility when wider alignments change;
- reputation built through visible performance;
- the ability to return home rather than be absorbed permanently by an external campaign.

These are gameplay tendencies inferred from strategic role and reported campaign range, not claims about private psychology.

### 13.2 Restraints and aversions

Aimery is likely to resist:

- unlimited ducal claims over local capacity;
- vague service without reward or duration;
- command without authority or supply;
- absence that leaves Thouars exposed;
- another magnate monopolizing access or prestige;
- punishment for refusing obligations not actually owed;
- permanent incorporation into another ruler's household;
- occupation burdens without local benefit;
- agreements that erase viscountial identity.

### 13.3 Decision tendency

Aimery should usually:

1. identify the effect on Thouars;
2. examine obligation, request, or opportunity;
3. estimate reward, prestige, access, and household benefit;
4. examine local defense and succession exposure;
5. test force, route, supply, duration, and command terms;
6. consider William VIII, Anjou, Normandy, Lusignan, church, and neighboring responses;
7. negotiate limits and compensation;
8. accept, narrow, delegate, delay, redirect, or decline;
9. convert results into household security or political leverage.

This remains conditional. Honor, piety, kinship, rivalry, threat, revenge, debt, opportunity, or misjudgment may alter the sequence.

### 13.4 Character risks

- appearing unreliable because alignments change;
- overvaluing immediate reward;
- accepting distant command while local security weakens;
- returning from success too powerful for the existing balance;
- provoking William VIII by treating service as pure transaction;
- provoking peers through rank, compensation, or command precedence;
- exposing household continuity;
- splitting a coalition through disputed leadership;
- using military prestige without sufficient administrative capacity.
