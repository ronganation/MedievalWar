# 1. Concise Verdict

Implement Aquitaine Historical Emergence as seven conditional situations that convert validated world conditions and Historical Momentum eligibility into strategic choices without forcing recorded history. The pathways cover composite-principality settlement, title-specific succession, Crown service or refusal, corridor assembly and access, Atlantic capacity, neighbor balance, and the optional Barbastro expedition.

Core rule:

**Momentum may make a pathway eligible. A valid actor and owner event activate it. Commands and affected owners create consequences. No date, pressure band, or historical expectation selects the result.**

# 7. Composite Principality Settlement

## 7.1 ID

`emergence.aquitaine.composite_principality_settlement`

## 7.2 Thesis

Aquitaine, Gascony, and Poitiers share a ruler but retain distinct title histories and political orders. Sustained pressure may create a strategic need to confirm, renegotiate, decentralize, or contest their working union.

## 7.3 Eligibility

- all three title identities and current holders resolve;
- `pressure.aquitaine.unfinished_union` is Eligible or stronger;
- a live governance, route, contribution, recognition, office, or lordship issue exists;
- at least one valid actor can issue or answer a proposal;
- affected regional and institutional channels can receive notice.

## 7.4 Activation

A validated assembly, mandate review, regional refusal, succession event, route crisis, accepted mediation request, or owner-issued settlement proposal creates the runtime instance.

## 7.5 Resolution families

- confirmed composite rule with title-specific procedures;
- negotiated regional autonomy;
- stronger itinerary and assembly coordination;
- bounded administrative consolidation;
- unresolved personal-union dependency;
- temporary regional noncompliance;
- peaceful title-specific separation after lawful succession;
- contested reordering routed through titles, claims, governance, and conflict owners.

## 7.6 Prohibitions

No automatic title merger, direct-domain conversion, cultural homogenization, permanent obedience, or collapse on William VIII's death.

# 8. Succession Settlement

## 8.1 ID

`emergence.aquitaine.succession_settlement`

## 8.2 Thesis

A continuity crisis may require separate but coordinated decisions for Aquitaine, Gascony, and Poitiers.

Eligibility requires a real lifecycle, marriage, parentage, claimant, vacancy, recognition, or incapacity condition; validated title-specific succession rules; and `pressure.aquitaine.succession_uncertainty` eligibility. Activation requires an owner event such as incapacity, death, lawful inquiry, candidate recognition request, disputed claim, or assembly summons.

Possible resolutions include recognized continuity, coordinated but title-specific succession, negotiated partition, collateral settlement, regency or delegated protection where owner systems permit it, contested succession, delayed recognition, or lawful vacancy handling.

No invented spouse, child, heir, claimant, primogeniture rule, later marriage, William IX, or Eleanor leakage is permitted.

# 9. Crown Service or Refusal

## 9.1 ID

`emergence.aquitaine.crown_service_or_refusal`

## 9.2 Thesis

A valid French Crown request or shared issue may require Aquitaine to recognize, negotiate, substitute, defer, refuse, or contest service without presuming rebellion or submission.

Eligibility requires a France-owned summons, mediation request, church issue, title dispute, security concern, or diplomatic proposal; valid communication; an authorized Aquitaine actor; and exposed France compatibility interfaces. Activation occurs only when the request is delivered or Aquitaine initiates a recognized negotiation.

Resolution families include ceremonial recognition, bounded service, payment or substitute aid, mediation, negotiated delay, lawful refusal, disputed refusal, reciprocal settlement, widening practical autonomy, or owner-resolved conflict.

France owns Philip I, Crown authority, summons state, French claims, reverse relationships, and French consequences. Aquitaine cannot write them.

# 10. Corridor Assembly and Access

## 10.1 ID

`emergence.aquitaine.corridor_assembly_and_access`

## 10.2 Thesis

A live Charente-Garonne access, lordship, security, or contribution problem may justify a bounded assembly or negotiated access settlement.

Eligibility requires validated jurisdiction and purpose, an authorized convener, a viable location, valid invitations, travel and communication access, and a live dispute or decision. Momentum may reference `pressure.aquitaine.charente_garonne_corridor` and `pressure.aquitaine.unfinished_union`.

Possible outcomes include an executable access agreement, limited attendance, contribution settlement, route protection mandate, mediated lordship settlement, visible refusal, rival gathering, adjournment, or failed assembly exposing weak capacity.

The pathway cannot create attendees, roads, bridges, safe passage, direct domain, permanent consent, or a realm-wide parliament.

# 11. Atlantic Capacity Transition

## 11.1 ID

`emergence.aquitaine.atlantic_capacity_transition`

## 11.2 Thesis

Validated coastal and estuarine opportunities may support a strategic transition from access to sustainable revenue, defense, or transport capacity.

Eligibility requires a real port, toll, property, merchant, route, security, treasury, or coastal-defense opportunity; an owner-authorized proposal; and `pressure.aquitaine.atlantic_revenue_conversion` eligibility. Activation requires an accepted investment, grant, protection, access, property, or administrative command.

Resolution families include durable bounded capacity, conditional commercial access, protected route or port arrangement, disputed toll or property settlement, costly overextension, stalled investment, local resistance, or loss of opportunity.

No synthetic wealth, port level, trade route, ship, fleet, garrison, market, or revenue modifier is created by Emergence.

# 12. Neighbor Balance Transition

## 12.1 ID

`emergence.aquitaine.neighbor_balance_transition`

Resolution families include reciprocal recognition, mediated settlement, access agreement, guarded neutrality, limited cooperation, failed negotiation, deterrent posture, bounded confrontation, or conflict executed through diplomacy, claim, military, route, and settlement owners.

No automatic hostility, alliance, marriage, inheritance, war, or merged Anjou-Toulouse behavior is permitted.

Aquitaine Historical Emergence turns accumulated pressures and owner-published circumstances into a small number of consequential campaign situations. Emergence does not exist to reward the player for reproducing later history. Emergence exists to expose choices whose participants, costs, information, geography, military feasibility, religious consequences, and political settlements are all current campaign facts.

```text
Historical possibility != scheduled event
Eligibility != player interruption
Player interruption != mandatory choice
Choice != guaranteed consequence
Military victory != political settlement
Refusal != failure
No event != missing content
```

The system must permit a successful Aquitaine campaign in which Barbastro never occurs, Bernard II never revolts, the three title channels remain loosely connected, the Atlantic remains underdeveloped, or relations with France, Anjou, and Toulouse follow paths unlike recorded history.
