Yes, Aquitaine should close the French Realm set, and it can be one of the strongest campaigns in the game. The key is to prevent it from becoming “southern France with more cavalry.” It should feel like a **vast, wealthy but incompletely integrated princely federation**, looking simultaneously toward Paris, the Atlantic, Gascony, and Iberia.

# Duchy of Aquitaine: Initial Faction Outline

## Verdict

**Implement as a major playable principality.**

Aquitaine’s central fantasy should be:

> **Rule more land than the King of the Franks can directly command, while possessing less reliable authority than your titles suggest.**

The duchy is formally within the French realm, but direct Capetian control in the eleventh century is limited. Large principalities such as Aquitaine operate with substantial practical independence, despite remaining within the hierarchy of the kingdom. citeturn1search22

Aquitaine therefore completes the French Realm by showing its opposite face:

- **France:** a relatively small royal domain trying to turn kingship into government.
- **Normandy:** a militarized principality with unusually concentrated ducal authority.
- **Anjou:** an aggressive territorial machine built through castles, marriage, and opportunism.
- **Flanders:** commercially wealthy, urbanizing, and politically entangled with several powers.
- **Burgundy:** aristocratic, ecclesiastical, and caught between French and imperial worlds.
- **Aquitaine:** territorially enormous, culturally plural, diplomatically southern, and only partially governable.

# 1. Historical Opening Position

## Opening ruler

**<External>William VIII of Aquitaine</External>, born Guy-Geoffrey**

Opening titles:

- Duke of Aquitaine
- Duke of Gascony
- Count of Poitiers
- Senior prince of the Ramnulfid or Poitevin ducal house

He acquired Gascony in 1052 and succeeded as Duke of Aquitaine and Count of Poitiers in 1058. Thus, in the project’s 1061 to January 1062 opening, his combined dominion is comparatively recent rather than an ancient, perfectly unified territorial state. citeturn1search26turn1search30

That gives us an excellent Opening State:

> <External>William VIII</External> has accumulated several powerful titles, but the political integration of those titles remains unfinished.

This is much better than depicting Aquitaine as a single solid block.

## Opening dynastic problem

<External>William VIII</External> is married to Matoeda or Mathilde in the opening period, but his eventual male heir, the future <External>William IX</External>, has not yet been born. The duke’s later marital and legitimacy problems belong to future emergence, not to the opening facts. citeturn1search26

Therefore:

- Do **not** script the later divorce.
- Do **not** place <External>William IX</External> in the opening dynasty.
- Do **not** assume the eventual succession.
- Do expose a real **continuity problem**:
  - a powerful adult duke,
  - insufficiently secure male succession,
  - collateral dynastic interests,
  - magnates calculating what happens if the combined titles separate.

This creates pressure without scripting history.

# 2. Faction Identity

## Core identity

**The Great Southern Principality**

Aquitaine should possess:

- immense geographic reach,
- several distinct political centers,
- strong agricultural and Atlantic wealth,
- significant noble military resources,
- weak administrative penetration outside the Poitevin heartland,
- dangerous internal distance,
- access to Iberian politics,
- limited reason to fear France immediately,
- many reasons to fear fragmentation.

Its strength is **scale**.

Its weakness is also **scale**.

## Campaign archetype

Aquitaine is not primarily an expansion faction at the opening. It is a **consolidation and orientation faction**.

The player decides what Aquitaine is becoming:

1. **A Poitevin-centered French principality**
2. **A trans-Garonne Aquitaine-Gascony union**
3. **An Atlantic commercial power**
4. **A Pyrenean and Iberian intervention power**
5. **A federated realm of negotiated noble liberties**
6. **A centralized ducal state**, at substantial political cost

These should remain strategic directions, not mutually exclusive scripted “paths.”

# 3. Geographic Structure

## Recommended high-level territorial model

Aquitaine must not receive direct ownership of every territory historically associated with the ducal title. Title extent, claimed superiority, practical authority, and direct territorial control should remain separate.

### A. Poitou: ducal core

**Principal center: Poitiers**

Poitiers should be the opening capital and strongest site of:

- household government,
- dynastic identity,
- direct revenues,
- ducal patronage,
- dependable military following,
- ecclesiastical relationships.

Poitiers was the ducal capital during this period. citeturn1search29

Possible campaign region:

- Poitou
- Settlement focus: Poitiers
- Secondary sites: Châtellerault, Niort, Lusignan or Thouars depending on map scale

**Gameplay:** the most governable portion of the faction.

### B. Saintonge and the Charente corridor

Possible campaign regions:

- Saintonge
- Angoumois, if map density permits

Important centers:

- Saintes
- Angoulême
- coastal or river access through the Charente country

**Gameplay function:**

- connects Poitou to Bordeaux,
- creates a contested inland-coastal route,
- gives local lords leverage over ducal movement,
- supports salt, wine, riverine trade, and ports.

### C. Bordeaux and western Gascony

Important centers:

- Bordeaux
- Dax
- Bayonne, depending on opening authority and map scale

Bordeaux should be commercially important but must not replace Poitiers as the political capital in 1062. The historical duchy’s center shifted toward Bordeaux only later as its territorial shape changed. citeturn1search29

**Gameplay distinction:**

- Poitiers generates political authority.
- Bordeaux generates trade potential and Atlantic reach.
- Gascony generates military mobility, difficult terrain, and regional autonomy.

### D. Gascon south

Gascony should not be treated as ordinary Aquitanian land merely because <External>William VIII</External> holds the ducal title.

Recommended treatment:

- some territory under direct or strong ducal influence,
- some governed through local counts, viscounts, bishops, and castellans,
- substantial linguistic and aristocratic regional identity,
- Pyrenean routes that matter strategically,
- difficult enforcement outside major corridors.

This becomes the testing ground for whether the combined Aquitaine-Gascony rulership survives.

### E. Eastern and southeastern relationships

Périgord, Limousin, La Marche, Auvergne, and neighboring counties require careful title-by-title research. They should not automatically be painted as directly owned Aquitanian provinces.

Preferred model:

- independent or semi-independent lords,
- some owing varying forms of recognition or service,
- some holding dynastic connections,
- some acknowledging the duke only situationally,
- some effectively outside ducal command.

The duchy’s historical boundaries fluctuated markedly, which strongly supports a layered authority model rather than a single territorial block. citeturn1search27

# 4. Opening Territorial Principle

I recommend a three-layer map interpretation.

## Layer 1: Direct ducal domain

Territories, settlements, revenues, and garrisons directly administered for the duke.

Likely centered upon:

- Poitiers,
- selected Poitevin estates,
- important Gascon or Bordelais holdings justified by opening research.

## Layer 2: Ducal vassalage

Lords who recognize the duke but retain:

- their own courts,
- military followings,
- revenues,
- castle networks,
- local relationships.

The duke may request or demand service, but does not directly operate their holdings.

## Layer 3: Claimed or customary superiority

Areas in which the ducal title carries:

- prestige,
- ceremonial precedence,
- historical claims,
- disputed rights,
- intermittent service.

This does not equal direct ownership and should not provide ordinary province income.

That distinction is crucial. Aquitaine is the ideal faction for teaching players that:

> **A title can be enormous while the ruler’s usable power remains geographically narrow.**

# 5. Government and Internal Politics

## Government model

**Negotiated territorial principality**

Aquitaine should not receive a unique government framework. Existing governance systems should express it through faction content and Opening State records.

### Principal governing problems

- Several titles joined in one person
- Long travel distances
- Uneven ducal presence
- Powerful local castellans
- Ecclesiastical landholders
- Regional legal customs
- Independent noble warfare
- Weak routine extraction
- Dependence upon household agents
- Uncertain succession

## Ducal progress

The duke’s physical presence should matter, but without creating a constant travel-click mechanic.

The ruler’s location could influence:

- petition resolution,
- local compliance,
- noble attendance,
- judicial credibility,
- military assembly,
- settlement disputes.

The player should issue broad itinerary or regional-priority commands, not manually move through every holding on administrative errands.

Possible policy choices:

- Remain at Poitiers and strengthen the heartland
- Hold court at Bordeaux
- Conduct a Gascon progress
- Attend a frontier assembly
- Visit an abbey or episcopal center
- Muster for a southern expedition

The runtime then resolves political effects through existing governance, character, network, and authority systems.

## Delegated authority

Aquitaine should rely heavily upon:

- castellans,
- viscounts,
- seneschal-like household officers where historically appropriate,
- bishops and abbots,
- comital vassals,
- ducal kin,
- regional military deputies.

Each delegate receives explicit powers. Appointment does not make the agent loyal, competent, or locally accepted.

# 6. Dynasty and Succession

## Opening dynastic tension

The ruler is powerful, but the future of his titles is not secure.

The combined titles must have separate succession evaluation:

- Duchy of Aquitaine
- Duchy of Gascony
- County of Poitiers
- direct estates
- household offices
- claims and customary rights

A succession crisis should not merely mean “the same heir receives all territories with reduced public order.”

Instead, magnates may disagree over:

- who is eligible,
- whether each title follows the same successor,
- whether Gascony remains united with Aquitaine,
- whether a daughter, collateral male, or disputed offspring has rights,
- who controls a minor or politically weak successor,
- whether a regency is recognized regionally.

This uses Dynasty Succession, Founder Dependency, Underage, Claims, Governance, Character Networks, and Delegated Authority. It does **not** justify a new Aquitaine Succession Framework.

## Founder dependency

Although <External>William VIII</External> did not found Aquitaine, the **current union of Aquitaine, Poitou, and Gascony** is unusually dependent upon his person.

That dependency should be represented as a scenario condition:

- the component territories have not yet normalized joint rule,
- many obligations are personally mediated,
- the next succession tests whether the union is institutional or merely personal.

This is an ideal Founder Dependency-style case without falsely calling the duke the founder of the historic polity.

# 7. External Strategy

## A. The French crown

Aquitaine formally belongs within the French realm, but the Capetian king should feel distant.

The relationship should involve:

- formal homage or feudal recognition,
- prestige obligations,
- court summons,
- marriage diplomacy,
- royal arbitration,
- possible competition over ecclesiastical influence.

The French king should not have routine command of Aquitanian armies or income.

For Aquitaine, the crown is initially:

- a source of legitimacy,
- a possible arbiter,
- a diplomatic relationship,
- a long-term constitutional danger,
- not the primary opening military threat.

## B. Anjou and the Loire approaches

Anjou should be the most immediate northern political competitor.

Points of friction:

- influence in western France,
- contested castles and vassals,
- Poitevin border pressure,
- marriage networks,
- control of routes toward Tours and the lower Loire.

This rivalry gives Aquitaine a grounded northern campaign without forcing war with France.

## C. Toulouse

Toulouse should be the principal southeastern rival and alternative center of southern power.

Conflict should concern:

- influence rather than simple border conquest,
- marcher lordships,
- ecclesiastical connections,
- routes toward the Mediterranean,
- claims and inherited rights,
- alliances among southern counts.

## D. Iberian kingdoms

This is Aquitaine’s strongest differentiator.

<External>William VIII</External> later participated in the 1064 expedition against Barbastro, leading a major Aquitanian contingent. The campaign was associated with papal encouragement, although its precise status as an early crusade remains debated. citeturn1search50

Because 1064 is after the opening:

### Opening State may contain

- knowledge of opportunities beyond the Pyrenees,
- relationships with Iberian courts,
- religious and military pressure encouraging intervention,
- accessible Pyrenean routes,
- nobles interested in prestige and booty,
- potential papal diplomatic appeals.

### Opening State must not contain

- a predetermined Barbastro expedition,
- a guaranteed papal command,
- a scripted victory,
- guaranteed participation by <External>William VIII</External>,
- later marriage alliances presented as already settled,
- “crusader” identity as an accomplished faction condition.

The 1064 expedition should be **plausible emergence**, not a mandatory mission.

## E. Normandy and England

In 1062, Aquitaine should not behave as if the later Angevin or Plantagenet geopolitical world already exists.

The Norman conquest of England has not happened at the opening. Aquitaine may know Normandy as a formidable northern principality, but England should not yet dominate Aquitanian planning.

This avoids retroactively designing the faction around twelfth-century outcomes.

# 8. Economy and Logistics

## Economic identity

Aquitaine should be potentially wealthy, but its wealth should be difficult to convert into immediate ducal cash.

### Key economic pillars

- wine-producing districts
- Atlantic and river trade
- salt and coastal resources
- livestock and pastoral economies
- agricultural estates
- tolls along rivers and roads
- pilgrimage movement
- urban and ecclesiastical markets

## Strategic economic tension

The problem is not “Aquitaine is poor.”

The problem is:

> Local prosperity does not automatically become ducal revenue.

The duke needs:

- recognized toll rights,
- cooperative towns,
- safe river corridors,
- compliant castellans,
- functioning officials,
- secure ports,
- negotiated extraordinary levies.

Aquitaine can have a high regional economy and a mediocre ducal treasury simultaneously.

## Trade orientation

The player should choose investments among:

- Loire and northern French connections
- Atlantic shipping
- Bordeaux-centered export
- Pyrenean routes
- Iberian commerce
- internal river and road integration

Trade growth creates strategic vulnerability:

- coastal raids,
- toll disputes,
- blocked river crossings,
- castellans extracting unauthorized charges,
- neighboring rulers competing for merchant routes.

# 9. Recruitment and Military Character

## General military identity

Aquitaine should field a **broad but uneven feudal host**.

Strengths:

- numerous noble cavalry contingents
- strong household and comital mounted elites
- good regional manpower
- adaptable southern light troops
- access to Gascon and Pyrenean soldiers
- capacity to assemble a major expedition when politics align

Weaknesses:

- slow concentration
- inconsistent vassal attendance
- limited service duration
- regional contingents with different loyalties
- difficulty replacing losses far from home
- unreliable unity during succession or internal revolt

## Suggested roster direction

### Ducal and Poitevin core

- Ducal household knights
- Poitevin knights
- mounted sergeants
- castle sergeants
- urban or estate spearmen
- crossbowmen where chronology and regional evidence permit

### Gascon contingents

- Gascon cavalry or mounted retainers
- light spearmen
- javelin troops
- mobile frontier infantry
- Pyrenean scouts

The exact composition needs later military-source validation. “Gascon” must not become a universal bonus adjective.

### Feudal contingents

Vassal troops should arrive as attributed forces:

- tied to a lord or institution,
- limited by service terms,
- affected by loyalty and readiness,
- potentially withdrawn or withheld,
- not instantly absorbed into a permanent national army.

## Battlefield style

Aquitaine should excel when:

- its host has assembled successfully,
- cavalry has room to operate,
- allied contingents are coordinated,
- the duke possesses prestige,
- the campaign promises recognizable benefits.

It should struggle when:

- campaigning through difficult terrain without preparation,
- besieging multiple castles simultaneously,
- fighting after vassal service expires,
- internal loyalty is divided,
- a distant expedition loses its logistical route.

# 10. Ecclesiastical Character

Aquitaine requires a strong ecclesiastical layer because churches and monasteries are:

- landholders,
- diplomatic intermediaries,
- legitimacy sources,
- patrons of written administration,
- political actors,
- potential opponents of ducal marriage or succession choices.

The duke’s later religious patronage and dealings with Rome indicate fertile design material, but later events must remain future emergence. citeturn1search26

Opening gameplay should involve:

- relations with major bishops,
- monastic patronage,
- disputed appointments where appropriate,
- protection of church routes and estates,
- requests for arbitration,
- ecclesiastical support for southern intervention,
- canonical scrutiny of dynastic marriages.

Church relations should affect specific rights and actors, not produce a single universal “Church Approval” score.

# 11. Faction Personality and AI

## Suggested realm personality

**Proud, expansive, negotiated, southern-facing**

This personality explains preferences; it does not force behavior.

### AI priorities

1. Preserve the union of Poitou, Aquitaine, and Gascony
2. Maintain Poitiers as an effective political center
3. Prevent hostile control of the Charente-Garonne corridor
4. Balance major vassals rather than attacking all of them
5. Seek dynastic security
6. Expand influence toward Iberia and Toulouse when internal stability permits
7. Avoid unnecessary submission to Capetian administration
8. Exploit Atlantic commercial opportunities
9. Prefer prestige campaigns with bounded objectives over total conquest

### AI caution

Aquitaine should not launch the Barbastro campaign simply because the calendar reaches 1064.

The AI evaluates:

- papal request or encouragement,
- available treasury,
- ducal prestige,
- Pyrenean access,
- vassal willingness,
- Iberian relationships,
- internal stability,
- expected rewards,
- risks of northern opportunism.

It then proposes a command. The appropriate owning systems validate and execute it.

# 12. Player Experience

## Opening strengths

- Large recognized political sphere
- Prestigious ruler
- Strong mounted elite
- Several economic regions
- Atlantic and Iberian access
- Considerable diplomatic weight
- Few immediate existential enemies
- Potential to become the predominant western Frankish prince

## Opening weaknesses

- Insecure dynastic future
- Recently combined territorial titles
- Weak uniform administration
- Distant Gascon territories
- Powerful local lords
- Low conversion of nominal territory into usable revenue
- Long military assembly times
- Multiple strategic orientations competing for resources
- Risk that succession separates the realm

## Recommended campaign difficulty

**Medium to Hard**

Aquitaine looks easy because it is large. Experienced players quickly discover that only part of that size is immediately usable.

That is the desired lesson.

# 13. Signature Strategic Pressures

These should be pressures, not quest chains.

## The Unfinished Union

Can one household turn Aquitaine, Poitou, and Gascony into a durable political union?

## The Empty Future

Who inherits if the duke’s current dynastic position fails to produce an accepted successor?

## The Distant King

Does Capetian recognition protect Aquitaine, constrain it, or provide rivals with an appellate authority?

## The Southern Road

Do Aquitanian knights seek land, wealth, piety, and reputation across the Pyrenees?

## The Lords Between Rivers

Can the duke secure movement from Poitiers to Bordeaux without destroying the local power structure on which his armies depend?

## The Atlantic Treasury

Can Bordeaux and the western ports finance ducal power without shifting the realm’s political center away from Poitiers?

# 14. Recommended Faction Hooks

These are not rigid missions.

### “Hold What You Inherited”

Reward the establishment of functioning authority across separate titles, not simple territorial occupation.

### “The Court at Poitiers”

Develop Poitiers as the realm’s administrative and dynastic anchor.

### “Beyond the Garonne”

Increase Gascon recognition through presence, negotiation, marriage, or military protection.

### “The Pyrenean Summons”

Allow an Iberian or papal opportunity to emerge if diplomatic and military conditions align.

### “Wine, Salt, and Tolls”

Integrate trade corridors while managing local rights.

### “One Heir, Several Titles”

Secure an accepted succession settlement, understanding that the settlement may differ across titles.

# 16. Principal Risks

## Risk 1: Painting the title instead of modeling authority

**Mitigation:** separate direct domain, recognized vassalage, and customary superiority.

## Risk 2: Designing from Eleanor and the Plantagenets backward

**Mitigation:** freeze faction facts at 1061 or January 1062. Later Angevin outcomes remain only distant possibilities.

## Risk 3: Treating Gascony as a cultural skin

**Mitigation:** give Gascony distinct networks, obligations, geography, military access, and recognition problems.

## Risk 4: Predetermining Barbastro

**Mitigation:** represent Iberian opportunity as pressure and emergence. Do not place the 1064 outcome into Opening States.

## Risk 5: Making internal management exhausting

**Mitigation:** the player sets regional priorities, delegates powers, conducts occasional progresses, and responds to major disputes. Routine administration resolves through simulation.

## Risk 6: Making Aquitaine overwhelmingly strong

**Mitigation:** constrain deployable power through assembly, authority, revenue access, travel, loyalty, and title separation rather than arbitrary penalties.

# Final Faction Statement

> **The Duchy of Aquitaine is the largest promise in the French realm and one of its least completed political constructions. Its duke commands prestige from the Loire approaches to the Pyrenees, but his usable power depends upon roads, courts, marriages, vassals, bishops, and the continued acceptance of several lands that are united more securely in his person than in their institutions.**

This makes Aquitaine an excellent final French faction because it demonstrates that the “French Realm” is not a centralized country. It is a hierarchy of competing princes, and Aquitaine may be the grandest prince of them all.

## Smallest useful next action

Create **Aquitaine Opening State V0.1** with only six bounded sections:

1. Canonical realm and title IDs  
2. <External>William VIII</External> and opening household  
3. Direct domain versus vassal and claimed territory  
4. Principal internal lords and ecclesiastical actors  
5. Opening diplomatic relationships  
6. Initial pressures, including succession, Gascon integration, Anjou, Toulouse, and the Iberian road  

That would give us the authoritative skeleton before we decide exact provinces, settlements, or units.
