## 1. Concise Verdict

**Implement Brittany’s opening political network as directed, typed, asymmetric relationships rather than one mutual opinion score.**

The January 1062 network begins after Conan II’s restoration and before the later Dol rebellion, Breton-Norman war, Dinan campaign, Angevin expedition, and 1066 succession. The Network therefore represents accumulated grievances, guarded cooperation, kinship-aware access, uncertain trust, political dependency, and frontier tension without scripting later hostility.

The opening network centers on eight principal person-to-person pairs and one person-to-institution relationship family:

```text
Conan -> Odo       Odo -> Conan
Conan -> Hawise    Hawise -> Conan
Hawise -> Hoël     Hoël -> Hawise
Conan -> Hoël      Hoël -> Conan
Conan -> Rivallon  Rivallon -> Conan
Odo -> Hawise      Hawise -> Odo
Odo -> Hoël        Hoël -> Odo
Rivallon -> Church of Dol
Church of Dol -> Rivallon
```

Each direction may separately contain:

- contact;
- knowledge;
- trust;
- respect;
- fear;
- hostility;
- obligation;
- grievance;
- dependency;
- influence access;
- communication access;
- confidence and evidence.

Kinship, marriage, title hierarchy, and shared realm membership provide context only. None creates loyalty or knowledge automatically.

## 3. Non-Negotiable Network Rules

```text
Relationships are directed unless explicitly defined as symmetric.
Contact is not trust.
Knowledge is not contact.
Kinship is not loyalty.
Marriage is not shared knowledge.
Obligation is not willingness.
Fear is not respect.
Hostility is not open rebellion.
Dependency is not ownership.
Influence access is not authority.
A remembered grievance is not a historical fact.
A relationship dimension is not a universal opinion score.
```

### 3.1 No reverse-value inference

If Conan distrusts Odo, the system does not infer that Odo distrusts Conan. The opposite direction requires its own record or explicit framework rule.

### 3.2 No dimension inference

If Odo fears Conan, the system does not infer respect, hostility, obedience, or loyalty. Each dimension remains separately authored or updated.

### 3.3 No automatic kinship seeding

Dynasty relationships may authorize a content template to propose contact or familiarity, but the Network owner validates every actual opening seed. A sibling can be estranged; an uncle can be feared; spouses can hold unequal knowledge.

## 6. Knowledge Contract

### 6.1 Knowledge is scoped

A character does not simply “know another character.” Knowledge records specify what is known, believed, rumored, or unknown.

```yaml
KnowledgeRecord:
  KnowledgeId: stable_record_id
  HolderEntityId: canonical_id
  SubjectRef: canonical_or_runtime_id
  KnowledgeType: identity | location | title | office | claim | plan | event | secret | rumor
  EpistemicState: known | believed | suspected | rumored | contradicted | unknown
  Confidence: bounded_state
  SourceRef: optional canonical_or_runtime_id
  AcquiredDate: optional date
  ExpiryOrReviewDate: optional date
  VisibilityPolicyRef: canonical_id
```

### 6.2 No omniscience

Marriage, kinship, shared household, and common realm do not grant complete plans, secrets, or current locations. Communication, access, observation, messengers, evidence, and memory must support knowledge.

### 6.3 Opening visibility

The player may receive public political knowledge through realm UI even when a specific character lacks private knowledge. Player information and character knowledge are separate presentation layers.

## 7. Opening Seed Method

### 7.1 Seed classes

```yaml
OpeningSeedClass:
  Confirmed: directly supported and high confidence
  StrongInference: follows from secure opening facts with limited alternatives
  BoundedInference: strategically useful but materially uncertain
  ReviewOnly: not loaded into authoritative Opening State
```

### 7.2 Conservative seeding rule

When evidence is sparse:

- seed contact only if interaction is necessary or strongly implied;
- avoid detailed affection or personality claims;
- use guarded or low-confidence states rather than invented precision;
- attach the causal historical or institutional reference;
- keep later hostility out of 1062;
- prefer a review record over a dramatic assumption.

### 7.3 No scripted emergence

Opening hostility must not be calibrated to guarantee the later historical war. Network state creates possible pressure, not a timer.

## 25. Decay, Persistence, and Reconciliation

### 25.1 Dimension behavior

- **Contact:** persists until access or location changes; may downgrade from direct to indirect.
- **Communication access:** changes with household, constraint, route, office, or policy state.
- **Knowledge:** persists unless corrected, forgotten by an approved model, or invalidated by time-sensitive content.
- **Trust:** changes slowly except after major betrayal or exceptional settlement.
- **Respect:** changes through observed performance or reputation, not mere title possession.
- **Fear:** responds to demonstrated capacity and proximity; may decay if threat becomes remote.
- **Hostility:** may decline after settlement but does not erase grievances automatically.
- **Obligation:** persists until fulfilled, released, invalidated, or superseded by owner command.
- **Grievance:** persists as a tagged cause; intensity may change separately if the framework supports it.
- **Dependency:** recalculates when alternative access, resources, or political structures change.
- **Influence access:** responds to contact, office, household, trust, and communication channels.

### 25.2 No global monthly decay

Reject a universal relationship-decay tick applied equally to every dimension. The three-track temporal contract requires cause-aware and dimension-specific processing; no relationship dimension uses a universal monthly decay tick.

### 25.3 Reconciliation package

A political settlement may coordinate multiple Network update requests, but each dimension remains separately validated. A settlement cannot simply set “relations = friendly.”

## 26. Visibility and UI Contract

### 26.1 Internal versus player-facing state

Deep Network simulation remains internal. The UI selectively exposes:

- known contact channels;
- major trust or hostility state;
- active grievances and obligations relevant to the decision;
- major dependencies;
- confidence and uncertainty;
- reasons an action is likely to be accepted or refused;
- whether information is public, private, suspected, or unknown.

### 26.2 No false precision

Do not present hidden values as `Trust: 63.7`. Preferred UI language:

- “Very low trust”
- “Guarded respect”
- “Constrained formal contact”
- “Depends on Penthièvre cooperation”
- “Grievance: loss of regency”
- “Confidence: moderate”

### 26.3 Character perspective

Where useful, the UI can show:

- the player realm’s best assessment;
- the acting character’s known state;
- uncertainty caused by incomplete information;
- publicly known kinship or titles from other authorities.

The UI must not reveal private plans merely because the simulation stores them.

### 26.4 Consequence preview

Before a major command, the UI may preview:

- likely relationship dimensions affected;
- known obligations or grievances invoked;
- access gained or lost;
- confidence in the prediction;
- owner systems required for execution.

Predictions are explainable estimates, not guarantees.

## 32. Determinism Contract

### 32.1 Update ordering

Network updates in a turn process by:

```text
1. Effective pipeline phase
2. Effective date or turn substep
3. Source authority priority
4. Cause event canonical ID
5. Source entity canonical ID
6. Target entity canonical ID
7. Dimension canonical ID
8. Request stable ID
```

### 32.2 Duplicate protection

Each update request has a stable ID and cause reference. Reprocessing the same validated event must not apply the relationship change twice.

### 32.3 Equal-state rule

Equal:

- Network state;
- owner state;
- event inputs;
- definitions;
- versions;
- ordering;
- seed where explicitly allowed;

must produce equal results.

### 32.4 No random opening relationships

Randomness cannot select opening trust, hostility, access, knowledge, or grievances. Runtime uncertainty may affect AI estimates, but authoritative Network updates remain command- and event-driven.

## 33. Performance Contract

### 33.1 Sparse directed graph

Store only strategically meaningful or currently active edges. Absence of a record means no authoritative relationship state, not neutral friendship.

### 33.2 Bounded queries

Common queries:

- outgoing relationships for one actor;
- incoming influence edges for one target;
- edges touched by one current event;
- known contacts in one region or institution;
- succession-relevant relationships;
- communication channels available for one command.

Avoid full-world pair scans.

### 33.3 Caches

Cache:

- resolved canonical handles;
- current communication reachability;
- decision-relevant dimension summaries;
- derived UI strings;
- AI query subsets.

Invalidate through Network and owner events.

### 33.4 Tier interaction

Tier A characters receive full Network processing. Tier B records update event-first. Tier C and Historical identities retain references without routine relationship evaluation.

### 33.5 Institution-node limit

If institution nodes are supported, load only strategically active person-institution edges. Do not build a universal graph linking every character to every church or settlement.

## 36. Risks and Mitigations

### 36.1 Relationship overprecision

**Risk:** Sparse evidence becomes detailed fictional psychology.

**Mitigation:** ordinal states, low confidence, bounded inference, and conservative seeds.

### 36.2 Network becomes another diplomacy score

**Risk:** dimensions are recombined into one universal opinion value.

**Mitigation:** typed owner queries and reason-specific AI evaluation.

### 36.3 Kinship leakage

**Risk:** dynasty automatically creates loyalty and knowledge.

**Mitigation:** explicit Network records and no automatic seeding.

### 36.4 Omniscient AI

**Risk:** AI reacts to hidden plans or distant events.

**Mitigation:** scoped knowledge and communication routes.

### 36.5 Scripted war through hostile seeds

**Risk:** Rivallon or Odo is initialized so negatively that conflict is inevitable.

**Mitigation:** low or guarded hostility, separate dependencies, and multiple settlement paths.

### 36.6 Excessive player information

**Risk:** private relationship state and secrets are exposed.

**Mitigation:** visibility policies and perspective-aware UI.

### 36.7 Routine-click inflation

**Risk:** every minor relationship shift prompts the player.

**Mitigation:** automatic event processing and selective exposure for consequential decisions.

### 36.8 Graph performance

**Risk:** every character pair becomes an edge.

**Mitigation:** sparse graph, bounded queries, tier-aware updates, and relevant institution nodes only.

## Appendix D: Design Gate Review

### Gate 1: Who owns it?

Character Network owns directed social, informational, obligation, grievance, dependency, and access state. Dynasty, Historical, Household, Institution, Title, and Constraint owners retain their facts.

### Gate 2: Can typed content solve it?

Yes. Typed records solve Brittany’s Network needs.

### Gate 3: Is a framework necessary?

No Brittany-only framework is necessary. An institution adapter may be required if the current Network framework is person-only.

### Gate 4: Does it create meaningful strategy?

Yes. Cooperation, mediation, access, settlement, and conflict depend on distinct causes.

### Gate 5: Does borrowing pass selective inheritance?

Yes. CK3-style relationship causality informs politics without replacing the Medieval II campaign spine.

### Gate 6: Does it avoid routine clicks?

Yes, if minor updates process automatically and only strategic consequences are exposed.

### Gate 7: Can AI explain it?

Yes. Every proposal can cite known trust, grievance, obligation, dependency, and access reasons.

### Gate 8: Are runtime concerns covered?

Yes. Definitions, scenario data, runtime state, commands, events, saves, migration, determinism, performance, and validation are separated.

### Gate 9: Is it plausible and non-scripted?

Yes. Opening tension supports multiple pathways and forces no later war.
