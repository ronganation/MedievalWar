## 1. Character Verdict

Conan II is Brittany's recognized duke at the January 1062 opening, but he is not the personification of a centralized Breton state. His authority has been recovered from a prolonged minority and regency crisis, yet the practical power needed to govern, judge, travel, muster, supply, punish, reward, negotiate, and defend remains distributed across regional lords, households, castles, routes, settlements, church institutions, officers, communities, and negotiated obligations.

**Character fantasy:** Restore ducal authority, then prove it can survive beyond personal pressure.

**Strategic question:** Can Conan turn recovered recognition into durable government without breaking the regional bargains and institutions that make Brittany governable?

Conan must remain interesting if the campaign produces reconciliation, selective centralization, institutional growth, strategic restraint, military success, military failure, ecclesiastical compromise, regional autonomy, dynastic uncertainty, or a Brittany that never follows the later historical sequence.

A carefully negotiated settlement may strengthen the duchy more than a nominally victorious confiscation. A successful campaign may weaken Brittany if it empties garrisons, exhausts household cadres, creates arrears, or leaves no continuity during Conan's absence.

## 12. Character Interior Without Fabricated Psychology

Conan should be authored through recurring decision questions rather than invented private emotion:
- Which regional actor can actually perform this order?
- Is the objective worth exposing the ducal household or Rennes?
- Will a constraint produce compliance, grievance, martyrdom, or administrative paralysis?
- Does a settlement preserve recognition while admitting durable autonomy?
- Is direct presence necessary, or would a mandate and witnesses work better?
- Is a public commitment supported by money, routes, troops, information, and succession continuity?
- Does intervention at Dol protect Brittany or entangle Brittany in an externally owned ecclesiastical claim?
- Is an external campaign worth weakening the internal network that makes ducal power real?

These questions create a recognizable ruler without asserting unsupported fear, anger, affection, ambition, or diagnosis.

## 13. Political Style and Decision Tendencies

Potential qualitative tendencies, always conditional:
- prefer visible recognition of ducal dignity;
- distinguish ordinary cooperation from extraordinary service;
- use direct presence where authority is symbolically contested;
- seek narrow, legible objectives before major coercion;
- accept regional autonomy when performance, access, and recognition remain credible;
- prefer witnessed settlements and reviewable constraints;
- strengthen records, officers, routes, and handback procedures after crisis;
- preserve home defense before external commitment;
- resist arrangements that make ducal recognition purely ceremonial;
- escalate when repeated refusal is supported by a current legal and material cause.

These tendencies do not compel centralization, aggression, or distrust. Experience can shift them.

## 14. Political Blind Spots and Failure Tendencies

Conditional risks include:
- interpreting recovered recognition as broad practical obedience;
- relying on personal presence until government becomes dependent on Conan's body;
- using constraint faster than restoration can repair legitimacy;
- treating a title holder's pledge as delivered military readiness;
- making public commitments before routes, finance, supply, and command are secure;
- underestimating household and institutional access as political power;
- allowing succession ambiguity to remain private until it becomes destabilizing;
- exhausting scarce mounted retainers, select foot, officers, and garrisons;
- assuming battlefield victory settles property, title, recognition, and local cooperation;
- escalating several Breton problem lanes simultaneously.

A blind spot becomes runtime state only through decisions and results. It is not an opening debuff.

## 15. Ducal Rulership Boundary

Conan is the person who may hold and exercise the ducal title. Conan is not Brittany, the title, the government, the treasury, the court, the army, the Dynasty, or the Church. Strong personal rule may improve arbitration, attendance, confidence, concentration of effort, and speed of decision. It cannot replace collectors, officers, messengers, households, regional authorities, garrisons, courts, churches, communities, nor local knowledge.

A ducal command can be valid but impossible to execute. A regional response can be lawful but limited. A delayed response can reflect travel, supply, uncertainty, conflicting obligations, or failed communication rather than disloyalty.

## 22. Household, Court, Residence, and Access

Conan's household is a separate institution with officers, guards, clerks, seals, records, stores, hospitality, routes, residences, and continuity requirements. The profile does not invent the roster.

Access to Conan may be sponsored, delayed, narrowed, transferred, witnessed, or refused for legal, security, logistical, or political reasons. Audience does not guarantee outcome. Denial does not automatically create hostility.

Rennes is a primary-seat candidate, not proof of permanent presence. Travel may improve regional visibility while creating delay, expense, vulnerability, and reduced supervision elsewhere. A mobile court can strengthen authority and simultaneously expose the limits of household capacity.
