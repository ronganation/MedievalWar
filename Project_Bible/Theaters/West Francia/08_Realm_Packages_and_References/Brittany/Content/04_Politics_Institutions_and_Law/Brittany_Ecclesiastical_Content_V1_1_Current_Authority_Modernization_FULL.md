## 1. Current Verdict

Brittany belongs to the Latin Christian ecclesiastical world. Its church politics arise from distinct bishoprics, chapters, monasteries, patrons, protectors, properties, jurisdictions, archives, sacred places, communities, and external authorities. The Church of Dol is politically important and may assert metropolitan standing, but an asserted status is not the same as recognized metropolitan authority. Tours remains externally owned. Nantes is not a universal archdiocese governing Brittany. Local Breton traditions, saints, cults, liturgical practices, and monastic memories enrich regional identity without creating a separate universal Celtic-Christian religion.

The system must produce strategy through jurisdiction, appointment, patronage, charter confirmation, sanctuary, mediation, property, exemption, local protection, witnessing, communication, education, relief, burial, commemoration, and institutional continuity. Routine worship and ordinary administration remain background simulation. Major disputes, vacancies, contested appointments, sanctuary crises, damaged institutions, and politically consequential patronage become player-facing.

Institution != Office

Election != Confirmation
Confirmation != Consecration
Consecration != Installation
