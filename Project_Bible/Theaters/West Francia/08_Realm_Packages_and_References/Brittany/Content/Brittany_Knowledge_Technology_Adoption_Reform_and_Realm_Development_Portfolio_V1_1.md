## 1. Authority Verdict and Purpose

Brittany needs a Realm-specific development portfolio because knowledge, diplomacy, law, governance, population, economy, geography, ecclesiastical institutions, military capability, and Realm AI are already separately owned, but the current package has no single player/AI-facing contract for choosing development directions, testing adoption and reform, reading blockers, and determining whether a proposed capability can be realized at a specific Breton host.

The portfolio organizes decisions; it does not become the owner of the state those decisions depend on.

```text
Knowledge possession != communication
Communication != understanding
Understanding != capability
Capability definition != adoption
Adoption != institutional reform
Institutional reform != regional realization
Regional realization != fielded capability
Program progress != owner performance
Player or AI decision != execution
Research != creation of people, goods, equipment, animals, ships, resources, or pools
```

The player promise is a clear choice of what the Realm may try to improve, why it matters, who must participate, what it costs or displaces, where it may work, and how it can fail, pause, be rejected, be reversed, or recover. The campaign remains a Total War strategy campaign centered on map, settlements, armies, diplomacy, institutions, economy, and political relationships—not an abstract technology-tree minigame.

## 5. Brittany Development Thesis

Brittany's opening design problem is recognized ducal authority without completed territorial consolidation. The Realm is compact and coastal, but its political and material capacity is regionally mediated. Ducal Rennes, Penthièvre, Nantes-Cornouaille, and Dol-Combourg are useful analysis lenses, not fixed development factions or loyalty labels. Governance requires valid jurisdiction, access, cooperation, capacity, readiness, communication, and performance. Church institutions, local lordships, property, routes, household resources, external relations, and military service remain distinct owners.

The portfolio's Brittany-specific question is:

> Can the ducal center improve its ability to coordinate, communicate, administer, sustain, and negotiate without presuming uniform obedience, merging regional resources, or spending capacity that belongs to households, institutions, communities, or external owners?

Development should be uneven and conditional. A Realm-wide idea may have only one feasible sponsor or host; another region may reject, adapt, delay, or lack the inputs. Brittany may choose to preserve local arrangements, pursue a limited institutional reform, adopt a practice through a valid channel, invest elsewhere, or do nothing. There is no mandatory modernization ladder or historically predetermined centralization.

## 11. Brittany Portfolio Architecture

The following are candidate portfolio families for player/AI analysis, not a fixed technology tree, opening program list, approved historical fact, or guaranteed development. Each is subordinate to the current named owners. Program admission requires evidence and owner review.

### 11.1 Ducal communication, records, and mandate coordination

**Purpose:** consider whether authenticated messages, retained records, clear mandates, report-back, and review can make the duke's requests and delegated work more legible across a regionally mediated Realm.

**Potential lenses:** Statecraft; Civic; Economic where record/route resources are implicated.

**Inputs:** Governance/Delegated Authority for competence, scope, term, and accountability; Network/Diplomacy for contact, knowledge, delivery, authentication, audience, and receipt; Law for valid procedure and remedy; Economy for scribal labor, materials, and delivery only if evidenced; Geography for route and seasonal feasibility.

**Limits:** no invented chancery, office, archive, messenger, literacy level, dispatch speed, centralization, obedience, or cost. A record does not create authority; a delivered message does not create consent; improved coordination does not make regional administration uniform.

### 11.2 Regional obligation, consultation, and performance coordination

**Purpose:** evaluate bounded ways for ducal, regional, household, and institutional participants to clarify obligations, consultation, performance, exception, and handback without collapsing their capacities.

**Potential lenses:** Statecraft; Civic; Military only as a read-only service interface.

**Inputs:** Governance, Titles, Households, Law, Diplomacy, Network, AI, Population/Economy, and Military owner receipts as applicable.

**Limits:** no guaranteed attendance, levy, loyalty, unity, centralization, or compliance; no new offices, councils, mandates, troops, pools, equipment, or regional alignment. A defined obligation is not capacity, and capacity is not performance.

### 11.3 Lawful administrative practice, custom, and review

**Purpose:** consider improvements in notice, record custody, jurisdiction routing, appeal, remedy, enforcement request, and restoration where Brittany's current Law and Governance owners support them.

**Potential lenses:** Statecraft; Civic.

**Inputs:** Law/Custom/Justice for rule, jurisdiction, procedure, evidence, decision, appeal, enforcement and restoration; Governance for authority and execution; Ecclesiastical for distinct church jurisdiction; Economy/Property for rights and costs; Population and regional owners for local impacts.

**Limits:** no uniform code, automatic reform, new court, office, police, judge, enforcement force, confiscation, tax right, or sanction. Procedure must remain reviewable and source-qualified. Disagreement is not rebellion.

### 11.4 Ecclesiastical, documentary, and institutional continuity

**Purpose:** examine whether an owner-verified institution or community can preserve, transmit, or apply an accepted practice or record over time.

**Potential lenses:** Civic; Statecraft; Economic only for authorized material support.

**Inputs:** Ecclesiastical for institution, office, jurisdiction, property, community, recognition, and continuity; Households/Character for people and services; Network/Diplomacy for external contact; Law for jurisdiction; Geography for access; Economy for real resources.

**Limits:** no invented school, archive, scriptorium, officeholder, relic, manuscript, literacy rate, doctrinal position, or recognized Church of Dol metropolitan status. The metropolitan claim remains claim/dispute absent owner resolution; Tours remains external. Institution presence does not imply host consent or capacity.

### 11.5 Local production, property, and exchange coordination

**Purpose:** assess supported improvements to cooperation among property rights, seasonal labor, productive activity, stores, markets, finance, routes, and delivery where economic owners identify a meaningful decision.

**Potential lenses:** Economic; Civic; Statecraft where lawful administration, agreement, or external exchange is involved.

**Inputs:** Brittany Population/Economy/Property/Treasury owner and V1.1 compatibility addendum; Geography; Governance; Law; Diplomacy; relevant community and institutional owners.

**Limits:** no invented industry, crop, surplus, trade flow, market, port operation, tax receipt, price, equipment, fund, or production increase. A productive practice cannot violate finite labor, land, water, property, material, finance, maintenance, or delivery constraints.

### 11.6 Coastal access, route knowledge, and maritime coordination

**Purpose:** evaluate the ability to coordinate access, warning, communication, trade, transport, or return through owner-validated route/port relationships.

**Potential lenses:** Statecraft; Economic; Civic; Military only where the Military owner provides an interface.

**Inputs:** Geography for place, route, coast, port, physical capacity, season, access and safety; Diplomacy for permission and external agreement; Economy for commerce, labor, goods, and delivery; Military for force transport, supply, and return; relevant community and vessel/operator owners.

### 11A. Mature Candidate Program Definitions

These definitions make the seven families usable without turning them into opening selections. They are program candidates only. Their names may be presented to players after historical, localization, and owner review.

#### BRI-DEV-01 Ducal Messages, Receipts, and Return Reports

**Strategic question:** can a sponsor improve the reliability of authenticated requests and report-back across a regionally mediated Realm?

**Candidate benefit:** clearer mandate, delivery, receipt, exception, and review visibility.

**Required owners:** Governance, Law, Character Network, Knowledge/Communication/Diplomacy, Geography, Population/Economy, and the selected host.

**Material choices:** narrow the geographic scope; fund and staff a bounded route; prioritize urgent military, legal, fiscal, or ecclesiastical communication; or preserve existing local practice.

**Failure and recovery:** messages may be delayed, unauthenticated, refused, misunderstood, or unsupported by continued personnel and materials. Recovery requires owner-validated replacement channels, restored records, renewed permission, or reduced scope.

#### BRI-DEV-02 Regional Obligation Review and Service Agreement

**Strategic question:** can the ducal center and a named participant clarify an obligation, exception, duration, performance evidence, and handback without presuming general obedience?

**Candidate benefit:** more legible and reviewable service arrangements.

**Required owners:** Governance, Titles, Households, Law, Network/Diplomacy, Population/Economy, Military where relevant, and the affected regional or institutional owner.

**Material choices:** negotiate one obligation; accept partial or seasonal service; exchange scope for reliability; defer; or retain the current arrangement.

**Failure and recovery:** unclear mandate, unavailable capacity, disputed terms, nonperformance, or loss of sponsor continuity can suspend the agreement. Recovery uses consultation, remedy, narrowed scope, replacement performance, or orderly closure.

#### BRI-DEV-03 Notice, Record Custody, and Review Practice

**Strategic question:** can a competent host improve notice, custody, jurisdiction routing, appeal, and restoration for a bounded class of decisions?

**Candidate benefit:** fewer owner-visible procedural failures and clearer review paths.

**Required owners:** Law, Governance, Ecclesiastical where applicable, Household/Character services, Property/Economy, and the host institution or community.

**Material choices:** improve notice; protect custody; define review; prioritize restoration; or avoid reform where jurisdiction is disputed.

**Failure and recovery:** missing records, contested jurisdiction, invalid mandate, unavailable personnel, or weak enforcement can produce partial adoption or reversal.

#### BRI-DEV-04 Institutional Continuity and Documentary Stewardship

**Strategic question:** can an admitted institution preserve and transmit a bounded body of records or practice through disruption?

**Candidate benefit:** improved continuity, provenance, qualified access, and recovery after loss.

**Required owners:** Ecclesiastical or other institutional owner, Character/Household, Law, Geography, Economy, Network/Diplomacy, and Historical Evidence.

**Material choices:** protect one collection or practice; duplicate custody where lawful; improve access and authentication; or accept local vulnerability rather than overextend.

**Failure and recovery:** fire, conflict, displacement, custody dispute, rights restriction, or loss of skilled service may degrade capability without erasing remembered knowledge.

#### BRI-DEV-05 Local Production and Delivery Compact

**Strategic question:** can property holders, labor providers, communities, routes, and recipients coordinate one bounded productive or delivery problem?

**Candidate benefit:** more reliable owner-validated production, storage, allocation, or delivery for a defined use.

**Required owners:** Population, Economy, Property, Treasury, Geography, Governance, Law, Diplomacy where external exchange applies, and affected communities.

**Material choices:** reserve labor or materials; favor maintenance over expansion; prioritize military, civic, household, or institutional demand; accept reduced scope; or decline.

**Failure and recovery:** labor shortage, property dispute, material shortfall, route interruption, funding loss, or competing reservation may pause or shrink the compact.

#### BRI-DEV-06 Coastal Route, Port-Access, and Return Coordination

**Strategic question:** can Brittany improve one owner-validated coastal or river-connected movement problem without treating coastline as fleet or commerce?

**Candidate benefit:** clearer knowledge, permission, scheduling, warning, loading, receipt, and return conditions for a defined purpose.

**Required owners:** Geography, Diplomacy, Economy, relevant community and property owners, vessel/operator owner, and Military when force movement is involved.

**Material choices:** prioritize communication, trade, transport, warning, or return; select one route or host; negotiate safe conduct; or avoid maritime dependence.

## 13. Adoption, Reform, Hybridization, Rejection, and Reversibility

Adoption is a process by a competent owner, not a universal knowledge flag. Institutional reform additionally requires valid authority, lawful procedure, consultation where required, affected-party treatment, resources, implementation capacity, review, and remedy. A practice may be partially adopted, adapted locally, hybridized with another supported practice, refused, delayed, suspended, or reversed. Rejection may be rational if the costs, risks, legal conflict, or capacity burden outweigh the benefit.

Reversal does not automatically erase knowledge or historical memory. It may end a mandate, return a property/resource, restore a prior procedure, close a program, or leave an unresolved transition according to the owner. Recovery may require re-establishing access, replacing a host through valid authority, restoring records, retraining through the relevant owner, repairing property, renegotiating terms, or narrowing scope. No recovery path creates a person, resource, equipment, route, office, institution, or consent.

## 14. Opportunity Cost, Compatibility, and Choice

Programs compete for owner-reserved money, credit, goods, materials, qualified people, labor, institutional time, household attention, routes, access, legal authority, political trust, security, and maintenance. Competition is local and causal; it is neither an arbitrary binary fork nor a generic penalty. Two programs can coexist only if their owners confirm uncommitted capacity, compatible mandates, and valid sequencing.

Player choices may include investigate, ask, sponsor, consult, authorize a bounded effort, defer, pause, change scope, accept a partial outcome, reject, abandon, or seek recovery. An option cannot be selected if its hard owner/authority/access/capacity gates fail. The player may not be forced into automatic adoption because of elapsed time or a historical event.
