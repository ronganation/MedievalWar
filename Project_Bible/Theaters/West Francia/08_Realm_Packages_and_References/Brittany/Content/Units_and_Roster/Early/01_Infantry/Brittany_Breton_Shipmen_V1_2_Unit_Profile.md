## 2. Why This Candidate Is Genuinely Breton

Brittany is a peninsula bounded by the English Channel and Bay of Biscay and historically framed by sea and major river approaches. Nantes and the Loire were strategically important, while Brittany's distinct history was shaped by its position at the western edge of the Frankish world and by movement between Armorica and insular Britain.

## 3. One-Screen Total War Card

- **Unit:** Breton Shipmen
- **Class:** Maritime operational shielded infantry
- **Signature:** Embark, Approach, Land, Secure
- **Battle promise:** Preserve enough order during a coastal, riverine, quay, or shipboard operation to establish one usable landing route for the Breton host.
- **Best use:** Ship defense, landing places, beaches, quays, estuary banks, river crossings, port streets, temporary shoreheads, boat protection, and securing the movement of follow-on infantry.
- **Strong against:** Light troops contesting a landing, exposed missiles near the shore, disordered defenders, crew-level boarding threats, and ordinary infantry already pressured at a narrow landing point.
- **Conditional against:** Peer regional infantry in constrained coastal or port ground, ordinary spears not fully braced, and cavalry denied room by terrain.
- **Weak against:** Prepared heavy infantry at the waterline, concentrated crossbows, heavy cavalry after reaching open ground, deep spears, severe surf or current, loss of boats, split landings, and prolonged unsupported inland combat.
- **Campaign bargain:** Open a maritime means of moving soldiers and supplies around Brittany's difficult geography, but only through real vessels, crews, landing craft, routes, tides, weather, stores, and port capacity.
- **Player instruction:** Do not use Shipmen as better infantry. Use the company to make the transition between water and land work, secure the landing route, then hand the inland battle to the appropriate Breton formation.
- **Flavor:** **The victory is not merely reaching the shore. The victory is keeping the boats, route, stores, and first piece of ground usable after the enemy arrives.**

### Recruit when

- water movement creates real strategic advantage;
- a complete vessel, crew, leader, landing, and store packet exists;
- the army needs a port, estuary, river, island, or coastal route opened;
- follow-on infantry is available to exploit or take over the landing;
- the home port and transport economy can spare the packet.

### Do not recruit when

- the campaign is entirely inland;
- Levy, Muster, Retainer, or Warband infantry better fits the mission;
- ships exist without qualified people or landing capacity;
- people exist without vessels, stores, or route authority;
- the player merely wants distinctive Breton infantry statistics.

## 29. Visual and Audio Direction

### Visual anchors

- practical shielded infantry linked visually to boats, ropes, oars, poles, tools, stores, quays, and landing work;
- mixed maritime workers, guards, followers, leaders, and better-maintained veterans without modern uniformity;
- lighter and more practical equipment than Household Retainers;
- no Scandinavian costume shorthand;
- physical landing waves, boat guards, store movement, and reembarkation lanes where systems support them.

### Must avoid

- horned helmets, excessive fur, round-shield Viking clone, berserker styling, striped fantasy sails as unit identity, universal axes, elite marine uniforms, swimming heavy infantry, instant disembarkation, or automatic coastal camouflage.

### Audio anchors

- embark, push off, hold the boat, line, oars, rope, landing, shore, stores, route, tide, withdraw, and reembark calls;
- wood, rope, water, shield, oar, boat impact, surf, quay, and store-handling sounds;
- Breton, French, and Latin texture only after linguistic and regional review.
