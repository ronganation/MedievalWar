- **Flavor:** **The Warband does not merely survive the line. It makes the line move, then stands on the ground that movement wins.**

## 4. Historical and Regional Discipline

Cornouaille was a distinct historic region of south-western Brittany, centered on Quimper and known in Breton as Kernev or Kerne. The region's distinct political and cultural identity supports a regional provider concept, but does not prove one standardized military formation called a Cornouaille Warband.

Brittany remained politically fragmented in the tenth and eleventh centuries, with ducal authority limited outside direct lands. This supports militarily meaningful regional packets rather than a uniform national infantry model.

## 8. Core Battle Loop: Approach, Press, Secure

### Approach

Move toward one local objective using terrain, shields, friendly missile pressure, and section leadership. Approach is controlled movement, not automatic stealth.

### Press

Maintain local contact longer than Javelinmen and apply enough shielded spear-and-sidearm pressure to displace ordinary or disrupted opposition.

### Secure

Reform on the gained ground, face the next threat, reopen supporting routes, and prevent an immediate enemy recovery. Securing distinguishes the Warband from a one-impact assault unit.

### Preferred physical sequence

1. Spears establish the receiving line.
2. Bowmen harrow the target and approaches.
3. Javelinmen close and cast against a formation edge.
4. The target disorders, turns, or becomes fixed.
5. Warband approaches under that pressure and commits to the bounded objective.
6. Warband presses long enough to displace ordinary resistance.
7. Lancers exploit an exposed flank or reserve movement.
8. Warband secures the ground and reconnects Breton routes.
9. Household Retainers remain available for a critical counterattack elsewhere.

### Cornouaille Warband

*Western regional ground-taking infantry*

Cornouaille Warbands are practiced shielded fighting companies raised through the western region's local leaders and service relationships. The Warband advances against one bounded objective, sustains pressure against ordinary or already-disrupted troops, and secures the gained ground for Breton spears, missiles, and cavalry. Strong leadership and local cohesion make the company more aggressive than levy or muster formations, but heavy cavalry, elite shock infantry, prepared spears, concentrated crossbows, and unsupported pursuit can destroy its bargain.
