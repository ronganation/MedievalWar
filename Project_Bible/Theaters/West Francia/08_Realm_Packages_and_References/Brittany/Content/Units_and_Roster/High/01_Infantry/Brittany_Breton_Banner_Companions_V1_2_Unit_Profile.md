## 3. One-Screen Total War Card

- **Unit:** Breton Banner Companions
- **Class:** Selected regional banner infantry
- **Signature:** Gather, Uphold, Commit, Return
- **Battle promise:** Protect one named regional responsibility, deliver one strong bounded commitment, and return to the banner before the lordship following is destroyed by prolonged general melee.
- **Best use:** Protecting a regional standard or commander, holding a politically important flank or passage, supporting a lordship contingent, countercommitting into an important local fight, and escorting an ordered withdrawal.
- **Strong against:** Ordinary infantry, tired maintained foot, fragmented attacks, exposed missiles, and enemies already occupied by Spears or Men-at-Arms.
- **Conditional against:** Peer selected regional infantry, Men-at-Arms during a short supported commitment, and medium cavalry received in spear-supported order.
- **Weak against:** Prolonged Men-at-Arms contact, elite shock infantry, concentrated crossbows, heavy cavalry, encirclement, banner or leader separation, and politically wasteful commitment.
- **Campaign bargain:** Strong selected regional infantry without direct ducal ownership, purchased through negotiated access, independent lordship people and equipment, banner leadership, home-duty concessions, and provider-specific loss.
- **Player instruction:** Assign the responsibility before battle. Uphold it, commit only when that responsibility demands action, and return before the following becomes a generic assault unit.
- **Flavor:** **The banner does not make the Companions brave. The banner tells the Companions whose ground, people, and honor the company has come to preserve.**

## 4. Historical and Political Discipline

Brittany's political structure historically included powerful regional lordships and limited ducal power outside direct lands, providing a strong basis for independent provider packets rather than one centralized infantry establishment. Noble retinues and affinities more broadly tied selected followers to a lord through service, protection, office, tenure, maintenance, or personal connection, although later English affinity terminology should not be projected mechanically onto every Breton period.
