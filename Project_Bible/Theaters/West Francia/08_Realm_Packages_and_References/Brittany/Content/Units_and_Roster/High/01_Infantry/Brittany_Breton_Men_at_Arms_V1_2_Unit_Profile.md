## 3. One-Screen Total War Card

- **Unit:** Breton Men-at-Arms
- **Class:** Protected maintained High-Era melee infantry
- **Signature:** Advance, Engage, Hold, Recover
- **Battle promise:** Carry one important section of the Breton center into sustained protected contact without becoming an invulnerable heavy-infantry wall.
- **Best use:** Main-line contact, protecting Nantes Crossbowmen, receiving enemy medium infantry, holding after a Warband advance, and anchoring a combined-arms center.
- **Strong against:** Ordinary infantry, lighter maintained foot, tired regional companies, fragmented attacks, and enemy troops unable to bypass the protected center.
- **Conditional against:** Peer Men-at-Arms, selected regional infantry, medium cavalry in good order, and dismounted knightly packets with support.
- **Weak against:** Elite shock infantry, heavy cavalry on favorable ground, concentrated crossbows, polearm or anti-armor specialists, encirclement, prolonged missile preparation, and repeated unsupported contact.
- **Campaign bargain:** Stronger contact, protection, and field endurance than Sergeants, purchased with high cost, narrow people and equipment pools, servants, maintenance, and slow replacement.
- **Flavor:** **Sergeants keep the host in order. Men-at-Arms stand where order must survive the enemy's weight.**

## 4. Historical and Terminological Discipline

A man-at-arms was a military function rather than automatically a social rank: not all men-at-arms were knights, although the term generally referred to well-equipped, heavily protected warriors and often to mounted service. Men-at-arms could also fight on foot, especially in later medieval practice. The exact chronology and Breton infantry expression therefore require careful control.

Breton Men-at-Arms are costly maintained fighting companies equipped and supported for serious field contact.
