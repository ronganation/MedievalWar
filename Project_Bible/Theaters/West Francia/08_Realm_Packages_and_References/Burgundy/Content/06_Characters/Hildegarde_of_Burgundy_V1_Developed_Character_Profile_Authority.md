## 3. Concise Verdict

Hildegarde begins as a probable living young daughter of Robert I and Ermengarde whose value lies in present Burgundian household continuity, protection, education, kinship, patronage formation, and future choice. Her later connection to Aquitaine demonstrates long-horizon diplomatic relevance but does not create an opening engagement, marriage, alliance, adult diplomatic office, or relocation. The grand-campaign treatment should be meaningful without becoming a deep life simulation: the player manages the conditions of her formation and future agency while specialist owners retain every mutable result.

## 4. Core Character Thesis

**Hildegarde is a developing Burgundian person whose future political importance must grow from protection, education, household position, consent, relationships, and changing campaign conditions rather than being backfilled from her later Aquitanian marriage.**

Her central campaign question is what kind of adult political actor she can become if history diverges. She may become a Burgundian household principal, patron, mediator, dynastic communicator, marriage-negotiation principal, institutional actor, cross-Realm connector, or a person whose life never follows the Aquitanian path. None is guaranteed.

## 10. Opening Strategic Function

Hildegarde's opening function is deliberately modest but consequential:

1. **Household continuity.** Her care, residence, staff access, and security affect the ducal household without making her its property.
2. **Education and formation.** Language, literacy, religion, ceremony, household management, law, patronage, and communication may develop through age-appropriate policy and actual access.
3. **Directed family relationships.** Parents, siblings, half-siblings, and household figures may influence her, but every relationship direction remains separate.
4. **Future political agency.** A later adult role may emerge from demonstrated formation, not from age alone.
5. **Marriage diplomacy with consent.** Future proposals may become strategic, but no proposal creates consent, marriage, alliance, transfer, or shared knowledge.
6. **Patronage and institutions.** Religious and charitable participation may develop through resources, property rights, and institutional access owned elsewhere.

## 18. Decision Portfolio

Age and lifecycle determine which choices are hers directly and which are proposals or protections routed through proper owners. Her portfolio may include education priorities, residence and household requests, patronage participation, correspondence, mediation, ceremonial attendance, marriage proposal review, consent or refusal where applicable, property petitions, institutional engagement, travel preference, grievance repair, and later adult household strategy.

The game should show why an option is unavailable instead of silently treating her as inactive.

## 19. Failure, Recovery, and Divergence

Failure may arise from poor protection, interrupted education, household displacement, illness, isolation, coercive marriage handling, inadequate resources, damaged relationships, misinformation, ignored consent, institutional conflict, or being treated only as a diplomatic commodity.

Recovery may involve safer residence, new educators, household reconstruction, mediation, restitution, delayed negotiation, patronage support, repaired correspondence, legal review, revised marriage terms, withdrawal from a proposal, or an independent Burgundian path.

Possible futures include Burgundian household principal, patron, institutional mediator, diplomatic dynast, consensual cross-Realm spouse, independent unmarried dynast, religious path after valid choice and authority, alternate marriage path, politically marginal adult, or early death through valid lifecycle events. None is scheduled.

## 20. Player-Facing Experience

The player should understand Hildegarde through visible age band, protection, household access, education opportunities, directed relationships, information channels, future-choice surfaces, patronage access, consent, and unresolved chronology. The UI should not present her as a military unit, spouse bonus, guaranteed Aquitaine alliance, fertility instrument, or miniature adult politician.

The intended middle ground is enough character causality to make household and political decisions matter without requiring the grand campaign to simulate every private detail. Conflict-focused expansions may deepen a specific cast without forcing that density on every grand-campaign character.
