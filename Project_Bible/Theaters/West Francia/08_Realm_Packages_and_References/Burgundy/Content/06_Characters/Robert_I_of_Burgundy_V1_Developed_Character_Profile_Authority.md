## 3. Concise Verdict

Robert I begins January 1062 as Burgundy's living senior duke candidate, a Capetian prince with roughly three decades of ducal experience behind him, and the person on whom the duchy's present authority still depends most heavily. He gives Burgundy immediate legitimacy, experience, high-level military credibility, and a visible decision center. He also embodies the Realm's central danger: a long personal rule has not automatically produced a durable transfer of power, a settled relationship with every institution, a dependable regional command structure, or a successor able to inherit every function he performs.

Robert's campaign is therefore not "become Duke of Burgundy." Robert already occupies the historical ducal position within the current candidate authority chain. The campaign is to turn founder rule into a duchy that can survive Robert's absence, incapacity, defeat, institutional conflict, or death without merely replaying the recorded grandson succession.

## 4. Core Character Thesis

**Founder authority versus durable continuity**

Robert is strongest when personal rank, long experience, regional bargaining, household leadership, military credibility, and institutional settlement reinforce one another. Robert is weakest when coercion substitutes for administration, immediate compliance is mistaken for durable recognition, disputes with one institution are generalized into universal hostility, or succession is left to genealogy after practical government remains concentrated in Robert's person.

The profile must remain useful if Robert reconciles with institutions, governs coercively, delegates to Henry, elevates a collateral child, prepares a grandson, survives beyond historical comparison, dies earlier, loses a campaign, is captured, suffers incapacity, or leaves the duchy politically plural rather than centralized.

## 6. Evidence and Source Treatment

### 6.1 Controlling project evidence

The Burgundy Historical Reconciliation, Evidence and Chronology Register, Historical Uncertainty Register, ID Registry, and accepted Realm Character Authorities control the project baseline. They support Robert as a living adult opening duke candidate, a Capetian cadet founder, and a long-serving strategic principal while preserving uncertainty about exact birth precision, health, residence, property, household structure, command, relations with individual institutions, and the most dramatic hostile narratives.

### 6.2 External research use

The external research pass identified general-reference summaries that report Robert as Duke of Burgundy from 1032 to 1076, a son of Robert II of France and Constance of Arles, and founder of the Capetian House of Burgundy. Those summaries also identify Constance Brittain Bouchard's *Sword, Miter, and Cloister*, Georges Duby's *The Knight, the Lady and the Priest*, and related scholarship as stronger research leads. General-reference summaries are discovery and cross-check evidence, not sufficient authority for exact private motive, universal moral judgment, or controversial event detail.

### 6.3 High-risk claims

Reports that Robert seized ecclesiastical revenues or property, attacked institutions, killed Dalmace of Semur, or was a "robber baron" require claim-level treatment. They may support institution-specific grievances, hostile memories, contested reputation, restitution demands, fear, resistance, or later narrative reception. They do not justify a universal cruelty trait, universal Church hostility, automatic excommunication, or a fixed personality diagnosis.

### 6.4 Source dependence

Multiple websites repeating the same secondary summary do not count as independent corroboration. Exact dates, charter references, quoted phrases, and causation require edition-level or scholarly review before promotion.

## 10. Opening Strategic Function

Robert's opening function is to give Burgundy immediate agency while forcing the player to decide what must become durable before the founder can no longer carry it personally.

### 10.1 Convert tenure into government

Robert may seek better records, reliable officers, bounded mandates, regular consultation, secure routes, predictable dues, enforceable judgments, and repeatable regional cooperation. These improvements require specialist owners and cannot be created by biography.

### 10.2 Prepare continuity

Robert must decide whether continuity is centered on Henry, widened across adult collaterals, prepared through the grandchildren, or left deliberately plural. Succession preparation may involve education, household placement, delegated responsibility, recognition, guarantees, title-specific review, and institutional reception.

### 10.3 Settle or manage institutions

Robert may negotiate restitution, patronage, protection, arbitration, jurisdiction, sanctuary, property, appointment interfaces, and public reconciliation separately with each relevant institution. One successful settlement does not erase another grievance.

### 10.4 Build military credibility without exhausting the duchy

Robert may inspect force sources, test obligations, preserve household cadres, improve muster routes, appoint commanders, reinforce corridors, or lead a bounded campaign. Military action requires finite recruitment pools, equipment, supply, transport, home defense, recovery, and settlement.

### 10.5 Define Crown relationship

Robert may cooperate with the Capetian Crown, negotiate recognition, resist overreach, provide limited service, seek arbitration, or exploit cadet status without allowing France to absorb Burgundy's internal state.

## 11. Demonstrated Pre-Opening Formation

### 11.1 Capetian royal-house formation

Robert's birth into the Capetian royal family supports familiarity with dynastic rank, court competition, royal succession, princely bargaining, and the difference between family relationship and political settlement. It does not prove literacy, temperament, friendship, trust, or adult policy.

### 11.2 Conflict surrounding the royal succession

The current evidence architecture treats Robert's participation in conflict around the 1031 succession and the 1032 Burgundian settlement as a major formation surface. This supports experience with contested recognition, bargaining under pressure, and cadet autonomy. It does not require permanent hostility toward the Crown.

### 11.3 Long ducal tenure

Nearly three decades between the 1032 settlement and the opening support strong qualitative experience in rulership, dispute, travel, household direction, military policy, and institutional contact. Tenure is not a universal competence rating and does not prove every result was successful.

### 11.4 Family and household formation

Robert's marriages, adult children, deceased elder son, current spouse candidate, and grandchildren place him at the center of a multigenerational continuity problem. The profile may recognize experience with marriage, inheritance, household division, and dynastic loss while leaving each exact relationship and household record externally owned.

### 11.5 Institutional and regional encounters

Reports involving Autun, Dijon, Auxerre, Langres, Semur, and other Burgundian institutions and lordships establish a field of repeated contact and dispute requiring claim-specific validation. They support experience with coercion, negotiation, patronage, opposition, and settlement, not one fixed moral reputation.

## 13. Personality and Decision Tendencies

### 13.1 Supported design values

Robert is best interpreted as valuing ducal dignity, visible recognition, preservation of the cadet line, practical military credibility, household authority, defensible regional access, and settlements that confirm rather than dissolve Burgundy's autonomy. These are design tendencies derived from role and formation, not private psychological facts.

### 13.2 Likely reasoning tendencies

Robert may prefer personal intervention when dignity, disputed jurisdiction, military credibility, or succession is at stake. Robert may also favor narrow delegation when trusted capability exists and the mission does not endanger household continuity. A long-serving ruler may rely on proven personal methods even when institution building would produce stronger succession.

### 13.3 Restraints and aversions

Robert is likely to resist public humiliation, an external attempt to treat Burgundy as a province, a settlement that weakens ducal recognition without compensation, service demands that expose the duchy, and a succession arrangement that leaves the cadet line politically dependent. These remain bounded authoring tendencies.

### 13.4 Character risks

- personal concentration of government;
- coercion that produces immediate compliance but long grievance;
- neglect of written, household, or institutional continuity;
- overestimating what title and reputation can mobilize;
- underpreparing Henry or the grandchildren;
- empowering one branch or regional principal without review;
- turning an institution-specific dispute into wider coalition opposition;
- military absence that exposes property, household, routes, or succession;
- allowing pride or sunk costs to widen a limited objective.

- command appointment or recognized authority;
- actual force packages;
- finite recruitment sources and available pools;
- household, regional, castle, and allied contributions;
- equipment, horses, officers, specialists, and replacements;
- route, crossing, transport, intelligence, season, and supply;
- local-defense floor and garrison consequences;
- objective, duration, conduct, occupation, return, demobilization, and recovery plans;

The profile supports an experienced ruler with credible military judgment. It does not assert exact tactical preferences, battle record, troop totals, or opening readiness without evidence.

Battle success may improve confidence or reputation while empowering subordinates and exhausting scarce pools. Robert may preside, hear petitions, appoint valid delegates, seek mediation, order investigation, negotiate settlement, confirm custom, or propose enforcement when jurisdiction and capacity exist. Robert cannot turn a disputed claim into fact by personal assertion alone.

Possible tensions include ducal dignity versus local custom, discretionary coercion versus repeatable procedure, property recovery versus institutional legitimacy, and immediate punishment versus long-term regional cooperation.

Refusal, delay, legal disagreement, incomplete performance, and inability are not automatically rebellion. Enforcement may require officers, witnesses, records, routes, garrisons, local cooperation, and recovery after action.

## 29. Crown and Cross-Realm Interfaces

Robert's Capetian birth gives Burgundy a high-status relationship with France but not shared administration. France may own Crown-facing recognition, requests, ceremonies, arbitration, kinship interpretation, diplomatic posture, and public knowledge from its side. Burgundy owns Robert's response, Burgundy's internal capacity, and Burgundy's implementation.

The County of Burgundy remains externally owned and separate. Similar naming does not create shared treasury, government, claims, army, route access, or map color.

Anjou-facing contact through Ermengarde, Aquitaine-facing contact through Hildegarde's possible future, and other neighboring relationships remain conditional and directed. Marriage potential does not create alliance. Kinship does not create passage, troops, money, intelligence, or support.

## 35. Conditional Development Paths

### 35.1 Institutional Founder

Robert converts personal rule into records, mandates, repeatable hearings, routes, officers, and succession preparation. The risk is that growing procedure alienates actors who benefited from personal access or ambiguity.

### 35.2 Delegating Patriarch

Robert develops Henry, selected adult children, or regional principals through bounded responsibility. The benefit is continuity and wider execution. The risk is independent power, conflicting households, and disputed recognition.

### 35.3 Forceful Regional Arbiter

Robert uses credible military and political pressure to settle bounded conflicts. The path can secure routes and service but may create long-lived grievance, institutional opposition, exhausted pools, and dependence on personal presence.

### 35.4 Reconciled Patron

Robert repairs relationships through restitution, patronage, protection, and institution-specific agreement. Reconciliation does not erase old memory or transfer church property and jurisdiction to the duke.

### 35.5 Crown Partner

Robert works with France as a senior cadet prince while preserving Burgundy's internal ownership. Service may produce recognition, mediation, or security, but it can also create demands and suspicion.

### 35.6 Autonomous Cadet

Robert minimizes Crown involvement and strengthens Burgundy's separate networks. This may improve autonomy while reducing arbitration, kinship leverage, or external support.

### 35.7 Overextended Founder

Robert fights, travels, intervenes, and decides too much personally. Immediate visibility rises while household, succession, garrisons, routes, institutional settlement, and recovery weaken.

### 35.8 Interrupted or Recovered Rule

Illness, injury, captivity, defeat, isolation, or political settlement may narrow Robert's role. Recovery can involve restitution, mandate reform, successor preparation, strategic withdrawal, household reconstruction, or acceptance of durable regional pluralism.

## 48. Final Design Summary

Robert I is Burgundy's opening founder-duke, the strongest available source of immediate direction and the greatest concentration risk in the Realm. His long tenure supports serious political and military experience, but not omnipotence, moral certainty, institutional unity, or a ready-made succession. The player's central task is to use Robert's remaining authority to make Burgundy governable, defensible, and transferable while preserving several credible futures.

The profile is successful only if Robert can be formidable without generating free capacity, controversial without becoming a caricature, dynastically central without scripting Hugh I, Capetian without making Burgundy French property, and historically grounded without turning later outcomes into orders.
