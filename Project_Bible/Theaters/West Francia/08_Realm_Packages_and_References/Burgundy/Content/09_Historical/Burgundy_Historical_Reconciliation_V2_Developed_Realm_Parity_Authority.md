## 1. Reconciliation Verdict
Burgundy begins on 1 January 1062 as a distinct Capetian cadet duchy whose ducal dignity is real, whose relationship to the French Crown is politically meaningful, and whose internal reach must not be mistaken for a later centralized territorial state. Robert I is the principal ducal anchor candidate. The opening problem is not whether Burgundy exists as a polity. The opening problem is whether an aging, forceful founder-duke and a still-developing dynastic succession can convert title legitimacy, household continuity, regional cooperation, ecclesiastical relationships, castles, settlements, routes, resources, and armed service into durable rule.
**Campaign fantasy:** Hold a royal bloodline outside the royal household.
**Developed identity:** The Cadet Duchy.
**Modern Medieval II formula:** Medieval II campaign spine + finite regional capacity + cadet-dynastic politics + institutional consequence + historical uncertainty + real-time battle consequence.
Burgundy must not become a reskinned France, Aquitaine, Normandy, or Flanders. France starts with sacred Crown dignity and narrow direct machinery. Aquitaine starts with broad connected titles and negotiated composite authority. Normandy starts with coordinated opportunity-capable ducal power. Burgundy starts with cadet Capetian legitimacy, founder-age pressure, household succession risk, regional ducal consolidation, and a continuing need to preserve autonomy without severing useful Crown relations.

## 9. Core Historical Thesis
Burgundy is a cadet-princely Realm created through Capetian family settlement and sustained through a ducal line whose legitimacy is related to, but not consumed by, the Crown. The duchy is neither an independent kingdom nor a royal province. Its political life turns on ducal title continuity, household command, regional lordship, institutional recognition, property access, negotiated military service, and the ability to keep succession credible when the founder generation weakens.
The central opening contradiction is:
```text
Capetian legitimacy and long ducal tenure
+ regional ducal dignity
+ potential military and institutional weight
- uncertain exact household and succession state
- uneven direct possession and enforcement
- founder-age and generational vulnerability
= a powerful duchy whose continuity must be made durable
```
## 10. Political Topology
### 10.1 The duke
Robert I is the principal title and household anchor candidate. Ducal authority may include strategy, arbitration, protection, justice, patronage, war leadership, confirmation, and negotiation where supported. It does not create universal property or obedience.
### 10.2 The ducal household
The household should coordinate access, correspondence, ceremony, family protection, officers, guards, hospitality, stores, travel, and continuity. It is not the duchy, dynasty, treasury, army, or one fixed palace.
### 10.3 The ducal dynasty
The Capetian House of Burgundy carries cadet royal descent and succession claims. Dynasty membership does not by itself determine title eligibility, readiness, recognition, residence, command, or loyalty.
### 10.4 Regional lordships and castles
Local lords, castellans, households, communities, and institutions preserve their own property, service, jurisdiction, and military interfaces. Disagreement does not equal rebellion.
### 10.5 Ecclesiastical institutions
Bishops, monasteries, churches, chapters, and religious houses can support, mediate, witness, resist, litigate, preserve records, and shape reception. They must not be flattened into one Church approval score.
### 10.6 Towns, markets, and routes
Dijon and other candidate places may have political, economic, military, route, or institutional importance. Importance does not prove ducal possession, exact settlement class, current residence, or guaranteed income.
### 10.7 Crown relationship
The French Crown can recognize, negotiate, summon, mediate, expect service, or contest. Crown relationship does not make Burgundy a royal demesne province.
### 10.8 Eastern and external interfaces
The County of Burgundy, imperial spaces, Champagne, Nevers, Autun, Langres, Auxerre, and other neighbors require separate owner and chronology review. Geographic adjacency does not create ownership or hostility.

### 13.6 Military role
Robert may be an experienced military-political ruler candidate. Experience does not create a standing army, current command assignment, guaranteed tactical superiority, or unlimited readiness.
