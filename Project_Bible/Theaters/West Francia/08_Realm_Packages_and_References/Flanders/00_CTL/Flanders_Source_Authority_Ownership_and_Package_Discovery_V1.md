## 6. Flanders and Capetian France

Flanders is deeply entwined with Capetian France at the January 1062 opening, but the relationship must remain a typed political, dynastic, governmental, and diplomatic interface rather than a merger of ownership.

```yaml
capetian_entwinement:
  Adela_of_France:
    role: Countess_of_Flanders_and_Capetian_dynastic_bridge
    effect: household_continuity_patronage_mediation_and_French_communication
    boundary: does_not_create_unrestricted_French_or_Flemish_command
  Baldwin_V:
    role: Count_of_Flanders_and_senior_regency_actor_for_Philip_I
    effect: prestige_access_information_mediation_and_royal_obligation
    boundary: regency_does_not_create_ownership_of_France_or_transferable_royal_authority
  Philip_I:
    role: underage_King_of_the_Franks
    ownership: France
    boundary: external_sovereign_not_Flanders_controlled
  Anne_of_Kiev:
    role: queen_mother_and_France_owned_minority_government_actor
    ownership: France
    boundary: cooperation_with_Baldwin_does_not_create_mutual_command
  Crown_Flanders:
    role: French_facing_title_and_superior_lord_relationship
    boundary: holding_specific_obligations_do_not_apply_automatically_to_imperial_facing_lands
```

Modernization must therefore preserve both truths:

```text
Flanders is entwined with Capetian France.
Flanders is not a locally authored copy of France.
```

France owns Philip I, Anne of Kiev, French Crown state, French government, royal commands, French resources, French consent, French reverse relationships, and France-side interpretation of Baldwin's regency. Flanders owns its local dynastic relationship, its own obligations and costs, its received information, and its proposals through validated external interfaces.
