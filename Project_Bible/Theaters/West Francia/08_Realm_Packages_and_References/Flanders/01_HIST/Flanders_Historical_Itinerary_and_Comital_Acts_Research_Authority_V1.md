## 1. Executive Verdict

This authority establishes a disciplined research surface for reconstructing the movements, presences, acts, witnesses, titles, offices, beneficiaries, and institutional relationships of Baldwin V and the Flemish comital household near the January 1062 opening. It does not claim a complete day-by-day itinerary and does not invent lost charters, dates, places, witnesses, seals, subscriptions, or institutional transactions.

The current evidence supports a polycentric and itinerant model of comital government rather than a single permanent-capital model. The strongest safe implementation use is therefore a network of attested and research-pending act locations, each carrying provenance, confidence, date precision, role, and owner routing. Exact opening location remains an Opening State decision after evidence and owner review.

## 6. Documented Research Foundation

### 6.1 Comital charter corpus

A modern diplomatic study by Georges Declercq addresses the charters of the first counts of Flanders through 1071, with particular attention to records for Saint Peter's Abbey at Ghent. Its abstract describes the transition from private-charter forms toward princely diplomas, the institutional concentration of surviving material at Saint Peter's, and the need to address authenticity and the introduction of the comital seal. This makes the study a priority locator and methodological authority, not a substitute for record-by-record verification.

Source locator: Georges Declercq, "From Private Charter to Princely Diploma. The Charters of the First Counts of Flanders (especially for St Peter's Abbey, Ghent)," translated from the study published in Bulletin de la Commission royale d'Histoire 176/2 (2010), pp. 41-77.

### 6.2 Saint Peter's Abbey, Ghent

The University of Cambridge Kemble resource identifies the mid-eleventh-century Liber Traditionum as an important source for the early endowment history of Saint Peter's Abbey and points to editions and critical studies of the abbey's charters. This confirms Saint Peter's as a major research node for documentary transmission, beneficiary memory, and Anglo-Flemish institutional connections. It does not establish that every preserved instrument was issued at Ghent or in Baldwin V's physical presence.

### 6.3 Baldwin V chronological frame

The Henry Project identifies Baldwin V as count from 1035 to 1067, records the 1030 reconciliation at Oudenaarde/Audenarde after rebellion against Baldwin IV, and supplies cited annalistic and genealogical references for his death and burial at Lille in 1067. These facts frame the reign and demonstrate the need to separate pre-opening, opening-adjacent, and later-history evidence.

### 6.4 Regency and external travel pressure

Modern summaries consistently place Baldwin V in an important French regency role after 1060. This authority treats that role as a research driver for examining presence, absence, delegation, witness networks, and acts involving the French royal court. It does not infer an exact 1062 itinerary or transfer ownership of French royal acts to Flanders.

## 12. Polycentric Government Translation

The research authority supports a government model in which Lille, Bruges, Ghent, Saint-Omer, Oudenaarde, Ename, and external court locations can carry different functions and confidence levels. A place may be a residence, act location, archive, beneficiary center, assembly site, route node, or later memorial location. These categories must not be collapsed.

```yaml
player_facing_translation:
  safe:
    - show_attested_or_research_supported_comital_presence
    - explain_why_a_place_matters
    - expose_documentary_confidence_and_source_type

    - distinguish_local_and_external_act_contexts

    - publish_complete_1062_itinerary_without_evidence
```
