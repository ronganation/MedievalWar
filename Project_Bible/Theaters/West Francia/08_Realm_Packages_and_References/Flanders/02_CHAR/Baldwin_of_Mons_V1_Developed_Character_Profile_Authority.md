## 3. Developed Profile Verdict

Baldwin of Mons begins as the adult eldest son and expected successor of Baldwin V, husband and political partner of Richilde, and an established ruler in Hainaut. He is not a waiting courtier, a free Flemish general, or Baldwin VI of Flanders at opening.

**His central campaign tension is proven independent capacity versus orderly inherited continuity.**

His Hainaut position makes him more credible as heir because he already governs, negotiates, and faces imperial-border pressures. The same position prevents Flanders from treating him as an ordinary household subordinate. Every Flemish assignment must respect Richilde's rights, Hainaut's defense, local consent, routes, resources, and the possibility that repeated service creates a stronger personal following.

## 5. Core Character Thesis

**Can an already established territorial ruler become the prepared successor to Flanders without sacrificing Hainaut, subordinating Richilde, provoking Robert, or turning transition preparation into premature co-rule?**

Baldwin's strength is that he can arrive with experience rather than merely a claim. His weakness is divided obligation. Flanders benefits when he is consulted and tested, but Hainaut pays whenever he, its forces, officers, money, or attention are pulled westward.

The profile remains valid if Baldwin never succeeds, if Hainaut remains separate, if he refuses a Flemish command, if a succession dispute is peacefully settled, or if campaign events create a different lawful future.

## 30. Capture, Incapacity, Death, and Removal

If Baldwin is captured, incapacitated, disgraced, absent, or dead, Hainaut government, Richilde's position, children, forces, offices, household, property, and succession require owner review. Flanders' succession certainty falls, but Robert does not automatically become heir and Hainaut does not revert to Flanders.

If Baldwin V is removed, Baldwin of Mons does not automatically install himself. Title succession, recognition, presence, security, Hainaut continuity, seals, oaths, institutions, castellans, and possible opposition must be resolved by their owners.
