## 1. Developed-Realm Verdict

Flanders begins with a compact but unusually consequential ruling family whose members connect the county to the Capetian minority government, Hainaut, Normandy, French-facing lordship, imperial-facing holdings, ecclesiastical foundations, castellany government, trade routes, and succession. The faction must not reduce this network to Baldwin V plus passive relatives. It must also not overcorrect by turning kinship into obedience, later history into destiny, or external family members into Flemish assets.

Baldwin V is the sole normal opening ruler perspective. Adela is a principal political, household, dynastic, documentary, patronage, and mediation actor. Baldwin of Mons is the adult heir and a ruler in Hainaut whose cooperation requires Hainaut owner state. Richilde is a rights-bearing Hainaut principal whose authority cannot be collapsed into her husband. Robert is an adult younger dynast with credible capacity but no predetermined command, territory, marriage, grievance, epithet, or rebellion. Matilda is a Normandy-owned duchess and cross-realm connector whose household and decisions remain external.
Arnulf III and Baldwin II are admitted as individually reviewed underage next-generation profile candidates. Arnulf is the senior child-formation and conditional Flemish-succession stakeholder. Baldwin is the junior independent-formation and alternate-continuity stakeholder. Neither receives active title, claim, household binding, guardianship, relationship, command, or Opening State activation here.

The player-facing character fantasy is **distributed family capacity under divided jurisdiction**. The player gains exceptional access and several capable adults, but every deployment creates opportunity costs, relationship effects, local affinities, external-owner dependencies, and succession consequences.

No character profile owns active title possession, office occupancy, command, force, household state, property, directed relationships, knowledge, promises, obligations, Memory, Momentum, Emergence, or Opening State bindings. Those remain separately owned.

## 16. Family and Realm Gameplay Loops

### 16.1 Regency and Presence Loop

Baldwin's French service can produce access, information, recognition, and mediation while reducing home presence. Adela, household officers, castellans, and institutions can preserve continuity only within mandates, resources, routes, and current capacity. Overdelegation creates delay, concealment, local affinity, and inconsistent enforcement rather than a generic control penalty.

### 16.2 Heir and Hainaut Loop

Baldwin of Mons provides succession depth and eastern coordination. Cooperation depends on Richilde, Hainaut rights, local defense, compensation, information, and separate owner state. Treating Hainaut as a passive recruitment pool weakens the relationship and practical availability.

### 16.3 Robert Assignment Loop

Robert can solve immediate capability gaps. Every command or mission also creates experience, contacts, followers, promises, reward expectations, and possibly an independent affinity. Denial of responsibility protects centralized simplicity but can create grievance or external opportunity seeking. Neither path guarantees conflict.

### 16.4 Norman Family Loop

Matilda gives Flanders a valuable path to Normandy. The quality of the route depends on directed relationships, message delivery, Norman household priorities, and the subject of the request. Family connection cannot commit Norman ships or armies.

### 16.5 Castellan Oversight Loop

Role-based local actors make itinerant rule possible. Trust accelerates response. Inspection improves information but consumes ruler or officer time. Neglect can hide readiness problems, toll abuse, institutional conflict, or local capture.

### 16.6 Ecclesiastical Patronage Loop

Baldwin and Adela may support institutions, records, education, relief, and dynastic memory. Patronage consumes real property or revenue and strengthens an institution with its own interests. Protection can become advocacy abuse if custody, accounts, jurisdiction, and review are weak.

### 16.7 Succession Preparation Loop

Recognition of the adult heir can stabilize expectation while sharpening questions about Hainaut, Robert, Adela's household role, Richilde's rights, and external recognition. Succession confidence is not title transfer, and preparation does not force history.

### 16.8 Individually Reviewed Child Formation Loop

Arnulf and Baldwin II replace the prior undifferentiated child-review placeholder at profile-definition scope. Shared care may conserve capacity, but it cannot mirror development, erase individual needs, create rivalry, or convert either child into a title token.
