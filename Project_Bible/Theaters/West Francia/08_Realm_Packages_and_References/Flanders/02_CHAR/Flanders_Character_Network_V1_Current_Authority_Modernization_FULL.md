## 1. Network Verdict

Implement the January 1062 Flemish political network as a sparse but strategically dense set of directed, asymmetric records connecting family, household, succession, Hainaut, Normandy, the French minority government, comital officers, castellanies, towns, markets, and ecclesiastical institutions. The network must explain who can approach whom, what each participant may know, which expectations or obligations exist, how communication travels, and why cooperation can succeed, fail, narrow, or recover.

The network is not one mutual opinion value. Family connection does not create obedience. Marriage does not merge households or realms. Access does not create knowledge. Knowledge does not create truth. A relationship does not create a promise, obligation, command, title, property right, military force, or guaranteed response.

Flanders gains exceptional reach from Baldwin V, Adela, Baldwin of Mons, Richilde, Robert, and Matilda. That reach is powerful precisely because it is conditional. French regency access depends on France-owned state. Hainaut cooperation depends on Hainaut rights and capacity. Norman communication depends on Matilda and Normandy-owned interests. Local execution depends on officers, institutions, routes, evidence, resources, and valid mandates.
Arnulf III and Baldwin II now enter the definition-only network as underage Hainaut-associated child nodes. Their edges model care, access, formation, recognition, sibling contact, and bounded senior-family interfaces. They do not create active feelings, guardianship, custody, household placement, claims, promises, obligations, or external-owner state.
