## 1. Developed Dynasty and Household Verdict

The House of Flanders opens with old comital legitimacy, Capetian marriage, an adult Hainaut-based heir, a Norman ducal marriage, a capable adult younger son, a living next generation, extensive ecclesiastical patronage, and an itinerant household able to govern a compact but institutionally dense county. This is exceptional dynastic reach, not unity of ownership.

Baldwin V and Adela head the senior Flemish comital household. Baldwin of Mons and Richilde head a separately owned Hainaut household. Matilda belongs to the Norman ducal household. Robert is a Flemish dynast whose exact opening establishment, resources, residence, command, and marriage position remain unresolved. Arnulf III and Baldwin II are individually admitted next-generation candidates. Both remain underage Hainaut-associated persons whose exact life state, residence, custody, guardianship, education, maintenance, household binding, and title-specific recognition require owner receipts. Arnulf carries senior-child formation and conditional Flemish succession relevance. Baldwin carries independent younger-child formation and alternate-continuity relevance. Neither is activated here.

The dynasty game is not a linear succession ladder. It is a negotiated system of kinship, household membership, protection, dignity, service, access, maintenance, marriage, dower, property expectation, patronage, guardianship, education, fosterage, succession recognition, branch formation, Memory, and external-owner relationships. Each instrument creates capability and dependency. No family connection automatically creates obedience, alliance, treasury access, military command, inheritance, or shared information.

## 22. Dynasty and Household Gameplay Loops

### 22.1 Succession Preparation Loop

Clarity can reassure institutions, markets, officers, and external audiences but may sharpen Robert's exclusion and force earlier decisions about Hainaut. Flexibility preserves Baldwin V's discretion but leaves more unresolved if incapacity arrives suddenly.

### 22.2 Robert Settlement Loop

Command, maintenance income, marriage, diplomacy, and external opportunity each provide dignity in a different form. Every settlement creates costs, witnesses, expectations, affinities, and possible branch formation. No settlement guarantees loyalty and no delay guarantees hostility.

### 22.3 Hainaut Partnership Loop

The heir and Richilde add succession depth and eastern capacity. Joint action requires recognition of separate household, rights, resources, protection floors, and local obligations. Flanders cannot consume Hainaut as an army pool.

### 22.4 Norman Marriage Loop

Matilda's marriage provides access, prestige, mediation, and possible deterrence. Normandy remains independent, and family requests can fail, narrow, or carry conditions.

### 22.5 Household Itinerary Loop

Moving the household increases presence and information in one place while consuming transport, hospitality, market supply, and officer attention. Remaining too long imposes local burdens. Moving too quickly weakens case handling and continuity.

### 22.6 Patronage Loop

Patronage produces memory, literacy, legitimacy, hospitality, relief, and mediation while transferring or reserving real resources and strengthening institutions with their own jurisdictions and interests.

### 22.7 Child Formation Loop

Arnulf and Baldwin II require separate formation plans even when sharing caregivers, tutors, or residence. Formation may shape later options but never scripts adult behavior, title, loyalty, rivalry, or competence.

### 22.8 Household Capacity Loop

A larger household improves display, guard, administration, diplomacy, and resilience while increasing expenditure and supply burden. A lean household conserves resources but limits protection, hospitality, records, and itinerant reach.
