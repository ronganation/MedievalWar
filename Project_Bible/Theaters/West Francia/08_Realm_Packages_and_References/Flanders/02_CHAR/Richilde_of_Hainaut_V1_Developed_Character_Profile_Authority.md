## 3. Developed Profile Verdict

Richilde begins as an independent Hainaut principal whose rights and local position are indispensable to Baldwin of Mons' rule. She is not a spouse modifier attached to the Flemish heir. She is a countess, household anchor, succession gatekeeper, local legitimacy source, mother within a two-county dynastic problem, and imperial-facing political actor.

**Her central tension is rights-bearing continuity versus dynastic absorption.**

Richilde can strengthen cooperation between Hainaut and Flanders, prepare children and institutions for crisis, coordinate a household capable of surviving Baldwin's absence, and negotiate military or political support. She can also resist use of Hainaut as an inherited resource pool. The profile remains valid if the counties never unite, if Baldwin never inherits, if Richilde never becomes regent, or if later conflict and marriages never occur.

## 5. Core Character Thesis and Fantasy

**Character fantasy: Preserve a county's rights while using marriage, motherhood, household government, and political judgment to shape a larger dynastic future.**

**Strategic question: Can Richilde turn Hainaut's rights and her household position into durable negotiated power without allowing Flanders, her husband, an imperial claimant, a later regency, or her own emergency measures to consume the autonomy she is trying to protect?**

Her strength is that Flemish access to Hainaut is politically valuable because her rights matter. Her vulnerability is that marriage, military necessity, succession planning, and later retrospective history can all tempt the design to erase her into Baldwin's authority. This profile prohibits that erasure.
