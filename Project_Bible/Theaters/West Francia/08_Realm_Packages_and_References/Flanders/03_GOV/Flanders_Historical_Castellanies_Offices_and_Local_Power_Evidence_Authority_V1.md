## 1. Executive Verdict

Flanders requires castellanies to function as first-class local government nodes rather than decorative castle slots. The controlling sources support a compact, polycentric, itinerant county in which fortified centers connect defense, custody, detention, reporting, local justice, toll oversight, stores, route protection, and muster preparation. They do not support treating every castle as an owned garrison, every castellany as a uniform district, every castellan as a named hereditary lord, or every local right as comital property.

This authority therefore creates a strong evidence and integration model around role-based local actors, bounded mandates, separate ledgers, local constituencies, information reliability, enforcement capacity, and restoration after abuse. Bruges, Ghent, Lille, Saint-Omer, Kortrijk, Douai, Cassel, Ypres, Oudenaarde, and Ename remain distinct research portfolios. Personal names, exact office tenure, exact garrisons, exact revenues, exact district boundaries, and exact opening conditions remain unknown unless verified by record-level evidence and accepted by the proper owner.

The resulting campaign model preserves Medieval II clarity while adding deeper political causality. A castle can anchor defense and administration without collapsing title, property, office, custody, jurisdiction, command, revenue, and loyalty into one object.
