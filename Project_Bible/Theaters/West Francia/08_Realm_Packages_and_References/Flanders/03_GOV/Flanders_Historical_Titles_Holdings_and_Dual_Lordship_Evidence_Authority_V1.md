## 1. Executive Verdict

Flanders in January 1062 must be represented as one comital polity assembled from holdings, rights, offices, superior-lord relationships, jurisdictions, properties, revenues, protections, and practical capacities that do not all share one legal channel. The project may use the conventional distinction between Crown Flanders and Imperial Flanders as a high-level explanatory model, but it must not flatten every site west of the Scheldt into one uniform French obligation or every eastern interest into one uniform imperial fief.

The safe controlling interpretation is a holding-specific dual-lordship topology. The count's French-facing comital title, imperial-facing acquisitions and interfaces, dynastic relationship with Hainaut, local castellanies, ecclesiastical properties, urban rights, waterways, crossings, and disputed edge interests require individual records. Each record must separate title, claim, possession, occupation, property, custody, jurisdiction, revenue, service, recognition, immunity, protection, command, and practical control.

This authority is strong enough to control later title, governance, diplomacy, geography, economy, military, Registry-candidate, and Opening State research. It is not evidence that every holding boundary or obligation is resolved, and it creates no active title, owner, revenue stream, garrison, agreement, or map boundary.

## 4. Historical Foundation

As of the 1062 scenario opening, the internal Flanders historical authorities describe Baldwin V's realm as French-facing in its western and southern core while holding significant imperial-facing eastern lands and rights. The west and east therefore carry different superior-lord interfaces. The same authorities prohibit applying one side's obligations automatically to the other and preserve broad but conditional comital autonomy.

External reference material supports the conventional distinction. Encyclopaedia Britannica describes Crown Flanders as the part held as a fief of the French king, principally west of the Scheldt, and Imperial Flanders as eastern lands held in fealty to the German king within the Empire. These summaries establish the broad distinction, not a complete site-level January 1062 inventory. Record-level inclusion still requires primary or critical-edition evidence.

Baldwin V's reign also follows the eastward expansion and imperial conflicts of Baldwin IV and Baldwin V. This provides historical context for the eastern interface, but a past conquest or treaty cannot be converted automatically into an uncontested opening holding, exact border, collectable due, or available force.

## 8. Dual Lordship Topology

### 8.1 French-facing core

The French-facing core is associated with the count's relationship to the French king. It includes the conventional Crown Flanders category, but the exact legal content of a holding, service, court relationship, or royal reservation must be recorded individually. Baldwin V's position in the minority government of Philip I adds access, prestige, information, mediation, and divided-attention pressures. Regency influence does not grant ownership of the French Crown, Philip I, Anne of Kiev, royal lands, royal commands, or royal resources.

### 8.2 Imperial-facing east

The imperial-facing east includes lands and rights conventionally grouped as Imperial Flanders. Its superior-lord interface runs toward the German king or imperial institutions. Recognition, service, frontier defense, local possession, and contested settlement may differ by place. The presence of an imperial relationship does not make the entire county imperial, and a Flemish claim does not grant access to imperial forces, decisions, or private state.

### 8.3 Hainaut interface

Baldwin of Mons and Richilde form a distinct Hainaut household and polity interface. Their marriage and succession relevance do not make Hainaut an ordinary Flemish holding. Hainaut rights, estates, offices, institutions, forces, routes, treasury, and consent remain Hainaut-owned. Flanders may own only its directed relationship, proposal, expectation, received information, and effects on Flemish succession planning.

### 8.4 Local and institutional lordship

Within both broad superior-lord channels, castles, castellanies, towns, abbeys, chapters, churches, estates, markets, mills, tolls, ferries, bridges, waterways, and communities may have distinct ownership, custody, advocacy, jurisdiction, immunity, and revenue arrangements. The count's general lordship and peacekeeping role do not erase those arrangements.

## 12. Gameplay Translation

The system should expose dual lordship through consequences and choices rather than CK3-style permission locks. Baldwin V may attempt military, fiscal, diplomatic, legal, or infrastructural action, but the result must consider the affected holding's legal basis, superior-lord channel, recognition, local institutions, access, resources, enforcement, and external response.

## 17. Validation Catalogue

### 17.1 Fatal errors

- Treat all of Flanders as held through one superior-lord channel.
- Treat Crown Flanders and Imperial Flanders as exact, uniform runtime containers without site evidence.
- Give Flanders ownership of France, Hainaut, Normandy, imperial actors, or ecclesiastical institutions.
- Merge title, claim, property, possession, custody, jurisdiction, revenue, service, recognition, command, and control.
- Convert a past conquest, marriage, regency role, or later investiture into automatic January 1062 ownership.
- Create exact borders, taxes, forces, garrisons, offices, or obligations without owner evidence.
- Activate an Opening State title or holding through this research authority.

### 17.2 Major errors

- Treat a castle as a garrison or castellany.
- Treat a settlement as wholly comital property.
- Treat patronage or advocacy as ecclesiastical ownership.
- Treat revenue right as collected cash.
- Treat route or waterway existence as transport capacity.
- Treat kinship as alliance, consent, military access, or succession certainty.
- Treat unknown as zero or unimportant.
