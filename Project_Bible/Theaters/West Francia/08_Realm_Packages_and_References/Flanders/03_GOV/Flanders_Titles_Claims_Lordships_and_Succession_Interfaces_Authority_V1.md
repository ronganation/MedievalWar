## 1. Strong Flanders Design Verdict

Flanders requires a dedicated title, claim, lordship, and succession owner because comital rulership, Crown-facing and imperial-facing holdings, the Hainaut succession connection, the Norman marriage connection, castellany custody, household dignity, service rewards, property expectations, and the unsettled position of Robert cannot safely remain implicit across Dynasty, Household, Character, Network, Governance, Law, Historical, and Opening State authorities.

The Flanders title game is **a connected inheritance under divided legal horizons**. Baldwin V possesses strong comital dignity, practical authority, and extensive recognition, but Flanders is not a single modern territorial title that silently absorbs every holding, castle, office, estate, town, institution, Hainaut relationship, Norman relationship, or future inheritance. Crown-facing and imperial-facing components require title-specific and owner-specific reasoning.

Succession is a process rather than a queue. Baldwin of Mons has strong dynastic expectation and independent Hainaut responsibilities. Robert remains an adult younger dynast whose opening establishment, resources, residence, command, marriage position, title expectation, and branch status require owner validation. Matilda is a Flemish dynast in the separately owned Norman ducal household. Adela possesses comital-consort and household dignity without automatic succession, regency, title ownership, or universal government.

No known later accession, partition, conflict, marriage, title grant, political combination, battle, or branch outcome is scripted into January 1062. Arnulf III and Baldwin II are separate underage Hainaut-owned title-interface candidates. Arnulf has bounded future Flemish succession relevance without opening heirship or claim. Baldwin has alternate-continuity relevance without an opening Hainaut title, Flemish claim, or reserve-ruler status.
