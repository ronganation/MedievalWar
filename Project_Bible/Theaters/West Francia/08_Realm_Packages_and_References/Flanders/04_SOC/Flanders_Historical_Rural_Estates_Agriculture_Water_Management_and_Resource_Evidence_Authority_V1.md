## 1. Strong Verdict

Rural Flanders in January 1062 is not empty land surrounding towns. Rural households, estates, mills, fisheries, pasture, meadows, woodland, heath, peat, marshes, waterways, crossings, drainage labor, carts, draft animals, and institutional properties form the material base of comital government, urban provisioning, military supply, recruitment, transport, construction, relief, and recovery.

The opening landscape must remain environmentally and legally varied. Coastal and estuarine zones, wet maritime lowlands, sandy inland districts, river corridors, fortified market hinterlands, and the more rolling southern country do not share one agricultural profile. Property, occupation, production, jurisdiction, rent, labor, service, water access, and protection remain separately owned.

Water management is collective capacity, not a modern technology tree. Ditches, embankments, causeways, crossings, and local drainage knowledge may be essential, but later wateringue institutions, mature polders, large documented drainage finance systems, and later hydraulic landscapes cannot be projected into 1062 without evidence.
