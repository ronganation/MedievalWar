## 1. Strong Flanders Population Verdict

Flanders begins with high relative population density, strong settlement continuity, growing urban concentrations, a rural majority, specialized river and coastal labor, strong institutional capacity, and exact counts unknown. Population strength is distributed among households, communities, estates, towns, castles, religious institutions, routes, waterways, markets, and mobile groups. It is not one universal pool.

The population campaign is **Dense Human Capacity Under Competing Demands**. Agriculture, craft, transport, drainage, fishing, herding, construction, household service, institutional life, market exchange, town watches, garrisons, and field recruitment draw from overlapping but nonidentical people. The player gains durable strength by protecting households, scheduling burdens, preserving specialists, supporting migration and return, and avoiding simultaneous extraction of labor, transport, supply, tax, and recruits from the same community.

Population informs and constrains finite recruitment pools without using Rome: Total War's population-as-army-manpower model. A recruitment order does not subtract an exact soldier from a settlement headcount as the whole system. Recruitment consumes owner-published eligibility, availability, obligation, training, equipment, horse, officer, household, labor, seasonal, and recovery inputs. Named underage people remain Character Lifecycle and Household owner state. Arnulf III and Baldwin II may contribute only separate age-bounded dependency, care, formation, and future-capacity interfaces; neither is a worker, recruit, officer, pool value, or demographic shortcut.
