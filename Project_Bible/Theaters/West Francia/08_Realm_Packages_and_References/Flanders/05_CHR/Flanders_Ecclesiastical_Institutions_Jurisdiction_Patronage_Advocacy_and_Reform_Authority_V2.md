## 1. Authority Purpose and Refined Verdict

This authority preserves and strengthens the complete Flanders ecclesiastical baseline. It defines the Realm-facing interpretation of diocesan plurality, monasteries, collegiate churches, parish life, patronage, advocacy, jurisdiction, reform pressure, property interfaces, information, legitimacy, relief, sanctuary, diplomacy, and institution-specific campaign choices.

Flanders is not governed by one church bar, one bishop, or one comital religious office. It is a dense Latin Christian political landscape in which bishops, abbots, abbesses, chapters, canons, parish clergy, advocates, castellans, patrons, tenants, and communities hold distinct and sometimes overlapping interests. The count and countess may protect, endow, negotiate, witness, mediate, and influence. They do not own the Church, its offices, its communities, its archives, or its property.

The strategic promise is a Modern Medieval II ecclesiastical game built from visible pressure and meaningful choice. Ecclesiastical cooperation can strengthen legitimacy, records, mediation, relief, education, information, and settlement resilience. Abuse can damage trust, standing, memory, access, and political reception. No action is converted into a farmable favor currency.

## 4. Selective Inheritance and Design Constitution

### 4.1 Preserve from Medieval II and its strongest traditions

- visible Papal and ecclesiastical consequences;
- legible requests, warnings, mediation, and sanctions;
- major religious institutions as strategic map actors;
- excommunication as serious pressure rather than automatic deposition;
- religious diplomacy, patronage, pilgrimage, and rare major missions;
- freedom to continue a campaign while accepting political and religious risk;
- clear player choices with map, army, settlement, succession, and diplomatic consequences.

### 4.2 Modernize

- Papal Favor into an explainable derived summary of typed evidence;
- missions into requests with authority, cause, alternatives, deadlines, and consequences;
- clergy from conversion projectiles into people, offices, institutions, communication channels, and bounded agents;
- church buildings from instant favor generators into institutions requiring property, operators, maintenance, recognition, and capacity;
- Catholic conflict into contextual evaluation of treaties, conduct, evidence, knowledge, standing, and reception;
- institutional property into distinct ownership, custody, use, revenue, exemption, and protection records.

### 4.3 Reject

- CK3-style casus belli or Papal permission gates;
- one universal Pope Approval or Church Loyalty truth;
- repeated low-cost gifts, construction, clergy creation, prisoner release, or conversion farming;
- automatic excommunication for Catholic warfare;
- Papal omniscience;
- church land treated as comital land;
- every cleric promoted to a full character;
- later dioceses, orders, procedures, or buildings back-projected into 1062;
- Catholic mechanics copied unchanged onto non-Catholic religious worlds.

## 18. Conduct, Sanctuary, Relief, and Hospitality

Institutional response may read protection or harm to clergy, sanctuary, sacred property, prisoners, settlements, capitulation terms, refugees, and dependents. One mercy action cannot erase unrelated misconduct. Ordinary lawful treatment may prevent loss without granting unlimited favor.

Sanctuary is conditional protection based on institution, law, jurisdiction, communication, custody, and negotiation. It is not absolute immunity and not a license for immediate seizure.

Relief and hospitality consume stores, lodging, personnel, security, and transport. Institutions may provide alms, burial, shelter, mediation, and negotiated store release within capacity. Military occupation of religious space may have tactical value but creates discipline, property, legitimacy, diplomatic, and Memory consequences.

## 19. Relics, Saints, Pilgrimage, Education, and Records

Relics and sacred sites are institutional and evidentiary objects, not inventory bonuses. Movement requires valid custody, authority, route, security, destination, ritual terms, and return conditions.

Pilgrimage requires a character, purpose, cost, route, safe conduct, absence, risk, evidence, and reception. It does not automatically grant favor or reconciliation.

Saint Peter's supports a strong educational interpretation. Saint-Bertin supports an exceptional archival and narrative role. Saint Bavo's supports cult, pilgrimage, local identity, and river-confluence communication. Exact curricula, relic lists, pilgrim numbers, and feast attendance remain bounded.

## 23. Developed Decision Fixtures

### 23.1 Saint-Bertin winter stores

Purchase preserves trust but consumes treasury. Privilege exchange reduces immediate cash but creates a durable expectation. Emergency seizure supplies the army but creates property, legitimacy, standing, and Memory exposure. No option guarantees delivery until stores, route, transport, and institutional consent or valid coercive authority are resolved.

### 23.2 Ghent abbey rivalry

Favoring either house strengthens one relationship and risks harming the other. Mixed inquiry produces better evidence while delaying settlement. The count cannot merge the two abbeys or treat rivalry as a single loyalty score.

### 23.3 Adela's patronage

Messines, Lille, or distributed support create different institutional capacities, obligations, and political signals. Patronage requires real resources and does not create instant Papal standing.

### 23.4 Abusive advocate

Removal, restitution with retention, narrowed mandate, mixed oversight, or support each require evidence and authority. Institutional complaint is not automatically true, while officeholding never immunizes abuse.

### 23.5 Diocesan dispute

Episcopal referral maximizes canonical legitimacy but may be slow. Mixed settlement preserves comital participation but increases coordination burden. Acting solely under the comital peace may resolve violence while leaving spiritual and office issues untouched.

### 23.6 Reform petition
