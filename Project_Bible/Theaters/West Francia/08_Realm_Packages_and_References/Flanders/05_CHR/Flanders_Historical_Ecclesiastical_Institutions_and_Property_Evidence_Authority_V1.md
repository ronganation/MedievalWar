## 1. Strong Verdict

Flanders in January 1062 belongs to Latin Christianity but does not possess one unified Flemish Church, one religion score, or one comital chain of ecclesiastical command. The county crosses several episcopal and institutional spheres. Tournai remains joined with Noyon, Thérouanne shapes the western landscape, and Arras and Cambrai form another southern and eastern interface. Saint-Bertin, Saint Peter's at Ghent, Saint Bavo's at Ghent, Messines, the Lille collegiate church, Aire, Ename, Arras institutions, parish churches, estate chapels, and outside metropolitan or papal endpoints remain separately owned subjects.

The institution, office, officeholder, chapter or community, building, property, privilege, immunity, jurisdiction, patronage, protection, advocacy, archive, school, relic, sanctuary, stores, dependents, routes, and relationships attached to a religious center are not one object. Baldwin V and Adela may protect, endow, confirm, witness, negotiate, mediate, request, and influence where valid. Neither secular actor owns the Church, every appointment, institutional property, canon law, or external ecclesiastical policy.

Ecclesiastical strategy emerges from evidence, jurisdiction, procedure, property, communication, capacity, local reception, and enforcement. It does not become a permission token for war or government. A ruler may act beyond accepted authority, but consequences propagate through institutions, households, markets, communities, archives, Memory, recognition, and practical cooperation.
