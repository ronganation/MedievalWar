## 1. Strong Flanders Logistics Verdict

Flanders must treat supply as an owned, observed, and interruptible chain from production or acquisition through custody, storage, allocation, reservation, loading, transport, transfer, delivery, receipt, distribution, consumption, loss, return, repair, and recovery. Dense settlement, strong markets, short relative distances, rivers, coastal access, and an itinerant comital household create potential resilience and rapid concentration. None creates automatic abundance.

The Flanders campaign problem is concentrated competition. The senior household, castellanies, town watches, markets, abbeys, local communities, bridges, waterworks, repair projects, garrisons, field forces, displaced households, and the Hainaut, France, and Normandy interfaces all compete for food, water, fodder, fuel, carts, draft animals, boats, crews, handlers, skilled labor, storage, escort, access, finance, and time.

Flanders should feel more connected than a sprawling realm, but also more exposed to cascading disruption. Seizing boats damages fishing and trade. Taking draft animals damages harvest and transport. Emptying a castle store exposes local defense. Requisitioning institutional stores damages ownership and cooperation. Concentrating the household burdens hosts and markets. A successful outward march without return support can leave Flanders strategically weaker.

No exact opening source, stock, storage site, capacity, wagon, boat, animal, crew, consumer demand, convoy, endurance limit, spoilage rate, attrition value, repair rate, or recovery rate is created here.
