## 1. Strong Historical Verdict

Flanders in January 1062 is a densely connected and comparatively urbanizing frontier principality whose strength comes from the interaction of rural production, fortified places, markets, abbeys, river landings, coastal labor, craft specialization, transport knowledge, multilingual mediation, and negotiated comital protection. It is not a landscape of later sovereign communes, guild governments, industrial cloth cities, belfry republics, or mass civic armies.

A town is not one corporate actor. A market is not merely a building or income bonus. A charter is not a timeless menu of urban rights. Custom is not uniform statute. A leading inhabitant is not automatically a burgomaster. Collective bargaining may occur through merchants, property holders, craft masters, clergy, estate representatives, officers, innkeepers, warehouse operators, and respected households without creating later municipal sovereignty.

The player-facing system is the governance of interdependent capacity. Towns need rural food, waterways, roads, mills, security, coin, credit, and predictable custom. The comital household needs taxes, tolls, loans, lodging, carts, boats, artisans, intelligence, watches, repairs, and market confidence. Castellans, advocates, collectors, institutions, and communities can cooperate, compete, delay, conceal, petition, negotiate, migrate, or resist. No community is a hive mind, and no extraction can maximize revenue, labor, recruitment, transport, watch service, and production at the same time.
