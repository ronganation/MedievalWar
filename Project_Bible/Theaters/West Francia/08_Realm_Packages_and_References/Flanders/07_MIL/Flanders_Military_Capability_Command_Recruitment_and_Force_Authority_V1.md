## 1. Strong Flanders Military Verdict

Flanders is **The Connected Comital Host**. It is not a large standing army, a cavalry-only faction, an urban militia republic, or an inexhaustible levy state. Its power comes from a strong comital household core, castellany garrisons, differentiated noble and landed service, local-defense communities, fortified crossings, short internal routes, market and craft capacity, river and coastal support, hired specialists, and an experienced ruler able to concentrate otherwise distributed forces.

The military campaign question is: **How much force can Flanders lawfully assemble, equip, move, feed, command, and bring home without uncovering its crossings, consuming its markets, exhausting households, damaging water and transport labor, weakening Hainaut cooperation, or turning a useful dynastic command into an independent power base?**

Flanders wins through prepared defense, rapid concentration, route control, intelligence, bounded command, negotiated external support, and counterattack against overextended enemies. Wealth improves options but creates no troops by itself.

### 8.1 Unit-development rule

Development may change source, obligation, training, equipment, armor, shield, weapon, mounts, officers, formation, battlefield role, supply burden, availability, recovery, regional identity, institutional support, or political meaning. It is not a flat sequence in which every earlier unit disappears. Older or less prestigious units may remain useful because their pools are larger, cheaper, faster to recover, more locally available, less equipment-intensive, or better suited to garrisons and difficult ground.

### 9.1 Recruitment chain

```text
Valid authority and purpose
then eligible source and people
then obligation, contract or voluntary basis
then current household, community and local-defense availability
then pool reservation without double counting
then training cadre and time
then equipment, horses, officers, pay and supply
then route, notice, assembly and inspection
then unit formation and command assignment
then field release, service monitoring, casualty recording, demobilization, return and recovery
```

A building, title, culture label, map owner, cash balance or roster entry alone never creates soldiers.

Flanders doctrine favors defended crossings and strongpoints, short-line concentration, local guides, protected markets, flexible river support, reconnaissance, combined household and regional forces, disciplined reserves, and counterattack against stretched supply. ## 14. Garrisons, Strongpoints, and Field-Force Tradeoffs

The Flanders network depends on defended nodes rather than a single fortress. Lille, Bruges, Ghent, Saint-Omer, Kortrijk, Douai, Cassel, Oudenaarde-Ename and other validated castles have different purposes and owner states. Priority bands in the Opening State describe strategic concern, not exact soldiers.

Garrison release must evaluate custody, minimum defense, prisoners, stores, gates, crossings, local watch, refugees, warning, replacement, relief, route, supply, duration and return. Removing strong troops for a field army can increase mobility while exposing the very route and logistics network that makes concentration possible.

## 15. Siege, Engineering, Occupation, and Restoration

Flanders can fund or contract skilled labor and specialists, but begins with no enormous permanent siege train. Siege operations require a valid target, authority, force, engineers, artisans, labor, tools, materials, transport, supply, intelligence, camp security, weather, access, replacements and post-assault policy.

Battle victory does not transfer every castle, property, abbey, market, toll, office or community relationship. Occupation requires separate custody, government, law, supply, route, institution, population, prisoner, property, compensation, security and restoration receipts. Destructive occupation may produce refugees, damaged pools, broken transport, unavailable markets, institutional grievance and long recovery even after tactical success.

## 16. Maritime and River Military Access

Flanders may negotiate or requisition merchant shipping and local craft through lawful authority, owners, crews, pilots, payment, loading points, weather, tides, routes, escorts, landing rights and destination consent. It has no large standing war fleet at opening. River and coastal workers are not automatically soldiers, and civilian craft removed from fishing, trade or transport create opportunity costs.

```text
Maritime intention
then external and local consent
then vessel and owner availability
then crew and pilot availability
then military passenger and cargo suitability
then loading, tide, weather, route and escort
then destination and landing access
then unloading, beachhead, resupply, relief and return
```

No Norman, English, Zeelandic, Frisian or Hainaut access is automatic.

## 17. Casualties, Prisoners, Demobilization, and Recovery

Casualties affect people, households, pools, officers, equipment, horses, communities, obligations, morale, memory and political expectations through their owners. Wounded personnel require care and time. Captured personnel require custody, information control, exchange, ransom or release decisions. Missing and deserted states require evidence and review. Equipment and horses may be lost, captured, damaged, retained or disputed separately from people.

Demobilization routes forces back into households, garrisons, communities, estates, markets and institutions. It settles service duration, arrears, rewards, compensation, equipment return, animal return, wounded care, prisoner obligations, misconduct, grievances, commendations and surviving cadre. Pool recovery depends on eligible people, households, training, equipment, officers, horses, economy, security, season and time. Elite mounted and specialist pools should generally recover more slowly than common local-defense capacity unless their real support systems remain intact.

## 18. Military Knowledge, AI, and Player Presentation

The player and AI must reason from bounded observations rather than omniscient force totals. Reports may identify known commander presence, last observed force family, approximate size or readiness band, visible baggage, garrison activity, route use, casualties, banners, equipment, local testimony or uncertainty. Stale information remains stale.

The player-facing military summary should answer:
- what force or defense is possible;
- who can authorize and command it;
- where recruits and support would come from;
- what is available, reserved, depleted, blocked or unknown;
- what equipment, horses, officers, routes and supply are missing;
- what home-defense, economic, labor and political costs follow;
- what can be achieved before the season, route or service window changes;
- what release, casualty and recovery burden follows.

AI uses the same typed inputs and cannot bypass pools, equipment, routes, supply, external consent, garrisons, service windows or recovery. It should prefer bounded objectives, preserve key crossings, avoid multi-front overextension, retain a reserve, protect markets, investigate unknown readiness, and demobilize when continued service costs outweigh the objective.

### 19.41.2 Exact Roster Verdict

Flanders receives a compact, purposeful Early Era candidate roster built around scarce household and landed mounted service, limited armored household foot, finite spear and shield infantry, locally bounded town and rural defense, castle and crossing troops, conditional missile specialists, military labor, river and coastal support, and contract-only external forces. It remains strongest when it protects connected strongpoints and concentrates a mixed host for bounded objectives.

The roster is not a clone of Normandy, France, Burgundy or England. Flanders differs through its dense settlement network, castellany structure, Lys-Scheldt and coastal geography, multilingual communities, market and craft potential, divided French and imperial obligations, conditional Hainaut cooperation, and the need to avoid stripping transport, waterwork, food, town-defense and garrison capacity.

### 19.41.11 Recruitment, Replenishment, and Reconstitution

```text
Admitted unit definition
then valid source and current controller
then eligible finite pool
then household, population, labor and local-defense review
then obligation, contract, consent or emergency authority
then officer, cadre and training availability
then equipment, mount, tool, ammunition and specialist availability
then finance, route, muster, season and supply review
then personnel and material reservations
then assembly, training, equipping and inspection
then unit-instance request
then force or garrison assignment through the military owner
then pool, obligation and owner commitments reconciled
```

Source-pool recovery, field-unit replenishment, wounded or dispersed veteran recovery, and full unit reconstitution remain separate. Replenishment integrates compatible people and equipment into an existing unit. Reconstitution rebuilds a unit whose cohesion or viable cadre has failed. Neither is free healing or an automatic percentage.

Disbandment returns only living, reachable and legally releasable personnel, surviving equipment, mounts and specialists after travel, custody, pay, loss and recovery are reconciled. No instant pool refund occurs.
