## 1. Opening Verdict

Flanders begins as a wealthy, compact, and highly connected county whose strength depends on maintaining the links among comital households, castellanies, markets, abbeys, rivers, crossings, and foreign relationships.

Baldwin V is one of the strongest princes in the northern French world and serves alongside Queen Mother Anne in the government of the young Philip I of France. That role grants prestige, access, and diplomatic influence, but divides Baldwin's attention between France and Flanders. The county's western core faces the French crown, while eastern lands and rights face imperial authority. Baldwin's eldest son rules Hainaut through Richilde. His younger son Robert remains useful, capable, and unsettled. His daughter Matilda connects the dynasty to Normandy.

The opening campaign is not a crisis already decided. Flanders is prosperous, defensible, and influential, but its advantages depend on choices made during the first Winter:

- where Baldwin directs personal attention;
- how the household governs during his absence;
- whether the eastern frontier receives stores, recognition, or restraint;
- how markets and infrastructure are protected during mobilization;
- how succession and Robert's place are prepared without creating conflict;
- how Hainaut, France, Normandy, and ecclesiastical institutions are engaged without assuming obedience.
