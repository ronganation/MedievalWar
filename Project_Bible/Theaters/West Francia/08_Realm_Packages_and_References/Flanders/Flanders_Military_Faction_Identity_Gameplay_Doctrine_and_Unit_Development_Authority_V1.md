## 1. Authority Purpose and Hierarchy

This authority does not replace the general military authority. It governs how Flanders military candidates are interpreted, compared, named, matured, merged, separated, deferred, or rejected before any unit profile becomes canonical.

```text
The Connected March = realm-wide strategic identity
The Count's Web = player-facing military-development fantasy
The Connected Comital Host = force-assembly and battlefield doctrine
Candidate Family = design possibility
Mature Unit Profile = evidence-backed playable definition
Canonical Unit = Registry-admitted definition after owner gates
```

Where this authority conflicts with the controlling military authority, the controlling military authority wins. Where a unit concept conflicts with historical evidence, ownership, opening boundaries, or a mature faction comparator, the unit concept must change.

## 2. Strong Identity Verdict

Flanders is a compact, wealthy, highly connected county whose title is smaller than its practical political reach. Flanders does not win through the largest territory, the largest standing army, a single dominant military arm, unlimited civic infantry, or effortless elite replacement. Flanders wins by preserving a network of households, castellanies, settlements, markets, crossings, rivers, roads, coastal approaches, obligations, and external relationships, then concentrating a mixed host at the decisive place before a larger or more dispersed enemy can respond.

### 2.1 Realm identity

**The Connected March** remains the frozen package identity.

### 2.2 Military-development identity

**The Count's Web** describes the player's military problem: every strong field force is assembled from connected but separately burdened sources. The player must decide which threads may be pulled into war without breaking the web at home.

### 2.3 Force doctrine

**The Connected Comital Host** describes the assembled force: a scarce comital and household spearhead, a dependable castellany and landed-service backbone, regional and communal infantry, strongpoint and crossing defenders, bounded missile and light support, technical and transport support, conditional mercenaries, and separately owned allied contingents.

### 2.4 Player promise

The player begins with influence, wealth, dynastic reach, defended nodes, and expansion opportunities, but not a preassembled national army. Flanders can grow through conquest, protection, negotiated submission, recognition, inheritance, dynastic settlement, intervention, strategic access, or influence over neighboring powers. Military development must support several campaign approaches rather than force a single scripted conquest route.

## 4. Five Pillars of Gameplay

### 4.1 Pillar One: Concentration Without Centralization

Flanders military capacity is distributed. The comital household, castellanies, landed households, regional communities, towns, crossings, coastal and river communities, institutions, contractors, and external allies remain distinct sources.

**Player behavior:** assemble a useful host by coordinating sources rather than purchasing a complete army from one settlement.

**Benefits:** flexible mixed forces, rapid concentration across secure short corridors, several viable force compositions.

**Costs:** garrison exposure, local-defense loss, labor pressure, service-duration disputes, transport competition, and slower source recovery after casualties.

**Unit-development requirement:** every unit profile must identify its real source, competing burdens, home-defense floor, availability, service reach, and recovery path.

### 4.2 Pillar Two: Defend the Web, Then Strike

Flanders is defensive in preparation and aggressive at the decision point. Strong battle plans protect or exploit crossings, fortified nodes, river approaches, roads, causeways, towns, constrained wet ground, short reinforcement routes, and prepared reserves.

**Preferred battle sequence:** observe, delay, channel, hold, degrade, counter-concentrate, commit the household or mounted reserve, pursue selectively, then restore exposed nodes.

**Unit-development requirement:** the roster needs reliable line holders, strongpoint specialists, route defenders, useful missile and light support, and scarce decisive reserves. It must not become a passive wall faction or a cavalry-only shock faction.

### 4.3 Pillar Three: Small Elite Core, Broad Conditional Middle

The comital and household elite is powerful but scarce. The main playable identity must live in the middle of the roster: castellany professionals, service cavalry, regional spear and shield infantry, town and crossing defenders, bowmen, scouts, and locally sourced support.

**Unit-development requirement:** middle-roster units must be fun, distinct, and dependable. Elite units may be decisive but cannot carry the whole faction. Low-tier units must retain practical local-defense, cost, terrain, or recovery value.

### 4.4 Pillar Four: Wealth Buys Readiness, Not People

Money may improve equipment completion, repairs, contractors, engineering, carts, animals, campaign preparation, muster reliability, and endurance. Money does not instantly create trained knights, experienced sergeants, officers, military horses, cooperative communities, foreign consent, or recovered population.

**Unit-development requirement:** cost, equipment, pool size, replacement, training, officers, horses, and service must remain separate. Expensive units are not automatically available; cheap units are not automatically disposable.

### 4.5 Pillar Five: Political Expansion Matters as Much as Conquest

Flanders can expand influence through war, protection, negotiated settlement, claims, marriage, inheritance, overlordship, royal service, corridor control, dynastic cooperation, and external intervention.

**Unit-development requirement:** the roster must excel at bounded campaigns, relief, deterrence, concentration, escort, siege support, intervention, and holding strategic nodes. The faction should be less comfortable with indefinite deep occupation, mass replacement, or permanent campaigning far from its network.

## 5. Campaign and Strategic Personality

### 5.1 Opening posture

Flanders begins as a normal playable realm with strong political reach and meaningful military possibilities, but no mandatory first conquest and no single required rebel-settlement route. Nearby opportunity may take the form of an exposed strongpoint, disputed lordship, vulnerable corridor, protection request, weak neighboring ruler, French royal intervention, Hainaut coordination, imperial-facing recognition dispute, coastal access question, or internal consolidation.

### 5.2 Expansion styles

### 5.3 Strategic risk

Flanders has limited depth. Loss of a major crossing, fortified node, market corridor, or concentration route can damage several systems at once. The faction should feel powerful when connected and increasingly brittle when its network is severed.

## 6. Battlefield Doctrine

### 6.1 Battlefield identity

Flanders fields a mixed defensive-offensive host. The army establishes a stable line, uses terrain and strongpoint relationships, preserves a scarce reserve, and wins through timed concentration rather than raw numerical superiority.

### 6.2 Preferred engagements

- relief battles near forts or settlements;
- battles around bridges, ferries, roads, causeways, river margins, and constrained fields;
- defense of markets, towns, camps, baggage, and strategic approaches;
- bounded offensives with secure routes and defined objectives;
- counterattacks against overextended forces;
- escort, interception, and corridor-security operations.

### 6.3 Unfavorable engagements

- prolonged campaigns without secure supply and return routes;
- wide open battles where superior enemy cavalry can maneuver freely;
- wars of attrition against realms with greater strategic depth;
- distant occupations requiring permanent large garrisons;
- repeated elite losses;
- naval war requiring a standing combat fleet;
- simultaneous stripping of garrisons, towns, households, and labor sources.

### 6.4 Battle rhythm

```yaml
battle_rhythm:
  Phase_1: ObserveScreenAndSecureApproaches
  Phase_2: AnchorOnLineStrongpointOrTerrain
  Phase_3: AbsorbChannelAndDisruptEnemyAction
  Phase_4: CounterConcentrateRegionalAndCastellanyForces
  Phase_5: CommitHouseholdInfantryOrMountedReserve
  Phase_6: PursueSelectivelyPreserveFormationAndRoute
  Phase_7: RecoverCasualtiesRestoreGarrisonsAndReleaseSources
```

## 7. Core Strengths

### 7.1 Connected concentration

Secure short routes and strongpoints permit faster concentration than a more dispersed realm, but only when route, crossing, command, and supply owners confirm access.

### 7.2 Dependable infantry ecosystem

Flanders should gain strength from the combination of spear lines, shield infantry, castellany professionals, town defenders, crossing specialists, household foot, and missile support rather than one universally superior infantry unit.

### 7.3 Decisive but scarce reserve

Comital knights, mounted sergeants, service knights, and armored household foot provide battle-winning intervention. Their scarcity, cost, horses, officers, and recovery prevent routine sacrifice.

### 7.4 Positional flexibility

Several formations may perform above their nominal open-field quality when defending gates, streets, crossings, river approaches, camps, fortifications, or constrained ground.

### 7.5 Campaign support depth

Scouts, guides, engineers, artisans, pioneers, crossing personnel, boatmen, pilots, transport workers, and contractors improve the faction's ability to prepare and sustain bounded operations without becoming universal combat units.

## 8. Core Weaknesses

### 8.1 Limited strategic depth

Key losses matter. Flanders cannot casually trade a chain of important nodes for time.

### 8.2 Elite scarcity and slow reconstitution

Household cavalry, armored retainers, trained sergeants, officers, engineers, missile specialists, and suitable horses recover slowly.

### 8.3 Overextension pressure

Long-distance campaigning strains service duration, local defense, household willingness, carts, animals, markets, labor, supply, and political cooperation.

### 8.4 No single dominant arm

Flanders is not intended to surpass every neighbor in cavalry, infantry, missiles, siege, naval power, or numbers. Its excellence is combined-arms concentration and context-sensitive use.

### 8.5 Political seams

Different sources may have different mandates, obligations, priorities, service windows, precedence, and external owners. A force assembled successfully may still be difficult to retain or redirect.

## 9. Roster Architecture: Four Development Channels

A single Tier One to Tier Four ladder is prohibited as the primary roster model. Quality tiers may exist inside each channel, but source and purpose control development.

### 9.1 Channel A: Comital and Household Power

**Purpose:** scarce direct instruments of the count, household protection, decisive reserve, command credibility, elite intervention.

**Candidate families:** Comital Household Knights; Comital Mounted Sergeants; Household Armored Foot.

**Design rules:** very limited pools; strong morale and cohesion; high equipment, horse, officer, and recovery burden; no automatic availability from wealth; no universal superiority in every terrain.

### 9.2 Channel B: Castellany and Landed Service

**Purpose:** professional and obligation-bound strength outside the direct household; garrison capacity; regional leadership; mobile and foot service.

**Candidate families:** Landed Service Knights; Castle and Castellany Sergeants; Select Castellany Spearmen; castle and crossing defense relationships.

**Design rules:** source-specific obligation, service duration, readiness, and home-defense costs; field use may weaken garrisons; overlapping spear-professional candidates must merge or differentiate materially.

### 9.3 Channel C: Regional, Communal, and Local Defense

**Purpose:** the central playable army, regional line infantry, town and market defense, broad local levies, bows, scouts, and emergency defense.

**Candidate families:** Flemish Regional Spearmen; Flemish Shield Infantry; Rural Levy Spearmen; Town and Market Defense Companies; Flemish Archers; Scouts, Guides, and Light Foot.

**Design rules:** this channel must contain the faction's most useful ordinary units; lower status does not mean useless; local-defense floors and labor costs matter; town troops remain pre-commune and pre-guild unless later state supports otherwise.

### 9.4 Channel D: Route, River, Siege, Maritime, Contract, and External Support

**Purpose:** strongpoint and crossing defense, intelligence, transport, engineering, siege labor, coastal and river warning, mercenary access, and allied contribution.

**Candidate families:** Crossing and Causeway Guards; Coastal and River Watch; Siege Engineers and Artisans; Military Labor and Pioneers; Coastal Crews and Embarkation Support; contracted mercenaries; Hainaut contingents; Norman or other external contingents.

**Design rules:** not every capability becomes a field unit; support capabilities may remain campaign systems, attachments, garrison benefits, or conditional event access; mercenaries remain Theater-owned; external contingents remain externally owned.

## 12. Naming Doctrine

### 12.1 Naming goals

Names should be concise, faction-readable, historically plausible, and useful in a Total War roster. A name should tell the player at least one of the following: source, social basis, region, battlefield role, equipment identity, institutional relationship, or prestige.

### 12.2 Naming hierarchy

```yaml
preferred_name_sources:
  - historically_supported_local_term
  - faction_and_service_source
  - region_or_castellany_plus_role
  - role_plus_distinctive_Flemish_context
  - general_clear_gameplay_name
avoid:
  - generic_database_labels
  - false_later_institutional_names
  - unnecessarily_long_administrative_names
  - invented_exotic_terms_without_evidence
  - names_that_hide_external_or_mercenary_ownership
  - names_that_imply_a_standing_national_army
```

### 12.3 Working-name treatment

Current candidate names remain working names. Flavor is welcome, but role and evidence control final naming. Names may change without deleting the underlying concept if the mature profile preserves its source, role, identity, and disposition.

## 14. Counter-Faction Differentiation

### 14.1 Against France

Flanders expresses density, leverage, and concentrated execution. France may possess broader royal legitimacy and dispersed vassal resources. Flanders must not become a smaller duplicate of a French royal roster.

### 14.2 Against Normandy

Flanders is less cavalry-dominant and less purely expeditionary. Flanders emphasizes connected strongpoints, towns, rivers, routes, castellanies, mixed service, and negotiated contributions.

### 14.3 Against Burgundy

Flanders is geographically tighter and more concentration-oriented. It should not copy Burgundy's internal regional and political fragmentation profile.

### 14.4 Against Brittany

Flanders is more densely connected to continental markets, dynastic politics, and divided superior-lord interfaces. It should not import a peninsular or isolation-centered military identity.

### 14.5 Against Aquitaine

Flanders is smaller, denser, and faster to coordinate when the web remains intact. It should not copy Aquitaine's breadth, regional distance, or highly dispersed military structure.

### 14.6 Against Scandinavian realms

Flanders does not organize around a Hird, broad tribal levy identity, or Ledung-style naval service. Its center is household, castellany, landed obligation, settlement defense, crossing control, contracted support, and mixed political ownership.

## 15. Player-Facing Information Standard

For every mature unit, the player should be able to understand:

- where the unit comes from;
- why Flanders can recruit it;
- whether it is household, landed, castellany, regional, town, local, mercenary, or external;
- what it does in battle;
- which terrain and situations favor it;
- what counters it;
- what it costs the campaign beyond money;
- how quickly its pool recovers;
- whether field recruitment weakens a garrison, town, household, labor source, or route function;
- whether availability depends on an agreement or external owner.

Hidden complexity may support the system, but the roster card and recruitment panel must remain readable.

## 16. AI Doctrine

Realm AI may compare strategic need, source availability, local-defense floor, route access, supply, command, service duration, recovery, and political cost. Realm AI may propose a force composition or candidate use.

Realm AI may not:

- invent a unit definition;
- admit a candidate;
- create a recruitment pool;
- recruit from Unknown;
- convert wealth directly into soldiers;
- use external contingents without consent;
- strip every garrison for one field army;
- treat low-tier troops as disposable without recovery and community consequences;
- backfill later communes, guild armies, heraldry, equipment, or institutions into 1062.

## 17. Validation Fixtures

### 17.1 Positive fixtures

- A mixed army holds a crossing with regional spears and castellany professionals, then commits household cavalry as a reserve.
- A town company remains useful in gates and streets without becoming a mature commune army.
- A rural levy remains cheap and responsive near home but creates labor and service-duration costs.
- A crossing specialist outperforms a nominally higher-status general unit in the correct terrain.
- Wealth improves readiness and equipment but does not refill an exhausted elite pool.
- Hainaut support remains unavailable until the external owner accepts terms.
- A campaign support capability improves siege, transport, warning, or restoration without becoming a compulsory field regiment.

### 17.2 Negative fixtures

- Reject a universal Tier One to Tier Four upgrade ladder.
- Reject four spear units differing only by statistics and adjectives.
- Reject unlimited knights from high income.
- Reject castle construction as automatic soldiers.
- Reject town defense as later guild or commune armies at opening.
- Reject Coastal and River Watch as a standing fleet or universal naval infantry.
- Reject mercenaries as permanent domestic pools.
- Reject Hainaut, Norman, French, or other external troops as Flemish property.
- Reject one mandatory opening conquest path.
- Reject a unit merely because another faction has an equivalent slot.

1. Flemish regional spear backbone.
2. Castellany professional infantry.
3. Rural local-defense levy.
4. Town and gate defense.
5. Shield-oriented close-combat infantry if distinct.
6. Household armored foot.
7. Comital mounted sergeants.
8. Landed service knights.
9. Comital household knights.
10. Bow and light-foot support.
11. Crossing and causeway specialist.
12. Evidence-gated crossbow and technical support.
13. Campaign support capabilities.
14. Mercenary and external interfaces.

This order is a design workflow, not a recruitment ladder or final roster order.
