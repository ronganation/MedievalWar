**Alignment decision.** Peasant Mixed Levy is the twenty-third exact-roster candidate family recognized by the controlling Flanders military authority. This alignment does not redesign the unit. It preserves the developed lowest-pool movable mixed-weapon formation, its ordinary safe recruitment and emergency-overdraw modes, its severe civilian opportunity costs, and its separation from Rural Levy Spearmen. The family persists into later eras through source-supported improvements in warning, grouping, leadership, local support, and demobilization without automatic conversion into spear, civic, regional, household, or professional infantry.

**Peasant Mixed Levy are Flanders' lowest-pool movable mixed-weapon task formation:** eligible released household and labor contributors grouped for simple supported duties when using better military pools would be wasteful or impossible. They can guard camps, carts, stores, prisoners, labor areas, rear approaches, and quiet local positions, then move, join a host, enter a supported site, and return when service terms and support pass. In crisis, the same family may be expanded or degraded through Last-Resort Gathering and emergency overdraw. They remain below Rural Levy Spearmen in frontage, weapon coherence, mounted deterrence, and direct battle value.

The card survives only if mixed weapons create a distinct repeatable task formation rather than incidental assignment-only civilian defense. A minority of spears may shelter tool-armed contributors. Axes, clubs, knives, staves, mallets, hooks, and other source-valid implements dominate the close armament. Small bow or sling minorities may appear only where actual local access supports them. The formation cannot combine every weapon virtue. Variety creates inconsistency, command difficulty, and uneven reach rather than tactical flexibility.

## 3. Player-Facing Description

Peasant Mixed Levy appear when warning is short, weapons are scarce, and every available hand seems necessary. Contributors bring wood axes, hatchets, clubs, knives, staves, mallets, hooks, poles, farming implements, a scattering of spears, and occasional bows or slings where those tools actually exist. Clothing remains ordinary work dress. Shields and helmets are exceptional rather than expected.

The unit's value lies in immediate local action. The levy can help move carts, guard animals and stores, crowd a narrow lane, reinforce a ditch, protect an evacuation, and overwhelm a few isolated raiders. A small spear-bearing group may face outward while tool-armed contributors remain behind or around it, but the unit cannot maintain a proper anti-cavalry front.

The formation is brittle. Different weapon reaches, little training, poor protection, household fear, and confused leadership make sustained combat dangerous. If trapped in open ground or exposed to missiles, cavalry, or professionals, the levy should break quickly. Survival and successful evacuation are often better outcomes than battlefield victory.

## 12. Gathering and Service Cycle

1. A supported local task, garrison need, short field duty, or emergency protection need is identified.
2. A local authority or accepted caller identifies contributors and essential reservations.
3. Tools, weapons, carts, materials, leaders, routes, and objectives are checked.
4. The response is classified as protective labor, mixed defense crowd, spear-qualified Rural Levy Spearmen, scattered response, or no formation.
5. Contributors gather near a visible point and receive a simple task.
6. Clusters form around leaders, carts, obstacles, stores, or spear-bearing minorities.
7. The formation performs a supported local task or short field mission; emergency overdraw remains separately flagged.
8. The crowd disperses, withdraws, or demobilizes when the task ends or cohesion fails.
9. People, tools, carts, stores, households, injuries, and losses are accounted for.
10. Civilian functions recover separately and may remain damaged.

Battle rhythm:

1. Assemble around a recognized leader, supported site, or clearly bounded task.
2. Place any spear or shield minority toward the evident threat.
3. Keep tool-armed contributors near cover, carts, obstacles, or stronger troops.
4. Use tiny missile groups only for harassment where safe.

9. Regroup only at another known local point.
10. Return for personnel, property, equipment, service, and support accounting.

## 19. Strengths

- Cheap real movable local garrison and task utility where safe capacity passes.
- Fast relative local assembly and emergency expansion where warning and source conditions pass.
- Potentially broad contributor base when reservations pass.
- Mixed tools may support several noncombat protection tasks.
- Useful around carts, stores, exits, ditches, hedges, and narrow lanes.
- Can deter looters or overwhelm isolated light threats.
- Supports evacuation and rear-area security.
- Represents a meaningful alternative when spear-qualified troops are unnecessary, reserved, depleted, or unavailable.
- Preserves stronger pools for frontage, relief, missile, mounted, and household tasks.

## 20. Weaknesses

- Almost no formation discipline.
- No reliable spear frontage.
- Negligible anti-cavalry capability.
- Extremely vulnerable to missiles and professional troops.
- Mixed reach and equipment complicate command.
- Little armor and almost no shield coverage.
- Very poor movement and withdrawal order.
- Panic and scattering risk are high.
- Very low campaign endurance and rapidly rising pressure outside supported local service.
- Severe civilian, household, tool, and transport cost.
- Casualties can devastate the source community.
