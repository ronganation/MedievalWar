**Causeway Guards are Flanders' organized crossing and wet-route specialists:** locally familiar defenders capable of controlling prepared causeways, bridge approaches, ferry landings, banks, dike roads, raised tracks, and other exact constricted passages where movement, footing, water, and route knowledge matter. They are not generic spearmen with a terrain bonus. Their value comes from knowing where a crossing narrows, where carts turn, where banks collapse, where water or mud limits approach, and how to hold or yield one passage without losing the entire route network.

The campaign decision is coverage. Crossing defense uses people who may also support route watch, ferries, carts, boats, banks, maintenance, water management, warning, and local transport. Releasing a company or concentrating several sites into one formation strengthens the field army but creates a **Crossing Gap** at the source. Flanders cannot protect every route and simultaneously strip every crossing specialist into one host.

## 2. One-Screen Player Card

- **Unit:** Causeway Guards
- **Class:** Organized causeway, crossing, and wet-route specialist infantry
- **Source:** Eligible releasable defenders, route watchers, crossing-service contributors, and locally experienced personnel attached to validated causeways, bridges, ferries, banks, dike roads, or related strongpoints
- **Availability:** Narrow to moderate, exact-site bound, seasonal, route-dependent, labor-sensitive, and blocked when crossing, ferry, warning, transport, or waterwork coverage would fail
- **Battle promise:** A specialist route-defense company that can deny one qualified passage and cover movement through it only when approach geometry, footing, barriers, warning, fallback stages, signals, and source coverage all pass together
- **Best use:** Causeways, bridge approaches, ferry landings, raised roads, dike tracks, banks, fords, narrow route entries, wagon chokepoints, wet-ground margins, and controlled crossing relief
- **Worst use:** Broad open field, deep pursuit, dense urban interiors, mountain-style broken ground, shock assault, distant campaigning, and any battlefield where the route has no meaningful physical constraint
- **Strategic cost:** Weakens route watch, ferry and landing security, cart and crossing control, warning, waterwork protection, transport confidence, and local response
- **Recovery:** Gradual through surviving site personnel, restored route functions, repaired shields and spears, replacement tools, known leaders, safe access, and functioning water and transport systems
- **Counters:** Missiles across exposed approaches, fire, flooding or water change, blocked retreat, simultaneous crossings, flanking by an alternate route, professional assault infantry, and heavy cavalry after the formation is drawn off the constriction
- **Notable distinction:** The unit receives no benefit merely from standing near water or on a road; specialist value activates only through a qualified crossing mission and collapses when the passage is bypassed, physically altered, or no longer controls movement

## 3. Player-Facing Description

Causeway Guards defend the narrow threads that allow Flanders to move across difficult ground and water. Their sources may be connected to a bridge approach, ferry landing, raised road, bank, dike, ford, landing place, route watch, or another validated crossing function. The profile combines only compatible defensive contributors who can form a movable company without inventing a universal crossing institution.

On the battlefield, Causeway Guards exploit physical constraint. Spears and shields face the entry, shorter weapons and tools work close to barriers and banks, and local leaders control fallback points on the route. The unit can delay a stronger enemy if the enemy must approach through a known passage. Once drawn onto open ground, bypassed, or cut off from the crossing, the specialist advantage collapses.

The unit expresses Flanders as a connected county whose prosperity and defense depend on routes, rivers, crossings, water-managed land, settlements, and short concentration paths. A crossing is valuable because movement depends on it. Wealth or geography alone does not create trained defenders.

## 11. Muster, Release, and Return Cycle

1. A crossing-defense, route-relief, convoy, landing, escort, or bounded field mission is identified.
2. The exact source reviews route condition, season, water, warning, traffic, maintenance, and competing threats.
3. Essential crossing, ferry, waterwork, cart, boat, warning, and local-defense functions remain reserved.
4. Eligible people, leaders, weapons, shields, tools, food, signals, and routes are checked.
5. A releasable company assembles around one exact passage or mission.
6. The company deploys and records the Crossing Gap.
7. The source tracks threat, route use, water change, sabotage, bypass, and recall pressure.
8. The company completes, transfers, withdraws, disperses, or is recalled.
9. People, equipment, tools, transport property, casualties, and maintenance damage are accounted for.
10. Crossing coverage, local confidence, route function, and field availability recover separately.

Battle rhythm:

1. Inspect the exact crossing, approaches, water, ground, route, barriers, and fallback points.
2. Place spear-and-shield files at the narrowest useful entry.
3. Keep short-weapon and tool-equipped files near barriers, carts, banks, or second positions.
4. Preserve a compact reserve at the next route stage.
5. Force the enemy into narrow frontage and poor approach order.
6. Hold long enough for traffic, civilians, convoys, or friendly troops to pass where required.
7. Yield the forward point before encirclement or complete disorder.
8. Fall back through known marked stages.
9. Reoccupy a forward barrier only when enemy momentum and bypass risk permit.
10. Avoid pursuing beyond the constricted route.
11. Return before the source Crossing Gap becomes strategically dangerous.

## 19. Strengths

- Strong exact-crossing identity.
- Excellent passage denial for its quality band.
- Familiarity with approaches, banks, landings, raised roads, and fallback stages.
- Better order than emergency levies in route-defense missions.
- Useful against enemies forced into narrow frontage.
- Can cover convoy, civilian, cart, boat, and friendly-force passage.
- Staged fallback makes local withdrawal more controlled.
- Preserves professional troops for major strongpoints and field anchors.

## 20. Weaknesses

- Narrow exact-site source.
- Release creates a Crossing Gap.
- Specialist value collapses if bypassed or drawn into open ground.
- Vulnerable to missiles along exposed approaches.
- Vulnerable to fire, flood, water change, rubble, and route destruction.
- Multiple simultaneous crossings split leaders and reserves.
- Less versatile than Town and Gate Companies.
- Weaker general field frontage than Flemish Spearmen.
- Less professional and durable than Castellany Sergeants.
- Poor pursuit, shock, and expeditionary endurance.

## 26. Collision Firewall

### Versus Flemish Spearmen

Flemish Spearmen own general organized frontage, broader sources, and greater usefulness across ordinary field missions. Causeway Guards own exact crossing preparation, staged fallback, local route knowledge, and passage control.

### Versus Town and Gate Companies

Town companies own settlement interiors, markets, stores, gates, streets, yards, alarms, and built-objective relief. Causeway Guards own crossings, approaches, banks, landings, raised routes, and controlled withdrawal along one passage.

### Versus Castellany Sergeants

Castellany Sergeants own professional custody, stronger equipment, sustained order, prepared mounted-threat control, and reliable relief. Causeway Guards may outperform them in local crossing familiarity and route staging, but not in general combat quality.

### Versus Rural Levy Spearmen

Rural levies own fast countryside emergency response. Causeway Guards own recurring route knowledge, prepared positions, signals, barriers, and specialist passage denial.

### Versus Engineer, Naval, or Missile Families

Causeway Guards do not build major works, operate as marines, crew vessels, or become organized archers. Tools, boats, and missiles remain conditional support interfaces.
