This file formalizes the development of Flanders' foot archers and foot skirmishers from the Early Era into the High Era. It creates no new unit profile. It reconciles the six current mature profiles, establishes their era relationships, preserves their distinct battlefield purposes, and identifies the actual remaining roster gaps before any further unit is written.

## 1. Controlling Verdict

Flanders currently possesses a coherent six-family missile and light-foot structure:

1. **March Skirmishers**: Early mobile javelin screen and forward-light-foot contest.
2. **March Bowmen**: Early broad ordinary organized bow support.
3. **March Veteran Bowmen**: experienced cadre-backed upper bow company.
4. **Flemish Crossbowmen**: High Era trained medium-protected crossbow line.

The branch does not need another generic archer, another generic skirmisher, a levy crossbow card, or another selected crossbow tier. The current families cover forward screening, broad bow coverage, veteran bow concentration, maintained civic bow frontage, trained core crossbow pressure, and selected professional crossbow control.

The remaining design work is developmental rather than numerical:

- clarify which Early families persist into High;
- define how veteran bow companies arise without replacing ordinary bowmen;
- establish how crossbow institutions emerge without rewriting the Early opening;
- preserve skirmisher utility in later armies;
- identify only genuinely different later missile or light-foot candidates.

## 2. Branch Identity

The branch expresses the Connected March through **layered observation, pressure, and protected firing relationships** rather than one dominant national missile tradition.

```text
March Skirmishers find and contest the forward ground.
March Bowmen cover the army broadly.
March Veteran Bowmen concentrate experienced fire where it matters.
Flemish Crossbowmen make the High Era infantry front dangerous to armor and enemy missiles.
```

The branch supports the Mustered Web by making developed sources more capable of organizing shooters, cadres, weapons, ammunition, repair, and field service. It does not grant perfect coordination, unlimited ammunition, or automatic realm-wide availability.


March Skirmishers remain useful because later armies still require:

- forward observation;
- short-route and flank security;
- ordinary light-foot contest;
- limited javelin pressure;
- warning and report transfer;
- pursuit denial;
- bounded screening before decisive contact.

High Era development may improve signals, report quality, section leadership, equipment consistency, route integration, and return discipline. It must not convert the family into formed javelin infantry, mounted scouts, archers, crossbowmen, or aggressive melee light infantry.

March Bowmen remain the broad and economical bow family. They own:

- ordinary firing-lane coverage;
- approach pressure;
- protected settlement and strongpoint fire;
- camp, crossing, and withdrawal support;
- several companies across compatible sources;
- lower institutional and recovery burden than upper missile families.

High Era improvement may strengthen equipment inspection, arrow storage, section leadership, shelter relationships, and route coordination. The family must remain below Veteran Bowmen in fire control and below Crossbowmen in hard-target bolt pressure.

### 3.3 March Veteran Bowmen

```yaml
era_origin: EarlyDevelopmentOrCrossEraVeteranExpression
relationship_to_High: PersistentConditionalUpperBowFamily
primary_role: PriorityBowFireArrowReserveDisplacementAndReform
core_method: MarkLooseReform
HighEra_action: PersistAndBecomeMoreSupportableAtQualifiedSources
free_parallel_pool: false
```

March Veteran Bowmen do not replace March Bowmen. They arise from:

- returning practiced shooters;
- surviving section leaders;
- current collective practice;
- better-maintained compatible bows and strings;
- reserved inspected arrows;
- source capacity capable of sustaining the upper company.

The player chooses between wider ordinary bow coverage and narrower experienced concentration. Creating a Veteran company can reduce March Bowmen numbers, leadership depth, equipment quality, and recovery capacity.

The Veteran card should remain conditional on current readiness. Veteran status is not permanent, automatic, or created through a single battle.

Flemish Crossbowmen are a separate High Era family. They emerge through developed domestic providers able to sustain:

- qualified operators;
- serviceable crossbows;
- compatible strings and mechanisms;
- bolts and containers;
- spanning equipment;
- maintenance and repair;
- company leadership and practice;
- local defense reservations;
- field or siege support.

They own:

- stronger per-hit pressure against suitably exposed armored targets;
- credible missile dueling within their trained tier;
- siege, wall, gate, and strongpoint fire;
- station-to-station support of an advancing Flemish infantry host;
- deliberate loaded-shot response;
- medium protection and credible brief self-defense.

They do not erase March Bowmen or Veteran Bowmen. Their slower cycle, equipment burden, greater replacement cost, more direct firing needs, and vulnerability during reload preserve the bow families.

## 7. Source and Pool Relationships

### Light-foot pool

March Skirmishers draw from qualified regional, community, household, route-facing, and edge sources. They compete with guide, messenger, watch, route, and local-defense duties.

### Broad bow pool

March Bowmen draw from compatible community, household, settlement, town, and castellany bow sources. Bow ownership or hunting skill alone does not create a company.

### Veteran bow concentration

March Veteran Bowmen draw from the best current experienced portion of the broad bow ecosystem. The upper family therefore costs ordinary bow coverage and cadre depth.

### Crossbow provider pool

Flemish Crossbowmen draw from developed domestic town and castellany providers with operators, weapons, maintenance, bolts, leadership, and supporting service. Markets and workshops support equipment but do not create operators.

## 8. Branch Development Axes

### 8.1 Light-foot practice and reporting

Supports better screening, javelin handling, warning, report confidence, and Fold discipline for March Skirmishers.

### 8.2 Bow practice and section leadership

Supports improved March Bowmen target control and can help generate Veteran-qualified cadres without automatically creating a Veteran company.

### 8.3 Ammunition production, storage, and transport

Supports arrows, bolts, containers, dry storage, campaign resupply, and recovery. It does not create shooters or operators.

### 8.4 Crossbow craft and maintenance

Supports mechanisms, strings, prods, tillers, bolts, spanning tools, inspection, repair, and replacement.

### 8.5 Town and castellany military organization

Supports regular service, leaders, protected firing positions, provider-specific recruitment, local-defense reservations, and field release.

### 8.6 Repeated war experience

Creates surviving shooters, section leaders, judgment, and formation memory while simultaneously inflicting casualties and equipment losses.

### 8.7 Combined-arms integration

Improves physical cooperation with spears, pikes, shield infantry, sergeants, strongpoints, routes, and mounted reserves. It does not create passive faction bonuses.

## 13. Actual Remaining Gaps

The branch is not empty, but it may still support later candidates only when a different source and battlefield decision survive.

### 13.1 Possible later selected crossbow family

A future heavy, sergeant, or pavise crossbow card would require a materially different:

- source;
- weapon and spanning method;
- protection package;
- movement and setup burden;
- target and range behavior;
- recovery cost;
- campaign role.

It is not required now. The current Flemish Crossbowmen must first mature through normal refinement and battle testing.

### 13.2 Possible later urban shooting institution

A guild-linked or town-specific shooting company belongs behind chronology, actual institutional emergence, and town-specific evidence. It may not be created merely because High Era Flanders has strong towns.

### 13.3 Possible later aggressive light foot

A stronger javelin or melee-capable light-foot family would need a narrower source, better movement and withdrawal practice, greater campaign persistence, and a role materially beyond March Skirmishers. It is not currently demonstrated.

### 13.4 No additional generic bow family

The ordinary and veteran bow relationship is already complete. Another generic High Era bow unit would likely become a statistics tier and is therefore blocked.

## 31. Superseded V1.0 Gap Statements

### Urban shooting institution

The earlier “possible later urban shooting institution” gap is closed at the static-design level by Flemish Town Bowmen. The mature card uses a town-supported civic company without asserting a universal guild institution, special longbows, or a historically certified `Stads-Schutters` corps.

### Selected crossbow family

The earlier “possible later selected crossbow family” gap is closed at the static-design level by Flemish Crossbow Sergeants. The selected card survives through its source concentration, Sergeant Salvo, missile-contest control, station endurance, and ordered withdrawal.

### Heavy or dedicated pavise crossbows

This remains only a reserved design space. It is not the next authorized unit. Both existing crossbow families already permit physical pavise support where equipment and chronology pass. A separate card would need a different weapon, movement, protection, spanning, target, and campaign proposition.

### Aggressive light foot

This remains unproven. March Skirmishers already own forward light-foot contest. A later aggressive family would require materially different people, equipment, movement, persistence, melee behavior, and campaign purpose.

### Another generic archer

Blocked. The three bow families already cover distributed coverage, experienced reforming fire, and maintained civic frontage. Another archer requires a genuinely new source and battlefield purchase, not symmetry or a higher statistic line.
