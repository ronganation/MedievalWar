## 1. Reconciliation Verdict

Flanders already possesses a complete Early Era cavalry triangle:

- **Count's Mounted Sergeants** provide escort, warning, interception, flank continuity, withdrawal cover, controlled pursuit, mounted relief, and disciplined repeat utility.
- **Landed Service Knights** provide distributed regional knightly shock, flank action, mounted-melee reinforcement, contingent leadership, standards, and a conditional second commitment.
- **Count's Household Knights** provide direct comital protection, immediate crisis counterstroke, extraction, Return to Guard, and second-crisis judgment.

All three are persistent cross-era families. Their current full master profiles already contain era persistence, equipment and horse development, formations, command, recovery, whole-battle behavior, and collision controls. The obsolete V1.0 conclusion that three duplicate High Era master profiles are necessary is therefore superseded.

## 9. Candidate A: Civic Mounted Family

### Working label

**Flemish Patrician Horsemen** remains a planning label only.

### Source hypothesis

The candidate may draw from one or more compatible civic mounted-service sources, but those sources cannot be assumed interchangeable:

- qualified civic households with recurring military obligations;
- urban office holders with valid mounted service;
- wealthy service families maintaining actual riders and horses;
- lesser urban elites possessing equipment, practice, and recognized field obligations.

The full profile must determine whether these can credibly form one company or whether the composite is too broad.

### Proposed battlefield role

A civic medium-to-heavy mounted reserve used for:

- protecting an urban contribution or civic flank;
- reinforcing a mounted fight;
- exploiting disruption created by town infantry and missiles;
- controlled pursuit;
- protecting a withdrawal or camp route;
- giving developed town hosts mounted weight below knightly first-charge quality.

### Candidate promise

> Developed Flemish towns contribute mounted weight that enters after civic infantry and missiles create the opening, rather than replacing the landed knightly charge.

## 12. Candidate A Collision Firewall

### Against Count's Mounted Sergeants

Count's Mounted Sergeants retain escort, warning, screen, interception, withdrawal cover, route and command service, and economical repeat utility. The civic candidate must provide stronger protected combat, heavier local reserve value, and better exploitation, but worse route-service economy and narrower access.

### Against Landed Service Knights

Landed Service Knights retain knightly first-charge weight, mounted melee, charge echelons, countercharge, standards, contingent leadership, and lordship-based service. The civic candidate must remain below that ceiling.

### Against Count's Household Knights

Count's Household Knights retain direct comital trust, command protection, immediate crisis response, extraction, and Return to Guard. The civic candidate cannot become an urban bodyguard substitute.

### Against civic infantry and missile families

The candidate must draw separate qualified riders and horses while competing for civic leadership, finance, transport, support personnel, political willingness, armor, horse care, and town-defense capacity.

## 16. Shared High Era Development Axes

### Horse quality and pairing
Better selection, conditioning, remount support, care, and rider-horse familiarity improve readiness without creating one universal horse grade.

### Equipment completion
Helmets, shields, mail, weapons, saddles, bridles, tack, and support may become more consistent. High Era does not automatically mean universal plate or barding.

### Leadership and formation
Section leaders, standards, signals, pace, alignment, contact limits, breakaway, rally, and second-task discipline may improve.

### Service administration
Notice, records, duration, release, compensation, ransom, return, and recovery may become more organized without becoming perfect central control.

### Combined-arms integration
Cavalry gains clearer relationships with pikes, Town Spearmen, Men-at-Arms, crossbows, bowmen, camps, crossings, command nodes, and withdrawal passages. No relationship is a passive proximity bonus.

### Political and household recovery
Household losses, heirs, leaders, horses, standards, ransom, source defense, and willingness remain campaign consequences. Better institutions improve accounting, not loss removal.

## 18. Combined-Arms High Era Relationships

- **Pikes and Town Spearmen** deny enemy cavalry and fix infantry; cavalry protects flanks, exploits exposure, or answers crisis.
- **Crossbows and bowmen** create disorder and vulnerable targets; cavalry protects missile flanks and withdrawal routes.
- **Men-at-Arms, March Shieldmen, and Goedendag Companies** fix or clear targets; cavalry exploits, countercharges, or protects withdrawal.
- **Causeways, camps, and strongpoints** preserve routes and rally nodes; cavalry operates beyond and between them where terrain permits.

No automatic rear-charge or proximity bonus is created.

## 37. Army-Composition Purchase Matrix

| Army need | Preferred family | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Screen several lanes, escort command, intercept light threats, cover withdrawal, and preserve repeat utility | Count's Mounted Sergeants | Broad practical mounted service at lower battle weight |
| Support a town-heavy army and win one local medium-cavalry fight | Town Horsemen | Civic source, formed supporting charge, exploitation, and rally |
| Deliver distributed knightly shock through landed contingents | Landed Service Knights | Strong first knightly commitment, standards, contingent weight, and political expression |
| Hold a later heavy reserve for decisive charge, countercharge, or planned dismounted action | Mounted Men-at-Arms | Reviewed heavy people, horses, harness, captains, support, and battle-form choice |
| Protect the count or command group and answer the decisive immediate crisis | Count's Household Knights | Trust, proximity, extraction, Household Counterstroke, and Return to Guard |

A normal army does not require all five. Provider access, battle plan, terrain, politics, campaign duration, horse depth, and support capacity determine the useful mix.
