This is one new Late High Era cavalry candidate. It creates no opening company, recruitment pool, captain, retinue, horse supply, provider, stable ID, Registry entry, payload, runtime binding, or Unreal asset.

The profile is intentionally later and heavier than Town Horsemen. It does not replace Count's Mounted Sergeants, Town Horsemen, Landed Service Knights, or Count's Household Knights.

## 1. Strong Unit Verdict

**Mounted Men-at-Arms are Flanders' later professional heavy-service cavalry: reviewed, well-equipped mounted combatants assembled under recognized captains or service relationships to deliver decisive heavy charge, countercharge, sustained mounted melee, and planned dismounted heavy combat when the battlefield warrants sacrificing mobility.**

They are not simply Landed Service Knights with improved armor. Their distinction is functional and institutional:

- accepted heavy-service standard rather than one landed social source;
- captain, retinue, contract, household, comital, civic, or other compatible service relationships;
- formal review of people, horses, harness, weapons, support, and readiness;
- greater operational campaign support than obligation-bound knightly contingents;
- deliberate mounted-versus-dismounted battle planning;
- expensive recovery in people, horses, harness, captains, servants, and formation memory.

## 2. One-Screen Total War Card

- **Unit:** Mounted Men-at-Arms
- **Class:** Late High Era professional heavy cavalry
- **Weapons:** Heavy mounted lance or chronology-compatible primary spear, sword, mace or axe where supported, and shield where appropriate to the equipment band
- **Protection:** Mature composite heavy protection appropriate to date and provider, without universal full plate
- **Horse:** Strong heavy-service warhorse with finite remount and harness support
- **Battle promise:** Present reviewed heavy cavalry for the decisive charge or countercharge, remain dangerous in mounted melee, or deliberately dismount before battle for one planned heavy-foot mission
- **Best use:** Breaking a critical infantry formation, meeting enemy heavy cavalry, reinforcing a decisive mounted fight, holding an operational reserve, or fighting dismounted on terrain where mounted shock cannot work
- **Strong against:** Exposed or disordered infantry, medium cavalry, tired knightly cavalry, vulnerable flanks, and heavy targets already fixed by combined arms
- **Competitive against:** Peer men-at-arms, developed knightly cavalry, and household cavalry outside its command-protection advantage
- **Weak against:** Prepared pikes and spears, concentrated crossbows, mud and constricted terrain while mounted, repeated exhausted charges, broken captain or standards, horse loss, and unsupported dismounted isolation
- **Campaign bargain:** Exceptional battlefield power purchased with costly people, horses, harness, captains, servants, pay or obligation, fodder, repair, ransom, and slow recovery
- **Player instruction:** Choose the battle form before commitment. Mounted power wins the open decision; dismounted power holds or assaults ground at the cost of mobility and horse utility
- **Flavor line:** **Reviewed for war. Armed for the decision.**

## 3. Why the Card Survives

### Against Count's Mounted Sergeants

Count's Mounted Sergeants retain:

- escort;
- screen;
- interception;
- warning;
- command links;
- withdrawal cover;
- economical repeat utility.

Mounted Men-at-Arms are much heavier, costlier, slower to recover, and less appropriate for routine mounted service.

### Against Town Horsemen

Town Horsemen retain:

- civic medium-cavalry identity;
- local mounted contest;
- civic flank defense;
- exploitation beside town infantry and missiles;
- broader qualified town access;
- lower horse and harness ceiling;
- better economy for town-heavy armies.

Mounted Men-at-Arms own the later heavy-service decision, not civic reserve work.

### Against Landed Service Knights

Landed Service Knights retain:

- landed-household source;
- obligation, consent, precedence, and home-defense politics;
- regional standards and contingent identity;
- strong first knightly commitment;
- rally by household standards.

Mounted Men-at-Arms are defined by accepted heavy service, captain or retinue relationships, reviewed equipment and readiness, and stronger later campaign sustainment. A person may qualify through a landed source, but the card is not simply the entire Landed Service Knight family upgraded.

### Against Count's Household Knights

Count's Household Knights retain:

- direct comital trust;
- command protection;
- immediate crisis response;
- extraction;
- Return to Guard.

Mounted Men-at-Arms provide general heavy battle service. They do not gain the household card's proximity, protected duty, or command-facing reliability.

## 13. Signature: Reviewed Heavy Service

The full player cycle is:

```text
Muster → Review → Choose → Commit → Break or Hold → Rally
```

### Muster

Assemble eligible people, horses, harness, support, captains, and terms.

### Review

Confirm the company can actually perform the intended mounted or dismounted mission.

### Choose

Select mounted heavy shock, mounted countercharge, operational reserve, or planned dismounted heavy combat before the commitment.

### Commit

Deliver the chosen heavy action with the full cost and limitations of that battle form.

### Break or Hold

Mounted troops decide whether to break contact and preserve shock value. Dismounted troops decide whether to hold, assault, or withdraw without immediate horse access.

### Rally

Restore command, sections, pairings, standards, and a reduced later mission where survival permits.

The choice is not a frictionless battle toggle.

## 16. Heavy Charge Contract

Town Horsemen turn a local opening. Mounted Men-at-Arms should break a critical formation or overturn a heavy cavalry decision.

### Suitable targets

- exposed heavy or medium infantry;
- disordered pikes or spears after combined-arms disruption;
- enemy heavy cavalry in a favorable countercharge;
- an exposed elite reserve;
- a fixed flank;
- a key line whose collapse justifies the expense.

### Poor targets

- intact prepared pikes or spears;
- firm anti-cavalry frontage with reserves;
- concentrated crossbows on the approach;
- deep mud, hedges, ditches, walls, and congestion;
- low-value troops that do not justify horse and harness risk.

## 17. Mounted Melee and Countercharge

Mounted Men-at-Arms should:

- defeat medium cavalry in sustained combat when readiness is comparable;
- credibly contest peer heavy cavalry;
- reinforce or decide a knightly melee;
- countercharge heavy cavalry threatening the Flemish line;
- remain dangerous after the first impact;
- disengage before horse, captain, or formation failure makes recovery impossible.

They should not:

- ignore pikes;
- remain indefinitely in compression;
- receive automatic superiority over Count's Household Knights near the protected command;
- cycle charge without fatigue and cohesion loss;
- treat every cavalry fight as worth the recovery burden.

## 18. Dismounted Heavy Combat Method

When dismounted, the company should function as heavy combatants capable of:

- holding difficult ground;
- resisting mounted pressure in supported terrain;
- assaulting a gate, breach, courtyard, or steep position;
- reinforcing Count's Household Foot or Castellany Men-at-Arms;
- anchoring a counterattack;
- consolidating an objective after mounted access fails.

The exact infantry weapon package remains chronology- and evidence-dependent. Dismounted troops cannot simultaneously preserve full mounted readiness.

## 19. Mounted Versus Dismounted Choice

The core player question is:

> **Is mobility and heavy mounted shock more valuable than a planned heavy-foot presence on this battlefield?**

Choose mounted when:

- open or usable ground exists;
- decisive charge or countercharge is likely;
- operational movement matters;
- horses can be protected and recovered;
- the enemy presents exploitable mounted or infantry targets.

Choose dismounted when:

- pikes, terrain, walls, streets, or constriction remove the mounted bargain;
- a hard objective must be held or assaulted;
- horse custody is secure;
- infantry and missile support can integrate the heavy foot;
- remount delay is acceptable.

Count's Household Knights remain superior for:

- ruler protection;

- Return to Guard;

## 23. Combined-Arms Contract

### Pikes and Spearmen

Fix targets, deny hostile cavalry, and create countercharge security. Men-at-Arms exploit turned or disrupted formations rather than charging through the pike front.

### Goedendag Companies and March Shieldmen

Create compressed or stabilized infantry fights. Mounted Men-at-Arms attack exposed reserves, heavy flanks, or mounted support.

### Castellany Men-at-Arms and Count's Household Foot

Provide hard foot combat. Mounted Men-at-Arms remain mobile until the battle plan specifically requires dismounting.

### Bowmen and Crossbows

Disrupt armor, shields, horses, and formation. Mounted Men-at-Arms convert the created opening into heavy contact.

### Count's Mounted Sergeants

Screen the approach, remove light threats, preserve reform space, and control pursuit.

### Town Horsemen

Protect the civic wing, defeat lighter cavalry, and exploit secondary openings so the heavy card can be preserved for the decisive target.

### Landed Service Knights

Provide distributed knightly weight. Men-at-Arms may form the reviewed heavy reserve, countercharge body, or later campaign heavy-service layer.

## 24. Whole-Battle Role

### Opening

- remain in operational reserve;
- protect horses from unnecessary missile exposure;
- review the mounted or dismounted plan;

### Main battle

- deliver decisive charge or countercharge;
- reinforce the critical cavalry melee;
- attack the fixed heavy target;

- cover withdrawal;
- defend a captured position;
- preserve remaining heavy people, horses, harness, and captains.

## 29. AI Doctrine

### Campaign AI

Recruit when:

- mature heavy service is needed;
- people, captains, horses, harness, servants, and supply exist;
- the campaign duration and opposition justify the burden;
- Landed Service Knights provide a different political and field bargain;
- Town Horsemen are insufficient for the expected heavy fight;
- mounted-versus-dismounted flexibility has strategic value.

Avoid when:

- the true need is screening, escort, civic reserve, or command protection;
- horses or harness are hollow;
- pay, credit, supply, and servants cannot sustain the company;
- terrain makes both mounted action and horse custody impractical;
- recruitment duplicates existing reserved people and horses.

### Battle AI

AI must:

1. select mounted or planned dismounted employment;
2. hold the company for a decisive target;
3. avoid prepared pikes and concentrated missiles;
4. exploit combined-arms disruption;
5. countercharge heavy cavalry when geometry passes;
6. disengage and rally before collapse;
7. dismount only with a battle plan and horse custody;
8. avoid instant remount or repeated exhausted charging.

## 30. Player-Facing Ability Package

### Review for Battle

Represents the pre-commitment readiness and mission choice. This is primarily campaign and deployment logic, not a combat buff button.

Deliver the decisive formed mounted action against a suitable target.

### Fight Dismounted

A planned deployment choice that trades mounted mobility and charge for heavy-foot presence. It is not a free live toggle.

## 32C. Mature Battle Rhythm

The card retains the deeper campaign cycle:

```text
Muster → Review → Choose → Commit → Break or Hold → Rally
```

The immediate battle cycle is clarified as:

```text
Read → Form → Strike → Decide → Recover
```

### Read

Identify whether the battle requires mounted shock, heavy countercharge, operational reserve, or planned dismounted combat. This is target and mission judgment, not omniscience.

### Form

Establish captain control, standards, leading and supporting files, horse condition, approach geometry, reserve depth, and a rally relationship.

### Strike

Deliver the selected mounted charge, countercharge, mounted-melee reinforcement, or dismounted heavy action.

### Decide

Choose among breaking away, passing through, reinforcing the melee, holding the objective, or abandoning a failed mission.

### Recover

Restore surviving people, horses, standards, sections, and command for a reduced later task. Recovery never recreates lost horses, fatigue, or harness.

## 32D. Heavy Company Formation

### Full commitment

Committing all useful files produces maximum shock and mounted-melee weight, but removes the fresh reserve needed to protect breakaway, answer another threat, or rally coherently.

### Held reserve

- counter-countercharge protection;
- breakaway security;
- rally;
- pursuit control;
- reduced second-task value.

This gives the player a meaningful echelon decision without turning sections into separate deployable units.

## 32E. Four Mounted Commitment Geometries

### Decisive frontal charge

Use only against a target whose reach, facing, cohesion, terrain, and support have already been compromised. This provides the greatest local shock and the greatest horse-and-harness risk.

### Oblique heavy strike

Attack the shoulder or exposed side of a formation fixed by pikes, spears, missiles, or another cavalry body. This is the safest path to the unit's intended decisive result.

### Heavy countercharge

Meet enemy heavy cavalry while alignment, pace, support, and reserve depth pass. The goal is to spoil the enemy's clean commitment and overturn the local mounted decision.

### Mounted-melee reinforcement

Enter an existing fight with enough spacing to add fresh heavy files rather than congesting every rider into one immobile mass.
