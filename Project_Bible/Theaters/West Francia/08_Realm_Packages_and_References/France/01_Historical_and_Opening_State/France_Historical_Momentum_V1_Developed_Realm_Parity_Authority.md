## 1. Booyah Verdict

France begins with a Crown whose recognition, sacred dignity, and jurisdiction exceed its immediately executable capacity. Philip I is already crowned and recognized, but he is underage. Government is distributed; the Royal Demesne is narrow and incompletely connected; the great princes remain operationally independent; and the Crown must constantly convert recognition into communication, attendance, service, judgment, revenue, military force, and durable control.

Historical Momentum turns those conditions into directional pressure without scripting France toward centralization, collapse, personal rule, war, or any familiar later outcome.

**Campaign fantasy:** Wear the crown before possessing the kingdom.

**Momentum question:** Which forces are currently pushing the Capetian Crown toward greater capacity, continued dependence, negotiated balance, princely domination, or fragmentation, and what current owner state reinforces or resists each direction?

## 10. Stage and Transition Model

```yaml

  Dormant:
    meaning: definition exists but current material sources are absent or inaccessible
  Forming:
    meaning: at least one valid source exists but opposition relevance or durability remains limited
  Active:
    meaning: multiple valid current inputs create actionable directional pressure
  Elevated:
    meaning: pressure materially constrains or encourages high-consequence decisions
  Critical:
    meaning: current state requires prompt owner or AI consideration but does not force an outcome
  Stabilizing:
    meaning: resistance settlement or capacity is reducing volatility
  Redirected:
    meaning: pressure persists but has changed target direction participant or strategic expression
  Suspended:
    meaning: evaluation cannot proceed because required owner state identity or access is unavailable
  Closed:
    meaning: current pressure instance has a valid closure receipt
```

No universal numeric thresholds are authorized. Implementations may use internal tuning only after specialist owners provide validated data, with qualitative player-facing explanations retained.

## 24. Reinforcement, Resistance, Dissipation, Redirection, and Closure

### 24.1 Reinforcement

A pressure may strengthen when new owner state increases its current relevance, source diversity, geographic scope, consequence, or inability to defer. One dramatic event may matter greatly, but recurrence from one source chain does not automatically equal independent confirmation.

### 24.2 Resistance

Resistance is not the opposite of historical plausibility. It is current state that counters, absorbs, delays, narrows, or stabilizes a pressure. Examples include accepted mandates, working routes, reliable officers, sufficient supply, mediated agreements, redundant protectors, clear title records, and limited executable demands.

### 24.3 Dissipation

Pressure may dissipate when sources expire, interests change, a dispute loses relevance, a route reopens, an actor becomes unavailable, a claim is abandoned, or owners execute a stable settlement. Dissipation does not erase facts or memories.

### 24.4 Redirection

Pressure may redirect from one actor, place, or issue to another. A failed Norman service demand may become mediation, a Flemish dependency concern may become domestic officer investment, or a demesne acquisition opportunity may become a route-right agreement.

### 24.5 Closure

Closure requires an owner-backed receipt identifying what changed, what remains unresolved, which pressure state closes, and whether a successor pressure should be evaluated. Historical analogy is insufficient.
