## 0.1 Booyah Verdict

France opens as a recognized sacred monarchy whose crown is stronger than its direct machinery. Philip I is already king, but he is a child. The realm therefore begins neither as a collapsed state nor as a centralized kingdom. It is a functioning political order in which royal dignity, household custody, delegated government, princely autonomy, ecclesiastical recognition, roads, castles, revenues, and armed service do not automatically move together.

The campaign question is not merely whether France can conquer. It is whether the Capetian Crown can turn recognition into repeatable capacity without allowing the minority government, a great prince, a neighboring duke, or a temporary military solution to become a substitute sovereign.

**Campaign fantasy:** Wear the crown before possessing the kingdom.

**Modern Medieval II formula:** Medieval II campaign spine + finite capacity + political causality + historical uncertainty + real-time battle consequence.

This successor is deliberately deeper than the discarded France V2 surfaces. It uses the developed-faction standard visible in Wales, Ireland, and Norway: explicit opening topology, actor and institution portfolios, evidence classes, owner routing, non-scripted pressures, conditional emergence, campaign-map translation, battle consequence, workload controls, deterministic validation, and honest implementation boundaries.

## 1. Concise Verdict

Implement the faction as the **Kingdom of France** for player-facing clarity, while retaining **King of the Franks**, **Capetian Crown**, and **Royal Demesne** as historically specific titles and descriptors.

The January 1062 faction begins as a consecrated hereditary monarchy under a crowned child king. The Crown has broad recognition, sacred standing, and feudal jurisdiction, but limited direct territorial and military capacity. Great princes may recognize the king while retaining independent households, armies, revenues, courts, and strategic interests.

The core campaign fantasy is:

> **Wear the crown before possessing the kingdom.**

France must remain immediately recognizable as a classic Medieval II faction. Deeper systems should appear primarily through map conditions, army composition, diplomacy, succession, regency, settlement control, royal summons, and visible consequences rather than constant management screens.

## 3. Player-Facing Identity

### 3.1 Recommended naming

- **Faction selection name:** Kingdom of France
- **Short map label:** France
- **Royal title:** King of the Franks
- **Ruling dynasty:** House of Capet
- **Government descriptor:** Capetian Crown
- **Direct territorial descriptor:** Royal Demesne
- **Working development label:** Royal French
- **Adjective:** French, with Frankish reserved for appropriate ceremonial or historical context

### 3.2 Naming rationale

`Kingdom of France` provides immediate player recognition and preserves the classic Medieval II faction experience. `King of the Franks` and `Capetian Crown` preserve the historical texture without forcing the player to decode the faction-selection screen.

The faction name does not imply a later centralized nation-state. Internal governance must distinguish the kingdom, the Crown, the royal household, the royal demesne, great principalities, local lordships, church institutions, and negotiated obligations.

## 4. Opening Historical Boundary

### 4.1 Required opening condition

On 1 January 1062:

- Philip I is alive, crowned, underage, and the recognized king.
- Henry I is deceased and has no runtime agency.
- Anne of Kiev is alive and remains a major maternal, household, dynastic, ceremonial, and governmental actor.
- Baldwin V of Flanders is alive and remains a senior minority-government and cross-realm actor.
- Hugh, Philip's younger brother, is alive and underage.
- Normandy, Flanders, Blois, Valois, Burgundy, Anjou, Aquitaine, and Brittany retain separate rulers, title structures, households, and operational capacity.
- No later French territorial consolidation is completed merely because it occurred historically.
- No future Flemish succession crisis, Norman conquest of England, Breton-Norman war, Aquitanian campaign, or Angevin transformation is guaranteed.

### 4.2 Strategic interpretation

The Crown is recognized more widely than it can act directly. The king's legal and sacred importance gives France diplomatic reach, claims to counsel and service, and opportunities to mediate. Execution depends on regents, household officers, castellans, bishops, roads, resources, and princely cooperation.

### 4.3 Direct demesne versus kingdom

The kingdom is not the royal demesne.

The Royal Demesne should be represented through validated title holdings, settlements, castles, rights, revenues, routes, and institutions. Great principalities are not ordinary directly controlled provinces simply because their rulers acknowledge the king.

The exact January 1062 demesne ledger remains a downstream geography and title-authority task. Paris, Orléans, Senlis, Étampes, Melun, Poissy, and Saint-Denis are priority review identities rather than automatically approved holdings in this document.

## 12. France Campaign Arc

The player begins with one of the most prestigious crowns in Western Europe, but direct capacity is narrow and the king is a child.

- protect Philip and Hugh;

Centralization is possible, not guaranteed.
