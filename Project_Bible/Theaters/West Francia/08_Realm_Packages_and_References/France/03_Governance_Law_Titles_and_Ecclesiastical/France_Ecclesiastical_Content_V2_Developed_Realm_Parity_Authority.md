## 1. Refined Verdict

Implement Capetian France as a consecrated monarchy embedded within the Latin Church, while preserving the plurality of sees, monasteries, chapters, clerical offices, property regimes, jurisdictions, patrons, advocates, communities, and cross-Realm metropolitan relationships that make ecclesiastical politics strategic.

**Consecration strengthens recognition and dignity; it does not create troops, money, adult agency, direct control of bishops, or automatic obedience from princes.**

The opening ecclesiastical game is the conversion of sacred recognition into usable but bounded political capacity. Philip I is already crowned and consecrated yet remains underage. Reims carries coronation and metropolitan significance. Saint-Denis joins royal memory, commemoration, patronage, property, and ceremonial practice without becoming ordinary demesne. Orléans, Sens, and Tours connect the Crown to distinct diocesan, metropolitan, Loire, and cross-Realm problems. The royal chapel supports worship, literacy, ceremony, education, witnessing, and documents without becoming a second Church hierarchy. Anne, Baldwin, Ralph, great princes, bishops, abbots, chapters, monasteries, and outside authorities may cooperate, resist, mediate, protect, judge, witness, or bargain through separate powers and relationships.

The player should confront recognizable French problems:

- how a child king's consecration protects continuity without granting adult rule;
- how the Crown seeks witnesses, mediation, safe conduct, patronage, and appointment influence without owning the Church;
- how royal monasteries can support memory and government while retaining property, community, privilege, and institutional autonomy;
- how metropolitan boundaries cross secular Realm boundaries;
- how local bishops, abbots, chapters, princes, advocates, and communities may align differently;
- how disputes over marriage, oaths, sanctuary, property, appointment, consecration, and jurisdiction become political without collapsing into one religion meter;
- how reform, custom, papal intervention, episcopal autonomy, princely patronage, and royal necessity may produce several plausible paths rather than one scripted Investiture narrative.

## 6. Ecclesiastical Content Classes

### 6.1 Realm-Anchor Sacred Institution

An institution whose memory, ceremony, property, jurisdiction, or political relationship changes Crown-level strategy. Reims and Saint-Denis are principal candidates, but their functions remain different.

### 6.2 Diocesan or Metropolitan Interface

A see or metropolitan institution relevant through jurisdiction, appointment, communication, mediation, travel, local reception, or cross-Realm authority. Orléans, Sens, Reims, and Tours may occupy different combinations of these functions.

### 6.3 Royal Household Ecclesiastical Component

The royal chapel supports worship, ceremony, education, literacy, witnessing, and communication through household structures. It is not automatically a territorial church institution.

### 6.4 Officeholder Candidate

A historically established person whose office, lifecycle, date, and jurisdiction can be validated by Character, Office, Institution, and Opening State owners.

### 6.5 Unknown Holder Function

A supported office or function whose holder is unknown or unresolved. The institution and office may remain usable where owners permit, while holder-specific irreversible actions remain blocked.

### 6.6 External or Shared Institution Endpoint

An institution whose jurisdiction crosses Realm boundaries or whose canonical owner is shared or external. France may reference the endpoint but cannot duplicate it or create reverse relationships.

### 6.7 ReviewOnly or Quarantined Claim

A disputed officeholder, privilege, property claim, advocacy relation, foundation date, canonical judgment, later reform outcome, or supposed universal right with no Opening State effect.

## 31. Historical Fact, Evidence, Memory, Reputation, Momentum, and Emergence

## 32. Ecclesiastical AI Intent

### 32.1 Crown-facing priorities

- protect consecrated royal dignity and direct-line continuity;

- distinguish patronage from control and nomination from appointment;
- protect church property when obligation, strategy, and capacity align;

### 32.2 Institution-facing priorities

- negotiate when enforcement is weak or costs are excessive.

### 32.3 AI restraints
