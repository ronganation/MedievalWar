## 1. Refined Verdict

Implement January 1062 France as **The Consecrated Arbiter**, a prestigious but capacity-limited Crown that prefers dynastic continuity, protection and connection of the Royal Demesne, executable judgment, balance among great princes, ecclesiastical cooperation, selective enforcement, negotiated service, and gradual conversion of recognized precedent into dependable royal action.

France is not passive. France may defend, punish, intervene, call a host, acquire territory, exploit opportunity, support allies, and wage ambitious war. France differs because the AI must evaluate whether an action can be authorized, communicated, accepted, supplied, commanded, enforced, and retained without producing a coalition that the Crown cannot survive.

**Core question:** How does the Crown convert recognition into effective royal power without uniting the princes against it?

**Campaign fantasy:** Wear the Crown before possessing the kingdom.

Realm Personality explains how France interprets circumstances. Realm AI proposes how France might act. Neither owns authority, information, armies, territory, money, relationships, or outcomes.

## 9. Risk Policy Portfolio

### 9.1 Unenforceable Command Policy

When a proposed command exceeds known execution capacity, prefer narrowing scope, seeking mediation, requesting assurance, securing route or agent, negotiating service, gathering evidence, or delaying. A high-risk command remains possible when survival, law, dignity, or extraordinary opportunity justifies it.

### 9.2 Protector Concentration Policy

If Anne, Baldwin, Ralph, an officer, household, or institution concentrates too many access, information, custody, seal, diplomatic, fiscal, and military functions, propose redistribution through actual mandates rather than a balancing meter.

### 9.3 Direct-Line Security Policy

Avoid exposing Philip and Hugh to the same avoidable threat, route, household capture, siege, disease environment, or travel hazard when one incident could destroy the direct line. Do not split them reflexively if separate security is worse.

### 9.4 Expansion Retention Policy

Before conquest or annexation, evaluate title or possession basis, route, season, supply, recruitment and recovery, garrison, local administration, institutions, population response, treasury, likely service duration, neighboring claimants, and whether the acquisition connects the demesne.

### 9.5 Coalition Exposure Policy

Avoid giving several strong princes a shared immediate reason to oppose the Crown unless the threat is existential or the Crown has credible allied, military, diplomatic, and ecclesiastical support.

### 9.6 Credibility Recovery Policy

After ignored judgments or summonses, do not repeat the same demand automatically. Consider local enforcement, narrower venue, compensation, mediation, evidence repair, a different actor, or deliberate non-escalation.

### 9.7 Information Uncertainty Policy

When evidence or reports conflict, seek corroboration, delay irreversible action, prepare reversible options, or communicate uncertainty. Do not treat Unknown as favorable or neutral.

## 10. Minority-Government AI

### Anne of Kiev

Value Anne for maternal access, household continuity, ceremony, correspondence, patronage, institutional links, and owner-approved mandates. Never infer universal regency from motherhood.

### Baldwin V of Flanders

Value Baldwin for protection, mediation, diplomacy, and coordination. Account for Flemish responsibilities, family interests, travel, succession pressure, and external ownership.

### Ralph IV of Valois

Value Ralph for local weight, advice, route support, escort, and execution. Separate access to Anne or court from custody, regency, seal use, treasury, or Crown command.

### Philip I

Support age-appropriate observation, consultation, education, ceremony, and gradual participation. Personal rule must emerge from current biological state, readiness, information access, office and household arrangements, and recognized capacity.

### Hugh of France

Protect Hugh as direct-line depth without manufacturing adult rivalry, office, title, command, marriage, or claimant faction.

### Decision patterns

- preserve Baldwin's diplomatic mandate while locating custody elsewhere;
- permit Anne to supervise household access while another actor coordinates defense;
- request Ralph's escort without seal or regency scope;
- expand Philip's supervised participation after owner-published readiness evidence;
- protect Hugh separately when danger warrants it;
- review mandates individually during transition rather than terminate all at once.

## 11. Great-Prince Evaluation

### 11.1 Normandy

Respect immediate military and administrative capacity; preserve negotiation and service channels; guard against domination; avoid automatic loyalty or hostility; evaluate each summons through obligation, route, duration, target, capability, current relations, and Normandy-owned state.

### 11.2 Flanders and Hainaut

Preserve bounded protection while reducing permanent dependency; monitor owner-published succession and family fragmentation; distinguish Flemish, Hainaut, Norman, and French interests; never infer troops, funds, knowledge, or French authority from kinship.

### 11.3 Valois

Use Ralph when Crown and regional interests align; monitor concentration near Anne and direct royal centers; distinguish military utility, household access, regional rights, and Crown mandate.

### 11.4 Blois and Champagne Interests

Expect formal recognition with negotiated performance; guard key corridors; mediate family and title disputes where current authority supports it; do not preload later Champagne consolidation or permanent hostility.

### 11.5 Burgundy

Value Capetian cadet kinship, mediation, and strategic cooperation while preserving Burgundy as a separate Realm. Service remains negotiable. Burgundy is not France-owned and is not generic rebel territory.

### 11.6 Anjou

Monitor Loire routes, Maine, Blois, Normandy, Brittany, and Aquitaine interfaces; use mediation and balance; resist corridor domination that isolates direct royal holdings.

### 11.7 Aquitaine

Recognize distance and practical autonomy; prefer negotiated service, marriage inquiry, reciprocal recognition, or joint opportunity over routine coercion; do not treat southern titles or forces as direct Crown assets.

### 11.8 Brittany

Preserve one separate diplomatic and owner interface; mediate Norman-Breton pressure where advantageous; do not duplicate Brittany AI, characters, Network, institutions, or military state.

### 11.9 Minor Factions, Local Polities, Contested Holdings, and Rebels

Evaluate each entity by actual class. Smaller factions may possess rulers, diplomacy, armies, titles, and strategic objectives. Local polities may negotiate or submit. Contested holdings may lack effective administration. Rebels require revolt, mutiny, claimant, resistance, or collapse context. AI must not absorb all gray or undeveloped map entities into one rebel category.

## 12. Royal Demesne AI

Priority sequence:

1. preserve validated direct holdings;
2. protect royal children, household, officers, seal, and archives;
3. maintain roads and routes among direct centers;
4. maintain garrisons and local agents;
5. improve dependable revenue and supply;
6. repair documentary, judicial, and office execution;
7. acquire connecting rights, bridges, castles, tolls, forts, outworks, and settlements when validated;
8. avoid disconnected acquisitions whose costs exceed strategic value.

A bridge, passage right, local castle, or accepted castellan may be more valuable than a large but isolated county. AI explanations should make that legible.

## 13. Royal Itinerary AI

Itinerary proposals consider government priorities, ceremony, child safety, routes, weather, season, supply, audiences, justice, church institutions, military threats, information needs, household capacity, archives, officers, and delegated authority.

Routine safe travel should be automated. Request player attention when priorities conflict, the king's presence is uniquely valuable, passage is disputed, a crisis invalidates the route, major ceremony is involved, or both royal princes would face unacceptable common exposure.

Presence improves some local interactions but creates absence elsewhere. The AI must compare sending the court, an officer, an envoy, a prince, a cleric, a military escort, or only a sealed message.

## 14. Diplomacy AI

Prefer specific assurances, bounded terms, mediated settlements, reciprocal recognition, narrow demands, ratification, safe conduct, marriage inquiry, service clarification, and guarantees tied to actual capacity.

Avoid simultaneous hostility with several great princes, promises beyond treasury or force capacity, vague alliance assumptions, future historical knowledge, and treating homage as unlimited service.

Diplomacy may seek balance, access, time, force concentration, route security, recognition, information, or peaceful incorporation. Peace is not always passivity, and war is not always aggression.

## 15. Ecclesiastical AI

Preserve consecrated kingship and maintain Reims, Saint-Denis, Orléans, Sens, and Tours interfaces where owners validate them. Seek witnessing, mediation, safe conduct, reconciliation, patronage, confirmation, or canonical review when useful.

Respect institutional identity, chapters, officeholders, property, jurisdiction, routes, and procedure. Avoid universal appointment claims and coercion the Crown cannot sustain. Church support may improve acceptance or mediation, but does not grant obedience, troops, revenue, or adult agency.

## 16. Military AI

### 16.1 Direct defense

Protect the direct line, Royal Demesne, household force, garrisons, routes, officers, and supply first when threats are credible.

### 16.2 Royal host

Before calling a host, assess lawful basis, likely responders, contingent strength, service duration, arrival times, routes, compensation, substitutes, command, cohesion, supplies, recovery, refusal consequences, and government exposure.

### 16.3 Preferred wars

- defense of Philip, Hugh, direct holdings, or essential routes;
- enforcement of a high-value executable judgment;
- containment of a dominant threat;
- acquisition connecting or protecting the demesne;
- rescue of a critical ally or institution;
- bounded intervention with clear diplomatic and retention value;
- exploitation of an opportunity only when government can survive the campaign.

### 16.4 Guarded wars

- distant prestige expeditions;
- multi-front conflict;
- campaigns dependent on expiring service;
- wars likely to align great princes or Church authorities against the Crown;
- conquest without routes, garrison, administration, recovery, or treasury;
- battles fought only to award experience.

### 16.5 General development

AI may value safe but meaningful command opportunities for valid adult commanders. Experience remains evidence-based through Character Development. Participation or victory does not guarantee a level.

## 17. Economy and Development AI

Prioritize dependable demesne revenue, household and garrison affordability, roads, bridges, markets, tolls, storage, supply, defenses, chancery and office execution, messenger capacity, port or river access where relevant, and development that reduces brittle dependence.

Every proposal requires confirmed resources and owner-published effects. Avoid abstract bonus chasing, uncontrolled debt, construction without protection, and economic plans that assume princely revenue belongs to the Crown.

## 18. Succession and Dynasty AI

Read but do not own Philip and Hugh's safety, candidate evaluations, acting dynasty authority, marriage possibilities, branch relationships, recognition, church standing, and claims.

Prioritize preservation of both princes, Philip's formation, an appropriate future for Hugh without manufactured rivalry, avoidance of shared catastrophic exposure, and maintenance of useful Burgundian and Flemish dynasty channels without automatic loyalty.

Do not force marriage, child, title, branch, or succession outcome without legal, biological, diplomatic, and owner validation.

## 19. Governance and Office AI

Consume current typed mandates and office state. Prefer actions with a real actor, authority source, scope, seal or procedure, route, resources, reporting, review, and termination.

When an office is vacant or the holder is Unknown, route routine functions only as owners permit. Do not fabricate a holder. Weigh whether an officer, Anne, Baldwin, Ralph, a prince, an envoy, a clerical mediator, or the supervised king is the appropriate actor for one task.

Royal Reach is an explanation input, not a utility score.

## 20. Information, Intelligence, and Uncertainty AI

AI may use only delivered, observed, witnessed, owner-published, or validly inferred information. Reports carry confidence, age, source, and access limits.

When evidence conflicts, AI may seek corroboration, send an envoy, request witnesses, inspect locally, delay, prepare reversible options, or act under explicitly stated uncertainty. AI cannot read hidden private posture, undelivered messages, undiscovered claims, foreign internal state, or later history.

## 21. Campaign-Phase Behavior

### 21.1 Protected Crown

Survive the minority, secure the household, protect demesne routes, maintain bounded Baldwin support, preserve Anne's useful roles, use Ralph carefully, avoid humiliating demands, cultivate institutions, and develop direct capacity.

### 21.2 Recovering Crown

Possible conditions include Philip's growing readiness, reliable officers, connected routes, repeatable judgment and summons performance, reduced protector dependency, and improved treasury and military capacity.

Behavior may expand mediation and justice, acquire strategic rights, cultivate lesser lords and institutions, improve the host, and balance great princes more actively.

### 21.3 Effective Monarchy or Alternate Settlement

Possible outcomes include coherent Capetian monarchy, prestigious restricted Crown, militarized expansion, ecclesiastical arbitration, prince-dominated monarchy, negotiated confederative order, dynastic crisis, collateral transition, or another recognized ruler controlling or replacing the Crown through valid systems.

No phase is guaranteed or tied to a calendar date.

## 24. Explainability Contract

Good explanations retained and expanded:

- The Crown proposes mediation because both princes recognize Philip while direct enforcement is weak.
- The summons is narrowed because Normandy disputes scope and the host cannot sustain a long campaign.
- The route near Orléans is prioritized because it connects direct holdings and supports itinerary, messages, and supply.
- Baldwin's mandate remains because Philip is vulnerable, but custody is not expanded.
- Aquitaine receives a negotiated request because distance and autonomy reduce direct enforcement.
- No immediate action is proposed because reports conflict and an irreversible command would expose royal credibility.
- A small connecting holding is preferred over a larger disconnected conquest because garrison and retention are achievable.

Bad explanations:

- Authority score is too low.
- Opinion is minus twelve.
- France follows history.
- The AI wants centralization.
- The player is weaker.
- Burgundy is a rebel region.

### 39.7 Event-chain enrichment
