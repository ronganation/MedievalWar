## 4. One-Screen Player Card

- **Unit:** Demesne Spear Service
- **French name candidate:** Service de Lance du Domaine
- **Class:** Organized direct-demesne spear-and-shield infantry
- **Source:** Qualifying direct demesne districts, estates, settlements, castles, route points, and associated recurring service
- **Availability:** Finite, source-bound, equipment-dependent, leader-dependent, and narrower than Rural Levy Spearmen
- **Battle promise:** A dependable defensive spear frontage that braces against mounted pressure, protects archers, holds key ground, and gives France time for relief and mounted shock
- **Best use:** Defensive lines, protected flanks, crossings, roads, camp edges, settlement approaches, archer protection, and mounted-threat control
- **Worst use:** Unsupported assault, broken terrain with lost cohesion, prolonged pursuit, powerful missile exposure, elite infantry grinding, and distant service without rotation
- **Strategic tradeoff:** Removing the best established direct-demesne defenders weakens estates, castles, routes, stores, settlements, officers, and later replacement capacity
- **Counterplay:** Missiles, dismounted heavy infantry, flank attacks, fatigue, terrain disruption, officer loss, forced movement, and attacks away from the spear point

## 5. Player-Facing Description

Demesne Spear Service formations are drawn from qualifying places within the Crown’s direct military reach that can sustain recurring defensive service. Their personnel are not household elites or a standing national army. Their advantage comes from repeated muster, maintained weapons, recognizable leaders, familiar defensive tasks, and the ability to form a more dependable spear frontage than emergency rural levies.

On the battlefield, the formation establishes the line around which early France operates. Demesne Archers fire from behind it. Sergents des Bourgs relieve threatened sectors or press enemies fixed by the spears. Castle Mounted Sergeants and other validated mounted support protect the flanks. Milites du Domaine and other validated knightly service exploit disorder after the line has held.

Recruiting the unit is never free. A field army gains a stable spear formation while a demesne center, castle, estate, crossing, route, store, or settlement loses part of its established defense and recovery capacity.

## 27. Battlefield Rhythm

1. March in a controlled column and deploy before contact.
2. Establish frontage, facing, spear spacing, and protected flanks.
3. Provide Demesne Archers a safe field of fire and withdrawal route.
4. Hold fire lanes and terrain rather than chasing skirmishers.
5. Brace against frontal mounted pressure when formation conditions pass.
6. Fix or delay enemy infantry without entering a wasteful offensive grind.
7. Accept relief from Sergents des Bourgs before cohesion collapses.
8. Support knightly or mounted counterattack after the enemy is fixed or disordered.
9. Withdraw in order if the position, flanks, or mission becomes untenable.

## 28. Strengths

- Dependable Early Era defensive frontage.
- Better cohesion, leadership, and equipment consistency than Rural Levy Spearmen.
- Strong frontal mounted-threat control when properly braced.
- Excellent combined-arms partner for Demesne Archers.
- Useful at roads, crossings, camps, settlement approaches, and defended flanks.
- Clear direct-demesne campaign identity.
- Persistent and developable without automatic replacement.
- Valuable rally and withdrawal anchor.

## 29. Weaknesses

- Limited offensive flexibility.
- Vulnerable to missiles during static exposure.
- Vulnerable to flanking and rear attacks.
- Vulnerable to elite infantry in prolonged grinding combat.
- Poor pursuit.
- Formation dependent.
- Mixed equipment, officer loss, fatigue, and broken terrain reduce value sharply.
- Scarcer and more institutionally costly than Rural Levy Spearmen.
- Not a substitute for household or armored professionals.
