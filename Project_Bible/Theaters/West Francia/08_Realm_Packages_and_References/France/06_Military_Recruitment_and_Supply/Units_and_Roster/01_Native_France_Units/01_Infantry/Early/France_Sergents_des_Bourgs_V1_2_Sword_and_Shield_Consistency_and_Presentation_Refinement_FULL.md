Sergents des Bourgs are the proposed reliable early sword-and-shield infantry layer between Rural Levy Spearmen, Demesne Spear Service, castle-specific defenders, and owner-specific household foot. The family draws not from an abstract middle tier but from established communities and posts whose markets, crossings, tolls, routes, stores, officers, and recurring security duties can sustain more accountable service.

The unit survives only if the source and battlefield cycle remain distinct. Sergents des Bourgs must not become Demesne Spear Service with better armor, Foot Sergeants under a decorative French name, or town militia projected backward without evidence.

## 4. One-Screen Player Card

- **Unit:** Sergents des Bourgs
- **English name:** Borough Sergeants
- **Class:** Established borough, market, crossing, and route-service sword-and-shield infantry
- **Source:** Validated market communities, bridgeheads, toll or route posts, borough-like centers, and direct royal centers with recurring service capacity
- **Availability:** Narrower than Rural Levy Spearmen; finite, institution-dependent, equipment-dependent, and subject to local-security reservations
- **Battle promise:** A sword-and-shield relief formation that can secure a threatened frontage, press disorganized infantry, protect a route, and exploit openings without pretending to be heavy professional infantry
- **Best use:** Defensive field anchors, road and crossing protection, bridgeheads, market approaches, convoy relief, garrison relief, supported line fighting, and controlled counterpressure
- **Worst use:** Unsupported shock assault, long pursuit, open-flank exposure, cavalry engagement without formation, extended service after local obligations expire, and attritional use as disposable heavy infantry
- **Strategic tradeoff:** Better reliability than rural levy in exchange for removing scarce security personnel, officers, equipment, and route capacity from economically important places
- **Counterplay:** Concentrated missiles, flank pressure, elite household infantry, dismounted heavy troops, prolonged fatigue, cavalry after cohesion breaks, and threats against the home source

## 5. Player-Facing Description

Sergents des Bourgs are established nonknightly infantry drawn from qualifying market communities, bridge approaches, toll and route posts, and direct royal centers able to maintain recurring security and military service. The unit is not a national regiment. Every card remains connected to a source that normally guards roads, stores, crossings, officials, revenues, or community access.

On the battlefield, Sergents des Bourgs provide the early Capetian player with a reliable anchor. Thick padded protection, swords or substantial one-handed sidearms, sturdy shields, recognizable local leadership, and repeated service allow the formation to hold longer and recover formation more effectively than Rural Levy Spearmen. The unit still lacks the equipment, cohesion, and protected household basis of elite retainers or knightly households.

Recruiting Borough Sergeants makes an economic and security choice visible. A market, bridgehead, toll point, or royal center contributing its best available sergeants may gain a stronger field army while weakening local watch, route security, toll collection, convoy reliability, store custody, and emergency response.

The V1 spear identity did not survive the whole-roster collision review. France already has Rural Levy Spearmen and the planned Demesne Spear Service. Retaining another early spear card would make Sergents des Bourgs depend too heavily on source text while weakening battlefield differentiation.

The V1.1 card therefore adopts **sword-and-shield infantry** as its Total War combat identity. This follows a roster principle already visible elsewhere in the project: <File>Norman Town Militia Swordsmen</File> and <File>Norman Militia Swordsmen</File> use an Early Era settlement sword-and-shield role, while <File>Anglo-Norman Swordsmen</File> owns a professional sword-and-shield development role distinct from <File>Anglo-Norman Sergeants</File>, which remains spear infantry. citeturn85search68turn85search86turn85search113turn85search110

If research shows that sword access is too narrow for a recruitable family, this card must narrow, become High Era conditional, or revert to an assignment rather than duplicate the spear line.

## 20. Battlefield Rhythm

1. Enter behind or beside the spear line in an ordered column.
2. Move toward a threatened route, crossing, market approach, or weakening frontage.
3. Use shields to close under light missile pressure where conditions permit.
4. Relieve Rural Levy Spearmen or support Demesne Spear Service rather than duplicate their brace role.
5. Press disorganized infantry with controlled sword-and-shield counterpressure.
6. Exploit gaps created by missiles, spears, terrain, or mounted shock.
7. Refuse unsupported cavalry contact and avoid open-flank pursuit.
8. Restore order around the route, objective, or relieved formation.
9. Withdraw or rotate before losses destroy scarce cadres and source security.

## 21. Strengths

- More reliable than Rural Levy Spearmen.
- Clear early field-anchor role.
- Better leadership and accountability.
- Better shield, blade, and close-order consistency.
- Useful at roads, crossings, markets, stores, and bridgeheads.
- Flexible between field, escort, relief, and defensive assignments.
- Supports missiles and weaker infantry.
- Strong source identity tied to the Crown’s routes and economically important centers.
- Persistent into the High Era through maturation rather than automatic replacement.

- Better close-quarters pressure and frontage relief than spear-centered levy families.
- Stronger in streets, gates, baggage lanes, and congested objectives where long spears become awkward.

## 22. Weaknesses

- Narrower source than rural levy.
- Expensive in local security and route capacity.
- Not elite in prolonged close combat.
- Vulnerable to concentrated missiles.
- Poor independent cavalry resistance and dependent on spear, terrain, or mounted support.
- Limited pursuit and shock action.
- Mixed source quality may reduce uniformity.
- Heavy casualties erase scarce cadres and equipment.
- Distant campaigning may exceed ordinary service and support.

## 29. Garrisons, Settlements, Roads, and Sieges

### Garrison relief

The unit can temporarily relieve or reinforce a garrison, but it is not a universal castle-custody formation.

### Settlement defense

The unit performs well at streets, gateways, market approaches, bridgeheads, and store areas where formation and familiar leadership remain intact.

### Siege defense

The unit can defend secondary walls, gates, internal streets, breaches after preparation, and critical stores. The unit is not an elite breach counterattack force.

### Siege offense

The unit may guard approaches, camps, roads, convoys, siege stores, and labor teams. Assault use should be limited and costly.

## 30. Combined Arms

Sergents des Bourgs provide the stable center around which France’s early combined-arms roster can operate:

- Rural Levy Spearmen extend frontage and absorb emergency tasks.
- Demesne Archers and other separately validated missile families provide protected missile support.
- Sergents des Bourgs provide the principal ordinary early foot-sergeant expression through shielded relief and controlled close pressure; any additional generic Foot Sergeants card remains held behind a separate full-card gate.
- Castle Mounted Sergeants and other separately validated mounted support screen and protect flanks.
- Milites du Domaine and other validated knightly service deliver shock after the line fixes the enemy.
