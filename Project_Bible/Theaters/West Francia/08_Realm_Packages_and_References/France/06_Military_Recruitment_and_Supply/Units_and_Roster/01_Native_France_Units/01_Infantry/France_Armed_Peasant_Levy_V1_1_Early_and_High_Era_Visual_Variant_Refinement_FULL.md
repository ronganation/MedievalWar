## 3. One-Screen Player Card

- **Unit:** Armed Peasant Levy
- **Class:** Lowest-pool mixed-weapon levy infantry
- **Source:** Eligible, unreserved, released local contributors who can form a real movable unit but do not meet or should not consume a standardized spear-family requirement
- **Availability:** Broad in potential population reach but finite, protected-minimum limited, equipment limited, source owned, cooperation dependent, and recovery sensitive
- **Battle promise:** Cheap bodies that gather quickly enough to obstruct, guard, absorb secondary tasks, and survive in prepared or constricted positions
- **Best use:** Settlements, camps, baggage, stores, prisoners, labor areas, rear approaches, forts, low-demand secondary frontage, and emergency occupation security
- **Worst use:** Open-field line combat, fresh cavalry, sustained missile exposure, disciplined infantry, complex maneuver, pursuit, assault, and distant campaigning
- **Strategic tradeoff:** Preserves better military pools but consumes broad household, labor, transport, watch, and local support capacity
- **Counterplay:** Shock, missiles, maneuver, flank pressure, leader loss, fatigue, isolation, poor terms, and removal from supported local conditions

## 4. Player-Facing Description

Armed Peasant Levies are locally assembled formations drawn from people who can contribute to defense and military support but lack the compatible equipment and practice needed for France's spear-centered levy families. Their weapons and protection vary, their order is poor, and their endurance away from home is limited. What they offer is useful work: guarding a settlement, camp, baggage train, store, prisoner enclosure, labor area, or quiet approach while better troops take the decisive position.

They can leave a supported site and join an army, but local support ends or changes when they depart. Prolonged service raises household, labor, supply, discipline, and recovery pressure. Casualties are not disposable because the same people and property support food, transport, repairs, local warning, and future recruitment.

## 18. Battlefield Rhythm

- Gather around a recognized leader, site, or task.
- Group compatible weapons and assign a simple position.
- Occupy prepared, constricted, rear-area, or low-demand frontage.
- Guard, obstruct, move, carry, hold briefly, or absorb secondary pressure.
- Depend on terrain, stronger neighbors, and a visible retreat or rally point.
- Avoid fresh shock, missile exposure, open maneuver, and prolonged melee.
- Yield, withdraw, disperse, or rally before complete collapse where the mission permits.
- Return for casualty, property, equipment, and household accounting.

## 19. Strengths

- Broad potential source after protected minimums and competing assignments.
- Cheap local garrison and support utility where capacity exists.
- Lower equipment floor than spear-standardized or established formations.
- Useful for secondary tasks that would waste better troops.
- Can move, leave, join an army, enter a fort, and return as a real formation.
- Stronger utility in settlements, camps, constricted ground, and prepared defense.
- Preserves Rural Levy Spearmen, Demesne Spear Service, sergeants, garrisons, households, missiles, and mounted forces for more demanding work.
- Can remain relevant when better military systems develop because low-demand duties persist.

## 20. Weaknesses

- Very poor order and battlefield recovery.
- No reliable spear frontage or anti-cavalry identity.
- Weak open-field combat and maneuver.
- Vulnerable to shock, missiles, disciplined infantry, flank pressure, and isolation.
- Poor campaign endurance and increasing service pressure away from local support.
- Inconsistent equipment and leadership.
- Weak pursuit and assault capability.
- Broad household, labor, watch, transport, and recovery cost despite low coin value.
- Heavy losses may weaken future Armed Peasant Levy, Rural Levy Spearmen, transport, repair, food, and local-defense capacity.

### Settlement and garrison use

The unit is especially valuable as a real movable cheap garrison where actual support capacity, source relationship, cooperation, quarters, stores, food, and command pass. The unit does not appear automatically because a settlement exists.

### Fort and camp use

The unit may guard approaches, stores, baggage, animals, prisoners, labor areas, and quiet sectors. A fort or camp does not create the pool or the people.

### Siege defense

The unit may guard stores, carry supplies, protect noncombatants, occupy low-demand frontage, support barricades, and reinforce rear positions.

### Siege offense

The unit may dig, carry, move supplies, guard prisoners and stores, maintain camp works, or operate equipment where the siege system separately authorizes it. This does not make the unit an engineer or assault specialist.

## 45. Combined Arms

Armed Peasant Levy supports France by:

- guarding low-demand positions so Rural Levy Spearmen can hold spear-relevant approaches;
- protecting camps, baggage, stores, prisoners, labor areas, and rear routes;
- assisting movement and siege labor where separately authorized;
- filling secondary frontage behind Demesne Spear Service;
- preserving Sergents des Bourgs and Castle Sergeants for relief, passage, objective, and custodial work;
- allowing mounted, missile, household, and knightly forces to concentrate on tasks requiring their capabilities;
- providing a visible fallback when better local pools are depleted or reserved.

It should not be used as the formation that fixes a fresh cavalry charge or anchors the principal field line.
