Communal Guisarmiers are a High Era French communal infantry family drawn from an existing lower-service population rather than from a new social class or free technology-created pool. The formation exists to perform work that the spear line does not: cutting into compressed infantry, hooking and manipulating at close range, widening disorder, reinforcing assaults in constricted ground, and punishing mounted troops that have already stalled or lost formation.

The unit survives the roster gate only as a branch beside Rural Levy Spearmen, Demesne Spear Service, Sergents des Bourgs, and Castle Sergeants. It is not a universal replacement, a higher-stat spear unit, a household retinue, or a disguised Late Era halberd formation.

## 6. Player-Facing Description

Communal Guisarmiers are selected from communities already owing lower military or defensive service. Their appearance does not mark the creation of a new population. It marks a High Era change in organization: suitable contributors, available long-shafted cutting weapons, repeated muster experience, and recognized leaders are grouped into a formation with a clearer offensive purpose.

On the battlefield, Communal Guisarmiers work beside rather than above the spear line. Spearmen establish frontage, receive mounted pressure, and protect space with shields and reach. Guisarmiers enter where contact has become dense, where an enemy is already fixed, or where streets, gates, bridges, and broken frontage reward cutting and hooking pressure.

The formation is dangerous but exposed. Most members cannot use a substantial shield while operating the primary weapon. The unit therefore depends on timing, terrain, neighboring formations, and leadership. Wasteful commitment can remove scarce experienced contributors and serviceable polearms from the same communities expected to sustain local defense and future recruitment.

## 29. Battlefield Rhythm

- March behind or beside the formation expected to establish contact.
- Remain screened from avoidable missile fire.
- Identify compressed, fixed, stalled, or disordered targets.
- Enter on a favorable frontage rather than receiving the first shock.
- Use cutting and hooking pressure to disturb weapons, shields, balance, and local cohesion.
- Widen gaps for neighboring infantry or mounted exploitation.
- Avoid chasing mobile enemies into open ground.
- Withdraw and reform before fatigue and crowding destroy weapon spacing.
- Preserve leaders and serviceable polearms for the next commitment.

## 30. Strengths

- Distinct High Era communal offensive infantry role.
- Strong pressure against disordered levy and ordinary infantry.
- Useful in streets, gates, bridge approaches, camps, and constricted objectives.
- Effective follow-up behind spear or shielded infantry.
- Capable of punishing stalled, trapped, or congested mounted troops.
- Greater local cutting and manipulation pressure than ordinary spearmen.
- Persistent regional development potential without mandatory universal replacement.

## 31. Weaknesses

- Weak missile protection.
- Poor clean heavy-charge reception.
- No ordinary shield wall or spear brace.
- Lower cohesion and endurance than professional Late Era polearm infantry.
- Vulnerable to disciplined longer-reach formations.
- Vulnerable to flanking, open terrain, and premature commitment.
- More workshop- and repair-dependent than basic spear levy.
- Heavy losses damage the same communal pool used by other formations.
