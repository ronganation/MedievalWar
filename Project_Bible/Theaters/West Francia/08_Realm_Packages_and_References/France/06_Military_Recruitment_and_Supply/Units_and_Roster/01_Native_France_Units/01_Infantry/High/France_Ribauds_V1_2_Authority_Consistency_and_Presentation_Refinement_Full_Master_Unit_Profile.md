Ribauds are low-status royal-army irregulars used where the army needs people willing to enter dangerous, confused, or materially motivated work that formal infantry should not perform alone. Their battlefield value comes after stronger troops, terrain, missiles, or disorder create an opening. Ribauds exploit isolated and disrupted targets, move through broken spaces, respond around the camp, and participate in the material aftermath of combat.

Ribauds are not elite assassins, disciplined heavy infantry, generic camp followers, or a domestic label for every routier. Their aggression is useful only while leadership, attachment, protection, and withdrawal remain credible.

## 4. Player-Facing Description

Ribauds are rough, lightly equipped fighting men attached to the developing royal army. They accompany the host, remain close to its camp and support structure, and enter dangerous actions when stronger troops create disorder. In battle they are most effective against enemies who are isolated, compressed, distracted, retreating, or already losing formation.

Ribauds are dangerous but difficult to control. Their equipment varies, their formal line discipline is limited, and the prospect of spoils can pull them away from the objective. A capable leader can direct their aggression into a follow-up assault, camp response, or controlled exploitation. Without leadership and nearby support, they may disperse, become trapped in melee, or expose themselves to cavalry and counterattack.

## 22. Strengths

- Distinct aggressive High Era France identity.
- Effective follow-up pressure after another formation creates disorder.
- Useful in camp edges, broken ground, congested approaches, and confused melee.
- Lower formal equipment threshold than sergeant infantry.
- Can pressure isolated support troops and disordered enemies.
- Creates a meaningful discipline-versus-exploitation decision.

## 23. Weaknesses

- Poor formal line-holding ability.
- Uneven weapons and protection.
- Vulnerable to cavalry and missile fire.
- Weak against fresh heavy infantry and organized spears.
- High dependence on leaders, nearby support, and a withdrawal route.
- Plunder, dispersal, and friendly-congestion risks.
- Losses damage experienced irregular and command capacity.
