Voulgiers are France's developed polearm line-support infantry. They bring a heavier, more coherent, more disciplined cutting weapon to a battle line that already has something else holding the enemy in place. They are neither expendable farm-tool levies nor elite armored killers. They are the institutional middle ground that makes later France feel more organized, more dangerous, and more rewarding to continue playing.

## 2. Total War Unit Promise

The unit must deliver four immediate player-readable promises:

1. **A real upgrade in institutional capability.** The player sees that France has moved beyond improvised and loosely grouped polearms.
2. **A distinct battle tool.** The player uses Voulgiers to sustain pressure where Guisarmiers would make a shorter disruptive intervention.
3. **A combined-arms formation.** The unit becomes dangerous when protected and timed correctly, not when thrown unsupported into every matchup.
4. **A visible campaign achievement.** The card appears because parish sourcing, royal administration, equipment custody, inspection, captaincy, and recovery now work together.

## 14. Weapon Handling Doctrine

The Voulgier fights through measured two-handed cuts supported by short steps and disciplined recovery. Overcommitted swings create dangerous openings. The formation therefore values:

- staggered cutting space;
- controlled forward pressure;
- safe weapon recovery;
- file relief;
- avoiding shaft entanglement;
- leadership timing;
- maintaining a stable partner formation nearby.

The unit should not animate like axemen, pikemen, or halberdiers.

## 24. Contact Matchups

### Favorable

- ordinary infantry already fixed by another formation;
- exposed two-handed or lightly protected infantry at similar or shorter reach;
- disordered opponents in confined terrain;
- mounted troops that have stalled, become compressed, or lost charge momentum;
- exhausted enemies unable to exploit recovery windows.

### Unfavorable

- fresh heavy cavalry with charge space;
- disciplined longer-reach spear or pike formations;
- shielded heavy infantry able to absorb cuts and close safely;
- concentrated missiles during approach or prolonged exposure;
- mobile skirmishers and cavalry that refuse contact;
- elite assault infantry with superior armor and cohesion.

## 25. Anti-Infantry Identity

The heavy blade gives the unit credible cutting danger, but the card should not receive universal armor-piercing behavior. Its performance comes from:

- sustained contact;
- broad dangerous cuts;
- controlled formation pressure;
- exploiting exposed or disordered targets;
- relief that preserves combat output.

Against heavily armored elites, the unit should remain useful but inefficient without support or fatigue advantage.

## 26. Anti-Mounted Identity

The Voulgier is dangerous to mounted troops when:

- the charge has stalled;
- horses and riders are compressed near terrain or another formation;
- the unit attacks flanks or trapped targets;
- the polearm's cut, thrust, or hook can be used without receiving full momentum.

The Voulgier is not a spear wall and must not gain universal charge reflection or cavalry immunity.

## 34. Combined-Arms Doctrine

### Ideal partners

- spear infantry to absorb or deter mounted shock;
- shielded sergeants to secure frontage and objectives;
- archers or crossbowmen to pressure enemies reluctant to engage;
- cavalry to punish enemies held or disordered by Voulgier pressure;
- reserve infantry to protect relief and reform.

### Failure without partners

Unsupported Voulgiers become vulnerable to missiles, cavalry, longer reach, flank attacks, and fatigue collapse.
