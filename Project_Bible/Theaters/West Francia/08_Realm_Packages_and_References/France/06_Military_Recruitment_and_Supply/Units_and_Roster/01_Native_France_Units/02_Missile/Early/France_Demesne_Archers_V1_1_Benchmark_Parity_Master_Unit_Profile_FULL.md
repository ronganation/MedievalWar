Demesne Archers provide France with its first accessible missile family. The unit is neither a mass rural levy with bows nor a later formal royal militia. It represents the selected bow users, equipment, ammunition, local leadership, and defensive institutions that qualifying Royal Demesne, castle, estate, settlement, household, or community sources can actually sustain.

The family survives because Rural Levy Spearmen, Demesne Spear Service, and Sergents des Bourgs need missile support, while later crossbows, paid specialists, household archers, and mature urban institutions should retain separate sources and battlefield cycles.

## 4. One-Screen Player Card

- **Unit:** Demesne Archers
- **French name:** Archers du Domaine Royal
- **Class:** Conditional demesne and local bow infantry
- **Source:** Validated Royal Demesne, castle, estate, settlement, household, or community bow capacity
- **Availability:** Finite, source-dependent, skill-dependent, ammunition-dependent, and narrower than ordinary rural levy
- **Battle promise:** Early missile pressure that disrupts exposed light troops, protects approaches, and supports the spear and sword line from defended positions
- **Best use:** Behind Demesne Spear Service, protected settlement approaches, defensive crossings, walls and garrisons, camp edges, elevated ground, and fire against exposed or disordered light targets
- **Worst use:** Unsupported open flanks, melee, cavalry contact, heavily armored or well-shielded targets, ammunition waste, offensive assaults, and prolonged distant service without resupply
- **Strategic tradeoff:** Removing experienced bow users, ammunition, and missile equipment from local defense in order to support a field army
- **Counterplay:** Cavalry, shielded advance, armor, terrain masking, missile superiority, ammunition exhaustion, flanking, and forced melee

## 5. Player-Facing Description

Demesne Archers are selected bow users recruited from qualifying sources within the Crown’s direct military reach. Some may come from castle or settlement defense, estate and household service, local watch, hunting-related experience, or communities able to maintain bows and arrows. None of those sources creates the unit automatically.

In battle, Demesne Archers provide practical early missile support. The archers fire most effectively from protected positions, behind stable infantry, along settlement approaches, or from walls and prepared ground. The archers can unsettle exposed light troops and punish disorder, but the archers lack the protection, penetration, and close-combat confidence of later specialist missile families.

Recruiting the unit transfers real capacity away from the source. A field army gains bow support while a castle, settlement, estate, route, or local watch may lose skilled personnel, ammunition, warning capacity, or defensive firepower.

## 12. Service Scope

Ordinary use may include:

- defense of direct demesne settlements and estates;
- castle or fortified-site missile reinforcement;
- bounded local or regional field support;
- road, crossing, and approach defense;
- camp and baggage perimeter support;
- royal-host contribution where access and supply pass;
- limited skirmishing within a protected combined-arms formation.

Distant campaigns require stronger supply, ammunition transport, equipment repair, shelter, and renewed service authority.

## 20. Battlefield Rhythm

1. Deploy behind a spear line, obstacle, wall, embankment, or defensible approach.
2. Pressure exposed light troops and enemy missile units.
3. Encourage light cavalry to move without attempting to defeat cavalry alone.
4. Concentrate fire on disordered, unshielded, or positional targets.
5. Preserve arrows instead of firing continuously at poor targets.
6. Withdraw through Demesne Spear Service or Sergents des Bourgs when threatened.
7. Reposition to defend roads, gates, stores, crossings, or camp approaches.
8. Continue to contribute in settlement or garrison defense when field maneuver narrows.

## 21. Strengths

- Gives France an Early Era missile card.
- Strong combined-arms value.
- Useful in settlements, garrisons, crossings, and approach defense.
- Supports weak infantry without duplicating infantry roles.
- More flexible and mobile than a future crossbow family.
- Lower institutional threshold than a formal guild or professional corps.
- Persistent into the High Era.
- Clear visual silhouette.
- Real source limitations prevent spam.

## 22. Weaknesses

- Minimal protection.
- Vulnerable in melee.
- Poor independent cavalry resistance.
- Ammunition dependent.
- Weak against heavy armor and strong shields.
- Source narrower than rural levy.
- Experienced bow users recover slowly.
- Short or moderate effective range may invite return fire.
- Poor for unsupported offensive assaults.
- Formation performance varies with bow quality and leadership.

## 29. Garrisons, Settlements, Roads, and Sieges

### Garrison and settlement defense

This is a core use. Walls, gates, towers, elevated positions, streets, and protected approaches improve survivability and firing opportunities.

### Field defense

The unit should operate behind or beside infantry, terrain, wagons, ditches, fences, or other protection where valid.

### Siege offense

The unit may protect camps, suppress exposed defenders where range and position pass, defend labor and stores, and contest sorties. The unit is poor in breach assault and close storming actions.

### Roads and crossings

The unit supports defended approaches and crossings but needs infantry protection if enemy cavalry or melee troops can close rapidly.

## 31. Collision Review

### Versus improvised levy missiles

Improvised throwing weapons or occasional bows among Rural Levy Spearmen remain visual and situational variation. Demesne Archers require a dedicated bow source, ammunition, leaders, formation use, and missile role.

### Versus garrison missile assignments

Garrison missile duty may remain an assignment. Demesne Archers become a recruitable field card only where the source can release personnel and equipment without violating minimum defense.

### Versus later urban or communal archers

A later urban archer family would require a mature institution, more consistent training, ammunition, field persistence, and a distinct battlefield cycle. A town label or charter is insufficient.

### Versus household or paid archers

Household or paid archers would use a narrower owner or contract source and should exceed Demesne Archers in cohesion, campaign endurance, equipment consistency, or specialist performance.
