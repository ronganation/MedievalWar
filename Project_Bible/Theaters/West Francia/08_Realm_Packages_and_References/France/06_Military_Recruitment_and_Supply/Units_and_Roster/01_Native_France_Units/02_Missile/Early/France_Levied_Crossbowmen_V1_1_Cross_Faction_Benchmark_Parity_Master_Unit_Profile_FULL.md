## 4. Player-Facing Description

Levied Crossbowmen are basic French foot missile troops assembled from sources that can provide actual crossbows, compatible bolts, usable strings, people able to operate the weapons, and enough local leadership to form a battlefield unit. They are neither an improvised peasant mob nor a polished corps of professional Arbalétriers.

In battle, Levied Crossbowmen work best from protection. They take position, select suitable targets, loose deliberate shots, and reload behind walls, terrain, shields provided by the wider formation, or a stable French infantry line. They can threaten targets that ordinary bow fire struggles to influence, but they shoot more slowly than Demesne Archers and become vulnerable when cavalry, infantry, or enemy missiles disrupt the reload cycle.

The unit persists after better Arbalétriers emerge. A local source may possess crossbows and capable users without the officers, equipment consistency, drill, pay, or campaign endurance required for the superior family.

## 19. Battlefield Rhythm

1. Take a protected firing position.
2. Select targets worth the slower shot cycle.
3. Deliver a deliberate volley or controlled shot sequence.
4. reload behind protection or friendly frontage.
5. maintain spacing and firing lanes.
6. preserve bolts against unsuitable targets.
7. fall back before cavalry or melee contact.
8. reoccupy a second defensive position if the line moves.

## 20. Strengths

- Gives Early Era France a clear native crossbow card.
- Better deliberate pressure against protected targets than Demesne Archers.
- Useful on walls, at gates, in castles, at crossings, and behind infantry.
- Lower institutional threshold than later Arbalétriers.
- Persists after the better family emerges.
- Uses a clear and familiar Total War role.
- Supports sieges and settlement defense without becoming assault infantry.
- Finite weapons, users, and bolts prevent spam.

## 21. Weaknesses

- Slower shooting rhythm than bow infantry.
- Uneven crossbows and bolt lots.
- Limited formation recovery.
- Poor open-field independence.
- Vulnerable during reload.
- Vulnerable to cavalry and melee.
- Limited armor and shields.
- Low to moderate campaign endurance.
- Dependent on maintenance, equipment condition, and ammunition.
- Weaker command and target concentration than later Arbalétriers.
