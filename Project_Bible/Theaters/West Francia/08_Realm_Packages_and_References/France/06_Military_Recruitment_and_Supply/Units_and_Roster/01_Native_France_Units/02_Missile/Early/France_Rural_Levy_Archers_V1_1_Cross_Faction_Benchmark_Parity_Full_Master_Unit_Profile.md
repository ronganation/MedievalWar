Rural Levy Archers are France's lowest-tier native bow formation. The card represents eligible rural contributors who already possess enough bow familiarity, usable bows, arrows, and elementary leadership to provide emergency missile support. Rural population alone does not create the formation.

The unit is useful because it can be raised more broadly and cheaply than Demesne Archers. The tradeoff is weak discipline, uneven equipment, limited ammunition organization, poor endurance, and severe vulnerability when protection fails.

## 3. One-Screen Player Card

- **Unit:** Rural Levy Archers

- **Class:** Rural emergency bow levy
- **Tier:** Lowest native missile tier
- **Source:** Eligible and unreserved rural, estate, district, or nearby community contributors with real bow capability

- **Battle promise:** Cheap loose bow fire that harasses exposed targets and supports urgent local defense
- **Best use:** Behind Rural Levy Spearmen, village and estate approaches, defended roads and crossings, camp edges, slopes, hedges, walls where assignment permits, and fire against exposed light troops
- **Worst use:** Melee, cavalry contact, armored or shielded targets, prolonged missile duels, unsupported movement, assaulting fortified positions, and distant campaigning
- **Strategic tradeoff:** Pulling actual bow users, bows, arrows, labor, transport, watch capacity, and household support from the local source
- **Progression relationship:** Demesne Archers are better supported and more reliable but do not automatically replace this family

## 4. Player-Facing Description

Rural Levy Archers are locally summoned bow users drawn from qualifying rural, estate, district, and community sources. The formation arrives with mixed practical bows, uneven arrow supplies, limited protection, and whatever elementary leadership the source can provide. The card does not represent every rural resident or an instant conversion of farmers into archers.

In battle, Rural Levy Archers add cheap missile pressure to a local defense. The formation harasses exposed light troops, shoots from behind spear frontage, and helps cover roads, fields, crossings, stores, and settlement approaches. The archers should avoid polished missile duels and should withdraw before cavalry or trained infantry reaches the line.

Heavy losses are not disposable. Casualties remove people with actual bow familiarity from the same communities that must sustain agriculture, local defense, transport, and future recruitment.

## 24. Strengths

- Lowest-cost native French bow support candidate.
- Broader qualifying source potential than Demesne Archers.
- Useful behind Rural Levy Spearmen and other stable frontage.
- Effective enough against exposed and lightly protected targets.
- Useful for urgent local defense, roads, crossings, fields, camps, and settlement approaches.
- Persistent even after better bow and crossbow families develop.

## 25. Weaknesses

- Uneven skill and equipment.
- Weak fire control and target concentration.
- Limited arrows and poor ammunition organization.
- Poor morale under direct pressure.
- Weak withdrawal and formation recovery.
- Extremely vulnerable to cavalry and melee.
- Poor against armor and shields.
- Low campaign endurance and poor expeditionary suitability.
- Heavy losses damage rural labor and future local defense.

## 27. Counterplay

- Use shields and armor.
- Close rapidly with cavalry or trained infantry.
- Win the missile exchange with better archers or crossbowmen.
- Force repeated movement.
- Attack flanks or break the protective frontage.
- Use terrain, buildings, hedges, smoke, or poor visibility to deny useful targets.
- Prolong the battle until arrows, fatigue, or morale fail.
