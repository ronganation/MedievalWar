## 5. One-Screen Player Card

- **Unit:** Castle Mounted Sergeants
- **French name candidate:** Sergents Montés des Châteaux
- **Class:** Strong mid-tier restricted castle-service cavalry
- **Source:** Validated mounted patrol, escort, messenger, route-security, and emergency-response service attached to qualifying royal castles and castellanies
- **Availability:** Narrow, finite, castle-specific, horse-dependent, duty-constrained, equipment-dependent, leadership-dependent, and not granted by a stable or castle building alone
- **Battle promise:** Dependable mounted screening, escort, flank security, counter-reconnaissance, controlled pursuit, and exploitation against exposed or disordered targets
- **Best use:** Protect flanks and baggage, contest enemy light horse, escort vulnerable elements, reinforce threatened routes, support knightly action, and pursue enemies that are already breaking
- **Worst use:** Frontal attacks on formed spears, collision with intact knightly cavalry, prolonged missile exposure, unsupported deep pursuit, severe terrain, and distant campaigning without fodder or replacement horses
- **Strategic tradeoff:** The army gains a capable mounted-service formation while the source castle network loses riders, horses, patrol coverage, escort capacity, messenger speed, and emergency response
- **Counterplay:** Formed spears, heavier cavalry, concentrated missiles, difficult terrain, horse fatigue, remount denial, flank traps, and attacks on weakened routes and source castles

## 6. Player-Facing Description

Castle Mounted Sergeants are nonknightly mounted personnel released from qualifying royal castles and castellanies. Their normal duties may include route patrol, officer and messenger escort, convoy protection, warning, pursuit of raiders, communication between strongpoints, and rapid local response. A castle building does not create them. A valid formation requires actual riders, suitable horses, saddles and tack, weapons, protection, mounted leaders, practice, supply, and enough remaining mounted capacity to preserve critical home duties.

In battle, Castle Mounted Sergeants are flexible medium cavalry. They screen advances, secure flanks, counter enemy scouts, escort vulnerable troops, contest crossings and routes, and exploit disorder created by stronger formations. They can charge exposed infantry and weaker mounted troops under favorable conditions, but they are not discounted knights and should not be thrown into prepared spears or intact heavy cavalry.

Every field release creates a visible strategic cost. The source network may lose warning speed, patrol reach, convoy protection, messenger capacity, raid response, and horse reserves. Surviving riders and horses remain persistent assets whose condition, experience, and recovery must be recorded separately.

## 29. Battlefield Rhythm

1. Deploy behind or beside the reconnaissance screen with a clear route and rally point.
2. Protect the army flank, baggage, messengers, or vulnerable missile troops.
3. Contest enemy scouts and weaker mounted elements without overextending.
4. Maintain distance from prepared spears and intact knightly cavalry.
5. Identify exposed infantry, isolated skirmishers, disrupted cavalry, or an opening created by friendly troops.
6. Commit a controlled charge only when terrain, target state, horses, spacing, and withdrawal options pass.
7. Exploit briefly, then disengage before pursuit fragments the formation.
8. Rally behind friendly infantry, a protected flank, or a prepared route.
9. Preserve riders, horses, leaders, and equipment for repeated service and return to the source network.

## 30. Strengths

- Stronger combat capability, protection, and staying power than broad utility mounted scouts.
- Excellent screening, escort, flank-security, and counter-reconnaissance utility.
- Useful against exposed skirmishers, light horse, disrupted cavalry, and already disordered infantry.
- Capable of controlled charges without claiming knightly shock power.
- Strong route, camp, baggage, messenger, and officer protection.
- Good disengagement and reformation when leaders, terrain, and routes remain favorable.
- Flexible support for infantry, archers, Vavassors, knights, and household forces.
- Persistent rider, leader, guide, and horse-care cadres provide meaningful recovery value.

## 31. Weaknesses

- Narrow castle and castellany source.
- Severe rider, horse, patrol, escort, messenger, and emergency-response opportunity cost.
- Cannot reliably defeat intact knightly cavalry in direct shock combat.
- Highly vulnerable to prepared spears and constrained charge lanes.
- Horse casualties, lameness, fatigue, disease, and fodder shortages rapidly reduce capability.
- Mixed mounts and equipment can disrupt pace and charge consistency.
- Uncontrolled pursuit can scatter the formation and expose isolated riders.
- Poor in dense woodland, marsh, steep slopes, severe broken ground, and narrow obstacles.
- Extended distant campaigning weakens the source institution and increases horse losses.
