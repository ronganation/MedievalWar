- **Counterplay:** Prepared spears, deeper knightly cavalry, disciplined reserves, missiles, bad terrain, horse attrition, household fragmentation, and attacks on weakened local holdings

## 6. Player-Facing Description

Demesne Vavassors are lesser landed and mediate mounted contributors mustered from qualifying households within the Crown's demesne relationships. Their exact legal standing varies. Some may approach the lower edge of knightly society; others remain sub-vassalic or nonknightly landed military tenants. The card represents a functional mounted stratum, not one universal noble title.

They ride better war-capable horses and possess a stronger personal equipment ceiling than Castle Mounted Sergeants. Short mail, substantial shields, lances or mounted spears, swords, helmets, and household retainers may be present where the contributing households can actually provide them. None is created by title or technology alone.

In battle, Demesne Vavassors are credible armored cavalry. They can overthrow exposed light troops, defeat many lesser mounted formations, support knightly charges, strike vulnerable flanks, and remain in a limited mounted melee. Full knights retain superior status, horse and equipment depth, formation confidence, leadership weight, and decisive shock reliability.

## 29. Battlefield Rhythm

1. Assemble household contingents behind the scout and Castle Mounted Sergeant screen.
2. Form a mounted battle line or reserve under recognizable household leaders and standards.
3. Preserve spacing, horse condition, and a viable charge lane while infantry and lighter cavalry reveal the target.
4. Avoid prepared spear fronts and premature collision with intact recognized knights.
5. Strike exposed, disrupted, unsupported, or flank-presenting targets with a coherent charge.
6. Press a limited mounted melee when household cohesion, armor, leadership, and support remain favorable.
7. Reinforce or follow Milites du Domaine rather than competing with the principal knightly charge.
8. Break contact before attrition destroys scarce households, horses, equipment, and heirs.
9. Rally, reform, and preserve surviving contributors for repeated service and eventual household return.

## 30. Strengths

- Stronger horse, armor, weapon, retainer, morale, and charge ceiling than Castle Mounted Sergeants.
- Credible armored charge against exposed, disordered, unsupported, or flank-presenting targets.
- Strong against scouts, light cavalry, exposed skirmishers, levy infantry, and many nonknightly mounted formations.
- Capable of flank pressure, armored reserve work, cavalry-melee reinforcement, and support of Milites du Domaine.
- Better mounted-melee staying power than institutional sergeants without claiming full knightly depth.
- Household leaders, standards, veterans, and retainers improve rally and reformation.
- Politically and strategically meaningful because the formation mobilizes real lesser-landed households.

## 31. Weaknesses

- Narrow lesser-landed, mediate, sub-vassalic, and sub-knightly household source.
- Severe household succession, inheritance, local-defense, tenure, horse, equipment, and retainer opportunity cost.
- Inferior to Milites du Domaine in recognized status, charge reliability, warhorse depth, armor completeness, command weight, and mounted-melee confidence.
- Vulnerable to prepared spears, layered reserves, concentrated missiles, obstacles, and constrained charge lanes.
- Repeated unsupported charges rapidly degrade horses, cohesion, equipment, and household continuity.
- Mixed household equipment and horses reduce standardization.
- Uncontrolled pursuit can scatter contingents and expose household leaders or heirs.
- Poor in dense woodland, marsh, steep slopes, severe broken ground, and narrow obstacles.
- Losses recover slowly because replacement requires real households, adults, horses, equipment, retainers, and time.
