## 5. One-Screen Player Card

- **Unit:** Demesne Knights
- **French/Latin name candidate:** Milites du Domaine
- **Class:** Upper-tier restricted recognized knightly heavy cavalry
- **Source:** Qualifying recognized knightly households and royal-demense military service relationships
- **Availability:** Very small, finite, household-specific, tenure-dependent, politically constrained, horse-dependent, armor-dependent, retainer-dependent, and slow to recover
- **Battle promise:** Premier non-household shock cavalry capable of decisive charges, mounted melee, crisis intervention, command reinforcement, and exploitation
- **Best use:** Break exposed or disrupted infantry, overpower lighter cavalry, deliver a timed main charge, reinforce a decisive mounted melee, counterattack a crisis, and exploit a collapsing flank
- **Worst use:** Prepared spear fronts, repeated unsupported frontal charges, concentrated missiles, marsh or dense broken terrain, attritional pursuit, and wasteful combat that destroys scarce households and warhorses
- **Strategic tradeoff:** The Crown gains decisive armored cavalry while knightly households risk adult military members, heirs, trained warhorses, remounts, armor, retainers, local authority, succession, and future muster strength
- **Counterplay:** Prepared spears, layered reserves, comparable or superior knights, missiles, obstacles, bad terrain, horse attrition, disrupted charge lanes, and attacks on weakened household holdings

## 6. Player-Facing Description

Milites du Domaine are recognized knightly cavalry drawn from qualifying households within the Crown's demesne service relationships. Their formation combines trained riders, war-capable horses, substantial personal equipment, retainers, leaders, and household military memory. Land, title, money, or a stable never creates these assets automatically.

In battle, Demesne Knights are the main non-household shock arm. A coherent charge can break exposed or disordered infantry, overpower lighter horse, reinforce a cavalry melee, or decide a crisis. The result depends on approach, terrain, horse condition, spacing, leadership, target readiness, and the ability to withdraw and reform. Knightly status does not make prepared spears harmless.

Every casualty affects more than battlefield headcount. Losses may remove heirs and household heads, kill trained warhorses, scatter retainers, strip local defense, damage succession, weaken political relationships, and close future recruitment while surviving veterans persist.

## 29. Battlefield Rhythm

1. Assemble recognized knightly households behind scouts, Castle Mounted Sergeants, Vavassors, and supporting infantry.
2. Form a knightly battle line, household grouping, or heavy mounted reserve under leaders and standards.
3. Preserve warhorse condition, alignment, spacing, charge lanes, and a rally route while lighter forces expose the decisive target.
4. Avoid wasting the principal charge on prepared spear fronts, obstacles, or low-value skirmishers.
5. Deliver the main charge against exposed, disrupted, unsupported, flank-presenting, or operationally decisive targets.
6. Press the mounted melee when warhorses, armor, standards, leadership, reserves, and supporting arms preserve advantage.
7. Countercharge enemy cavalry when timing and formation integrity justify the risk.
8. Withdraw and reform before attrition destroys scarce knightly households, warhorses, armor, standards, heirs, and retainers.
9. Rally behind standards or supporting reserves and preserve the formation for another decisive intervention or organized return.

## 30. Strengths

- Premier non-household French heavy shock cavalry.
- Decisive charge capability against exposed, disrupted, unsupported, or flank-presenting targets.
- Strong mounted-melee and countercharge potential against lighter cavalry and many comparable mounted formations.
- Superior warhorse, armor, weapon, retainer, standard, leadership, morale, and command ceiling to Demesne Vavassors.
- Strong heavy-reserve, crisis-intervention, rally, and repeated-charge potential when losses and terrain permit.
- Knightly household leaders, standards, squires, retainers, veterans, and military memory strengthen cohesion and recovery.
- Significant army morale and command presence without claiming Royal Household status.

## 31. Weaknesses

- Very narrow recognized knightly household source.
- Severe household-head, heir, succession, local-defense, political, warhorse, remount, armor, standard, retainer, and command opportunity cost.
- Vulnerable to prepared supported spear fronts, layered reserves, concentrated missiles, obstacles, and broken charge lanes.
- Comparable or superior fresh knights can defeat Milites when alignment, support, horse condition, or leadership fail.
- Repeated unsupported charges and prolonged mounted melee rapidly degrade warhorses, cohesion, armor, standards, and household continuity.
- Mixed household equipment, horses, retinues, and regional practice prevent perfect standardization.
- Uncontrolled pursuit exposes leaders, heirs, standards, horses, and scattered household contingents.
- Poor in dense woodland, marsh, steep slopes, severe broken ground, and narrow obstacles.
- Losses recover extremely slowly because replacement requires recognized households, adults, trained horses, armor, weapons, retainers, political legitimacy, and time.
