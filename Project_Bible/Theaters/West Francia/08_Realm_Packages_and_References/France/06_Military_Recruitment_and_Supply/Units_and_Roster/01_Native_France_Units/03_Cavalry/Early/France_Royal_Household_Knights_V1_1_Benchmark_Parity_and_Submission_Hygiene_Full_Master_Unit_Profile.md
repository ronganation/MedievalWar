- **Strategic tradeoff:** Field commitment can expose the king, regency, command network, household continuity, standards, trusted officers, elite warhorses, and political legitimacy

## 6. Player-Facing Description

Royal Household Knights are trusted knightly personnel attached directly to the royal household and its authorized command-protection structure. Their source is narrower than the broader Milites du Domaine. Knightly status, noble birth, office, wealth, or proximity alone does not create household eligibility; trust, accepted household service, actual availability, horses, equipment, leadership, and protected-duty requirements must all pass.

In battle, the formation normally remains close enough to protect the royal person, regent, commander, standard, or command center. It can hold a heavy reserve, countercharge enemy cavalry, rescue a compromised command element, reinforce a decisive melee, or deliver an exceptional counterattack. The formation should not be spent as a routine first-wave shock unit merely because its members are highly capable.

Losses can damage royal security, command continuity, regency confidence, household trust, political legitimacy, standards, officers, warhorses, retainers, and future crisis-response capacity. Exceptional morale remains conditional on leadership, standards, protected-person status, cohesion, routes, and battlefield circumstances.

## 29. Battlefield Rhythm

1. Establish the protected royal person, regent, commander, standard, or command-center relationship.
2. Deploy close enough to preserve security, communication, rescue, and evacuation capacity.
3. Hold the main household body as a crisis reserve while scouts, sergeants, Vavassors, Milites, infantry, and missiles shape the fight.
4. Reject low-value pursuit or routine shock missions that expose command continuity.
5. Countercharge enemy cavalry, rescue a compromised command element, or stabilize a collapsing sector when the threat justifies commitment.
6. Deliver an exceptional controlled counterattack or shock charge only when protection, terrain, alignment, standards, horses, and return routes pass.
7. Press mounted melee only while the protected mission and reserve function remain secure.
8. Break contact before officer, standard, warhorse, and trusted-household losses become politically or militarily disproportionate.
9. Rally around the protected person, command post, or household standard and restore the crisis reserve.

## 30. Strengths

- Strongest direct command-protection and crisis-response cavalry in the candidate French roster.
- Exceptional trusted leadership, officer, standard, retainer, warhorse, armor, weapon, rally, and coordination ceiling.
- Strong countercharge, rescue, evacuation-cover, mounted-melee reinforcement, and controlled counterattack capability.
- Can stabilize nearby morale and command cohesion through visible household presence where systems support it.
- Capable of decisive shock against suitable targets without replacing the broader Milites main-charge role.
- Direct household familiarity supports rapid reaction to threats against the protected person or command center.
- Persistent trusted cadre has exceptional political and institutional value if preserved.

## 31. Weaknesses

- Extremely narrow direct royal-household and trusted-retinue source.
- Severe royal-security, command-continuity, regency, succession, legitimacy, officer, standard, warhorse, retainer, and political opportunity cost.
- Commitment can leave the protected person, command center, or evacuation route exposed.
- Vulnerable to prepared supported spear fronts, layered reserves, concentrated missiles, obstacles, encirclement, and command separation.
- Comparable elite cavalry can defeat the formation when support, horse condition, standards, terrain, or leadership fail.
- Repeated unsupported charges and prolonged mounted melee destroy reserve and protection value.
- Losses recover exceptionally slowly because replacement requires trusted relationships, recognized knights, trained warhorses, officers, standards, equipment, retainers, and time.
- Poor in terrain that prevents formation, communication, rescue, evacuation, or rapid return to the protected mission.
