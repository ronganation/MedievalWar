The Hommes d’armes represent later French heavy military service as a function rather than a single social rank. Eligible knights, nobles, retainers, and other suitably equipped and accepted men may contribute where the service, captain, muster, horse, harness, equipment, support, and recovery gates pass.

The family is distinct from Milites du Domaine because Hommes d’armes are built around later campaign service, review, captain or retinue relationships, sustained support, and meaningful mounted or dismounted employment. The unit is not created by improving armor alone.

## 5. Player-Facing Description

Hommes d’armes are later French heavy combatants defined by military function and service rather than knighthood alone. They muster under accepted captains, lords, households, contracts, retainers, obligations, or royal service relationships and must present viable people, horses, harness, weapons, support, and readiness.

Mounted, they provide heavy shock, countercharge, and sustained close combat. When a battle plan and terrain justify it, they may deliberately fight on foot, surrendering mobility and immediate horse utility in exchange for a dense heavy-combat presence. Re-forming, recovering horses, and restoring mounted cohesion are not instantaneous.

-> reform rider-horse pairings

## 27. Strengths

- Mature later heavy-service identity.
- Powerful mounted shock and countershock.
- Strong mounted melee when cohesion and support hold.
- Operational mobility before deliberate dismount.
- Meaningful mounted or dismounted battle-plan choice.
- Captain, retinue, and veteran cohesion.
- Potentially longer campaign service than earlier obligation-bound families.

## 28. Weaknesses

- High cost in people, horses, harness, armor, pay, servants, supplies, and recovery.
- Vulnerable to prepared anti-cavalry defenses and concentrated missiles while mounted.
- Dismounted deployment sacrifices mobility and immediate shock.
- Remounting requires surviving horses, custody, time, command, and space.
- Captain, standard, retinue, or servant losses damage capability.
- Mixed replacements dilute cohesion.
- Not yet supported by permanent ordonnance-company infrastructure.
