Coutiliers are late French mounted support combatants who accompany heavier cavalry, keep close screens and approach lanes usable, intercept exposed light threats, reinforce favorable mounted melees, exploit local disorder, pursue broken enemies, and return before the action becomes a contest of heavy horse mass and elite protection.

Their card is stronger and more battle-committed than routine scouts or messengers. It remains lighter, less prestigious, less protected, and less durable than Hommes d’armes or Gendarmes. The unit makes the French cavalry roster layered and enjoyable rather than creating another increasingly expensive shock card.

## 6. Player-Facing Description

Coutiliers are practiced French mounted support combatants drawn from developed retinues, royal companies, and other approved late service relationships. Lighter than Gendarmes and Hommes d’armes, they ride close to the main cavalry body, protect its approach, contest enemy scouts and skirmishers, reinforce a cavalry melee already turning in France’s favor, and punish enemies exposed by heavier shock.

Their strength is controlled commitment. A well-handled formation intercepts light horse, overcomes vulnerable missile troops, presses an open flank, rescues an isolated ally, or turns local collapse into disciplined pursuit. Against fresh heavy cavalry, deep infantry, or prepared reach weapons, that advantage disappears quickly. Coutiliers must preserve an exit, rally, and recover before recommitting.

## 22. Strengths, Weaknesses, and Counterplay

### Strengths

- Excellent companion for French heavy cavalry.
- Strong close screening, interception, flank pressure, and second-wave utility.
- More direct combat power than broad scouts and messengers.
- Better speed, response, and disengagement than heavy cavalry.
- Credible brief melee against exposed or disordered targets.
- Useful rescue, withdrawal-cover, pursuit, and pursuit-denial capability.
- Professional identity without consuming knightly or elite-heavy ownership.

### Weaknesses

- Cannot replace heavy shock cavalry.
- Vulnerable to prepared reach, obstacles, and narrow approaches.
- Loses direct contests with fresh heavy cavalry.
- Concentrated missiles disrupt riders and horses.
- Prolonged melee erodes the unit quickly.
- Poor lane discipline can sabotage the main cavalry charge.
- Horse, captain, signal, and veteran losses sharply reduce capability.
