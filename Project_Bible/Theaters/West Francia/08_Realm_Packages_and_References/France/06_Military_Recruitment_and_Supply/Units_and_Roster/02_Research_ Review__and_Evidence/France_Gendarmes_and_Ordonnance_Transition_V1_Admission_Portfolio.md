Gendarmes should be investigated as the elite heavy-cavalry component of a later royal institutional system rather than as a direct stat upgrade applied to every Hommes d’armes formation. The *compagnie d’ordonnance* is broader than the gendarme card: it governs company command, pay, garrison assignment, review, supporting personnel, discipline, horses, equipment, and royal service.

## 2. Historical Reform Baseline

A specialist <External>Valois Military Administration on the Norman Frontier, 1445–1461</External> summary states that in 1445 <Person>Charles VII</Person> dismissed many nominal defenders and organized the remainder into a limited number of *compagnies d’ordonnance*. It explicitly says that these companies were to receive regular pay, be assigned to specific garrisons, and be commanded by captains commissioned by the king. The summary also states that command and control developed gradually and administration remained partly ad hoc. citeturn50search13

A <External>Cambridge research-article extract</External> states that <Person>Charles VII</Person> created the *compagnies d’ordonnance* in the spring of 1445 and characterizes the reform as evolutionary, particularly through enforcement of earlier royal military measures directed against independent companies. citeturn50search14

A French <External>compagnie d’ordonnance reference</External> dates the creation to May 26, 1445 and states that the original ordinance has not survived. It says organization is known through ordinances from January 1446 through 1448, accounts, and contemporary witnesses. citeturn50search8
