## 1. Purpose and Decision Standard

This addendum tests whether currently retrievable Bouvines and military-muster evidence closes the source gaps identified by the two controlling research files. The addendum does not repeat all prior evidence. It evaluates the strongest new or newly focused material against five decisive questions:

1. Does the evidence identify mounted nonknightly military sergeants in a French field army?
2. Does the evidence show a collective or repeated contingent rather than an individual office?
3. Does the evidence establish army-level command or campaign service distinct from castle service?
4. Does the evidence support the proposed *de l’Ost* identity, service system, and recovery source?
5. Does the evidence justify a player-facing unit rather than an assignment, source layer, or existing-card state?

## 9. General Military-Sergeant Comparator Reassessment

The secondary <External>Sergeants, 11th–13th Centuries</External> extract states that sergeants covered many social and functional categories, that chroniclers could use the term broadly, and that nonnoble retainers usually fought mounted by the twelfth century. It mentions the 1133 Inquest of Bayeux as an example of mounted service with lance, shield, and sword. The same source cautions that mounted sergeants could be visually difficult to distinguish from knights in later material. citeturn34search193
