## 23. Battlefield Rhythm

1. Deploy behind or beside a stable holding line.
2. Remain screened from avoidable missiles and fresh shock.
3. Enter against fixed, compressed, or disordered targets.
4. Maintain more coherent pressure than Communal Guisarmiers.
5. Rotate, relieve, or reform before fatigue destroys spacing.
6. Avoid unsupported pursuit and open-ground exposure.
7. Preserve leaders, veterans, and serviceable weapons.

## 24. Strengths

- coherent later French polearm identity;
- stronger sustained pressure than the broader Guisarmier family;
- effective in constricted objectives and supported line combat;
- useful against disordered ordinary infantry;
- capable of exploiting stalled mounted troops;
- meaningful development reward for preserved institutions and workshops.

## 25. Weaknesses

- limited missile protection;
- poor preferred use against a fresh heavy charge;
- dependence on holding troops and flank security;
- vulnerability to disciplined longer reach;
- workshop and repair burden;
- finite experienced personnel;
- heavy losses damage future development.
