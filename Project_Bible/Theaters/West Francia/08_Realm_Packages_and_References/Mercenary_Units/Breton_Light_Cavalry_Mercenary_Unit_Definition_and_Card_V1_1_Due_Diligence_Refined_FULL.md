Breton Light Cavalry are a proposed source-bound mounted mercenary family whose battlefield promise is **Harass and Wheel**. They approach in loose mounted order, release finite javelins against exposed or slow targets, turn away before prepared spears, concentrated missiles, or heavier cavalry can fix them, and recommit from another angle. Once the target is disordered, isolated, or already breaking, they may use spears for a limited close commitment or controlled pursuit.

This is a Total War-facing combat card. It should be readable immediately as mobile javelin-and-spear cavalry, not as a campaign messenger abstraction. The modern Medieval II treatment adds finite riders, horses, javelins, command, contract, supply, fatigue, replacement, withdrawal, and source consequences without erasing that straightforward battlefield fantasy.

## 4. Player-Facing Card

### Short Description

Breton mounted skirmishers who harass exposed enemies with javelins, wheel away from retaliation, and use spears only when the opening is safe.

### Encyclopedia Description

Breton Light Cavalry are contracted mounted skirmishers drawn from an exact Breton source whose riders, horses, leaders, weapons, and service terms can support outside employment. Their value lies in speed of position, finite javelin pressure, flank threat, pursuit, and the ability to choose whether to close with spears after the missile phase.

They are not knights and should not fight as knights. Their light horses and variable protection allow useful movement but cannot endure a heavy mounted collision or an extended melee. Prepared spears, concentrated archery or crossbows, difficult ground, fatigue, and blocked routes can turn an apparently safe harassment run into a costly defeat. A skilled commander uses them to stretch an enemy screen, threaten vulnerable troops, punish movement, and preserve enough horse strength to exploit a later rout.

### Strengths

- high battlefield mobility on suitable ground
- finite mounted javelin harassment
- strong flank and rear pressure against exposed targets
- useful counter-skirmishing
- controlled pursuit of broken troops
- conditional spear commitment after disruption
- clear regional mercenary identity
- fills a real mounted role gap for infantry-heavy employers

### Weaknesses

- vulnerable to heavy cavalry
- vulnerable to prepared spears
- vulnerable to concentrated missiles
- limited javelin supply
- light and variable protection
- poor prolonged melee
- horse fatigue and remount losses matter
- confined, wooded, marshy, urban, or steep terrain reduces mobility
- external replacement remains slow and source-bound

### Player Guidance

Do not send Breton Light Cavalry into the same charge lane as knights. Spread them on a flank, choose exposed targets, release a short javelin sequence, and wheel away before the enemy response arrives. Commit with spears only against disordered, isolated, engaged, or routing targets. Rally before the horses and riders lose the ability to disengage.
