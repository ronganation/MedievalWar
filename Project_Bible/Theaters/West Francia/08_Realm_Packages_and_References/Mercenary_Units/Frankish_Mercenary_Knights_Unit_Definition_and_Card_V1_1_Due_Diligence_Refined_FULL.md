Frankish Mercenary Knights are the classic hired heavy-cavalry card: expensive armored riders whose lance charge can decide one important sector, but whose horses, armor, retainers, cohesion, and contract cannot be replaced by money alone. Their battlefield promise is **Charge, Rally, Recover**. They select one valuable target, confirm the approach and support, deliver a controlled lance charge, avoid dissolving into a prolonged melee, rally behind friendly protection, restore formation and horse condition, and recommit only when the next target justifies the risk.

This is unmistakably a mercenary unit. It is not merely a regional contingent with a payment wrapper, and it is not a domestic knight card copied into a market. Its campaign identity comes from bounded contract service, external source ownership, expensive sustainment, slow replacement, company continuity, employer risk, and possible departure, renewal, settlement, integration, or disbandment.

## 4. Player-Facing Card

### Short Description

Scarce hired knights whose armored lance charge can shatter an exposed formation, but whose horses, armor, retainers, and losses are exceptionally costly to restore.

### Encyclopedia Description

Frankish Mercenary Knights are contracted heavy cavalry drawn from exact western knightly sources whose riders, horses, armor, weapons, attendants, leaders, and service terms permit outside employment. They are not a universal pool of wandering nobles. Each company or following retains a real origin, captain, equipment base, political history, replacement route, and contract.

On the battlefield, they excel when committed deliberately. Their heavy mail, shields, lances, riding skill, and cohesion allow a powerful charge against fixed, exposed, or disordered troops. They can serve as a crisis reserve or countercharge force, but they remain vulnerable to prepared spears, pikes, crossbows, difficult ground, exhaustion, encirclement, and prolonged melee. A commander who spends them on low-value targets may win a moment and lose an irreplaceable company.

### Strengths

- powerful controlled lance charge
- high mounted protection for the intended context
- strong cohesion under intact captaincy and formation
- effective crisis reserve and countercharge
- dangerous against fixed, exposed, or disordered infantry
- useful for breakthrough exploitation and controlled pursuit
- strong classic mercenary cavalry identity

### Weaknesses

- very high campaign and contract burden
- scarce riders, horses, armor, retainers, and leaders
- vulnerable to prepared spears and pikes
- vulnerable to crossbows and concentrated missiles
- poor in woods, marsh, mud, steep ground, tight streets, and narrow crossings
- repeated charges exhaust horses and formation
- prolonged melee erodes the charge advantage
- external replacement is slow and politically dependent
- contract breach, recall, loss, or source closure may remove future access

### Player Guidance

Treat Frankish Mercenary Knights as a commitment unit. Pin the target with infantry or missile pressure, protect the approach, charge one valuable sector, then rally before the formation becomes trapped in melee. Do not use them as scouts, routine flank guards, or disposable pursuit cavalry.
