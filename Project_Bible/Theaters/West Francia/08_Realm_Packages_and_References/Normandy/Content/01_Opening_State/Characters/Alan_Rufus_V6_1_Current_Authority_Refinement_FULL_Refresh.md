## Core Character Thesis

Alan begins as a young but established Breton noble with meaningful Penthièvre standing, pre-conquest Norman connections, military promise, cross-cultural usefulness, and a career shaped by chosen service rather than membership in William's dynasty. His central tension is **chosen loyalty versus durable recognition**. He can become one of William's most dependable non-dynastic commanders when command, risk, public credit, reward, household protection, and respect for Breton identity remain connected. He becomes guarded, negotiative, or politically mobile when service is repeatedly separated from recognition and security.

His later greatness must be built in sequence. Military service may justify reward; reward may create possession; possession may permit household installation; and only sustained security, administration, local settlement, patronage, and succession can produce a durable cross-Channel lordship.

## Historical Identity

Alan Rufus, also known as Alan the Red or Alain le Roux, was a son of Eudon of Penthièvre and belonged to a powerful Breton dynastic network. His birth is generally placed around 1040, making him a young adult at the campaign opening. He appears with family members in a charter commonly dated within the 1056 to 1060 range and had connections to Rouen and Richemont in Upper Normandy before 1066.

Alan was neither a generic Norman knight nor an autonomous Breton sovereign. His family standing supplied rank, affinity, and opportunity, while his position as a younger son made external service attractive. His later English wealth and the lordship associated with Richmond establish a plausible transformation path, not an opening title, estate, household, or completed reputation.

A successful reward does not require constant territorial grants.  
Valid compensation can include:
- independent command
- court precedence
- marriage support
- revenue rights
- castle authority
- formal recognition of Breton service  
Repeated high-risk service with weak reward should apply UnderRewardedCompanion.  
That state should reduce enthusiasm before it reduces formal loyalty.

### Breton Affinity Component

Recommended Component:  
BP_BretonAffinityComponent  
Functions:
- tracks recruitable Breton noble and retinue support
- modifies cohesion in mixed Breton-Norman forces
- records whether Alan represents family interests or only personal advancement
- creates consequences when Breton contingents are sacrificed or ignored
- supports kin-based succession to granted honors  
BretonAffinity should not generate free troops.  
Recruitment requires:
- available kin or clients
- political permission or invitation
- transport and supply
- credible reward expectations
- no overriding crisis in Brittany

### Mixed-Contingent Command System

Alan receives a command bonus when leading forces that include both Breton and Norman elements if:
- command hierarchy is explicit
- interpreters or culturally compatible officers are present
- reward obligations are recorded
- no active ethnic-precedence dispute exists  
Potential bonuses:
- reduced command friction
- faster rally after localized disorder
- improved reconnaissance coordination
- lower desertion among allied contingents  
Potential penalties:
- rivalry over battlefield honor
- contradictory orders from Norman commanders
- unequal distribution of loot or land
- Breton withdrawal after broken promises

### Frontier Lordship System

Alan's strongest long-term progression is the conversion of expeditionary service into territorial lordship.  
A frontier grant should create a staged project.

#### Lordship Formation Stages

1. Military Possession  
2. Castle or Strongpoint Establishment  
3. Household and Garrison Installation  
4. Local Elite Settlement  
5. Revenue and Obligation Survey  
6. Religious Patronage  
7. Dynastic Succession Settlement  
8. Mature Regional Integration

Each stage requires resources and creates risks.  
Military possession without integration produces high revolt exposure.  
Patronage without garrison security produces weak enforcement.  
Castle construction without revenue planning produces an expensive isolated strongpoint.

### Richmond Formation Path

The Honour of Richmond must remain locked at campaign start.  
It can emerge only after a successful English expansion and an appropriate northern grant.  
Recommended unlock conditions:
- NormanVictoryInEngland is true
- AlanParticipatedInConquest is true or ExceptionalServiceOverride is active
- WilliamTrust is High
- suitable confiscated territory is available
- no conflicting grant has already assigned the regional core
- Alan accepts long-term territorial responsibility  
The title should begin as a developing honor rather than a fully integrated late-eleventh-century power bloc.

### Castle Construction Role

Alan may receive a specialization in frontier castle establishment after acquiring a territorial grant.  
Bonuses should apply to:
- selecting defensible regional nodes
- coordinating garrison settlement
- connecting castle and market development
- maintaining communications with ducal authority  
Balance Rule:  
No castle may be created without treasury, labor, materials, route access, and a valid construction period.  
Alan improves coordination.  
He does not bypass the construction economy.

### Governance Doctrine Unlocks

Potential doctrines:
- Breton Retainer Settlement
- Mixed Elite Council
- Frontier Castle Borough
- Rewarded Service Tenure
- Regional Oath Integration
- Monastic Legitimacy Partnership  
Each doctrine should carry tradeoffs.  
Breton Retainer Settlement increases military reliability but may increase local exclusion.  
Mixed Elite Council improves legitimacy but slows coercive decisions.  
Frontier Castle Borough strengthens revenue and defense but requires sustained investment.

### AI Strategic Restraints

Alan AI should not:
- abandon William after one minor disappointment
- accept unlimited campaigns without compensation
- command Breton contingents as if culture has no political effect
- obtain Richmond automatically by calendar date
- generate mature frontier governance immediately after conquest
- become a principal Norman succession claimant
- ignore Penthièvre family interests
- build castles without resources
- accept overlapping command and governorship loads beyond capacity

Alan becomes more assertive when:
- his service record is high
- reward satisfaction falls
- William depends on Breton support

### Final Design Summary

Alan Rufus should begin as a high-potential Breton companion, not as the completed Lord of Richmond.  
His strongest campaign sequence is:

Breton Affinity  
then  
Trusted Expeditionary Service  
then  
Meaningful Reward  
then  
Frontier Consolidation  
then  
Durable Cross-Channel Lordship

If service and reward remain aligned, Alan becomes one of William's most dependable non-dynastic magnates.  
If they separate, he does not instantly betray the duke.  
He becomes less willing, more negotiative, and increasingly conscious that Breton status can be used outside the Norman project.  
That tension gives him a distinct role beside FitzOsbern's operational authority, Robert of Mortain's family stability, and Eustace's sovereign opportunism.
