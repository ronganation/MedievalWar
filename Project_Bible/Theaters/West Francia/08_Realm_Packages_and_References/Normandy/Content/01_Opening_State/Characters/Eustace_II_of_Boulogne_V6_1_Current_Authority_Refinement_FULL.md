## Core Character Thesis

Eustace begins as an autonomous Count of Boulogne whose value to Normandy comes precisely from the fact that Normandy does not own his service, ports, forces, diplomacy, or future. His central tension is **coalition leverage versus home exposure**. He can widen a cross-Channel coalition, grant strategically valuable access, provide informed English and continental judgment, and constrain rivals. Every commitment must still preserve Boulogne's security, succession, rank, resources, and lawful exit options.

## Historical Identity

Eustace II was the son of Eustace I of Boulogne and Matilda of Louvain and had ruled Boulogne for more than a decade by the opening. He is an experienced adult count, commonly dated to a birth between approximately 1015 and 1020. His first marriage to Goda connected him to Edward the Confessor's family, while his marriage to Ida of Lorraine linked Boulogne to Lotharingian networks. He had participated in imperial conflict, experienced ecclesiastical discipline, and become directly entangled in English politics through the 1051 Dover crisis.

These experiences make him informed, connected, and wary of domination. His later participation in William's expedition, English rewards, rebellion, reconciliation, death, and his sons' later prominence are possible branches rather than opening commitments.

Eustace succeeded his father as Count of Boulogne around 1049.

His first marriage to Goda linked him to the English royal family.

### AI Strategic Restraints

Eustace AI should not:
- act as a Norman vassal
- join the English conquest automatically by year
- oppose Flanders in every circumstance
- rebel because of a historical date
- accept command humiliation for a minor reward

- commit the entire county to distant war

### AI Escalation Triggers

Eustace increases commitment when:
- Flemish pressure rises
- William offers enforceable reward terms
- Harold or another English regime appears hostile
- Boulogne's corridor becomes strategically indispensable
- Ida's network supports the alignment
- coalition success probability is high

Eustace reduces commitment when:
- Boulogne is threatened
- allies violate precedence
- promised rewards are withheld
- the coalition loses its objective
- dynastic succession becomes unstable
- alternative agreements offer stronger autonomy

### Final Design Summary

Eustace II should operate as a neighboring ruler whose cooperation is valuable precisely because William does not control it.  
His strongest campaign sequence is:

Boulogne Leverage  
then  
Negotiated Coalition  
then  
Selective Military Commitment  
then  
Reward or Grievance  
then  
Durable Alliance, Reconciliation, or Rebellion

His AI should be difficult but intelligible.  
He supports Normandy when the partnership protects Boulogne, constrains rivals, rewards service, and strengthens his dynasty.  
He withholds support when the agreement threatens autonomy or leaves his county exposed.  
That makes Eustace neither a guaranteed ally nor a random opportunist.  
He becomes a true external power center whose decisions can reshape the cross-Channel balance.
