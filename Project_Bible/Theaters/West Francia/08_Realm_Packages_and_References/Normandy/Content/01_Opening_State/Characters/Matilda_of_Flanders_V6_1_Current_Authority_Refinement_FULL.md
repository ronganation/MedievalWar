## Core Character Thesis

Matilda begins as Duchess of Normandy, a Flemish-born dynastic partner, mother of the opening heirs, principal ducal-household anchor, and a credible conditional regent. Her central tension is **protective continuity versus partiality and jurisdictional concentration**. She can keep household, correspondence, succession, patronage, and court government functioning during William's absence, but marriage, motherhood, and influence do not create unlimited regency, co-rulership, resources, or authority over every child and officer.

## Historical Identity

Matilda was the daughter of Baldwin V of Flanders and Adela of France and married William of Normandy around the early 1050s. Her birth connected the Norman ducal house to Flemish and Capetian prestige, but her campaign importance extends beyond lineage. At opening she is an experienced adult duchess, commonly dated to a birth around 1031, with a functioning marriage, household responsibilities, religious patronage, diplomatic access, and living children.

She begins as Duchess of Normandy, not Queen of England. Later regency, English coronation, dower, dual household, parent-heir conflict, future births, and patronage legacy remain conditional. The project does not assign her authorship or manufacture of the Bayeux Tapestry by default.

Matilda's opening function is to make William's active leadership sustainable through household continuity, controlled access, correspondence, heir security, patronage, diplomatic signaling, and preparation for absence or incapacity. ## Final Design Summary

Matilda begins as Normandy's dynastic and household anchor, not as a future queen preloaded into the start state.

Her strongest campaign expression is:

Ducal Marriage

then

Household Cohesion

then

Delegated Regency

then

Cross-Channel Queenship

then

Dynastic Settlement

Her power is greatest when William is absent, heirs need protection, or competing offices require coordination.

Her central risk is that protection becomes partiality and delegated authority becomes jurisdictional conflict.
