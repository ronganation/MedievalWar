## Core Character Thesis

Richard begins as an active senior count whose cooperation gives Norman ducal policy regional depth, aristocratic consent, and cadet-branch legitimacy. His central tension is **dignified cooperation versus constitutional estrangement**. He can support strong ducal government without becoming a household officer, and he can resist method, burden, or interference without becoming a rebel. His closing responsibility is to transfer not only the County of Evreux, but also officer confidence, archives, obligations, regional knowledge, and a workable relationship with the ducal center.

## Historical Identity

Richard belongs to the Evreux line descended from Robert, Archbishop of Rouen and Count of Evreux. At opening he is Count of Evreux, an older and established Norman magnate, husband or consort in the Godechildis or Adelaide naming tradition, and father of William of Evreux. His approximate birth around 1015 is uncertain. House Evreux is a cadet branch connected to the Norman ducal tradition but retains a distinct identity, household, regional interest, and succession.

Richard's age has strategic meaning because it shortens the safe preparation window for William of Evreux. Age does not make him inactive, incapable, or destined to die around 1067. His current health, stamina, travel tolerance, judgment, and performance remain mutable and separately owned.
