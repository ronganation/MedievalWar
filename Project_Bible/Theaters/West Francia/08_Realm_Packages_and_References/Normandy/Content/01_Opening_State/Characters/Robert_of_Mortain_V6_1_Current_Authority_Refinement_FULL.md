## Core Character Thesis

Robert begins as Count of Mortain, maternal half-brother of William of Normandy, full brother of Odo of Bayeux, and Normandy's most dependable territorial stabilizer. His central tension is **reliable sustainment versus cumulative overload**. He makes ambitious policy survivable by holding frontiers, preserving reserves, integrating local officers, mediating family strain, and maintaining continuity. That same reliability makes him the person most likely to receive too many governorships, family burdens, and neglected regions.

## Historical Identity

Robert belongs to the House of Conteville through Herleva and is the full brother of Odo of Bayeux and maternal half-brother of William of Normandy. At opening he holds the County of Mortain, a significant southwestern territorial and frontier responsibility. His exact birth year and some details of his early career are uncertain, but he is best represented as an experienced adult count with practical administrative, military-support, and family-political value.

His later English wealth and cross-Channel standing demonstrate a plausible future transformation, not an opening possession. He remains distinct from the direct House Normandy descent line and from Odo's episcopal and institutional power base.

## Classification and Strategic Relevance

Robert is a principal active character with regional, governance, dynasty, frontier, succession, and support-command relevance. He needs substantial depth because the realm's ability to hold gains, preserve reserves, sustain households, and survive family conflict depends on practical actors like him. His decisions can reduce or magnify risk across several systems without making him the grand strategist.

## Opening Strategic Function

Robert's current function is sustainment. He governs Mortain, maintains frontier awareness, preserves a reserve, supports logistics, integrates local officers, reviews practical risk, and can stabilize other territories under valid delegation. He also acts as the principal internal counterweight between Odo's ambition and William's need for family cohesion.

His contribution must be attached to actual responsibility. Mere presence should not create a universal realm bonus. Every additional assignment competes with Mortain, household, health, family, and communication capacity.
