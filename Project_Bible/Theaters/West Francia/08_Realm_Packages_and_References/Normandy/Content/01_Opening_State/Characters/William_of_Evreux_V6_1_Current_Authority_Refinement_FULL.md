## Core Character Thesis

William of Evreux begins as a young adult with real agency, regional identity, and eligibility for meaningful supervised assignments, but without possession of the county or his father's authority. His central tension is **preparation versus premature autonomy**. If Richard of Evreux, the ducal court, local officers, and William himself align status, responsibility, regional familiarity, and review, succession can become continuity. If recognition, authority, and competence diverge, the heir can become frustrated, politically recruited, operationally exposed, or unprepared at the moment of transfer.

## Historical Identity

William is the son and eventual historical successor of Richard of Evreux and belongs to the House of Evreux. At opening Richard remains count. William is best represented as a young adult, approximately eighteen to twenty-three, resident in or closely connected to Evreux and capable of real participation in household, regional, court, and military affairs.

His mother is identified in project material as Godechildis, also rendered Adelaide. His sisters include Godehildis and Agnes. A marriage to Helvise of Nevers is historically relevant but chronologically uncertain for the opening and must remain conditional until validated.

Later comital rule, English expedition service, private war, childlessness, collateral Montfort pressure, long patriarchal standing, marriage outcome, and death in 1118 remain conditional.

## Classification and Strategic Relevance

William is an active young-adult dynast, comital heir, developing regional lord, cadet-branch successor, and second-generation network actor. He merits a substantial but regionally focused profile because decisions around his apprenticeship affect Evreux continuity, local officer trust, levy and treasury knowledge, command qualification, ducal-cadet relations, succession recognition, and the quality of government after Richard.

Unlike Robert Curthose, he can undertake real assignments now. Unlike Richard, he lacks final comital authority. This difference is the foundation of his design.

## Opening Strategic Function

William's current function is to make succession preparation concrete through limited work. He can learn Evreux by carrying out defined tasks, representing Richard under instructions, building relationships with officers, observing the ducal court, and gaining command experience without displacing the count.

His contribution includes:

- controlled regional continuity;
- testing and demonstrating readiness before accession;
- connecting local officers to the next generation;
- carrying Richard's views when delegated;
- developing court and campaign relationships with Norman peers;
- reducing the shock of sudden incapacity or death;
- exposing where recognition, responsibility, and competence are misaligned.

3. William speaks, remains silent, or seeks private guidance.

### The Succession Deadline

1. Richard's health creates uncertainty.
2. Officers seek clarity.
3. William asks for recognition or further preparation.

5. Household unity is tested.

1. Expeditionary opportunity appears.
2. William compares prestige with succession security.
3. Richard assigns or withholds authority.
