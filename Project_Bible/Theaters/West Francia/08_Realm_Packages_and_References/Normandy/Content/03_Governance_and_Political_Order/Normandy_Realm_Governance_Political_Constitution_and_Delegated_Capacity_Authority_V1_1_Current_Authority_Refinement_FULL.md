## 1. Governance Verdict

Normandy opens as a comparatively strong and coordinated duchy under <Person>William of Normandy</Person>, but not as a centralized state with uniform bureaucracy, a standing civil service, an omnipresent ruler, or one undifferentiated pool of authority.

Norman government works through a negotiated hierarchy of:

- recognized ducal authority;
- an itinerant ducal household;
- current titles and offices;
- counts, lords, castellans, and regional political communities;
- service households and trusted executors;
- bishops, chapters, monasteries, clerks, and ecclesiastical institutions;
- noble obligation and extraordinary consultation;
- scoped mandates with resources and review;
- reciprocal protection, service, recognition, and reward;
- external agreements with autonomous rulers and affinities.

Normandy's distinctive strength is not unlimited ducal power. Normandy's strength is the ability to coordinate differentiated authority through a ruler and elite network with unusually high military-administrative experience. Normandy's central governance risk is that the same capacity remains overly dependent on <Person>William of Normandy</Person> and a small set of heavily loaded people.

## 3. Purpose

This file defines how Normandy governs, how political authority is constituted and exercised, how the ducal household and regional lordships participate, how consultation and reciprocal obligation work, how offices and mandates remain separate from people and titles, how delegated capacity is created and reviewed, how government survives absence or incapacity, and how a possible cross-Channel transformation must be constituted.

This authority is intended to prevent five recurring failures:

1. reducing Normandy to <Person>William of Normandy</Person>'s personal attributes;
2. treating every trusted person as a free extension of ducal capacity;
3. converting kinship, office, or military service into unlimited jurisdiction;
4. treating victory or title acquisition as completed government;
5. allowing AI or static content to create authoritative state directly.

## 7. Norman Political Constitution

### 7.1 The Duchy as a political order

Normandy is a duchy held together by recognized rulership, territorial lordship, household government, offices, castles, obligations, institutions, negotiated cooperation, and coercive credibility. The duchy persists beyond any one holder, but its opening operating capacity is unusually dependent on the current duke.

### 7.2 Ducal authority

The lawful duke may participate in:

- Realm strategy selection;
- major war authorization;
- appointment and confirmation where valid;
- final arbitration within lawful and practical limits;
- extraordinary consultation;
- major delegation;
- dynasty direction;
- defense of the ducal peace;
- recognition and reward proposals;
- emergency response.

Ducal authority remains bounded by:

- title and office jurisdiction;
- inherited or negotiated rights;
- practical reach;
- information and evidence;
- noble obligations and capacity;
- ecclesiastical jurisdiction;
- existing agreements;
- household and officer competence;
- treasury, routes, forces, and time;
- the political cost of exclusion, humiliation, broken commitment, or excessive burden.

### 7.3 Government is not constant personal presence

<Person>William of Normandy</Person> cannot personally hear every petition, inspect every castle, command every force, manage every household, supervise every child, negotiate every agreement, and execute every policy. Government therefore depends on distributed work.

Distribution must create capacity without erasing jurisdiction, accountability, or replacement planning.

## 8. Ducal Household Government

### 8.1 Household functions

The ducal household may support:

- access and audience;
- correspondence and records;
- treasury and expenditure interfaces;
- hospitality and ceremony;
- security and travel;
- children, wards, tutors, and custody interfaces;
- patronage and petitions;
- household justice;
- diplomatic reception;
- continuity during absence;
- preparation for separately authorized regency.

### 8.2 <Person>Matilda of Flanders</Person>

<Person>Matilda of Flanders</Person> is the principal household-government actor at opening and a major candidate for bounded continuity work. Marriage and household centrality do not create co-ducal title, universal authority, permanent regency, ownership of children, or automatic Flemish resources.

Any public or executive authority beyond household scope requires a valid title, office, mandate, regency bundle, or other recognized source.

### 8.3 Household officer dependency

Household capacity depends on actual officers, clerks, stewards, custodians, guards, messengers, witnesses, stores, routes, records, and money. A household is not a passive bonus attached to the ruling couple.

### 8.4 Presence and absence

The household may travel, divide, or maintain regional functions. A ruler's absence does not automatically activate regency. Absence requires an explicit review of:

- normal household continuity;
- delegated routine authority;
- urgent decision rights;
- custody and heir security;
- correspondence and seal use;
- treasury and appointment limits;
- military and diplomatic escalation;
- reporting, review, and termination.

## 9. Regional Lordship and Political Participation

### 9.1 Regional authority

Counts and major lords are not merely levy providers. Regional lordship may include:

- local justice and dispute handling;
- castles and officers;
- revenues and obligations;
- household and retinue;
- local political relationships;
- religious patronage;
- road, market, or frontier interests;
- military service;
- participation in wider ducal government.

Exact rights remain title-, law-, institution-, and scenario-owned.

### 9.2 Mortain

<Person>Robert of Mortain</Person> illustrates a supportive regional lord whose usefulness comes from active government, reserve and logistical capacity, family mediation, and continuity. Mortain does not become ducal inventory. Additional duties require mandates and increase responsibility load.

### 9.3 Evreux

<Person>Richard of Evreux</Person> remains the lawful Count of Evreux and senior House Evreux anchor at opening. House Evreux participates as a cadet branch and regional power, not as a department of the ducal household.

<Person>William of Evreux</Person> may receive scoped preparation and assignments without becoming count, co-ruler, or universal regional commander before lawful succession or separate authority.

### 9.4 Breteuil and service lordship

<Person>William FitzOsbern</Person>'s Breteuil lordship, household, seneschal office, personal trust, mandates, and commands remain separate. Strong ducal service does not turn the FitzOsbern household into House Normandy property.

## 10. Noble Consultation and Reciprocal Obligation

### 10.1 Consultation triggers

Consultation is appropriate when proposed action may impose:

- extraordinary or extended military service;
- unusual taxation, borrowing, requisition, or supply burden;
- castle surrender or major custody changes;
- interference in regional succession or title security;
- prolonged ducal absence;
- a major external expedition;
- a new coalition with command or withdrawal implications;
- large grants, office concentration, or politically transformative settlement;
- emergency authority affecting multiple jurisdictions.

### 10.2 Consultation is not a single vote

Consultation may involve affected people and institutions in different forms. The required participants depend on legal basis, burden, territory, office, institution, contract, and practical capacity.

Possible responses include:

- enthusiastic support;
- conditional support;
- formal compliance;
- limited or substitute contribution;
- delayed compliance;
- negotiated exemption;
- refusal with an alternative;
- principled objection;
- nonperformance;
- opposition;
- rebellion only under stronger conditions.

### 10.3 Reciprocal expectations

The duke may expect lawful service, peacekeeping, attendance, counsel, recognized obligations, and compliance with valid judgments.

Magnates and institutions may expect protection, lawful process, title and property security, consultation appropriate to burden, credible arbitration, proportional recognition, and performance of promises.

Repeated breach by either side may change future willingness, contract terms, consultation posture, and political alignment through the proper owners.

## 15. Government Continuity

### 15.1 Normal absence

Normal absence may be managed through ordinary offices, correspondence, household authority, regional government, and bounded mandates. Normal absence does not itself create a regency.

### 15.2 Prolonged absence or campaign

Before prolonged absence, governance review should cover:

- ducal seal and document authority;
- household leadership;
- heir custody and protection;
- justice and petitions;
- treasury and emergency expenditure;
- office appointments and vacancies;
- musters and home defense;
- diplomacy and treaty limits;
- regional escalation;
- church relations;
- reporting routes;
- recall and termination.

### 15.3 Incapacity

Incapacity must be established through the appropriate health, evidence, and political owners. It may be temporary, partial, intermittent, disputed, or permanent. The response must match the proven incapacity rather than applying one universal replacement state.

### 15.4 Death or vacancy

Death or vacancy triggers owner-routed title, office, household, dynasty, succession, relationship, mandate, promise, and institution reviews. No one inherits personal trust, Knowledge, competence, promises, commands, or officer loyalty automatically.

## 17. Government, Reward, and Political Debt

Normandy's governance is sustained by reciprocal performance. Extraordinary service may create promises and expectations involving:

- treasure;
- office;
- command;
- precedence;
- protection;
- marriage support;
- patronage;
- privilege;
- land or revenue;
- public recognition.

Reward must remain lawful, available, governable, and compatible with succession and local rights. Reward deferral requires a recorded reason, revised expectation, review, and possibility of dispute.

Reward debt may reduce willingness, increase demands for guarantees, strengthen household independence, create rivalry, or make future mandates more expensive. Reward debt does not automatically create treason.

## 18. Overmighty and Office-Capture Risk

Accumulated title, office, command, castle access, household strength, regional support, patronage, and reward claims may create concern without proving disloyalty.

Possible responses include:

- audit;
- narrower scope;
- divided jurisdiction;
- command rotation;
- stronger deputies;
- formal confirmation;
- guarantees;
- compensation;
- review frequency changes;
- voluntary surrender;
- lawful removal;
- mediation;
- negotiated restoration.

The response must consider current performance, authority source, political acceptance, replacement capacity, and the risk of damaging the very capacity Normandy needs.
