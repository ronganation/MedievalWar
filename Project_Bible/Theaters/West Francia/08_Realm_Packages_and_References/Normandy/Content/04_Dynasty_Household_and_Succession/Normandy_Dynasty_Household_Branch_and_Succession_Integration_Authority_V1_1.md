## 1. Integration Verdict

Normandy opens with a powerful ruling household and a recognized primary heir, but not with a self-executing succession system or one unified family bloc.

The Realm's dynastic order contains several politically connected but legally and institutionally distinct structures:

- House Normandy as the ruling dynasty;
- the ducal household as the ruler's operating household rather than the dynasty itself;
- <Person>Matilda of Flanders</Person>'s House Flanders birth identity and marriage integration;
- <Person>Robert Curthose</Person> as the protected underage primary heir;
- Richard and William Rufus as living supporting children under the current opening chronology;
- Henry Beauclerc and Adela as conditional future people rather than active opening characters;
- House Evreux as a cadet branch with its own countship, household, officers, dignity, and succession;
- Conteville kin as maternal half-sibling and relationship structure, not direct House Normandy descent;
- House FitzOsbern as a separate service dynasty and household;
- external dynastic connections that create access and opportunity without automatic alliance, resources, claims, or obedience.

Normandy's dynastic problem is not merely who inherits a title. Normandy must convert founder-centered authority into prepared people, accepted succession, functioning households, transferable offices, preserved relationships, and governable outcomes without collapsing distinct branches into one inventory.
