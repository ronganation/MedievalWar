## 1. Military Verdict

Normandy opens with a strong military culture, experienced leadership, fortified territorial practice, mounted elite capability, regional and household forces, and unusual potential to coordinate musters, siege work, transport, and occupation. Normandy does not open with a standing national army, infinite cavalry, a preassembled English invasion host, a universal levy pool, free Breton manpower, automatic Boulogne support, or ports that function as fleets without ships, crews, stores, weather, and access.

Normandy's military identity is **prepared combined-arms execution built from finite obligations, households, lordships, castles, ports, equipment, horses, officers, and recoverable recruitment pools**. Its primary risk is that high ambition can consume scarce trained troops, horses, ships, commanders, money, and political willingness faster than the Realm can replace them.

## 3. Purpose

This authority defines Normandy's military capability, roster families, recruitment sources, obligations, command interfaces, readiness, recovery, fortification, garrisons, siege practice, strategic access, maritime transport, expeditionary preparation, occupation, and demobilization.

It prevents:

1. turning military reputation into free forces;
2. using population directly as an army-manpower pool;
3. allowing unrestricted recruitment spam;
4. collapsing household, noble, regional, affinity, external, mercenary, and garrison sources;
5. treating ports as fleets or battlefield victory as occupation;
6. granting commanders forces or jurisdiction through character attributes alone;
7. preloading later conquest units, titles, rewards, or English holdings.

## 7. Normandy Military Identity

Normandy's opening military character should emphasize:

- disciplined mounted elite supported by other arms;
- castles and territorial strongpoints;
- experienced household and regional leadership;
- mustering and obligation management;
- siege, engineering, and fortified occupation capability;
- capable but finite infantry and missile support;
- ports and maritime preparation as potential rather than instant projection;
- combined Norman, regional, affinity, contracted, and external forces only when valid arrangements exist;
- commanders whose tactical roles and political authority remain distinct.

Normandy should not be homogenized into a generic feudal roster or reduced to cavalry-only play.

The ducal household may provide elite mounted and foot retainers, guards, officers, messengers, and selected specialists subject to actual inventory and readiness.

## 16. Formation and Tactical Identity

Normandy's tactical depth should reward role and formation quality rather than raw statistics alone.

Potential formation capabilities may include:

- disciplined spear or shield order;
- braced anti-mounted defense;
- controlled cavalry charge and recall;
- dismounted elite use where unit design supports it;
- reserve and relief maneuver;
- combined missile and melee support;
- siege assault and defensive stationing;
- reconnaissance and screening;
- pursuit discipline;
- mixed-contingent coordination.

Formation access requires suitable troops, training, officers, terrain, fatigue state, and AI understanding. A formation must not exist solely as a player-only exploit.

## 17. Horses, Equipment, and Military Production

Mounted capability requires:

- suitable horses;
- breeding, purchase, or trade sources;
- fodder;
- handlers and replacement animals;
- saddles, harness, weapons, and armor;
- trained riders;
- recovery after losses or hard campaigning.

Equipment availability depends on production, trade, treasury, maintenance, transport, and loss. Recruitment eligibility without equipment may create a delayed, downgraded, or blocked unit rather than instant full-strength deployment.

## 18. Castles and Garrisons

## 26. English Route Boundary

Normandy begins without an assembled English invasion force and without later English units, holdings, garrisons, grants, or offices.

An English campaign, if validly activated, must assemble forces from actual Norman, regional, affinity, external, and contracted sources. Losses deplete those sources. Victory does not replenish them.

English local recruitment or new military families require valid later control, political settlement, population and institution access, equipment, officers, obligations, and separate English owner authority.

## 27. Battle-to-Occupation Lifecycle

```text

```

A victorious army may be too depleted to occupy. A castle taken without garrison and supply may be lost. Military occupation remains distinct from lawful and durable government.

## 28. Casualties, Cadre, and Replenishment

Casualties affect:

- unit strength;
- experienced cadre;
- officer availability;
- household and regional politics;
- recruitment pools;
- families and labor;
- horses and equipment;
- future willingness;
- Historical Memory and Reputation.

Replenishment requires replacements from valid pools, equipment, officers, time, access, and supply. A surviving experienced cadre may improve reconstruction. Complete destruction may eliminate that advantage.

## 29. Demobilization and Recovery

- equipment custody;

- household and regional return;
- pool recovery;
- fatigue;
- grievances;
- future service availability.

Keeping forces mobilized extends readiness for immediate action but increases financial, supply, labor, household, and political burden.
