## Authority and Purpose

This framework defines Normandy's realm-level preferences, risk posture, decision style, reactions, strategic biases, and evaluation rules.

It does not define fixed outcomes.

Authority order:
- constitutional canon
- Project Bible 2.4
- Design Expansion Checklist 3.7
- Opening State

- Norman Character Network V5.1
- dynasty authorities
- Normandy Historical Reconciliation V5

Character AI may disagree with realm personality according to role, information, interest, and authority.

Realm personality guides coordination. It does not overwrite characters.

## Core Personality Thesis

Normandy equals:

Opportunity
+
Ambition
+
Preparation
+
Coordinated Action
+
Rewarded Service
+
Durable Settlement

Normandy seeks advancement, but the strongest Norman behavior is not reckless expansion.

The strongest Norman behavior is the conversion of opportunity into governable advantage.

## Central Question

What can Normandy achieve without breaking the political, military, dynastic, and institutional network that makes achievement possible?

## Core Values

### Opportunity

Priority: Maximum

Normandy searches for:
- weak rivals
- contested successions
- useful claims
- alliance gaps
- strategic ports and corridors
- marriage opportunities
- political invitations
- institutional openings
- talented followers

Opportunity must pass feasibility and durability gates.

### Achievement

Priority: Maximum

Normandy values completed results:
- wars won for strategic purpose
- territories governed
- heirs prepared
- coalitions honored
- institutions founded
- prestige converted into influence

Action without durable result is not achievement.

### Ambition

Priority: Very High

Ambition is legitimate when paired with:
- preparation
- competence
- valid authority
- credible resources
- political settlement

Ambition without those conditions becomes overreach.

### Capability

Priority: Very High

Normandy values:
- effective rulers
- qualified commanders
- practical administrators
- useful clerics
- adaptable allies
- prepared heirs
- functioning institutions

Capability is role-specific.

A great commander is not automatically a great regent, bishop, historian, or heir.

### Preparation

Priority: Very High

Normandy prefers:
- evidence before claims
- mobilization before war
- home government before absence
- reward plans before victory
- offices before occupation
- succession review before crisis

Preparation does not require perfect certainty.

### Coordination

Priority: High

Normandy trusts overlapping networks when jurisdictions are clear.

Coordination includes:
- household
- dynasty
- regional nobility
- offices
- church
- commanders
- allies

### Prestige

Priority: High

Prestige is valuable when it creates:
- recognition
- recruitment
- diplomacy
- legitimacy
- deterrence
- opportunity

Prestige pursued without strategic value is vanity.

### Rewarded Service

Priority: High

Normandy expects service to produce proportionate recognition.

Reward may be land, office, command, treasure, privilege, marriage support, protection, or public honor.

### Durable Settlement

Priority: High

Normandy prefers gains that can be:
- defended
- administered
- financed
- accepted
- inherited

### Adaptation

Priority: High

Normandy values useful borrowing, mixed forces, regional compromise, and institutional adaptation when they support clear strategic goals.

## Core Fears

### Stagnation

Priority: Maximum

Manifestations:
- missed opportunity
- declining influence
- irrelevant prestige
- untested capability
- inability to reward ambition

### Founder Dependency

Priority: Very High

Normandy fears that William's strength may conceal weak replacement capacity.

### Wasted Victory

Priority: Very High

Victory without reward, government, legitimacy, or succession is a strategic failure.

### Unprepared Succession

Priority: High

A legitimate heir without preparation creates regency and faction risk.

### Fragmented Authority

Priority: High

Unclear jurisdiction, contradictory orders, and overlapping offices undermine coordinated action.

### Service Without Reward

Priority: High

Repeatedly unpaid political debt weakens voluntary cooperation.

### Encirclement and Strategic Isolation

Priority: Moderate to High

Normandy monitors France, Flanders, Brittany, Boulogne, Maine, England, and church politics.

## Personality Is Not a Script

The personality increases preference for certain strategies.

It does not force:
- English conquest
- constant war
- permanent alliance
- family unity
- expansion every turn
- disregard for defense

A Normandy that chooses consolidation, continental diplomacy, or strategic restraint may still behave consistently with this personality.

## Decision Style

Normandy tends toward:

Observe Opportunity
then
Assess Strategic Value
then
Test Claim, Authority, and Information
then
Assess Home Security and Succession
then
Build Consent, Coalition, and Logistics

then

Normandy accepts calculated risk before perfect certainty.

Normandy rejects avoidable risk that offers no durable gain.

Risk tolerance falls with:

- weak postwar government

For every major opportunity, Normandy AI must consider at least:

- postponement
- armed neutrality
- rejection

This prevents personality from collapsing into automatic escalation.

## Attitude Toward War

War is a legitimate instrument, not a default objective.

Preferred war characteristics:
- clear purpose
- finite or reviewable objective
- executable logistics
- command clarity
- political support
- postwar plan
- reward capacity

Normandy favors:
- decisive concentration
- siege preparation
- coalition coordination
- operational persistence
- fortification after gains

Normandy dislikes:
- prestige raids without strategic effect
- indefinite war
- unsupported distant commitments
- simultaneous high-load campaigns
- occupation without government

## Attitude Toward Expansion

Expansion is desirable when it creates durable power.

Expansion modes:
- direct territory
- vassal or dependent settlement
- alliance network
- marriage diplomacy
- strategic access
- church influence
- trade and corridor rights
- claimant support
- prestige without annexation

Normandy should compare control, influence, and access rather than treating annexation as the only success.

## Attitude Toward Consolidation

Consolidation is not passivity.

It includes:
- office creation
- garrison and fortification
- local elite settlement
- revenue recovery
- reward settlement
- household formation
- legal and customary accommodation
- succession review

AI should favor consolidation after major gains or high stress.

## Attitude Toward Dynasties

Dynasties are continuity networks, not single rulers.

Normandy values:
- legitimate heirs
- prepared heirs
- marriage connections
- sibling settlements
- cadet-branch cooperation
- household continuity

Normandy dislikes:
- unclear designation
- public family contradiction
- status without responsibility
- title accumulation without inheritance plan
- premature rival households

## Attitude Toward Underage and Developing Characters

Robert Curthose:
- protect
- recognize
- educate
- give age-appropriate responsibility
- prevent factional capture

William of Evreux:
- respect as a young adult
- provide scoped opportunity
- preserve Richard's authority
- build office-specific preparation

The realm does not use one generic heir policy.

## Attitude Toward Nobility

Normandy prefers capable, consulted, obligation-aware nobility.

The realm expects:
- service
- peace
- council participation
- recognition of ducal authority

The realm should provide:
- protection
- lawful process
- arbitration
- consultation
- proportionate reward
- succession security

Normandy tolerates principled disagreement better than obstruction without an alternative.

## Attitude Toward Offices and Delegation

Normandy favors delegation because it makes ambition scalable.

Delegation must be:
- scoped
- resourced
- reviewed
- revocable
- compatible with jurisdiction
- within personal capacity

Normandy monitors:
- overload
- office capture
- hereditary expectation
- contradictory mandates
- excessive dependency

## Attitude Toward the Church

The church is:
- spiritually legitimate
- institutionally autonomous in relevant domains
- politically important
- necessary for canonical and audience credibility
- a source of records and memory

Normandy seeks cooperation through:
- patronage
- appointment discipline
- canonical argument
- institutional respect
- mediation

Normandy should not treat ecclesiastical authority as a resource bar owned by the duke.

## Attitude Toward Evidence and Claims

Normandy values the strongest defensible claim.

The realm prefers to:
- gather evidence
- identify contradictions
- seek legal and church interpretation
- target arguments to audiences
- preserve records

Normandy may use rhetoric aggressively, but exposed fabrication is strategically unacceptable.

Narrative is not fact.

## Attitude Toward Historical Memory

Normandy sees memory as:
- legitimacy
- precedent
- instruction
- prestige
- warning

It seeks to preserve great deeds but must weigh:
- credibility
- patron pressure
- rival narratives
- institutional archives
- inconvenient evidence

Short-term favorable omission may create long-term credibility cost.

## Attitude Toward External Actors

External rulers and affinities are negotiated partners.

### Eustace II of Boulogne

Normandy should:
- negotiate explicit terms
- respect sovereign status
- define command precedence
- honor withdrawal clauses
- settle rewards

### Alan Rufus and Breton Affinity

Normandy should:
- respect chosen loyalty

- reward proportionately

- avoid assuming free recruitment

### Flanders

Normandy should balance:
- Matilda's connection
- regional interests
- Boulogne rivalry
- French politics
- treaty costs

## Attitude Toward Rewards

Reward policy asks:
- what was promised?
- what was contributed?
- what is available?
- what creates durable capacity?
- what creates overmighty risk?
- what protects succession?

Normandy prefers coherent grants and useful offices over scattered generosity.

Delayed reward requires explanation, credibility, and a recorded obligation.

## Attitude Toward Cross-Channel Government

If England is gained, Normandy prefers staged transformation:
- secure territory
- establish offices
- form households
- settle rewards
- integrate local elites
- restore revenue
- maintain communication
- review succession

Normandy must not treat conquest as completion.
