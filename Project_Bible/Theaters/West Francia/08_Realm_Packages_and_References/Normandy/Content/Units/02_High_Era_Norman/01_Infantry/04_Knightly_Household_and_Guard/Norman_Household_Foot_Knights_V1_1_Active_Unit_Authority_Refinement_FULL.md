## 1. Strong Unit Verdict

Norman Household Foot Knights are retained as a separate active planned card because deliberate foot commitment changes the household's military problem. The Realm gives up mounted speed, charge impact, countercharge, pursuit, and rapid relocation in exchange for stability, close control, difficult-ground access, fortified-interior utility, and preservation of horses from unsuitable action.

The design fails its role-separation test if the card is implemented merely as Household Infantry with higher statistics, Miles Pedites with newer equipment, House Guard without a principal, or French Men-at-Arms with knightly labels. The defining player choice is whether the same scarce Household Knightly Service family should fight mounted or on foot for a bounded operation.

## 2. Designer One-Screen Card

- **Class:** High Era elite Norman household knightly foot formation.
- **Source:** Eligible Norman Household Knights and accepted associated knightly personnel, foot officers, standards, armor, shields, sidearms, approved reach or impact weapons, attendants, horse custodians, supply, medicine, transport, and lawful household authorization.
- **Battle promise:** Commit a compact elite body to terrain, fortifications, streets, gates, breaches, command areas, or crisis points where mounted action is unsuitable and ordinary household infantry lacks the required concentration.
- **Best use:** Breach consolidation, gate and courtyard action, fortified interiors, defended streets, difficult terrain, high-value counterstroke, command-area defense, and horse-preservation operations.
- **Worst use:** Cheap attrition, routine frontage, missile exposure, deep pursuit, broad anti-cavalry duty, prolonged unsupported assault, marsh, deep woods without guides, and missions better handled by Household Infantry or Castle Sergeants.
- **Strategic cost:** Removes elite riders from mounted service while retaining the cost of horse custody, household obligations, officers, armor, property, ransom exposure, and slow recovery.
- **Counterplay:** Missiles, fire, exhaustion, encirclement, reach weapons, loss of officers, broken passage, blocked withdrawal, repeated low-value engagements, simultaneous threats to horses and household command, and denial of supporting infantry.

## 3. Player-Facing Description

Norman Household Foot Knights are the dismounted fighting expression of Normandy's mature elite household cavalry. The warriors do not cease to belong to the mounted household family when fighting on foot. The warriors remain scarce, politically visible, expensive to equip, and connected to horses, officers, owners, standards, household duties, and ransom expectations.

The formation is chosen before battle or during a sufficiently controlled campaign reorganization. Mounts are secured with handlers at a safe rear position, returned to the household, or otherwise accounted for. The knights take foot-compatible weapons and form compact files intended for a specific mission. The formation may consolidate an opened breach, fight through a gate or courtyard, defend the command area, counterattack in constrained ground, or operate where slopes, mud, woods, obstacles, walls, or streets make mounted shock impractical.

Dismounting is not automatically superior. The household loses speed, rapid response, charge mass, pursuit, and easy withdrawal. Foot combat exposes rare knightly personnel to attritional melee and missile fire. A successful local action can still weaken the Realm if experienced riders, heirs, officers, armor, or horses cannot return to mounted readiness.

## 31. Battlefield Rhythm

1. Remain behind ordinary infantry or supporting arms.
2. Observe the specific objective and approach.
3. Avoid receiving unnecessary missile fire.
4. Enter only when access, terrain, and mission justify commitment.
5. Maintain compact file and officer relationships.
6. Secure the bounded objective or deliver the counterstroke.
7. Refuse uncontrolled dispersal and deep pursuit.
8. Consolidate, relieve, or withdraw.
9. Recover wounded, standards, equipment, and sensitive property.
10. Reconcile horse custody and mounted return.

## 32. Supported Assault Doctrine

The formation should not normally be the first expendable body into an unknown breach. Engineers, missiles, shields, lighter assault troops, local guides, or ordinary infantry may need to create information and access first.

Household Foot Knights commit when the opening has enough value and viability to justify elite risk. The formation then secures, expands, or holds the access against counterattack.

## 33. Defensive Doctrine

The formation can defend a gate, courtyard, command area, street, or narrow strongpoint. Defensive strength depends on preparation, route, support, fatigue, missile exposure, fire, flank security, and relief.

The unit does not automatically fight to extinction. Withdrawal, negotiation, surrender, or protection of household continuity may be preferable to destruction.

## 34. Anti-Cavalry Boundary

Dismounted knights can exploit obstacles, dense ranks, shields, shortened lances, spears, and difficult ground. The formation is still not a dedicated spear or pike unit.

## 35. Missile and Fire Counterplay

Armor and shields reduce but do not erase missiles. Crossbows, repeated arrow fire, elevated fire, flank fire, damaged shields, and confined routes remain serious threats. Fire, smoke, heat, and collapsing structures can force movement or separation.

The formation needs supporting missiles, pavises, mantlets, terrain, rapid passage, or suppression when crossing exposed ground.

## 36. Melee Counterplay

The unit remains vulnerable to:

- Deep fresh formations

- Encirclement
- File compression

- Repeated low-value engagements

## 44. Siege Defense

Strong assignments include:

- Inner gate reserve
- Courtyard counterstroke
- Breach containment
- Command-area defense
- Recovery of wounded officers
- Controlled withdrawal between defensive layers
- Counterattack against an exposed assault body

The formation does not replace permanent Castle Sergeants, wall watches, engineers, or ordinary garrison infantry.

## 45. Siege Offense

Strong assignments include:

- Consolidating a breach after access is achieved
- Securing an inner gate
- Protecting engineers during immediate counterattack
- Holding captured access
- Conducting a bounded elite assault after preparation
- Defending the command and rally point inside captured works

Ladders and unknown breaches remain high-risk. Elite status does not eliminate gravity, missiles, fire, congestion, or collapse.

## 46. Urban Combat

Courtyards, halls, markets, wider streets, gates, and defended intersections can support the formation. Alleys, rooftops, crowds, civilians, fire, smoke, locked passages, stairs, and blocked exits may fragment the unit.

Local guides and route knowledge can matter more than nominal armor advantage.

## 63. Positive Validation Fixtures

- Mounted Household Knights become unavailable when the same people receive the foot assignment.
- Horses remain visible in custody and consume support.
- AI prefers foot service for a valid confined or fortified mission.
- AI prefers mounted service when open maneuver and countercharge are more valuable.
- Norman Men-at-Arms remain the broader mature heavy-infantry line.
- Norman Castle Sergeants remain tied to custody and fortified institutional duty.
- Armored Sergeants remain the professional spear line.
- People preserve wounds and experience when changing assignment.
- Returning to mounted service requires mounts, pairing, equipment, and training.
- Recruitment closure preserves veteran Early Era formations.

## 64. Negative Validation Fixtures

- Mounted and foot duplicates appear simultaneously.
- Horses become free cavalry mounts while riders fight on foot.
- A research click creates knights or armor.
- A castle automatically recruits Household Foot Knights.
- Foot Knights replace Norman Men-at-Arms through higher values.
- Knightly status automatically creates castle custody or protected-access authority.
- AI instant-dismounts after contact.
- Foot Knights receive hidden anti-cavalry immunity.

- Normandy shares England or France recruitment pools.
