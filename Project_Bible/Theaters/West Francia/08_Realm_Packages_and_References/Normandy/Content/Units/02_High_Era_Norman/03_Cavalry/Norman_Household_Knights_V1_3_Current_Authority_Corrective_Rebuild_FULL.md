## 2. Strong Unit Verdict

Norman Household Knights should survive as a **Norman household elite shock family**, not as Normandy's final cavalry upgrade. The unit is created through qualified Norman households, lordship, horse supply, wealth, service relationships, campaign experience, and owner-approved elite household development inside Normandy.

The battlefield identity is a deliberately concentrated decisive reserve. These knights are more specialized toward a prepared high-value impact than the flexible Norman Knightly Cavalry. Their strength is conditional on intelligence, target fixation, firm approach ground, lance condition, horse and rider pairing, officer timing, protected flanks, and a viable exit. Their regional prestige cannot substitute for sustainment.

## 3. One-Screen Player Card

- **Class:** High Era Norman elite household heavy shock cavalry.
- **Source:** Qualified elite Norman household knightly households and service networks, regionally suitable riders, military horses, remounts, lances, swords, shields, armor, conditional horse protection, officers, grooms, farriers, forage, water, routes, staging points, finance, and accepted Normandy authority.
- **Availability:** Extremely narrow, household-bound, politically visible, horse intensive, and slow to recover.
- **Battle promise:** Deliver one of the roster's strongest conditional impacts against fixed, exposed, tired, moving, or disordered high-value targets, then disengage before elite assets become trapped.
- **Best use:** Firm open ground, prepared flank attack, reserve counterstroke, exploitation of missile or infantry fixation, destruction of exposed armored targets, command disruption, and decisive late commitment.
- **Worst use:** Fresh spear or pike fronts, concentrated crossbows, stakes, ditches, walls, narrow streets, mud, marsh, dense woods, forced crossings, hidden reserves, and prolonged fixed melee.
- **Strategic cost:** Concentrates elite riders, heirs and clients, household military horses, remounts, armor, barding, officers, household security, prestige, ransom exposure, staging points, forage, and future breeding or training capacity.
- **Counterplay:** Deny approach ground, screen with reach weapons, preserve reserves, expose the unit to missiles, attack remount and forage systems, block disengagement, force premature commitment, or create simultaneous regional and household threats.

## 4. Player-Facing Description

Norman Household Knights are the elite mounted expression of Norman household military power matured in Normandy. Their identity does not come from carrying a larger statistic than northern knights. It comes from a household military world created through lordship, estate and castle relationships, elite household service, horse and remount depth, equipment ownership, household competition, and repeated campaign experience.

On suitable ground, the unit is a devastating but finite instrument. Its officers wait until infantry, missiles, terrain, maneuver, or another cavalry body has exposed the target. The formation aligns, controls pace, preserves cohesion, and commits to a narrow battlefield purpose. A successful impact can open the decisive gap, break a counterattack, scatter a compromised elite body, or rescue a threatened battle plan. The next challenge is disengagement. A charge that wins contact but loses horses, officers, lances, exit space, or household leadership may still be a strategic defeat.

These knights are not a reward Normandy receives merely for retaining Norman identity. Normandy may preserve cultural and dynastic connections, provide migrants, exchange techniques, or interact with elite Norman household powers, but the formation itself needs a valid Norman household source. Final ownership belongs with future Normandy roster and military authorities.

## 38. Anti-Armor Boundary

The legacy anti-armor direction is preserved as strong charge performance against selected protected targets. Armor piercing is not guaranteed. Angle, lance integrity, speed, target formation, shield, armor construction, horse confidence, and impact geometry determine results.

## 39. Countercharge

The unit can countercharge enemy cavalry already committed, exposed, or disordered. Detection, alignment, reserve timing, mount condition, officer control, and support matter. It does not automatically defeat equally elite or specialized cavalry.

## 40. Infantry Cooperation

Spearmen fix and protect. Crossbows pressure armor and reserves. Norman Men-at-Arms or other suitable heavy infantry hold command-area relationships when present and eligible. Mounted Sergeants screen. Norman Raiders handle broader reconnaissance and economical pursuit. Norman Household Knights deliver the narrow decisive impact once other arms create the opportunity.

## 41. Disengagement

Disengagement requires viable horses, officers, spacing, limited fixation, protected lanes, friendly support, and rally ground. Charge success without safe disengagement can destroy household military and political capital.

## 42. Pursuit Restraint

Pursuit can capture leaders, horses, standards, baggage, and prisoners but risks ambush, exhaustion, fragmentation, abandonment of the household command, and loss of remount access. Routine pursuit belongs to cheaper cavalry when available.

## 43. Fixed Melee Boundary

Once fixed, lances lose utility, horses lose movement, sidearms dominate, armor fatigue rises, and expensive casualties accumulate. Doctrine prioritizes exit, recovery, command protection, and renewed threat over prolonged mounted melee.

## 44. Dismount Boundary
