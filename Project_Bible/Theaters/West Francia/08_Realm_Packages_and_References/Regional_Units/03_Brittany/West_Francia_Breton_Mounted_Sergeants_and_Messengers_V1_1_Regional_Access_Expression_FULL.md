## 2. Evidence and Chronological Boundary

The France-Breton portfolio identifies Breton mounted reconnaissance and screening as a strong regional variant or contingent candidate. It lists reconnaissance, screening, route and crossing security, flank connection, pursuit, and rapid message or coalition linkage while requiring collision review against French mounted scouts, Castle Mounted Sergeants, and Norman mounted service. It prohibits a universal Breton cavalry bonus, automatic javelin-cavalry identity, and unsupported feigned-flight mechanics. citeturn33search97

The Brittany exact roster authority explicitly defines Breton Mounted Sergeants and Messengers as a Brittany-specific MobileCavalry family sourced from household, castle, or office service. It gives the family narrow availability, slow recovery, lighter protection, shield, spear, sidearm, riding or military horses, and the primary uses of screening, escort, reconnaissance, pursuit, and message security. citeturn33search125

The broader Brittany military authority similarly defines Mounted Sergeants and Messengers as household, castle, or lordship cavalry used for screening, pursuit, escort, communication, and secondary charge, with mount and equipment bottlenecks and no elite-shock identity. citeturn33search126

This profile does not claim:

- automatic French access in January 1062;
- one standardized Breton corps;
- universal javelins, thrown spears, feigned flight, mail, helmets, shields, or heraldry;
- that every messenger, castle rider, retainer, lordship rider, or horse owner is militarily eligible;
- that all Breton owners answer one ducal or French request;
- superiority over France's native mounted families;
- permanent recruitment or replenishment in Paris.

## 6. One-Screen Player Card

- **Unit:** Breton Mounted Sergeants and Messengers
- **Class:** Narrow mobile regional mounted service
- **Ownership:** The contributing Breton household, castle, office, lordship, or frontier owner retains people, horses, equipment, replacement pools, command claims, and recall rights
- **Availability:** Narrow, source-specific, horse-dependent, duty-constrained, politically conditional, and not automatically available at opening
- **Battle promise:** Connect separated contingents, screen movement, secure routes, protect messages, escort commanders and vulnerable elements, intercept hostile observers, and pursue already disrupted enemies
- **Best use:** Coalition flank connection, march screening, commander and messenger escort, route security, counter-observation, rapid warning, controlled pursuit, and limited secondary charges
- **Worst use:** Heavy shock combat, prepared spears, intact knightly cavalry, prolonged melee, unsupported deep pursuit, and distant operations without relay, fodder, or return
- **Strategic tradeoff:** The requesting Realm gains a versatile regional screen while Brittany loses mounted communication, local warning, escort, frontier connection, horses, and rapid response
- **Counterplay:** Combat cavalry, layered missiles, ambush, route denial, severed relays, false reports, horse fatigue, blocked return, owner recall, and threats to the source region

## 28. Battlefield Rhythm

1. Establish contact with the contributing Breton commander, adjacent allied wing, relay points, and return route.
2. Move in pairs, files, or a dispersed screen ahead of or between coalition components.
3. Inspect roads, crossings, terrain, likely concealment, enemy observers, and threatened communication lines.
4. Deliver reports and maintain flank connection before seeking combat.
5. Escort commanders, messengers, missiles, baggage, or vulnerable formations when assigned.
6. Intercept enemy scouts or exposed light threats only when withdrawal remains secure.
7. Commit a secondary charge against an already disrupted or flank-presenting target when support and mission permit.
8. Pursue briefly under control, then break contact before the detachment scatters.
9. Rally, report, reconnect the coalition, and preserve riders and horses for the next mission.

## 29. Strengths

- Combines screening, escort, communication, route security, and controlled pursuit in one native Breton service family.
- More combat-capable than France's broad utility Demesne Mounted Scouts.
- More information, relay, and coalition-connection oriented than France's Castle Mounted Sergeants.
- Can connect separately commanded Breton and French components without erasing ownership.
- Useful against enemy scouts, messengers, skirmishers, isolated light cavalry, and broken troops.
- Owner-specific sources and partial Breton response create meaningful campaign variation.
- Veteran guides, messengers, leaders, and horse-care personnel have persistent value.

## 30. Weaknesses

- Narrow, owner-specific, horse-dependent, and slow to recover.
- Multiple Breton owners can reduce, delay, separately command, or recall service.
- Inferior to French Castle Mounted Sergeants in sustained combat protection and to Vavassors or knights in shock.
- Vulnerable to prepared spears, heavy cavalry, concentrated missiles, ambush, and blocked routes.
- Wide dispersion and mixed horses can weaken cohesion.
- Field release damages Breton messages, escort, warning, frontier, and coalition-assembly capacity.
- Reports can be delayed, contradictory, captured, stale, or lost.
