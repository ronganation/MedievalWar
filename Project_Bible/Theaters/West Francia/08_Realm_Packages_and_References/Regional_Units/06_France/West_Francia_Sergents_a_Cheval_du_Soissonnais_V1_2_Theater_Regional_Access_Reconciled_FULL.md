The Sergents à Cheval du Soissonnais are a regionally sourced, nonknightly mounted contingent associated with the valley of Soissons. Their battlefield identity is not routine mounted service and not a cheaper knightly charge. They enter the dangerous opening phase of battle, disturb or fix the enemy, absorb the first violent response, and create an opportunity for selected knightly forces to follow.

The card carries genuine strategic cost. Riders, horses, equipment, local leadership, provider trust, regional duties, and formation memory all belong to a bounded Soissonnais source. A realm with access can command the contingent under valid terms, but cannot refill it from a generic national cavalry pool.

## 5. Player-Facing Description

Sergents à Cheval du Soissonnais are mounted nonknightly combatants drawn from a bounded Soissonnais regional source and released into an authorized host. They are used to engage first, disturb the enemy’s alignment, force an early response, and create the moment for following knightly cavalry to strike.

They are capable mounted fighters but do not replace the principal knightly charge. Their purpose is to bear the opening risk and shape the engagement. Severe horse losses can leave individual survivors fighting locally on foot, but the formation loses mobility, cohesion, charge capability, and much of its battlefield contract when dismounted.

## 22. Signature Battle Cycle

```text
Confirm regional and host command
-> inspect riders, horses, equipment, signals, and rally route
-> form ahead of or adjacent to the following knightly force
-> identify the enemy element whose disruption will create the strongest opening
-> advance and initiate controlled contact
-> force reaction, disturb alignment, or fix the enemy
-> absorb the first counterattack risk without pursuing beyond support
-> yield the lane as the knightly follow-up commits
-> continue limited local action, withdraw, or fight for survival after horse loss
-> rally behind the main force
-> recover riders, horses, equipment, and regional cohesion
```

## 23. Strengths

- Distinctive regional source and battlefield role.
- Strong battle-opening and disruption utility.
- Useful coordination with Milites, Bacheliers, Vavassors, or another valid following force.
- Able to force an early enemy reaction.
- Regional cohesion and veteran formation memory where preserved.
- Potential local resilience after horse losses.

## 24. Weaknesses

- Narrow regional recruitment and replacement source.
- High exposure to horse and rider casualties in the opening engagement.
- Less protection, consistency, and shock weight than recognized knights.
- Weak as a prolonged unsupported melee formation.
- Vulnerable to prepared spears, layered missile support, fresh heavy cavalry, bad terrain, and failed follow-up.
- Relationship loss can close recruitment.
- Recovery is slow and cannot use a generic France pool.
